# Steven Bonta’s partial decipherment: source review and conditional tests

Steven Bonta proposes that the Indus script combines phonetic and logographic writing with notations of assets and measures, and that its principal language is early Indo-Aryan. He starts with recurring inscription fields, proposes values for selected signs from metrological and graphic clues, and cross-checks those values against Sanskrit words, names and titles. The [first-pass review](../../research/literature/2026-10-08-bonta-first-pass/README.md) already describes that argument and the twelve lectures. This package adds a claim ledger, candidate concordance, corpus tests, a lexical permutation test and an audit of the claimed ICIT check.

The strongest reproduced evidence concerns sign position and restricted clustering. In 2,469 selected objects, the jar sign occurs 1,196 times and is final 838 times, or 70.07%. The arrow is final in 192 of 225 occurrences, or 85.33%. Neither rate is typical of internally shuffled versions of the same texts. The elementary fish proxy has 757 runs, none longer than three and none repeating a retained code. These observations support the proposed structural distinctions. A complete replication of P/M/C/T fields remains unavailable because the manuscript page text omits its sign images and the field boundaries are not supplied as data. [Distribution output](run-2026-10-08-v1/distributions.json), `populations.longest_eligible_object_faces`, `principal_reference_tests`, `fish_proxy`.

The six-role lexical panel produces whole dictionary matches for 9 of 10 reading types. Under the fixed normalization, 13 of 720 permutations score at least nine, a conditional tail fraction of 0.01806. The alternative exact-headword endpoint, which still permits the prescribed value variants, gives 3/720, or 0.004167. This is an excess relative to this particular permutation model. Bonta selected these examples and values during development, and the test does not reproduce that search or his acceptance of plausible unattested names and historical repairs. His prefix-intersection example also retains 35 dictionary candidates under our rule. These results leave the language and the proposed sounds unestablished. [Lexical output](run-2026-10-08-v1/lexical.json), `normalized`, `exact`, `consistency_intersection`; [frozen panel](selection.json).

The available sources do not establish a held-out validation rate. Bonta does not identify the entire claimed 12–15 readable ICIT set or the number of attempted readings. The paper contains ICIT-labelled examples that also have Mahadevan references. Manual inspection confirms three repeated catalogue objects among six parser overlap candidates, rejects two parser overlaps and identifies one catalogue conflict. There are eight same-entry MH cross-references, seven without an explicit question mark. Their membership in the lecture’s validation set is unknown. No inscription in the available inputs has an independently measured quantity joined to Bonta’s proposed M-field values, so the physical metrology test has zero usable joins. [Validation audit](validation-audit.json); [metrology output](run-2026-10-08-v1/metrology.json).

They describe this transcription population, not uncertainty about damaged signs or the surviving archaeological record. ## Readings and lexical chance

The Monier-Williams dictionary was acquired from `sanskrit-lexicon/csl-orig` at commit `55e8addbd96e8d9001b8789026b026763f4049c1`. The repository’s opened LICENSE specifies CC BY-SA 4.0. The source hash is recorded with the run. All lexical payloads and per-query matches stay local. The input has 286,525 k1 rows, 194,059 distinct alphabetic headwords and 171,644 normalized headword classes. Thirty-two k1 rows fail the alphabetic screen. Repeated k1 rows explain the remainder of the difference. [Source and licence](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-orig); [manifest](run-2026-10-08-v1/manifest.json); [lexical output](run-2026-10-08-v1/lexical.json).

The panel freezes six roles: MA, TRA, PALA, DHANA, PU and PATI. Ten deduplicated role sequences come from the paper’s MS discussion and Tables 13–17, segments 52–53 and 67–75. The six bundles of Table 18 alternatives are permuted as whole bundles across the same six roles. Every one of the 720 bijections is evaluated on the same ten strings, including the observed assignment.

Whole-headword normalization collapses vowel length, stop voicing and aspiration within place of articulation, the three sibilants, and retroflex/dental n. Dental and retroflex stops remain distinct. Only the fixed alternatives in [selection.json](selection.json) may be generated, with maha restricted to the first role. No added consonants, substring matches or semantic substitutions are allowed. SU/so equivalence is absent from the implementation because no retained role is SU. The exact endpoint compares whole generated forms to headwords without normalization, but retains the listed alternative values. It should not be described as a one-form or one-sound test.

| Fixed endpoint | Observed matched types | Permutation mean | Assignments scoring at least as well | Tail fraction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Normalized whole headword | 9/10 | 4.85833 | 13/720 | 0.0180556 |
| Exact whole headword among fixed variants | 9/10 | 3.93333 | 3/720 | 0.0041667 |

The unmatched type is DHANA–PALA–PATI. A dictionary hit is credited whenever one allowed form exists; this can credit a different permitted form from the one emphasized by Bonta. The test does not filter words by Vedic date, name/title usage or suitability to an inscription. Conversely, it does not accept unattested plausible compounds, ligature-specific omissions, syllable metathesis or other historical repairs found elsewhere in his readings. The six-role coverage indicator is 6/55 = 10.91% of the stated sign count, with pooled allographs; it is not token or inscription coverage. Thus this result assesses a bounded lexical condition on a source-selected panel, rather than his complete matching procedure.

The L2 consistency calculation asks for a nonempty prefix X such that X plus each of the DHANA, MA and PALA suffix bundles is a dictionary word after the same normalization. It retains 35 prefix classes, including the normalized PU/bhu class. Across all 20 unordered three-bundle sets from the six roles, candidate counts range from 18 to 60. Reordering the same three suffixes cannot change their intersection. Neither this count nor the L1 tail includes the author’s earlier trials, abandoned CV method, choice of roots or selection of examples. A confirmatory next test needs values and finite acceptance rules frozen before object-level reserved texts are selected. [Output](run-2026-10-08-v1/lexical.json), `consistency_intersection`; [protocol](PROTOCOL.md).

## Metrology and evaluation criteria

The paper, segments 23–24, compares seven fish entries with the binary multiples 1–64 and three oval entries with 160/320/640. This links two inventory sizes and the non-repetition property. It does not assign each graphic entry a independently checked mass, and it supplies no object-level correspondence between an M field and weighed contents.

G’s corrected Dholavira analysis estimates 13.716 g with signal CV 1.591% for 90 intact certain 5–100 g weights and 14.280 g with CV 5.777% for 147 weights in the 1.3–5 g band. Its western compatibility counts fail their chance comparison. These physical distributions are useful constraints on future unit proposals, but there are no source-qualified joins to Bonta’s signs here. Fitting the same masses again would add no sign-value evidence. [G results](../2026-10-06-weight-metrology/runs/2026-10-07-review-response-v4/mass-band-estimates.json), `fits`; [metrology gate](run-2026-10-08-v1/metrology.json), `usable_inscription_physical_quantity_joins=0`.

Mukhopadhyay’s 2019 [*Interrogating Indus inscriptions to unravel their mechanisms of meaning conveyance*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-019-0274-1) analyzes functional classes, connective and phrase-final signs, and numerical/metrological collocations. It provides a precedent for separating document-specific syntax from linguistic syntax without assigning phonetic values or choosing a language. Its numerical-class interpretation does not authenticate the Sanskrit anchor words. The available external-quantity constraint remains the same: freeze a graphic map, locate an independently counted or measured object, and test the predicted quantity. [Sources](sources.json), `Muk19`; [prior numerical-source audit](../../research/methods/NUMERICAL_CONSTRAINT_AUDIT.md).

Lecture 12 proposes six diagnostic questions. Applying them gives the following assessment; the questions themselves are source-located in the ledger.

Anna Morpurgo Davies’s [“Decipherment”](https://www.ling-phil.ox.ac.uk/files/amd_decipherment_encyclopedia_of_linguistics_v._i_2nd_ed_2003.pdf), *International Encyclopedia of Linguistics*, 2003, pp. 417–422, emphasizes testing results against internal and external context, linguistic coherence and historical/geographical compatibility (p. 420). She also warns about a purely etymological method. Bonta supplies internal comparisons; independent referents, chronology of reconstructed forms and reserved-text prediction remain open. The current numerical tests address structure and a limited lexical condition.

Mahadevan’s author essay [*The Indus Script*](https://www.harappa.com/script/maha15.html), “Parpola’s Methodology of Decipherment,” sets out four requirements for a rebus: recognizable pictured object, phonetic reuse, contextual meaning and linguistically satisfactory homophones. For Bonta’s anchors, the pictured object is partly interpretable, but the intended referent and phonetic reuse are inferred together. Field position and Sanskrit homophones supply hypotheses; an external object/quantity or bilingual join would discriminate among them. These criteria also expose the same weaknesses in earlier Dravidian rebus proposals.

## Precedence, responses and reusable work

Structural segmentation, positional grammar and rebus reasoning precede the 2024 value system. Mahadevan’s concordance and grammar, Parpola’s structural grids and Dravidian readings, and Fairservis’s functional grids and numeral proposals provide earlier approaches. Fairservis’s opened [1992 book](https://mc.dlib.nyu.edu/files/books/brill_awdl000035/brill_awdl000035_lo.pdf), PDF pp. 30, 57–58, 70–77 and 84, discusses suffixing, terminal honorifics, numerical relations and storage tokens. The specific language and sign readings differ; shared distributional tools do not adjudicate those readings.

Rao’s 2018 [*The Indus Script and Economics*](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/The-Indus-Script.pdf), printed pp. 518 and 523–525, explicitly follows Wells and Bonta in dividing patterned and complex texts. Its four-part field model assigns quantities to the medial field, commodities to the core and a transactional function to terminals. This is an opened scholarly use of Bonta’s earlier structural work, with different semantic hypotheses. Wells’s 2012 and 2015 books are identified in Rao’s bibliography; their full contents were not newly reviewed here.

Sengupta’s 2023 [*Fish Symbolism in Indus Valley Epigraphy and Protohistoric Accounts*](https://journal.fi/store/article/download/110917/88837), p. 90, describes Bonta’s weight/mina interpretation alongside competing fish readings and stresses their lack of general verification. Khanna and Merriam’s [2025 publisher page](https://www.ijcaonline.org/archives/volume187/number64/a-computational-analysis-of-the-indus-script-identifying-sign-functions-in-logo-syllabic-writing-systems/) cites Bonta 2010 in reference 7; only its abstract and references were inspected. These are citations to earlier work, not independent tests of his 2024 sound assignments.

Yajnadevam’s [2024 manuscript](https://rarebooksocietyofindia.org/book_archive/A_cryptanalytic_decipherment_of_the_Indu.pdf), PDF pp. 1, 11 and 26, presents an allograph-reduced proto-abugida Sanskrit proposal and regular-expression/set-intersection method. It cites Bonta’s compounding argument, while its script model differs from Bonta’s logosyllabic/notational account. The shared language target and lexical filtering cannot function as independent corroboration. The requested 2023 Bonta presentation and hosted Yajnadevam video could not be opened, so their content was not reconstructed from titles.

Kalyanaraman’s [*Interpretation is not decipherment*](https://www.academia.edu/113408961/Interpretation_is_not_decipherment), dated 13 January 2024 in the opened page, challenges the step from Bonta’s functional classes to metrological/phonetic readings and advances his own Meluhha/metals interpretation. It supplies a competing proposal, not a controlled replication. [Language Log’s March 2025 discussion](https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=68508) includes Yves Rehbein’s chance-match concern and Martin Schwartz’s stated lack of conviction; these are comments, not peer-reviewed assessments. The Sanskrit objection in the Harappa interview is the interviewer Omar Khan’s question, and should not be attributed verbatim to Parpola. No exact November 2023 Elst post or opened formal peer-reviewed review of the complete 2024 value system was found in the bounded search. [Sources](sources.json) records access and failed searches.

This search supports crediting Bonta’s earlier field analysis and evaluating his particular later anchor system. It does not support a priority claim for field segmentation, Sanskrit readings, lexical intersections or metrological interpretation in general. The project can reuse his field categories as proposed object-ledger features once each field has an explicit sign inventory, boundary rule and exception treatment. The clean next replications are the full seven-entry fish rule, the oval order, the conditional 033 suffix claim, and the U-plus-number secondary-face limit. They require the missing figures and source-qualified identities before new outcomes are scored.
