# Steven Bonta’s partial decipherment: revised source review

Bonta proposes that the Indus script records an early Indo-Aryan language through phonetic signs, logograms and asset or measure notations. His strongest reproducible evidence here concerns sign position. Among 2,469 selected objects, the jar is final in 838/1,196 occurrences (70.07%) and the arrow in 192/225 (85.33%). Both strongly exceed within-text shuffle expectations. The exceptions matter: 358 jar tokens (29.9%) and 33 arrow tokens (14.7%) are non-final. The strong final tendencies reproduce; a categorical final-position rule fails. Fish signs also cluster, with 135 adjacent pairs against a shuffle mean of 54.5505. These observations support positional structure. [Frozen distributions](run-2026-10-08-v1/distributions.json), `principal_reference_tests`; [exception counts](response-2026-10-08-v2/terminal-exceptions.json), `signs`; [fish adjacency](response-2026-10-08-v2/fish-adjacency.json).

The lexical evidence is weak because the tested examples helped construct the values. Nine of ten selected strings match dictionary headwords exactly. All 13 permutations reaching at least nine normalized matches keep Bonta’s PU bundle; none of the 600 that move it reaches nine. Eight strings contain PU or PATI, whose values he reports deriving by searching this dictionary against those same contexts. The 13/720 tail is therefore close to circular for these signs. Holding both values fixed gives tails of 8/24 normalized and 2/24 exact; dropping their eight strings leaves only two examples, with tails of 8/24 and 4/24. These Exploratory sensitivities provide little evidence beyond the construction procedure. [Lexical sensitivities](response-2026-10-08-v2/lexical-sensitivities.json), `endpoints`; B24 segments 69–75; Lecture 7, 5:28–11:34; [review census](review/role-pair-census.json).

The available sources leave the validation rate, complete field boundaries and physical metrology unresolved. Three repeated catalogue objects have different uses in the paper: one ICIT-first reading, one alias within an MH-first reading and one iconography remark. Three other overlaps are parser errors. The lecture’s 12–15 readable inscriptions and attempted/failure denominators remain unidentified. Missing manuscript figures prevent full field reconstruction, and zero source-qualified inscription-to-physical-quantity joins are available. [Corrected audit](response-2026-10-08-v2/validation-audit-v2.json); [metrology gate](run-2026-10-08-v1/metrology.json), `usable_inscription_physical_quantity_joins`.

## Precedence and reusable work

Field segmentation, positional grammar and rebus methods precede the later Bonta value system. Fairservis’s [*The Harappan Civilization and Its Writing: A Model for the Decipherment of the Indus Script*](https://mc.dlib.nyu.edu/files/books/brill_awdl000035/brill_awdl000035_lo.pdf), Brill (1992), PDF pages 30, 57–58, 70–77 and 84, discusses suffixing, honorifics, numerical relations and storage tokens. Mahadevan and Parpola provide earlier concordance/structural frameworks. Rao’s [*The Indus Script and Economics*](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/The-Indus-Script.pdf) (2018), printed pages 518 and 523–525, explicitly follows Wells and Bonta in distinguishing patterned and complex texts, with different semantic hypotheses. These opened precedents support crediting Bonta’s field analysis without a general priority claim for segmentation or metrology.

Sengupta’s [*Fish Symbolism in Indus Valley Epigraphy and Protohistoric Accounts*](https://journal.fi/store/article/download/110917/88837), *Studia Orientalia Electronica* 11(1) (2023), 86–102, discusses Bonta’s mina proposal on p. 90. Khanna and Merriam’s [2025 publisher page](https://www.ijcaonline.org/archives/volume187/number64/a-computational-analysis-of-the-indus-script-identifying-sign-functions-in-logo-syllabic-writing-systems/), *International Journal of Computer Applications* 187(64), cites Bonta 2010 in reference 7. These references concern earlier work. Yajnadevam’s [2024 manuscript](https://rarebooksocietyofindia.org/book_archive/A_cryptanalytic_decipherment_of_the_Indu.pdf), PDF pages 1, 11 and 26, proposes an allograph-reduced Sanskrit proto-abugida and lexical set intersections, citing Bonta’s compounding argument.
