Which parts of Steven C. Bonta's partial-decipherment proposal can be reproduced as observations, and do a specified subset of his lexical matches exceed a fixed chance comparison? The prior [first pass](../../research/literature/2026-10-08-bonta-first-pass/README.md) supplies the lecture inventory and argument outline. This package adds a claim ledger, explicit catalogue mappings, distribution calculations, a lexical permutation harness and an audit of the claimed ICIT check.

Inputs are the twelve supplied auto-caption witnesses and the supplied 2024 second-edition page text in `data/raw/2026-10-08-bonta-review/`; the pinned Lipi observational export; Mayig lines and its direct candidate crosswalk; and the reviewed Helsinki 1979 interface. Captions are not audio-verified. The page text lacks figures. A form-feed segment index will identify passages without pretending that its index is a verified printed page. A PDF arriving later can resolve images in a separately dated amendment.

Read before execution: repository instructions, README, knowledge index, writing and redistribution rules, corpus report, contribution assessment, relevant registry entries and retrospectives; D2, G and H reports; and the Frenez caption check. D2's published terminal-sign figures and G's mass fits are reused comparisons, not new blind outcomes. No old endpoint is rerun or changed.

## Lexical rule and selection safeguards

The paper's Table 18 and Lecture 7 allow vowel length, aspiration and voicing variation; Lecture 8 also allows sibilant and su/so variation. The primary normalization therefore collapses long/short vowels, voiced/unvoiced/aspirated consonants within Sanskrit stop classes, sibilants, and su/so. It retains consonant order and number, nasal identity except retroflex/dental n, and all other vowels. It permits only the contracted alternatives expressly listed in Table 18 (for example pra/para and kra/kara), implemented as fixed variants of the assigned value. No arbitrary substring, added consonant, synonym, meaning or invented name is accepted. Exact matching is a stricter secondary endpoint.

Bonta also accepts plausible unattested compounds, special ligatures, multiple readings and historical sound changes. These have no finite general acceptance rule in the available witness. L1 will consequently be a bounded necessary lexical check, not a complete replication of his own discretionary acceptance procedure. Coverage and exclusions must accompany its result. Source-derived text rows, mappings that reconstruct Lipi sequences and per-permutation or per-query details stay local. Tracked outputs contain identifiers, our annotations and aggregates.

The panel will be selected from legible proposed transcriptions, not from dictionary hits. Repeated occurrences of the same proposed sequence will be deduplicated for scoring. At least one analysis will use a small anchor panel with identifiable sign roles; larger value-label-only panels will be labelled conditional and Exploratory. Input hashes and the panel selection manifest will precede scores. Newly acquired sources and software are pinned with hashes. Scripts refuse to overwrite a run directory.

## Source and reporting rules

Record exact source titles, authors, year, venue, URLs, access route and failures in `sources.json`. Use paraphrases throughout; at most one short quotation below 15 words per source. Include no bulk third-party payload in this owned directory. Every ledger row has a source location, offered evidence, testability and test ID. Repeated examples supporting the same assertion may be grouped, but every individual proposed value and every selected text reading needs a row. Any incomplete exhaustiveness must be stated.

Use the repository status vocabulary. A specified fixed conditional calculation can be a Frozen evaluation; source-informed extensions and proxy selections are Exploratory. Verified computation means arithmetic and rerun checks passed. It does not authenticate a reading. Preserve each endpoint, run the manual prose scan, and finish the report and retrospective even when a required source remains missing.

## Pre-outcome source clarification, 8 October 2026

Bonta's publicly posted six-page introductory letter was acquired after registration. Its PDF pages 2 and 4 preserve images of the ma, tra, pala, dhana, PU and pati signs. These permit a six-role anchor panel without the full manuscript PDF. Pati and pala each pool explicitly illustrated variants; candidate fine-code sets remain in the concordance. The primary lexical panel is ten distinct types: ma-tra, dhana-pala, PU-pala, PU-ma, PU-dhana, PU-tra, dhana-pati, pala-pati, PU-pati and dhana-pala-pati (second-edition segments 52–53, 67–75). All 720 permutations of these six proposed value roles will be enumerated. This is a source-selected anchor panel, with the hypotheses already chosen for lexical fit by Bonta; it is not a held-out validation of the entire proposal.

The seven-entry fish inventory is provisionally reconstructed as five single glyph classes (basic fish, internal stroke, horizontal stroke, caret, extra whiskers) and the basic fish with either short or long paired strokes. Missing full-manuscript images prevent certifying this reconstruction. Count an elementary five-class run and a sensitivity that absorbs adjacent 002/032 before or after the basic fish; do not call either an exact M-field replication. The three oval identities remain unresolved, so no oval-order outcome will be computed. Lecture 4, 9:55–10:17 gives reading order O–F–MS. Second-edition segment 20 first prints MS–F–O, then explicitly gives the reading order O–F–MS; the original protocol's fish-before-oval comparison is withdrawn before scoring.

Terminal targets are 740 (jar) and 520 (arrow); juncture candidates are 001, 002 and 032. The latter reconstruction remains provisional because 032 also occurs in fish-adjacent strokes. MS uses the conservative 705/706 candidate pair followed by 033, with 704/707 included only in a separate sensitivity. Candidate suffix evidence is the position of 033, not a claim that it encodes a suffix. All comparisons include one-sign texts but also report length-at-least-two sensitivities.

The ICIT audit will distinguish an explicit non-MH assertion from an ICIT-first example. Segment 84 explicitly allows an ICIT-first entry whose MH transcription was less accurate. The paper's ICIT-first list is therefore a source-history proxy and cannot reconstruct the unknown dozen-to-fifteen attempted validation set exactly. The source excerpt for every parsed join stays local.
