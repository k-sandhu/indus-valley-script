# Retrospective: building the post-catalogue object list

This account records what we did to answer [Q-1026](../../knowledge/questions/Q-1026-post-catalogue-finds.md), what went wrong and how it was repaired. The delegated work is described run by run in [RETROSPECTIVE-CODEX.md](RETROSPECTIVE-CODEX.md); the result is in [README.md](README.md).

## Approach

We wrote the protocol before reading any object entry and froze it by hash ([PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md), `PROTOCOL.sha256`). Three decisions in it shaped everything after. The catalogue baseline is set per site, so that a CISI 3 number absent from the export counts as a catalogued object rather than a new find. The list carries identity and locators only, because the objects are a sampling frame for prospective tests and their texts, motifs and sizes must stay unread. And a join to the local corpus needs a registration, a cited plate or a find context that picks out one object; anything weaker stays a candidate, and the corpus's shortfall is reported as two bounds.

The source plan began from the evidence census's sites and reports and from the four archive.org reports the card names, and grew once we looked at what the Internet Archive and ASI actually hold. A check of the archive's catalogue found *Puratattva* 18–37, fifteen volumes of *Man and Environment*, a second Farmana report and Trivedi's Tarkhanewala Dera monograph, and a probe of the ASI Aurangabad Circle mirror found most IAR volumes from 1986–87 to 2013–14. We tested that the Codex sandbox could reach the network before giving it the acquisition, and gave the bibliographic web search to a second run in parallel.

## Problems and repairs

**Sleep, disk and capacity.** The machine slept for about 80 minutes after the first launch; we turned on keep-awake for the rest of the session. During extraction, drive C: fell from about 1 GB to under 200 MB free, mostly through other work on the machine. We deleted only our own redundant files (a byte-identical IAR download and three OCR renders, about 32 MB, with a `DELETED.txt` record), stopped our four extraction runs at a 400 MB floor, recorded their session ids, and resumed them with a continuation prompt once 11.7 GB was free, after re-hashing all 150 downloads. Two later runs stopped on the service message "Selected model is at capacity" and were resumed the same way.

**Two IAR volumes fell through.** The mirror's "1996–1997" file is the 1997–98 volume, and the only copy of 2003–04 that run 1A held was an Internet Archive OCR that reads English as Devanagari. We noticed the first while writing the Codex retrospective and the second from run 2N's list of unread sources. We fetched the Internet Archive copy of 1996–97 (its OCR was equally unusable, so we tested Tesseract on four pages first) and found 2003–04 on the ASI publication index, and gave each to its own OCR and extraction run. The two volumes added ten rows, mostly seasons at Bhirrana, Bagasra, Baror and Tarkhanewala Dera that the final reports also cover, which is why the duplicate links matter in the counts.

**Reports that are not what they seem.** The Internet Archive copy of Nath's Rakhigarhi report is a draft without its seal catalogue, the ASI downloads for the published Rakhigarhi and Dholavira reports are covers, the census's `rakhigarhi_inventory.pdf` is a monument inventory, and the file registered as Desset 2022 holds other material. Each is recorded in [DATA-UPDATE.md](DATA-UPDATE.md); none could be repaired from open sources.

**Matching.** The matching run's proposals were conservative in one direction and wrong in another. It left most Dholavira and all Farmana rows as candidates, because its normaliser compared `DHR-9697` with `9697 NM` only after stripping a prefix and could not compare `Trench IB6` with `1B6`, and it kept CISI-numbered families in candidate sets for finds of the 2000s. Its prefix-containment rule also matched registration `218` to `DHR-21897`. We reviewed every candidate and every absent row at a site with local objects, wrote the Dholavira grid-square rule down in [review/match_dholavira.py](review/match_dholavira.py) before applying it, and recorded each decision with run 3's proposal beside it in [join-decisions.csv](join-decisions.csv). The verification run then showed that joins resting on find context alone break the frozen protocol's rule for `matched`; we kept those rows as candidates under the protocol and reported the context joins in separate columns and figures, rather than changing the rule after seeing its outcome. The earlier Kanmer package's object ledger settled the Kanmer sealings and showed that run 2G had copied the wrong trench for 09-899.

**Publication-based eligibility misses inscribed objects.** The protocol makes an object a row when its publication states an inscription. At Dholavira the report's table leaves the inscription cell blank for 85 seals, and 13 of those carry the registrations of corpus objects with transcribed inscriptions. We added them as uncertain-status rows matched to those objects, which changes no absent count, and listed in [local-unnamed.csv](local-unnamed.csv) the 41 corpus objects at listed sites that no row names, so that the list's own omissions are visible.

## Outcome

The list has 503 rows; 301 are post-catalogue objects with a stated inscription at 21 sites. Under the frozen join rule 10 match a local object and the corpus lacks between 55 and 291 of them; with the context joins, 77 match and the corpus lacks between 55 and 224. Every acquired file has a proposed manifest entry with its hash, and every reader's access to source text is in [exposure-log.csv](exposure-log.csv). The verification run's results are in [verification/](verification/) and summarised in the README.

What we would do differently: check every IAR file's volume year and OCR quality before the sweep instead of after extraction, write the registration normaliser and the context rule into the matching prompt rather than leaving them to the run, and keep a disk floor from the first launch.
