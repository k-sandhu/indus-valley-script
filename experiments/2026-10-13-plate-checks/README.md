# Plate checks of 13 October 2026: the Lothal elephant sealings against M-895, and the closing signs of Shot A's large seals

13 October 2026. Protocol [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md), hashed before any image was opened ([PROTOCOL.sha256](PROTOCOL.sha256)), with [Amendment 1](AMENDMENT-1.md) (sources for seals without a crop) and [Amendment 2](AMENDMENT-2.md) (catalogue captions in the blinded crops). Readings are in [observations.json](observations.json), decisions in [decisions.json](decisions.json). Every reading is a visual reading of printed photographs by AI models: Claude Opus 5.5 as first reader, and for Check 1 a second Claude instance that saw only renamed images. They become observations of the objects when a specialist confirms them.

## Results

### Check 1: conjecture C-0004

The ten Lothal sealings and the Mohenjo-daro fragment M-895 carry the same four signs in the same order, but they were not made by M-895 or by a seal of identical design. The sealings come from one seal whose face is as wide as M-895's and whose signs are spaced differently, and their field shows an animal with a trunk and crossing tusk where M-895 shows a single horn-like curve.

| Prediction | Decision | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| P1: same four signs, same order | **Pass** | On the impression, left to right: a U, a pair of brackets enclosing two rows of short strokes, two small marks hanging from the top line, and an oval or heart enclosing a V. Both readers find this sequence on M-895 (impression and mirrored seal) and on every sealing whose band survives. L-161, L-162, L-163, L-164, L-165, L-166 and L-167 show all four signs or nearly so; L-169 and L-170 keep only the right end; L-168's band is worn smooth. |
| P2: elephant on the sealings, unicorn on M-895 | **Unresolved** | Both readers see an elephant on the sealings: a heavy body on columnar legs and a long element hanging from the head to the ground line, crossed near the head by a short bar (trunk and tusk). This is clearest on L-161 (both readers) and L-162 (second reader), and the second reader finds the trunk-and-tusk element under the last sign on all nine readable sealings. Both readers rate it low to moderate in confidence. On M-895 the first reader sees a single horn rising forward to the upper right, with oblique neck folds behind a rounded head, and reads a unicorn. The second reader calls the animal unidentifiable, possibly a horned animal facing right. Under the protocol the disagreement leaves the M-895 half open. Neither reader sees a trunk, tusk or ear on M-895, so the shared-animal outcome is not met either. |
| P3: the impressing seal differs from M-895 | **Pass** (carved form) | The bracket sign takes 0.38 to 0.45 of the line on six measured sealings (L-161 0.40, L-162 0.39, L-163 0.41, L-164 0.45, L-165 0.38, L-167 0.39) and 0.54 on M-895. The second reader, measuring independently, found 37–41% against 53–54% on both M-895 views. The second reader also found that the gap before the last sign is 16–19% of the line against 8%, that the strokes inside the brackets are short dashes on the sealings and long bars on M-895, that M-895's oval has an inner outline the sealings lack, and that M-895's U reaches below the line. The width criterion is not met: the impressed face on L-161 measures 23.4 mm (21.1–24.2 mm over the scale calibrations) and M-895 23.3 mm. |

Under the card's rules P3 adds 0.1 and P1 keeps the card alive, so C-0004 moves from 0.5 to 0.6. P2 has no effect while it stays unresolved. The second reader also judged the nine readable sealings compatible with a single seal, which agrees with Frenez and Tosi 2005 (PDF 16), who group L-161 to L-172 as single impressions of one elephant seal. The left edge of L-166's impression lies closer to the bracket sign than on the other sealings (about 35 px against 105–130 px in the second reader's measurements). This may be clay folded over the edge or a narrower seal, and it needs the object.

The same measurement decides Shot A's prospective prediction 1 (PP1 of C-0001). The text ends in sign 740, outside the set, so the impressed face was predicted to measure 19.9 to 35.4 mm; it measures 23.4 mm, and the prediction is met (+0.03 under the frozen rule).

### Check 2: the layout and direction rivals to Shot A

On the photographs, the closing sign of the large seals sits in the text band like any other closing sign. No seal in either group shows the patterns that the rivals predict.

| | Set seals (end in 001, 595, 679, 820) | Comparison seals |
|---|---:|---:|
| Seals | 29 | 29 |
| With a held image | 22 | 29 |
| Supports the grade reading (final sign on the line, normal size and spacing, direction not reversed) | 14 | 27 |
| Supports layout | 0 | 0 |
| Supports direction | 0 | 0 |
| Undecided: final sign a single short stroke | 3 | 0 |
| Undecided: other (crowded end, enlarged sign, segmentation, damage, illegible) | 5 | 2 |
| Undecided: no held image | 7 | 0 |

Read blind, the set seals give 12 grade verdicts in 18. The one-sided Fisher test of rival verdicts, set against comparison seals, has 0 of 22 against 0 of 29 (p = 1). Fourteen of 22 set seals with images support the grade reading, a majority, so Shot A's prospective prediction 3 (PP3 of C-0001) passes. The claim keeps its textual half, and the photographs give no support to either rival.

All seven held seals ending in 820 support the grade reading: M-6, M-16, M-649, M-998, H-1, H-10 and M-1134. On each the 820 sign stands at full height on the band line at the closing end, usually against the left edge of the impression; M-1134's rhinoceros seal starts its legend flush at the right and leaves its spare room at the left. M-1701, M-1761 and the Dholavira seal (family 4195) have no held image. Of the seals ending in 595, four support the grade reading (H-29, H-80, M-1203, M-1290). H-645's final sign stands closer to its neighbour than the other signs do, and M-74's lies at a broken corner. Of those ending in 679, three support it (M-393, M-1052, M-629), while M-792's final sign is worn and M-653's runs into a break.

The four seals ending in 001 are all undecided, and for a reason the protocol did not anticipate. Where visible, their final sign is a single short stroke. It sits at mid-height on the line at normal spacing on M-1 and M-1103, and at the top of the band, tight against its neighbour, on H-642. A short stroke is small by its form, so the size rule cannot separate a closing sign from a filler. On H-642 and M-1103 the visible units also do not segment cleanly into the catalogue's count. C-56, read on Mackay 1943 Plate LI no. 30, is too coarse to delimit any sign.

Where the space at the opening end of the legend lies unused, the spacing rule would point to a reversed direction. On unicorn and zebu seals that space is taken by the animal's horn rising into the band: 10 of 22 set seals and 14 of 29 comparison seals show this. The reader did not count it as unused space, and the shared rate shows it is the ordinary layout of these seals.

## Methods

The protocol was written and hashed before any image was viewed. [build_lists.py](build_lists.py) rebuilt Shot A's population by importing its `seal_table.py` and `rank_lib.py` read-only and reading the frozen split directly, without appending to Shot A's confirmation-access log. The population is seals of type `SEAL:S` or `SEAL:R` with both face dimensions, usable preservation, a complete single-line legend of at least two signs without unknown signs, one face per family and one family per component. It contains 1,172 seals over both halves, and 29 end in the set (18 discovery, 11 confirmation), as Shot A reports. Each set seal was matched before viewing to the seal of the same type, site and length that ends in another sign, has a Lipi crop, and is nearest in log face area; all 29 pairs matched at that first level. The list is [object-lists.json](object-lists.json); sign sequences are local.

For blind reading, [blind.py](blind.py) pasted each seal's impression and object photograph into one composite under a random label. The header showed only the photograph kinds, the catalogue's sign count and its direction code. The Lipi crops carry the catalogue caption with the CISI number, and the first two versions of the composites exposed it ([Amendment 2](AMENDMENT-2.md)). The third version cut each crop above its lowest row that is at least 45% non-white. Ten seals were read knowing their identity: M-393, M-629, M-653 and C-56 in the set, and M-180, H-679, M-396, C-2, Dlv-1 and M-414 among the comparison seals. Each reading was appended with a time stamp to `check2-readings.jsonl` before the key was opened by [decide.py](decide.py). The protocol's verdict rules were applied in order: no image or illegible, then reversed spacing, then a final sign set apart, off the line or reduced into leftover space, then a final sign on the line at normal size and spacing, then undecided.

For Check 1 the first reader read M-895 on the CISI 2 plate and its Lipi crops, and the sealings on the Lipi crops cut from the CISI 1 plates. Measurements were taken from ruled enlargements ([measure.py](measure.py)). The second reader received the eleven views renamed X01 to X11 ([second-reader-report.json](second-reader-report.json)) and recorded signs, animals and proportions without knowing which image was the seal or what was expected. The first reader's Check 1 readings were recorded on a second viewing, after the second reader had been launched and before its report arrived.

Scale. CISI 2 prints inscribed sides at 2:1 (introduction, printed pp. XXV–XXVI, PDF 28–29). On the render of printed p. 85 at zoom 8.34, M-894, M-896 and M-897 give 31.1 px per object millimetre; M-895's photographed width (724 px) then gives 23.3 mm, its catalogue width 23.4 mm, so both side edges of the fragment survive. On CISI 1 renders at zoom 4 the page size gives 22.7 px/mm at 2:1, which M-5 (22.2–22.7) and L-2 (22.0–22.6) confirm, while L-1 gives 24.4–25.2. The Lipi crop of L-161 is 0.496 of the render's scale, so 11.27 px/mm, which puts the impressed face of L-161 at 23.4 mm (21.1–24.2 mm over the calibrations). The text span from the U to the heart measures 18.6 mm on M-895 and 20.9 mm on L-161 (18.8–21.6 mm). The difference falls within the scale range, so P3 rests on proportions, which need no scale.

## Limits

The readings were made by AI models from printed halftones, and the sealings are worn clay. The elephant identification rests on the trunk-and-tusk element and the body, and both readers rate it low to moderate. M-895's animal is the point on which the readers disagree. Nobody looked at the originals, and the CISI scans are about 100 dpi for CISI 1. Blinding in Check 2 was partial. Ten seals were read knowing their identity, and a reader who knows the sign list could infer the group from the final sign. The comparison group's 27 of 29 grade verdicts show that the rules rarely fired on any seal, which limits what the null result can say: the visual rivals as defined do not appear, but subtler layout effects would need measured sign spacing across many seals. Seven set seals, three of them ending in 820, have no held image; their photographs are in CISI 3 or in excavation reports the project does not hold.

## Next tests

A specialist should confirm the reading of M-895's field (horn or not) on the CISI 2 photograph or the object, and the trunk-and-tusk element on the Lothal sealings. That would close P2 and C-0004's "catalogue error in either animal" rival. The single short stroke that ends the 001 seals needs its own adjudication, as a closing sign of the legend or a numeral stroke. Photographs of M-1701, M-1761, M-1764, M-1720, M-1989, H-1708 and the Dholavira seal DHR-16412 would complete PP3 for the remaining set seals.
