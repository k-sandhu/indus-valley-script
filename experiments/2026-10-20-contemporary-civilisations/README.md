# The Indus beside its contemporaries, 2600–1900 BCE

This package compares the Mature Harappan civilisation with 21 other societies of the same centuries. It covers how much land each occupied or controlled, how large its biggest settlements were, how many people lived in them, and which institutions its cities had. Every figure is a published estimate with its source. [NOTES.md](NOTES.md) explains how areas and populations were estimated, where the sources disagree and what we could not verify. [civilisations.json](civilisations.json) holds the rows. A research agent (Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code) compiled them on 20 October 2026 with five parallel passes and merged them. The coordinating session reviewed the data, wrote the page and checked it.

[civilisations.html](civilisations.html) presents the comparison as a world map, three range charts and a features table. Open it in a browser with an internet connection, since Leaflet and the map tiles load from the web. [build.py](build.py) regenerates it from the JSON and [template.html](template.html). The build adds display fields only: short names, the kind of area each extent measures, and the densely settled Indus core of 30,000–100,000 km² that the Indus row's own basis quotes from Fisher (2018). It changes no number.

## What the comparison shows

The Indus spread one material culture over 650,000–1,000,000 km². Only Akkad's political control at its height, 450,000–1,000,000 km², matches it, and even the densely settled Indus core is larger than Egypt's 17,000–22,400 km² of floodplain. Its largest cities stood in the second size class. Mohenjo-daro covered 200–250 ha, against 400–600 ha for Early Dynastic Uruk, Lagash and Umma, about 400 ha for Shimao and 630–800 ha for Liangzhu, whose walls predate the window. The Indus had several cities of similar rank spread over the whole region. It is the only society coded with writing, seals, sealings, standard weights, planned streets and drainage that has no building identified as a palace and no rich tomb. Every other society coded with writing has palaces or royal and elite tombs, at least in part.

## Limits

Extents measure three different things, the spread of sites, political control and settled land, and the charts label which is which. The population figures are estimates, mostly an area multiplied by an assumed number of people per hectare. Most map positions come from Wikipedia or Wikidata, because Pleiades refused automated requests. Several standard syntheses were read only through quotations, as NOTES.md lists. The features are coded from the sources reached in one day. "Not found" means no source we reached reports the feature; it does not mean the feature was absent.

The boldest reading this comparison supports is that the Indus ran a society with writing, spread over as much land as the Akkadian empire controlled, without a royal court. Its authority sat in offices and institutions shared across many cities, which is what the recurring seal titles and the sealings that travelled between cities suggest ([seal findspot maps](../2026-10-20-seal-findspot-maps/README.md)). Two findings would decide it. Excavating a large unexcavated city such as Ganweriwala would turn up a palace or a rich tomb if one existed. A deposit of sealings from one storeroom should carry titles that also appear on seals in other cities.
