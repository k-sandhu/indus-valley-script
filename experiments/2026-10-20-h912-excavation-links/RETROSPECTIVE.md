# Retrospective: H-912 excavation links

The starting task was to trace H-912's registration, depth and stratum to a container, food remains or an administrative context. We wrote and hashed the protocol before source searches and observation coding. An indexed RMRL bulletin supplied a lead to Vats pp. 58–60 and 435. We followed the original pages and kept the bulletin's gemstone and workshop interpretation separate.

The held Vats scans are image-only. Their local text files have page markers but no usable body. We acquired the Internet Archive OCR and page index, used OCR to locate candidates, then rendered and inspected the complete pages. The page index placed printed p. 348 at the wrong leaf, so the first table render's filename says350 although its visible header is348. The provenance record keeps the actual PDF and printed coordinates. OCR also confuses8/S, suffixes, and digits; it did not decide the registration.

The source table gives a 4–7 foot range where the export gives −5.5 feet. This changed the resolution of the context. The overview's Stratum IV and the bead chapter's Stratum III conflict remained visible. The same source describes three months of recovery, so we retained each suffix and later same-area find separately. We did not turn the shared registration root into a closed deposit.

The catalogue pass separated H-302's F.C. 3864 from excavation 8650c and its museum accession. It also exposed the exact 8650c448 reference shared by H-912 and H-1191 and the inconsistent 8650h assignment. No identity graph or transcription was changed. A further Vats chert-core passage was located by OCR but not accepted as a checked individual observation in this bounded run.

Belcher's complete plan and relevant context pages were inspected. Its fourteen numbered inscribed objects joined uniquely to the 1990 export registrations. We preserved plan labels, inferred group labels, uncertain boundary positions and missing excavation lots. Twelve fish-lot numbers from the separate street graph remain a distinct list. The plan is a new source-linked object frame; it supplies no direct object-to-fish-lot relation.

The full RMRL PDF returned 404; that failure is preserved. Two diagnostic commands encountered a missing UTF-8 argument while reading OCR and an overbroad whole-PDF text scan; neither produced an analytical endpoint. New outputs were written to separate directories, and the source-observation v1 remained unchanged after the parent study recorded its hash. Raw sources, OCR, renders and detailed export metadata remain local.

The source audit closes an acquisition gap and identifies the missing decisive evidence. A registered tablet-to-container or tablet-to-allotment relation with independently observed contents and quantity would test the literal four-vessel fish proposal. The present source supplies a repeated tablet series in which to compare proposed allocation categories.
