## Questions and prior work

Does graphic usage change among dated Harappa records? Do regional populations differ after standardizing object type and the influence of individual sites? Are external texts harder for a core-trained model to predict than core texts of the same length and object type? The search precedes computation. Yadav's 2013 *Sensitivity of Indus Script to Site and Type of Object* is a direct precedent for frequency and length comparisons. Rao et al.'s 2009 *A Markov model of the Indus script* already compares West Asian text likelihoods. The newly located *Diachronic Changes in the Indus Script: Based on the Proposed Arrangement of the Indus Seal* (Tsukuba) is a direct diachronic precedent. ## Period mapping and exclusions

Apply HARP period dates only to rows whose `site` is exactly `Harappa`. Kenoyer and Meadow (2010), *Inscribed Objects from Harappa Excavations 1986–2007*, CISI 3.1, printed xlv, Table 1 (PDF page 4 in the newly opened 17-page public file), gives approximate calibrated calendar dates. [Source](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer-Meadow-2010-HARP.pdf). The Beas survey Table 1 repeats the scheme. [Source](https://www.harappa.com/beas/chronology).

| Export label | Admissible major periods | Approximate BCE interval |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 1B, 1C | 1 | 3700–2800 |
| 2, 2A, 2B | 2 | 2800–2600 |
| 3A | 3A | 2600–2450 |
| 3B, 3B-1, 3B-2 | 3B | 2450–2200 |
| 3C, 3C-1 through 3C-4 | 3C | 2200–1900 |
| 3B/C | 3B or 3C | 2450–1900 |
| 3 | 3A, 3B or 3C | 2600–1900 |
| 4 | 4 | 1900–1800(?) |
| 5, 5A, 5B | 5 | 1800(?) to later than 1300 |

The literal `Period ` prefix is removed before this mapping. Retain subphase labels in descriptive tables; do not assign separate calendar dates to numerical subphases. `-`, empty or unrecognized labels remain undated. Record other sites' labels without transporting the Harappa chronology to them. Dates describe catalogue assignments and depositional contexts; residual objects and disputed catalogue assignments can alter manufacturing chronology.

The primary time comparisons use unambiguous major-period records only. Report interval-labelled records separately. Give sensitivity endpoints assigning all 3B/C families to 3B, all to 3C, and uniform admissible-period multiple allocation (100 draws), with each physical family kept together. These are allocation assumptions, not inferred dates. The numerical spread is an allocation envelope, not a confidence interval. Broad Period 3 records enter allocation draws across 3A/3B/3C but do not enter the two extreme 3B/C endpoints. Conflicting admissible sets within a family use their intersection; an empty intersection is excluded and counted.

For each sign present in dated Harappa, report the possible earliest and latest observed major periods implied by all admissible sets. These bound observed attestation, not invention or extinction. Count distinct physical components bearing each sign per period. Give Wilson intervals for component-level presence in unambiguous periods and the one-sided zero-count bound `1 - .05**(1/n)` for absence. These intervals assume sampled components are independent and say nothing about unexcavated objects. Rare first/last occurrences remain descriptive. Do not test hundreds of claimed origin dates.

## Region mapping and object types

Core: Inter-Riverine, Lower Indus, Gujarat/Kutch, Eastern Headwaters and Upper Indus. External primary set: Persian Gulf, Mesopotamia, Iranian Plateau and Central Asia. Exclude South India and Other from both models and count them separately. Retain site and literal object type in all raw tables. Normalize only the obvious `POsT:T:g` typo to `POT:T:g` and `POT:T:S` to `POT:T:s`; save those counts. Use exact object types (for example SEAL:S, SEAL:C, TAB:I and TAB:B) for primary adjustment. Do not collapse round and square seals into a single matched class.

Region is a deterministic function of site in this input. A region coefficient conditional on a full set of site indicators is unidentifiable, and within-site regional permutation has no moves. Report this design limitation. Adjustment uses equal-site contributions within each region and object type, alongside face-weighted results and site-by-type tables. Add within-region leave-one-site-out prediction where a region has two eligible sites. This measures sensitivity to its sampled sites, not a regional effect holding site fixed.

## Populations, dependence and estimation

Raw descriptive counts include every nonempty face. Exclude 000 from known-sign frequency tables; count unreadable tokens, affected faces, empty faces and incomplete faces in every site/type/time/region stratum. Length counts retain the observed slot count including 000, with known length separate. Frequency comparisons use nonempty known-token faces, including partial observations. Repeat principal frequency comparisons on `clean_rtl_singleline == true` as a recording-quality sensitivity. Initial, terminal and sequence analyses use complete observed, single-line R/L faces, at least one sign, with no 000 or blank space. The stored `signs_reading_rtl` order follows the repository's source-checked reversal; do not reverse it again. No minimum length of three is imposed.

Physical components join numeric `object_family_id` and simple CISI base aliases transitively, retaining aliases from every face before selection. Complex CISI references are not guessed. The components are the bootstrap and permutation units. For predictive splits, additionally join identical complete known single-line sequences over the full input before selecting eligible faces. No physical or exact-text component may touch both training and test. Report size distributions, the largest component, mixed labels and losses caused by excluding overlaps. Identical texts can reflect independently produced objects; this extra exclusion measures generalization beyond copied formulae.

For a pair of populations retain exact object types with at least 10 physical components on both sides. Give raw effects on the entire pair, raw effects on this common support and standardized effects on the same support. Equal weights over common types define the adjusted estimand. Regional adjusted distributions additionally average sites equally within each type. Report every retained type, face/component/token denominator and omitted type. Compute Jensen–Shannon divergence in bits for known-sign, initial and terminal distributions. Give mean observed-length differences, conditional bigram entropy and bigram-diversity summaries. Standardize lengths and sequence summaries over the same types with suitable eligible records.

Report effect intervals using 499 physical-component bootstrap replicates, stratified by population. Recompute distributions and site/type weighting in each replicate, and record invalid replicates when support disappears. The intervals are percentile intervals conditional on this corpus selection. The primary family-resampling intervals may remain optimistic when separate artifacts share a production batch. Report equal-whole-text weighting as a frequency sensitivity, and conservative text-closure permutation where feasible.

Test period and regional frequency divergence by 499 label permutations among whole physical components within exact object-type and face-multiplicity blocks. Mixed-type or mixed-label components are omitted from that permutation population, with its effect separately recomputed and denominators reported. This preserves expanded face counts and related faces, as motivated by Paper 002. Regional permutations cannot control site; label their p-values as site/type-population comparisons. Repeat using complete-text closure, retaining homogeneous components and blocking by type and component face count; abstain if fewer than 20 movable components or either population has fewer than 10. The reference assumption is exchangeability of these components within the declared blocks.

Only major periods and core regions with at least 30 physical components enter inferential pair comparisons. Test per-sign adjusted frequency differences for signs occurring in at least 20 known tokens across a comparison's permutation population, using two-sided absolute effects. Benjamini–Hochberg q-values apply jointly to all sign tests of that question; Holm correction applies jointly to omnibus frequency tests of that question. Report null allocation restrictions and do not interpret an unadjusted sign p-value as a selected discovery.

## Rarefaction and prediction

For each regional pair report 200 equal-known-token rarefactions at the smaller count and the same procedure within common object types, combined with equal type weights. Rarefaction draws tokens without replacement to equalize frequency estimation effort; its 2.5–97.5% simulation spread describes the sampling procedure, not uncertainty in the archaeological population. Component bootstrap intervals above supply the cluster sampling uncertainty. Record actual token budget per type. Sites are balanced in the standardized effect, while rarefaction within type is a separate check on sample-size effects.

Use a fixed first-order sequence model with a vocabulary of all observed known codes, a half-count unigram prior, and transition counts shrunk toward unigram probabilities with strength 20. No hyperparameter search is conducted. Raw models pool object types. Adjusted models fit the same model separately for each exact object type. Five folds are assigned to full physical/text components with deterministic seed 20261006. For each region compare held-out bits per sign under within-region and all-other-core-region training, removing all training components touching each test fold. Give paired face-mean score differences with physical-component intervals, raw token-mean scores, adjusted equal-type/equal-site scores, training/test counts and unseen-code rates. Restrict the within-versus-other comparison to types with at least 20 eligible training faces in each model in each fold; record unsupported cases. As a training-size sensitivity, downsample each model's training components to the smaller component count within type using the same seed schedule, without tuning on losses. Report the resulting scores separately.

For the external set train on core records only, excluding every component touched by any eligible external record. Score external and five-fold held-out core texts for per-sign surprisal and novel internal bigram/trigram fractions (length one and insufficient n-grams are reported as unavailable). External texts must have at least 20 eligible core comparator faces of exactly the same object type and observed length. Compare each external face with its comparator-cell mean, using its fold's core training model so external and comparator predictions share training data. Record raw pooled-model scores and exact-type-model scores. Reweight core comparator cells to the external length/type distribution. Give physical-component bootstrap intervals on the external-minus-matched-core difference and a two-sided component sign-flip p-value (499 draws) as a descriptive paired reference; list its symmetry assumption. Add per-region external summaries and leave-one-external-component-out effect ranges. Record-level predictions, match memberships, sequences and exclusions are local-only.

## Negative results, output and verification

An adjusted effect whose interval includes zero, an omnibus test failing Holm .05, or an unmovable reference is a negative or unresolved comparison. Positive divergence alone can arise from finite samples, excavation selection or missing types. A cross-entropy excess does not identify a language or social role. An external result that disappears under exact-type/length matching or component deletion does not support a general external-text distinction.
