# Readable seals: courtier source checks and missing catalogues

We inspected the held 1998 Urkesh courtier edition by images and established physical carriers and the reason each of four seal cases needs exclusion or further adjudication. No legend enters the comparison. Counts remain 11 Akkadian legends, 23 Tamil-Brahmi including coins and 16 Tamil seals and rings; the four-corpus test remains closed at its threshold of 25.

## Courtier evidence

Buccellati and Kelly-Buccellati, *The Courtiers of the Queen of Urkesh: Glyptic Evidence from the Western Wing of the Royal Storehouse AK* (1998), printed p.197 and plates III–IV, identifies two seals of the nurse Zamenna, h1 and h2. The text explains the mirrored reading and vertically flipped signs. Footnote 12 proposes a Hurrian reading for h1 and describes h2 as the Sumero-Akkadian version. Both need language adjudication before they can enlarge a strict Akkadian corpus. The physical photographs include A1.139 and A1.121. Different dies and the same interpreted phrase do not establish independent eligible legends.

The cook's h3 seal has physical carriers A1.213 and A1.182, photographed in figs.7–8, printed p.213. The prose on p.200 says the first half of the legend, where the personal name appeared, is completely abraded except for its frame. Figure 7's caption calls the rolling almost complete; that describes its surviving design and cannot restore the missing name. We exclude this incomplete legend.

The h4 seal carries the personal name Inni-šadû, printed I-nin-SA.TU on p.201. Figures 11–12, p.215, identify A1.90 and A5.80; fig.14, p.216, identifies a double rolling on A1.132. These carriers supply one seal case. The editor dates its style to the early Akkadian period. The period and a personal name leave language eligibility unresolved, so we defer sign-level admission. The isolated name supplies one orthographic word and cannot enlarge the two-word population.

These four cases are new adjudications of the first pass's unexamined image-only source. The six-row ledger separates the four seal cases from two acquisition targets.

## Catalogue access

Eight search strings and four direct institutional or publisher requests exhausted the frozen acquisition block. We obtained three HTML responses and retained one HTTP404 response. The old CICT book-detail URL failed. The current publications URL returned HTTP200, but extraction did not find the requested title; its response cannot supply catalogue pages. Search results describe later editions with inconsistent dates, so none supersedes the held 2020 title page. The revised pp.67–70 and plates remain missing, and the Vil Atiṉa... caption remains incomplete.

The Tell Brak excavation project's Publications page identifies Matthews's *The Early Glyptic of Tell Brak* (1997), OBO Series Archaeologica 15, and *The Excavations at Tell Brak 2: Nagar in the Third Millennium BC* (2001). Cambridge's accessible extract for Finkel's *Inscriptions from Tell Brak 1984*, footnote 2, identifies the 1978 seal impression Reg. No.257 and sends it to a forthcoming glyptic study. The publisher page presents access controls and an extract; no complete catalogue or full legend was acquired. Reg. No.257 is an acquisition target, with language and complete text unknown. We opened no Old Assyrian extension.
