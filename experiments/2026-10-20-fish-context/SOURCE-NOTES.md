# The vessel-and-fish example is H-912 B

The acquired version of Asko Parpola's 2010 *A Dravidian solution to the Indus script problem* labels Figure 6 as H-902 and cites *Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions*, volume 2, page 339. The cited page actually shows H-912 B. Its photograph matches Figure 6: the fish-like sign, open V-shaped sign, four strokes and the tablet's outline agree. H-902 A and B are on page 338 and show different signs. The primary observation is an object-reference correction in this version of the paper.

Exact witnesses are in [source-observations-v1.json](source-observations-v1.json), S2–S3, and the [primary scan record](source-witnesses-v1.json). The acquired PDF's SHA256 is `d255349265f16d5fb4b919600ab6864c7c88a1ef473e277d8a3af5eaa1fc415c`. Figure 6 is on PDF page 13, printed page 14. CISI 2 printed page 339 is PDF page 374 in the held scan; H-902 is on printed page 338, PDF page 373. Photographs, page renders and extracted text stay local under the source-specific rights rules.

The pinned corpus already distinguishes the two objects. H-912 is provisional family 1793 and has two recorded faces; its B-face candidate reading is a number followed by a V-shaped unit and a fish. H-902 is family 1784 and has no B220-family fish in its two transcribed faces. Neither source identity nor any sign sequence was changed for the frozen test.

This matters for the economic comparison. The number on H-912 B modifies a construction with an intervening V-shaped sign. The frozen adjacent-stroke model scores a stroke immediately before fish and therefore does not measure this vessel-and-fish construction. Its result cannot reject an economic reading that counts vessels containing fish. M-478 A is the comparison Parpola uses for the V-shaped sign: a person holds an angular object toward a tree, beside an inscription with a related shape. That pictorial comparison motivates a vessel interpretation, while a physical container, residue or measured allotment linked to the tablet would test it.

H-9 remains the graphic seven-plus-fish example. Its photograph and impression reverse the displayed order but show the same seven strokes and fish. M-410 supplies the separate pictorial fish comparison. [The source selection](source-selection-v1.json) also retains four source-table sample objects, a visible order counterexample, a two-short-stroke example and two cases lacking photographs. The targeted selection supplies source checks, not an error-rate estimate.

William R. Belcher's 1991 *Fish Resources in an Early Urban Context at Harappa, Pakistan* supplies a concrete route to an independent commodity test. It distinguishes Street NS2355, Rooms 1–3, the North Pit and an overlying grey-ash deposit on Mound E. Printed pages 114–117 distinguish several deposits and discuss movement of domestic debris. Our held object records do not join H-912, H-9 or the other fish objects to those lots. A shared site or mound does not establish that association.

Our strongest working reading of H-912 B is **four vessel-units of fish**, with the V-shaped sign naming the unit and fish naming the contents. This is Parpola's inherited functional proposal attached to the corrected object. The deciding evidence would be a source-linked series of these tablets attached to containers with identifiable contents or independently measured allotments.
