## Approach

This package closed the three scientific repairs identified in Section 7 of the 22 October novelty review and in the reading-evaluation readiness audit: (1) replacing the Sangam prefix-count proxy and silent phonological fallback in the Tamil blind-template scorer with exact written-form token counting and strict lookup from `lexical_inputs.py`, while enforcing sign-level dependent random-key draws; (2) evaluating the 24 October re-extracted Winslow table while keeping raw Winslow guarded boundaries strictly separate from *Tamil Lexicon*-assisted boundaries; and (3) executing the deciding Sanskrit cross-language control on the exact same 38 blind Indus sign pairs using Cologne Monier-Williams, with the 16-sign Sanskrit mapping frozen in `PROTOCOL.md` before inspecting any Monier-Williams compounds.

## Challenges and fixes

1. **Monier-Williams compound boundary markup (`—˚` + `<srs/>`).** A preliminary schema inspection of `mw.txt` on lines 50,000–65,000 (unrelated to the 16 target words) revealed three distinct `<e>3` compound encodings: explicit em-dash (`—`, 84,634 entries), bare vowel-sandhi coalescence (`<srs/>`, 14,170 entries), and mixed entries containing both `—` and `<srs/>` (3,335 entries). Inspecting twelve sample mixed entries showed a crucial structural rule: when a ring mark `˚` immediately follows `—` (as in `a-mf—˚tA<srs/>tman`), the `—˚` abbreviates the first member in print and the true compound boundary sits at `<srs/>`; without `˚` (as in `a-mfta—prA<srs/>Sana`), the compound boundary sits at `—`. Encoding that rule before running the scorer prevented 277 corrupted splits and recovered clean `(m1, m2)` boundaries across 101,164 compounds.
2. **Simplex spelling checks before freezing.** Checking only `<e>1`/`<e>2` simplex presence (zero `<e>3`/`<e>4` compounds read) caught two potential pitfalls before freezing: *nyagrodha* is parsed by Monier-Williams as a compound (`nyac + rodha`, `<e>3`) rather than a simplex, so *vaṭa* is the standard simplex Classical Sanskrit headword for the banyan; and the two simplex Monier-Williams headwords glossed "squirrel" are *kalandaka* and *romaśī* rather than *cikroḍa*.
3. **Sangam tokenization denominator (162,436 vs. 162,399).** `lexical_inputs.tamil_tokens` splits on non-letter and non-mark characters within the Tamil Unicode block (`U+0B80`–`U+0BFF`), yielding 162,436 tokens compared with 162,399 from the historical raw-block regex `[\u0b80-\u0bff]+`. The 37 extra splits occur at Tamil numeral symbols and leave exact written-form counts for every target word identical (*kā* = 9, *ā* = 24, *aṟu* = 23, *aṟṟu* = 8). Both counts are now verified explicitly in `verify.py`.
4. **Verifier bug on Tamil retroflex `ḷ`.** The first run of the independent verifier (`verify.py`) placed Sanskrit vocalic `ḷ` (`U+1E37`) into its vowel set, which caused it to strip the final Tamil retroflex consonant `ḷ` from *veḷ* and *kōḷ* and miss three Tamil hits (`T20`, `T27`, `T32`). A single-draw synthetic unplanted check also hit a 5.5% Poisson-zero fluke. Removing `ḷ` from the verifier's vowel set (matching `rebus.py`) and testing 20 unplanted keys resolved both verifier issues without touching either production scorer; all five verification checks then passed.
5. **Sign-level vs. slot-keyed joint random keys.** The 22 October blind-template script drew separate independent replacement words for `(sign, "first")` and `(sign, "second")`. Implementing unified sign-level pools (matched jointly on first- and second-member productivity and exact frequency) preserved shared-sign dependence across all 38 templates. As expected from variance amplification, the Tamil blind-pair random-key p-value moved from 0.0704 (slot-keyed) to 0.1037 (sign-level).
