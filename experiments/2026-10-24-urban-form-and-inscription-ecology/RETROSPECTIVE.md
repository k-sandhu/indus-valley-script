# Building a phase-based comparison

We started from DATA.md, the registry, the town-axis and town-phase packages, current documentary work and the repository's research protocols. Three agents first owned distinct source modules: western towns, eastern towns and the inscription bridge. The coordinator owned the comparison, methods and proposed tests. Later assignments used separate review and visual-production paths. No agent edited another active contributor's files or the generated knowledge map.

The source work changed the comparison's unit. Exact passages describe clearance, enclosure growth, added rooms, narrowed passages and drains rebuilt along older systems. The observed sequences could be recorded without calling every irregular feature unplanned or assigning the latest plan to a town's whole lifetime. Count denominators also changed the interpretation: blocks, rooms, extrapolated wells, reconstructed reservoirs and catalogue rows required separate columns and phases.

The most consequential source repairs were retained rather than reconciled by preference. These include Kalibangan's 180-versus-160 m transcription discrepancy, Farmana's early chronology disagreement and first-millennium AD sample, source-defined area conflicts, Lothal's unreconciled sealing populations and street directions, and numerical inconsistencies in the 2026 residential paper. A mismatched Kenoyer file was excluded. Some HTTP 403 responses were resolved through existing held witnesses; unacquired supplements remained gaps. A publisher PDF endpoint returning HTML was replaced by a separately recorded author-hosted route. All source derivatives were written on D: after checking its mount and the raw-data junction.

The first merge failed because one module used a different JSON wrapper. We preserved that endpoint. The second exported the observations but rejected null phases that already carried missing reasons. Independent review also caught misleading substring matches and an exact duration calculated from an open bound. The third run preserves raw labels and operators, uses reviewed feature tags, carries mixed evidence states and retains the multi-site relation. Review spot checks confirm the repairs. The full source hash check passed.

The theoretical review caught an omitted direct predecessor for the labour-token idea and two identification problems. Durable infrastructure can result from negotiation, supervision or engineering constraints. Provisioning can concern goods without establishing labour obligations. The revised cards now name those contrasts and require independent labels and added-value tests. Drafts and the response remain preserved. No new semantic or institutional result was scored.

Generated demonstrations supplied two useful checks. Fourfold orientation can be low globally while perfectly ordered within districts. Exact independent-pair power shows why twenty contexts are an entry minimum rather than a sufficient study. Neither calculation uses archaeological observations. The six published dimension-pair ratios are exploratory arithmetic, with a separate fraction check and source uncertainty retained.

The current endpoint is a source-backed comparison, a set of explicit gaps and a prospective deciding design. Its strongest supported conclusion is that shared urban frameworks persisted through substantial changes in local construction and activity. A new field or archive study must join the same architectural parts through independently dated episodes and connect primary documentary contexts to those episodes before testing the institutional explanation.
