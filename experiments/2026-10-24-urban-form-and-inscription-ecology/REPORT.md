# Indus towns changed within durable urban frameworks

The strongest recurring similarity is a way of coordinating construction through time. Shared streets, drains, entrances and enclosure lines could persist while houses changed, open spaces filled and districts expanded. Large rebuilding programmes also cleared existing occupation and introduced new arrangements. The sources establish earlier occupation followed by replanning, and established layouts followed by local additions. The pace and original coordination of those changes remain separate questions.

We assembled **287 source-qualified assertions for twelve core towns, including one Harappa-hinterland comparison**. The register contains **42 source-use records**, including modern methodological references, with **38 checked file paths representing 36 distinct held hashes**. These are not counts of independent excavations or publications. All source locators, quantities, original phase names, uncertainty and missing reasons remain in [run-v3](run-v3/summary.json). The separate Dholavira extraction covers **225 printed seal-context rows**. It adds a bounded catalogue denominator, not 225 new physical objects to the project corpus.

This is a source review and exploratory synthesis, with verification tier submitted and documented team review. The new computations are integrity checks, ratios of six published dimension pairs, arithmetic on explicitly closed source-model intervals, and generated demonstrations of orientation and future sample power. We have not measured a new Indus street graph, fitted a civilisation-wide similarity ranking or opened a fresh confirmation population.

## What the comparison covers

The cases are Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Dholavira, Lothal, Chanhudaro, Kalibangan, Banawali, Rakhigarhi, Surkotada, Kanmer, Shikarpur and Farmana. Eleven continue Q-1032's required frame; Farmana supplies a smaller excavated settlement with unusually explicit named alterations. The selection favours accessible excavation records and is not a random sample of Indus settlements. The [town profiles](COMPARISON.md) compare setting, chronology, materials, structures, quantities and changing layout. The [assertion table](run-v3/observations.csv) retains exact source coordinates; [structured JSON](run-v3/observations.json) preserves the additional quantities and relations.

The towns occupy different settings and use different resource combinations. Dholavira lies on Khadir island between seasonal channels, Harappa developed on a terrace in a riverine setting, and Lothal occupies the Bhal lowland. These settings affect the questions to ask about catchments, flooding, supply and construction. A modern river line cannot supply a Bronze Age transport route. The map carries forward Q-1032's indexed site points, including the corrected Surkotada and Farmana locations, while the source ledger retains conflicting older coordinates. Source records W-DHO-001, W-HAR-002 and W-LOT-018 support the settings. [Singh and colleagues 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-01643-9) supplies the regional requirement for dated river histories.

## Replanning follows earlier occupation

Banawali gives the clearest explicit transition. Bisht describes an open IA village, enclosure during IB, then IC clearance and rebuilding of houses, a new street arrangement and a bipartite settlement. Its radial Lower Town and rectilinear higher sector were deliberate arrangements. Period II retained the broad outline through six or more building phases. This is a direct reason to analyse coordination separately from rectangular geometry. [Bisht, *Puratattva* 29, pp. 15-20](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/85200.pdf); EAST-BANAWALI-002 to -008 and -015.

Harappa supplies a different ordered relation. Occupation beyond Mound E's enclosure was later incorporated inside another wall; Mound F followed a comparable growth-and-enclosure sequence. Dholavira's Stage II housing north of the early nucleus was cleared in Stage III for a multipurpose ground, while new enclosed districts developed elsewhere. Existing settlement therefore preceded major changes in urban boundaries and public space. The original plots' degree of coordination is not recovered by those passages. Kenoyer's exact source locators are W-HAR-014 to -016 in the [register](run-v3/sources.json); [Bisht pp. 95-100, W-DHO-009 to -014](https://archive.org/details/dholavirareportpreliminaryjan2015_860_K).

Mohenjo-daro adds evidence of later regularisation. Green and colleagues' 2026 GIS reanalysis reports that later houses align more closely with streets. Its five DK-G South samples have Gini coefficients of residence area declining from 0.39 to 0.23, while median areas vary rather than declining monotonically. The samples contain 12, 36, 34, 15 and 35 residence areas. They cannot be summed into a census of independent households. The chronology divides an assumed occupation span into approximate centuries. We retain the paper's inconsistent overall and subarea totals separately and do not use them as evidence of population growth. [Green and colleagues 2026, methods p. 1246 and Table 1 p. 1247](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2026.10359); W-MD-011 to -027 and [table conflict audit](modules/western/table1-conflicts.json).

## Local alterations follow established layouts

Kanmer's IIa central space was partly exposed over 7 by 5 m. In IIb, buildings occupied the space and became wider while retaining the earlier orientation. Later structures used or occupied the rampart differently. The retained alignment and changed allocation of space are distinct observations. Whether the infill was piecemeal, negotiated or one planned campaign remains a deciding question. [Kharakwal and colleagues, pp. 363-365](https://nichibun.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/1259/files/symp_016__357__355_376__357_378.pdf); EAST-KANMER-009 to -014.

Farmana has three named changes within a broadly retained plan: a bathing platform at the exposed end of East Area Lane 1, Structure 11A at the end of East Area Lane 2, and a strengthening wall extending into the street beside Central Area Structure 3I. Their identifiers remain separate across areas. Dholavira's later Lower Town has a Stage V wall narrowing a formerly 5.25 m street and other street-side additions. Lothal reports Phase IV encroachment alongside attempts to keep original alignments. These changes increased occupation of particular spaces or narrowed access, while portions of the wider framework endured. [Farmana pp. 70, 78, 117, Figs. 5.38 and 5.88](https://archive.org/details/excavationsatfarmana200608vshindeetal_212_v), EAST-FARMANA-009 to -011; [Dholavira pp. 134-137](https://archive.org/details/dholavirareportpreliminaryjan2015_860_K), W-DHO-023 to -025; [Rao pp. 85-87](https://archive.org/details/masi78lothalaharappanporttownraos.r.part1platesmissing_443_k), W-LOT-008 to -009.

Surkotada records reinforced defences, a shifted entrance and a later partly blocked intercommunicating passage. Kalibangan records multiple road and building levels and local adjustment between street and perimeter-wall alignments. Shikarpur's small trench sample establishes changes in masonry and repair but cannot supply a whole-town street history. The useful comparison is therefore which element changed, at which scale, and with what construction relation. Calling all late alterations urban disorder would erase the variation among these sequences. [Joshi pp. 44-50 and 58](https://archive.org/details/masi87excavationsatsurkotadaandkutchjagatpatijoshi_841_t); [*Indian Archaeology 1967-68*, p. 44](https://www.asiaurangabadcircle.com/assets/files/publications/1967-1968.pdf); EAST-SURKOTADA-003 to -014, EAST-KALIBANGAN-018 and EAST-SHIKARPUR-001 to -010. Exact held-file locators and acquisition routes are in the register.

## Structures, quantities and materials

Six explicitly reported dimension pairs illustrate variation within the enclosure tradition. Longer-to-shorter ratios are about 1.25 for Dholavira's reported inner arms, 1.39 for Kalibangan I, 2.00 for its Period II citadel and 1.50 for its Lower City, and 1.00 and 1.09 for Surkotada IC's citadel and annexe interiors. These are three towns and six architectural parts. Source dimension errors are unstated, Dholavira's southeast corner is unlocated, and no intention to use canonical ratios is inferred. The [exploratory comparison](shape-comparison-v1/results.json) retains the original scopes and an independent fraction-arithmetic check. A common enclosure practice therefore coexists with different reported proportions, including among parts of the same settlement.

We can compare construction functions more securely than whole-town totals. Mud brick is used widely in walls and platforms. Fired brick occurs in wet installations at Kalibangan, Banawali, Farmana and Rakhigarhi. Dholavira and Kanmer use substantial stone, while Shikarpur's excavated sequence changes from predominantly mud brick toward greater use of local stone. Harappa's early reed-and-post architecture, subsequent mud-brick buildings and later allocation of fired brick show change within a single town. Similar material choices can reflect common practice, available resources, recycling and functional requirements together. The [materials index](run-v3/towns.json) points to individual phase-qualified assertions.

Shared proportions also permit variation. Early Kalibangan and Banawali source passages describe 3:2:1 length-width-thickness bricks. Other Harappan passages report 4:2:1, often written as thickness-width-length 1:2:4. Harappa's reported gateway brick dimensions change through time. Ratios therefore retain their axis order, phase and structural use. They are not treated as a timeless binary signature. Claims about central production require independent fabric, mould or distribution evidence. EAST-KALIBANGAN-004 and -016; EAST-BANAWALI-003 and -017; EAST-FARMANA-006; W-HAR-008, -019, -020 and -025.

## Occupation duration remains a phase-specific question

Dholavira's preliminary source model spans about 3000 to 1450 BCE and contains interruptions. Its seven listed intervals add to 1,200 years within a 1,550-year endpoint span. The 350-year difference is arithmetic on the printed model, not measured years of abandonment. Kanmer's authors place much urban AMS evidence around 2500-2000 BC but leave the earliest and final boundaries unresolved. Banawali and Shikarpur retain relative phase histories without a comparable absolute duration in the inspected passages.

Other schemes need separate treatment. Thapar's 1975 Kalibangan BC ranges explicitly precede radiocarbon corrections. Joshi's Surkotada discussion has alternative endings and arithmetic BC ages. Farmana's early chronology is disputed within its own report; one sample carrying a room label is dated to the first millennium AD. Harappa's older Ravi start is an open bound, earlier than 3900 BC, preserved as a historical model rather than a selected founding date. Our [chronology export](run-v3/duration-spans.json) retains these conventions and suppresses an exact subtraction for open bounds. None supplies a new continuous occupation duration or a dated construction boundary.

## Writing connects activities before it identifies institutions

The documentary evidence distinguishes display, manufacture, marking, fastening and discard. Dholavira's ten-sign panel was recovered in a gate chamber; its mounting above the passage is a reconstruction. The descriptive gypsum identification and specialist anhydrite result for a sampled fragment remain separate. A reused inscribed slab has a secondary architectural context. Those observations locate writing in the built environment without dating every sign to a particular gate alteration. [Bisht and Law, printed pp. 228-231, 586-587, 750 and 773](https://archive.org/details/dholavirareportpreliminaryjan2015_860_K); INS-001 to -012.

The complete 225-row seal-context extraction contains 162 unqualified stage labels and 63 missing, mixed or qualified labels. Exactly 139 rows explicitly mark an inscription. The sole III A row says Nil. We therefore cannot use it to date the beginning of writing. The stage distribution is a catalogue worklist; differences in exposure, phase duration, survival and redeposition prevent a seal-use trend from those raw counts. [Bisht pp. 247-259](https://archive.org/details/dholavirareportpreliminaryjan2015_860_K); [raw-label aggregate](modules/inscriptions/context-table-summary.json).

At Harappa, five incised steatite tablets, eight moulded faience tablets and two blanks have captioned manufacturing-area joins in a courtyard fill. The H-2218-H-2239 series has a different relation: sixteen of its twenty-two members occur in a secondary clump outside wall 296. Late deposition does not date manufacture or locate an issuing office. Pre-firing vessel stamps add a third operation. At Lothal, fastening traces support package closure, while proposed jar stoppers and wooden boxes remain alternatives. The 65-, 69-, 90- and 93-object accounts have different source populations and remain unreconciled. [Primary citations and exact object relations](modules/inscriptions/RESEARCH.md); INS-013 to -043.

The project already has useful text evidence: long seal legends are individually distinctive, some shorter legends recur across sites, and sealings can match texts on seals found elsewhere. It also has failed functional and regional predictions. The household-name-office map failed its calibration, the city-specific variant predictor failed to improve prediction, and narrow fish and calendar interpretations do not supply observed contents or dates. We integrate **sixteen pinned prior outputs** in the [crosswalk](modules/inscriptions/prior-results.json). Their role is to define possible documentary relationships and exposed material, rather than to label buildings from unverified meanings.

## What we propose beyond the existing theories

Multicentric growth, corporate groups, collective provision and labour or ration tokens have direct predecessors. We credit Kenoyer, Vidale, Green, Rao and the prior project conjectures. The contribution here is a common source ledger and a phase-linked test joining changes in shared urban infrastructure to independently identified documentary operations.

We state three working claims in new cards. **C-0017: common rules preserved shared routes through local rebuilding**, credence 0.55. **C-0018: sealings recorded handovers between urban groups**, 0.35. **C-0019: repeated Harappa tablet series recorded obligations for shared works**, 0.10, the long shot. These are subjective starting judgments from examined evidence. Negotiation, central supervision and engineering convergence can all produce durable routes. Goods allocation can produce provisioning assemblages without a labour obligation. The cards and [test design](TEST-DESIGN.md) require contrasts that distinguish those alternatives before a specific institutional reading gains support.

The modern methods programme combines phase-keyed GIS, access and street graphs, local orientation districts, construction and material analysis, chronological modelling and recovery-aware context comparisons. The [methods](METHODS.md) state which measures the current sources permit. A recent orientation preprint is demonstrated on generated angles only. In the example, two perfect local grids 30 degrees apart each have Q4 equal to 1, while their global Q4 is 0.5. That is why aggregate geometric order needs local and chronological checks. The exact independent-pair planning example gives only 0.416 power with twenty non-tied pairs at a useful 70% sign probability. Neither example is an archaeological result.

## Audit and the next decisive evidence

Two failed integration endpoints remain preserved. Run-v1 failed on a record-wrapper mismatch. Run-v2 retained the raw assertions but failed phase validation; review also found false substring tags, an omitted multi-site association and lost chronological operators. Run-v3 replaces those tags with reviewed exact feature sets, preserves concurrent evidence and limits, retains the unknown-phase reasons and open bound, and passes the reviewer spot checks. The sixty readiness gaps are five unassembled comparison prerequisites for each town. They do not assert that the underlying archaeological features are absent. [Reviews and response](REVIEW-RESPONSE.md) record each repair.

All acquired reports, plans, imagery, extractions and large products remain local-only under `D:\projects\indus_valley_script`. This package records source and coverage changes for maintainer integration. It leaves the older experiments, their north references, horizon assumptions and uncertainty intact. No publication, visibility change, CI/CD or external deployment accompanies this study.

**The strongest reading is that Indus urbanism preserved shared frameworks while repeatedly changing the buildings and activities connected to them. The deciding test is a phase-keyed survey of the same routes, entrances and adjacent structures, independently dated and joined to primary documentary contexts, followed by a frozen test of whether access changes predict documentary operations beyond production, household and recovery effects.**
