# Comparing the towns through their building histories

We compare a settlement at several scales: the setting, enclosed district, street and drain network, plot, building, room and individual deposit. The comparison asks which arrangements recur, how those arrangements change, and which scale required coordination. A shared brick proportion and a shared town plan are different observations. So are a rebuilt drain, an added room and the abandonment of a district.

## The theoretical starting point

Smith's 2007 model distinguishes coordination within a settlement from standardisation across settlements. Architectural inventories, spatial arrangements, orientation, measures, access and visibility can vary independently. We adopt those distinctions and code the material sequence that produced each arrangement. His account of different levels of meaning also sets a useful order of work: reconstruct movement and access before proposing a political or cosmological message. Source: [Smith 2007](https://doi.org/10.1177/1538513206293713), printed pp. 6-8, 24-30, 33-40; local PDF indices 4-6, 22-28, 31-38.

Indus scholarship already explains urban growth through multiple corporate groups and successive enclosures. Vidale describes differently aligned walled districts and argues for powerful groups; Green emphasises collective provision and distributed governance. These positions share evidence for coordination but make different claims about unequal power. We retain both as competing institutional explanations. Our proposed extension joins their architectural predictions to independently coded documentary contexts and to specific rebuilding episodes. Sources: [Vidale, *Heterarchic Powers in the Ancient Indus Cities*](https://iris.unive.it/retrieve/39773d22-47c4-4b09-85a8-9111b50ccfd1/18-17-PB.pdf), printed pp. 6-10, PDF indices 11-15; [Green 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpos.2022.823071), sections on public goods, institutions and revenue. The journal issue containing Vidale's article is dated 2018 and includes a reference to a January 2019 conversation; we preserve that bibliographic discrepancy.

Green's 2018 reanalysis uses GIS and three-dimensional reconstructions to examine buildings at a street intersection. Its explicit reconstruction assumptions matter: a proposed shared upper floor and a reconstructed signboard have different evidential standing from surviving foundations and entrances. We use it as a precedent for comparing access through phases, with alternative reconstructions retained. [Green 2018](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0959774317000774), accepted manuscript, Figs. 6-9 and captions, PDF indices 44-47. Ramzy's author abstract supplies a related space-syntax comparison of access and visibility; the full article was not acquired, so we make no claim to reproduce its measurements. [Ramzy 2016](https://doi.org/10.1057/udi.2015.11), author publication record, Abstract.

Boeing's 2019 orientation and network measures offer reproducible descriptions of streets. Barthelemy and Boeing's September 2026 preprint adds spatially connected districts with common orientations, distinguishing local order from a city's aggregate direction distribution. We use this recent work as a proposed method and demonstrate one fourfold measure on generated angles. Their modern, drivable-street datasets do not validate the method for excavated Indus plans. [Boeing 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s41109-019-0189-1), methods; [Barthelemy and Boeing 2026, v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13029v1), sections II, III.3, IV and Supplement I. Source code for their full district partition is not reproduced here.

Recent comparative archaeology also separates occupation persistence from prosperity and from equality. Lawrence and colleagues find that scale can account for an association between longer persistence and housing inequality. Their analysis makes a direct long-duration-equals-good-governance inference untenable. We distinguish the span of an archaeological sequence, the duration of a particular urban arrangement, and evidence that occupation actually continued. [Lawrence and colleagues 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2400696122), Significance, Settlement Persistence and Material Inequality, and Discussion.

## A common observation model

Every assertion retains a town, architectural part or context, source identity and exact locator. Local periods remain separate namespaces. Kalibangan Period I, Kanmer Period I and Mohenjo-daro Intermediate I do not become a common time bin because their labels look alike.

| Domain | Fields to recover | Comparison and principal control |
|---|---|---|
| Setting | Coordinates, coordinate basis, elevation datum, landform, palaeochannel or coast, catchment, stone and clay sources | Compare dated landscapes. Modern river distance cannot stand in for Bronze Age access. |
| Extent | Survey boundary, mound footprint, enclosure footprint, occupied area, excavated area, exposed area by phase | Keep each area definition. A 100 ha site estimate and a 1 ha exposed enclosure have different denominators. |
| Building sequence | Earlier and later wall, cut, abutment, blocked doorway, resurfaced road, repair, hiatus | Store ordering relations in a directed graph. A phase label does not replace a physical join. |
| Street geometry | Phase-specific centreline, junction, width, slope, bearing, north type, scale and restoration mask | Compare equal spatial windows and alternative completions of missing streets. |
| Access | Entrances, passage widths, steps, closures, route depth, connected components | Treat uncertain doors as alternative graphs. Modern restoration must not create an ancient entrance. |
| Plots and houses | Plot boundary, building boundary, floor area, courtyard, rooms, stairs, subdivisions and amalgamations | Keep incomplete houses separate. A room count does not equal a household count. |
| Water supply | Well, reservoir, cistern, inlet, outlet, catchment and capacity estimate | Separate contemporaneous units, successive rebuilds and whole-site extrapolations. |
| Drainage | Household outlet, street drain, soak pit, cleaning access, slope, rebuilt or blocked segment | Compare connected systems and maintenance relationships rather than isolated drain lengths. |
| Manufacture | Kiln, furnace, firing canister, working floor, tools, unfinished products, waste and chemical evidence | Distinguish production in place from dumped manufacturing debris. |
| Construction | Mudbrick, fired brick, stone, timber impression, mortar, plaster, bitumen; dimensions and bonds | Model each material by component and function. A town can use several construction systems. |
| Public and ritual use | Plan, access, fixtures, residue, objects, use wear and excavator's label | Store the building label separately from the evidence for that use. |
| Documentary practice | Physical object, faces, type, manufacture, seal impression, fastening trace, text group, motif | Join through accession and excavation identifiers, keeping repeated texts separate from physical aliases. |
| Recovery | Excavation campaign, trench volume, mesh, retained material, collection policy, disturbance and publication coverage | Compare recovery opportunities before interpreting absences or object densities. |

The source ledger distinguishes a direct excavator observation, an original modern reanalysis, a project interpretation, an inherited local compilation and an abstract-only report. A full source copy can be held while a particular claim remains unverified. Conversely, a checked figure caption can support one narrow fact without supplying the entire report.

## Detecting incremental alteration, growth and replanning

We use incremental alteration for an ordered change to an existing arrangement: a new room occupies open ground, a buttress narrows a street, a plot divides, a doorway closes, or a lane absorbs a building addition. Growth additionally requires increased occupied area, density or accommodation. A change needs both the feature and its relation to an earlier layout. Its pace and social coordination require further evidence. Crookedness on a composite plan alone does not establish the process.

Coordinated replanning requires a linked change at a larger scale: multiple walls change alignment together, streets and drains are replaced as a system, an enclosure incorporates existing occupation, or a documented clearance precedes a new layout. The scale of coordination is recorded as building, cluster, district or settlement. A radial plan can be deliberately organised. A regular enclosure can surround houses that accumulated in several episodes.

For each episode we code the retained boundary, the changed element and the physical sequence. Changes can coexist: a household encroaches while the main drainage route remains open. We therefore allow a town-phase to carry both a local alteration and a larger coordinated work. The important test is whether the shared system continued to function and which arrangements constrained later builders.

Three rival accounts guide comparison. A single authority can impose a layout and supervise modifications. Several groups can negotiate common streets and drains while controlling their own plots. Independent builders can converge on similar solutions under topographic, hydraulic and material constraints. Their predictions diverge in the timing and spatial extent of repairs, access restrictions, construction variation and the location of documentary objects. Geometry alone leaves those alternatives open.

## Quantifying similarity when the evidence permits it

We publish a feature-by-feature comparison before a composite distance. A future distance will have six separately reported components: geometry, access, infrastructure, construction, building inventory and sequence. It will include the overlap in observed fields and the uncertainty of each component. We will not let many easy-to-code brick fields dominate a single difficult-to-code chronology field.

For nominal observations, agreement is exact equality within an explicit classification. For a positive count, the denominator and exposure must be comparable; count rates can use excavated area or volume only when that is the actual recovery opportunity. For dimensions, report observed ranges and measurement error rather than creating precision from rounded reports. For dates, compare interval overlap under the original dating convention and then under separately documented chronological models. An unknown is removed from the shared denominator and retains its reason. An asserted absence is a different value.

A Gower-style mixed-feature distance could combine such comparisons after weights, codings, minimum overlap and phase matching are frozen. This study does not calculate that distance: the present records mix partial exposures, cumulative structures and uncertain chronology. Publishing a ranked resemblance score now would conceal the very differences the source work recovered. The completed comparisons are the matched feature statements and phase transitions, each linked to its observations.

Network analysis needs an explicit graph. Street intersections become nodes and walkable segments become edges. We would report length-weighted and unweighted bearings, circuity, dead ends, intersection degree, block dimensions and accessibility in equal-area or equally complete windows. Betweenness is recomputed for plausible missing connections, because excavation edges can make a marginal room appear central. Doorway graphs and street graphs answer different questions and remain separate.

Our generated demonstration computes `Q4 = abs(sum(w * exp(4j * theta)) / sum(w))`. Right-angle bearings share a fourfold axis. Two equally weighted, internally perfect grids separated by 30 degrees have a global Q4 of 0.5 and local Q4 values of 1. The example shows why a town-wide orientation statistic can blur distinct ordered districts. These are generated angles with known district labels. They are neither a partition of an Indus town nor an implementation of the 2026 paper's full clustering algorithm.

## Chronology, duration and landscape

We preserve the source's start and end bounds, whether a determination is calibrated, the sample material, laboratory identifier and relationship to construction. An occupation interval is not a wall date. A discard context can postdate the making or use of a tablet. The new comparison carries forward the Q-1032 north definitions, errors and named systems through explicit pointers; it adds no new true-north survey or ancient-horizon reconstruction.

Occupation span is an interval calculation. If a start lies between 2800 and 2600 BCE and an end between 2000 and 1800 BCE, the possible elapsed span is 600-1000 years, subject to continuity. The midpoint is not a measured duration. We distinguish an excavator's chronological scheme from a sample-based model, and we leave hiatuses visible. For censored sites whose beginning or end is unknown, a survival model would require censoring and a stated sample frame. The twelve selected cases cannot supply a civilisation-wide abandonment hazard.

Location analysis also needs dates. Singh and colleagues' luminescence and provenance study shows that the former Sutlej channel had changed before urban settlement in the studied Ghaggar-Hakra sector. We use that result to require dated channel reconstructions, rather than assigning every town a perennial river from its modern position. It supplies a regional constraint, not the water history of every settlement. [Singh and colleagues 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-01643-9), Abstract, Results and Discussion; original article held through Harappa.com.

## Connecting structures to documentary objects

The critical join is object to deposit to architectural episode. We retain the relation type: found on a floor, in construction packing, in a drain, in a secondary dump, in surface collection or through an uncertain catalogue concordance. A named room can recur through centuries of rebuilding. A shared room label and a late date therefore do not identify a primary writing context.

A future test codes architecture without seeing sign sequences and codes object manufacture, fastening, wear and text identity without seeing architectural categories. It then freezes both tables and their join rules. Possible endpoints include whether sealing debris concentrates at controlled access points, whether repeated tablet series share production contexts, and whether text similarity crosses district boundaries more often than manufacturing style does. We compare like phases and recovery conditions, keep physical aliases and production series together, and include alternative explanations from storage, trade and household property.

The present review attaches the shared shots harness in synthetic mode and records prior population exposure. Its passed guards show that the workflow can block premature confirmation reads and detect spent material. No fresh archaeological score or credence update follows from that generated check. The prospective design will require enough new independently coded contexts and objects to distinguish a useful effect after within-town and phase controls. A pilot must establish that supply and its power before any confirmation access.
