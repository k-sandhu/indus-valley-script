We compare how Indus settlements were built, maintained and changed, and how their documentary objects relate to those processes. The core frame is Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Dholavira, Lothal, Chanhudaro, Kalibangan, Banawali, Rakhigarhi, Surkotada, Kanmer, Shikarpur and Farmana. These are purposively selected excavated cases, not a representative sample of the civilisation. The first eleven continue Q-1032's required towns; Farmana adds a smaller settlement with a published architectural and burial record.

## Selection and stopping rule

We search original excavation publications, excavation-project pages, author manuscripts and institutional repositories. Modern comparative methods are sourced from original methodological papers and archaeological applications. For each town we seek a dated sequence, plan or spatial description, materials, reported structures and counts, and evidence of alteration. We stop this first comparative pass after all twelve town cards carry at least one checked source or an explicit source-access failure and each required field carries evidence or a missing reason. We retain competing dates and counts. Additional sources are included when they resolve a specific conflict or furnish a new field. This bounded review does not claim an exhaustive bibliography or a census of all buildings.

## Observation units and comparisons

The observation unit is a source-qualified assertion about a named architectural part, a building episode, an excavated context or an object group. A whole settlement is a grouping variable. A count must retain what was counted, excavation extent, source date, period and whether it is an excavated total, minimum, estimate or inferred whole-town quantity. Approximate areas and source chronology stay in their original conventions. We do not turn BC labels into calibrated BCE dates or phase spans into continuous occupation without evidence.

We distinguish geometry, access, infrastructure, materials, building use, chronology and recovery. A regular plan is a geometric observation; coordination scale and institutional form are interpretations. Evidence for change requires an ordered relation such as an inserted wall, encroachment, plot subdivision, rebuilt drain, new enclosure, or a documented rebuilding horizon. The word organic will be operationalised through those changes. We preserve the reverse sequence, incremental settlement followed by coordinated replanning, wherever supported.

Similarity will be assessed by explicit feature comparisons with source locators and overlap counts. We will not compute a single town ranking from opportunistically reported features or count unreported structures as absent. Any numerical demonstration using invented street graphs or effects will be labelled synthetic. No town-level correlation with raw seal totals will be treated as a functional test: excavation extent, residual deposits, object identity, phase and publication effort must first be controlled.
