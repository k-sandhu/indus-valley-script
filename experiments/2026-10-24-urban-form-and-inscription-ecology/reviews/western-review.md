# Review of the comparative methods and C-0017 to C-0019

Reviewed 24 October 2026. This review covers the versions read before the coordinator's prospective test-design file existed. The coordinator is adding a data-sufficiency gate and a synthetic sign-test power grid. No archaeological test is frozen or reported as passed. The methods already preserve the principal source distinctions: local phase names, incomplete excavation, different area definitions, uncertain entrances, household groupings and secondary deposits. The fourfold-orientation example is explicitly synthetic, and its value of 0.5 for two equal grids separated by 30 degrees is correct.

The substantive repairs concern what a positive architectural association would support. A surviving shared route, a collection of used sealings and a tablet beside stored provisions each admit several institutional explanations. The cards can remain bold while making their proposed tests discriminate those explanations more directly.

## 1. C-0019 needs direct labour-token attribution and a discriminating update

**Priority: high.** The claim describes tablets recording obligations or entitlements for shared work. Its prior-proposal list includes C-0005 and Green 2022, but C-0005 already cites a direct predecessor: Rajesh P. N. Rao's 2018 chapter, *The Indus Script and Economics. A Role for Indus Seals and Tablets in Rationing and Administration of Labor*. Printed p. 518, PDF index 0, explicitly proposes miniature tablets as ration tokens allocated to porters or labourers. Pages 519–520 develop the ration comparison, and pp. 524–525 extend the proposal to repetitive transactions.

The checked local source is `research/literature/sources/rao_2018_economics.pdf`, SHA-256 `512bc92b4fb9134c1ceb1175b7e53ec8628fcea85db82a6dd3b5295d62cacaea`, eight pages with printed pagination 518–525. Primary links are [Rao 2018, author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00049) and [the author-hosted chapter](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/The-Indus-Script.pdf). Its proposed meanings and numerical quantities remain hypotheses; attribution does not validate them.

Add Rao directly to the card and define the extension as **phase-linked obligations for shared infrastructure, tested against domestic goods, ordinary ration distribution and workshop activities**. The current sentence “P1 alone moves credence to 0.18” is too permissive: a tablet association with provisioning could support ordinary ration or container records just as well. Require P3's independently coded distinction before any upward update of this specific claim. The coordinator has agreed to these two repairs.

## 2. C-0018 needs an independent definition of a handover

**Priority: high.** C-0018 P1 requires “independently identified controlled handovers,” while the claim lists controlled passages and storage access as the relevant architecture. A narrow doorway and a store do not identify a change of custody. Both occur in ordinary households. If “handover” is assigned because a deposit contains sealings, the proposed enrichment is circular.

Before the pilot, define an architectural category such as controlled receiving or dispatch access using access routes, repeated opening and closing, goods movement and independent use evidence. Code that category without seeing whether sealings are present. Keep independently evidenced custody transfer as a narrower category, which may initially have no eligible cases. If the pilot can establish only controlled storage access, report that result at that level and keep the handover interpretation separate. METHODS, “A future test codes architecture without seeing sign sequences,” should explicitly blind architecture coders to the target artefact occurrence as well as its text.

The claim also says a particular stamp identified an authorising holder. One person could use several dies and a group could share one die. Die identity is observable more directly than holder identity. Preserve the holder relation as part of the conjecture and identify a prospective observation capable of distinguishing individual from corporate authorisation.

## 3. C-0017's first predictions test durable infrastructure more directly than negotiation

**Priority: high for a future credence update.** P1 predicts that shared routes survive more often than plot walls. P2 predicts local construction differences alongside a connected repair. Both patterns also follow from centrally supervised work that preserves expensive infrastructure, or from builders adapting to hydraulic and topographic constraints. Passing P1 and P2 therefore supports a durable shared framework without uniquely identifying negotiation among autonomous building groups.

Retain the architectural prediction and write its institutional extension explicitly. Either reserve the proposed 0.70 update for a successful comparison against specified supervision and convergence models, or state that P1 and P2 update the architectural component while institutional negotiation retains its starting credence. Candidate discriminators include asynchronous locally commissioned repairs, independently sourced component choices, and access arrangements allowing multiple groups to maintain a common route. Their interpretation still needs a defined model of what settlement-wide supervision would predict.

P1 also needs a common preservation opportunity. Deep shared drains and substantial external walls can survive where thin partitions have been robbed away. Match or model material, foundation depth, disturbance and exposed extent before interpreting a route-versus-partition retention difference of 0.25 as a behavioural choice.

## 4. Four towns are an entry minimum, not a reliable cluster-inference design

**Priority: medium.** C-0017 P1 and C-0018 P1 combine small town counts with a “town-cluster interval.” Twelve episodes or twenty context pairs may contain much less than twelve or twenty independent comparisons. Four town clusters supply very limited information about between-town variation; ordinary cluster-robust intervals or a naive cluster bootstrap are unreliable at that scale.

The planned synthetic sign-test grid is useful if it is labelled an optimistic sensitivity calculation for independent discordant comparisons. Its sign probability is not automatically the conditional odds ratio in the cards, and concordant zero pairs add no sign-test information. Freeze the eventual inference unit, small-cluster method, expected discordance and town heterogeneity after the pilot. The number of independent towns and episodes, rather than only the number of artefacts or pairs, must pass the sufficiency gate. This is a prospective design dependency, not a reason to withhold the source study.

## 5. C-0017 P3 must not obtain its chronology from the connection being tested

**Priority: medium.** P3 predicts that repair timing groups by infrastructure connection more than by distance. Its phrase “independently model construction boundaries” is appropriate but needs a concrete exclusion rule. Connected drains can themselves be used to infer contemporaneity. If those inferred phase joins become the target dates, a connection-based model wins partly by construction.

Use independently dated construction or resurfacing relations for this endpoint. Flag dates or phase assignments that depend on the tested drain connection and omit them from the decisive subset. Retain all dated observations and test whether differences in excavation precision alone explain the predictive gain.

## 6. C-0018 P2's model must test the stated text-versus-style contrast

**Priority: medium.** The prediction says closing text units recur across handover settings more than manufacture style does, but its pass rule only requires a joint predictive model to beat rearrangements. A model can improve because of style alone and satisfy that rule. Text and manufacturing classifications also have different numbers and frequencies of categories, so raw recurrence rates are not comparable.

Specify a common prediction target and compare a context-and-style baseline with the same model after adding frozen text units. Report the added predictive information from text and the reciprocal contribution from style. Preserve frequency and production-group structure in the rearrangements. This makes a positive result answer the stated prediction while allowing the two features to contribute different information.

## 7. The methods should label small changes without assigning their social cause

**Priority: low.** METHODS defines “incremental growth” to include a blocked doorway and a buttress narrowing a street. These are incremental alterations; they can be undertaken in a coordinated building campaign and do not all increase settlement size. Use “incremental alteration” for the observed sequence and reserve “growth” for increased occupied area, density or accommodation. Assign planned or organic processes only after specifying the evidence for coordination and the scale involved.

This repair matters to the question being studied. Harappa's extra-mural growth followed by enclosure is established as an ordering relation; the available passage does not recover the households' initial plot decisions. Dholavira's clearance of Stage II housing in Stage III demonstrates replanning, while the original degree of coordination among those earlier houses remains open.

## Disposition

The source-backed comparison and its synthetic demonstrations are suitable for integration with the stated uncertainties. The conjecture cards are prospective acquisition targets. Direct Rao attribution, a discriminating labour-token update and independent handover coding should be repaired before the proposed functional tests are presented as ready for registration. The strongest supported architectural proposition is that shared routes and boundaries could persist through substantial local rebuilding. A deciding institutional test must compare the causes that can produce that persistence.
