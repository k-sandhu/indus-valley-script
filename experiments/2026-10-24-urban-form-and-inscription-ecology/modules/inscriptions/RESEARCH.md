# Writing belongs to particular activities and building episodes

The best link between town form and inscriptions is a chain from an identified object to a dated deposit and a particular structure. We now have useful pieces of that chain at Harappa, Lothal and Dholavira. They show different documentary activities in different places: manufacture in a bounded courtyard, discard outside a city wall, fastening of packages in a named passage, marking of vessels before firing and large writing associated with a gate. These observations give a basis for comparing how towns coordinated work and movement. They do not yet give a population of contemporary households, workshops or administrative buildings with comparable recovery rates.

This module supplies [43 source assertions](observations.json), [11 source records](sources.json), [16 exact prior-output references](prior-results.json) and a [prospective design](test-design.md). Its new context-table extraction covers all 225 printed Dholavira seal rows. Detailed tables, source text and twelve rendered pages remain on D:; [local-assets.json](local-assets.json) pins their paths and hashes. Two new download requests returned 403. We recovered the same publications from existing local holdings, so no new source binary or physical object is added. The read scope was recorded after initial source-location work, before the table totals. All work here is exploratory source work.

The status of these joins matters to urban comparison. A tablet in wall-side rubbish can tell us about disposal around a boundary. Its location cannot locate the office that issued it. A stamp fired into a goblet was selected during manufacture, while a later sump fill records the vessel's disposal. We preserve these events as separate relations, each with its own date and uncertainty.

## Dholavira: display, reuse and a usable context denominator

The preliminary report places the ten-sign panel in the western chamber of the Castle's North Gate. Its proposed original position above the central passage uses the approximate agreement between a 3.5 m passage and the panel with its frame impression. The find location is more secure than the reconstruction of its mounting. The inscription section does not join the panel to a named construction deposit. The Stage V narrowing of a Stage IV doorway, described elsewhere in the report, therefore cannot automatically date the panel or show that it remained displayed during the alteration. The four-sign limestone fragment from a southern Bailey underground chamber has a secondary context; its interpretation as a former doorway lintel leaves its original setting unresolved. [Bisht, preliminary report, pp. 228–231 and 586–587](https://archive.org/download/dholavirareportpreliminaryjan2015_860_K/Dholavira%20Report%20%28preliminary%2C%20Jan%202015%29.pdf).

The report also preserves a useful material distinction. Its descriptive section calls the panel elements gypsum. Randall Law's specialist chapter reports anhydrite in a tiny analyzed fragment and proposes heated gypsum as an explanation. This refines one sampled material identification; it does not establish the same treatment for every sign element. The chapter similarly identifies a visually tuff-like seal material as primarily kaolinite and leaves a local Khadir source unconfirmed. We retain description, laboratory result and proposed production process as separate assertions. They offer testable links between monumental writing and skilled manufacture, without deriving workshop ownership from material resemblance. [Law, “Identification and analysis of stones and metals,” pp. 750 and 773, in the same report](https://archive.org/download/dholavirareportpreliminaryjan2015_860_K/Dholavira%20Report%20%28preliminary%2C%20Jan%202015%29.pdf).

The stage-wise seal table supplies a bounded denominator for further work. [Our raw-label aggregate](context-table-summary.json) reports:

The single III A row is accession 13204 in the Castle; its inscription field says Nil. It cannot supply a III A inscription. Across the table, 139 rows explicitly say Yes or Yes, in the inscription column. Blank, Nil, Missing, illegible and uncertain values retain separate categories. The table includes objects without inscriptions and damaged examples, so 225 cannot become a count of readable seals. No accession string repeats within this extraction, but that check does not adjudicate physical aliases or the corpus-family crosswalk. The earlier 28-row identity audit is an overlapping source check and adds no rows to this population. [Bisht, pp. 247–259, section 8.2.2](https://archive.org/download/dholavirareportpreliminaryjan2015_860_K/Dholavira%20Report%20%28preliminary%2C%20Jan%202015%29.pdf).

These counts can guide a phase-qualified sampling plan. They cannot estimate changing seal use until excavation exposure, phase duration, primary versus secondary deposition and recovery practice are recorded. In particular, Stage VI objects may include retained, redeposited or newly produced pieces. The table gives that question an accession-level worklist; it does not answer it.

## Harappa: manufacture, circulation and discard have different maps

Kenoyer's Trench 54 report links inscribed-object manufacture to a small courtyard identified through robbed wall voids. Its Figure 3 captions name individual accessions and lots for tablets and blanks. The nearby production evidence includes faience wasters, sawn steatite and vitrified firing canisters. The excavator records no surviving kiln-like structure in the room and proposes a small canister firing arrangement. This is a useful control for structure counts: the absence of a recognizable built kiln does not entail an absence of high-temperature manufacture. The author also compares bicolour tablets with examples from Area D and Mound F. Those links merit material and mould checks before they become a measured distribution network. A possible mint and regulated production are published interpretations of this evidence. [Kenoyer 2005, PDF pp. 2–5, Figures 2–4](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer%202005%20Faience%20Workshop.pdf).

A different group, the 22 incised steatite tablets H-2218–H-2239, was discarded outside Mound E's curtain wall. The source places its debris after wall 296 and its revetment fill and before Period 3C material. That sequence dates incorporation into rubbish. At least three cutting hands and shared cutting conventions were inferred from microscopic examination, while the number and location of workshops remain unresolved. The sixteen-object clump is evidence of gathering before or during discard. The source's possible pouch is a reconstruction; no preserved pouch, direct attachment, contents, independent quantity, cancellation or object-specific wear is reported for this cohort. The wear passage concerns moulded terracotta tablets. [Meadow and Kenoyer 2000, author pp. 13–17 and endnote 3](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer2000_The%20Tiny%20Steatite%20Seals%20of%20Harappa.pdf), [complete field audit](../../../2026-10-24-tablet-use-evidence/REPORT.md).

The 47-object source frame gives a third map: forty pre-firing vessel stamps, six separate clay tags and an unbaked stopper. Only two vessel fragments have individual published fill joins in the bounded audit. H-1745 is registered as H95-2407/5080-500 from a Period 3C sump fill; H-1734 is H95-2526/4450-31 from Trench 10W fill. Their marks entered the vessels before firing. These are promising producer, commissioner or intended-use designations, with no independently observed contents yet. [Meadow and Kenoyer 1997, p. 161, Figure 12](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer1997_Excavations%20at%20Harappa%201994-1995%20New%20Perspective.pdf), [operation and context audit](../../../2026-10-20-archaeological-setting/REPORT.md).

Harappa also supplies a temporal comparison worth pursuing. The excavators report Late Harappan domestic floors and craft activity, with smaller fired bricks retaining approximately 1:2:4 proportions, while formal inscribed seals and inscriptions are absent from their recovered late evidence. Persistence of a building convention can therefore accompany a change in documentary practice. The same source warns that seal dates may rest on carving style and glaze, and that unbaked clay is easily lost. Those premises must enter the comparison of periods. [Kenoyer and Meadow 2010, author-manuscript pp. 7–12](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/KenoyerMeadow%202010%20Inscribed%20Objects%20from%20Harappa.pdf).

## Lothal: a package network inside an incompletely resolved deposit

Lothal has the clearest physical evidence of fastening among the cases reviewed. L-164 joins excavation 1864 and museum registration LTH SRG 1232. Rao describes cords on a flat underside and proposes a jar with a flat stopper. Frenez and Tosi include it among twelve impressions of one elephant die and tentatively reconstruct polished wooden boxes. Their heading is “Wooden Surfaces (Boxes?)”; shape, size and the role of the strings remain conjectural. Published repeated impression combinations on L-189, L-190 and L-193 supply a small co-use network. That is evidence about dies used on closures, with no automatic count of officers or simultaneous transactions. [Rao 1985, pp. 319 and 321–322](https://archive.org/details/masi78lothalaharappanporttownraos.r.part2_531_D), [Frenez and Tosi 2005, pp. 78–79](https://zenodo.org/records/5514283), [separate primary-page review](../../../2026-10-24-office-source-verification/REPORT.md).

Its architectural setting is less resolved. Volume I reports 65 sealings in successive layers near blocks 41i–41j; Volume II reports 69 of 90 in a small area of the easternmost passage. The later study uses a 93-object assemblage. These are source-specific populations, with no reconciled object inventory. Fire and low firing affect survival, and successive layers prevent the concentration from becoming one proven administrative event. The relevant denominator is the inventoried deposit or building episode, with objects assigned individually. [Rao 1979, p. 113](https://archive.org/details/masi78lothalaharappanporttownraos.r.part1platesmissing_443_k), [source-qualified evidence ledger](../../../2026-10-24-office-seal-evidence/REPORT.md).

## What the rest of the project adds

The existing statistical results favour a common set of written conventions with room for local use. In 1,430 eligible seal objects, 65 legends recur on 174 seals and 41 recurring legends span sites. Long legends are mostly individually specific: none of 445 legends of at least six signs repeats. The corrected sealing recount has 13 of 16 matching sealings matching only seals at other sites, representing four of six distinct texts. Text movement, seal movement and package movement remain different causal possibilities. The exploratory smaller-site concentration is a reason to seek named building contexts, with identity checks for a possible duplicate at Dholavira. [Exact counts and corrected recount](prior-results.json), PR-01 and PR-02.

The regional graphic-variant test failed its positive prediction: adding city reduced prediction by 0.01584 bits per target across 306 scored components, with a 95% interval from −0.04107 to +0.00610. That result supplies no city-specific graphic classifier for institutions. Ordered fish combinations are a positive structural result, while the narrow asterism-coverage prediction failed. The H-912 exact Fish construction has only one independent confirmation text component. Neither result supplies an observed fish ration or a calendar for an urban axis. The failed 118-sign household, name and office map and the still-ineligible lexical extraction also provide no building-function labels. We preserve these failed paths because a deep urban study would otherwise be tempted to treat their proposed meanings as independent data. [Versioned outputs and exact scopes](prior-results.json), PR-03 and PR-09–PR-16.

## The strongest reading and the deciding test

**Indus urban coordination joined durable shared places to several portable documentary practices.** Dholavira's monumental writing, Harappa's bounded production and manufacture-time vessel marks, and Lothal's cord-fastened closures support that reading. The project can now extend it through named building episodes. The same textual convention could operate in a household, a local production group, a wider corporation or a city authority; the differences become measurable through access, production and movement.

Our proposed long shot is: **rebuilding shared routes and gateways redirected documentary operations while the common stock of written formulae persisted**. Subjective credence: **0.35**, assigned after this source review, with no update from a new confirmation test. The deciding study will compare independently dated primary contexts before and after named access changes, identify production groups without reading their inscriptions, then test whether changes in route access predict the distribution of stamps, tags and tablets beyond household, workshop and preservation effects. A reproducible absence of that relation in adequately powered new contexts, or a complete explanation by redeposited manufacture debris, kills this proposed extension. [The prospective design](test-design.md) states the competitors, missing data and freshness gates.
