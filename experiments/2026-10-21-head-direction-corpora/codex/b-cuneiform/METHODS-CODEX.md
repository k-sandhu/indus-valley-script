# Cuneiform normalisation decisions

We preserve each edited seal legend as one record, including empty, damaged and unresolved cases. A surface marked as a seal starts a legend separate from its tablet or envelope account. A physical seal or a direct sealing can carry its legend in the body without a separate seal-surface directive. The catalogue object type identifies those cases. Records from an archive entry with no readable legend carry an edition-status note. The local review tables preserve competing editions and unresolved metadata.

The P-number identifies the catalogued object carrying the text. A sealing's die identifier comes only from a linked CDLI S-number; suffixes in those identifiers remain as printed. A physical matrix is classified from its object type and subgenre. A specific catalogue type such as bulla or envelope takes priority over a generic ATF tablet marker. For an impression whose catalogue describes a tablet and envelope together, the enclosing ORACC object directive supplies the support. The source object type is retained on every record. A catalogue seal whose edition calls itself a composite receives an unresolved identity flag; its connection to a physical matrix requires review before analysis.

The majority of ORACC word-language labels decides between Sumerian and Akkadian. We retain the counts and mark legends containing both languages. An exact tie is unresolved. CDLI has no word lemmatisation, so its ATF language declaration supplies the initial word labels, with explicit language switches respected. A single-language catalogue declaration supplies a label when no word-language declaration is available. Other ATF language declarations, such as `xhu`, remain outside the target corpora. Conflicts between the catalogue and edition are listed for review. The token labels classify the edition as supplied; deciding whether they represent the intended language is a philological review.

We use the ORACC edition when both sources supply the same P-number. CDLI catalogue identifiers supplement its aliases and metadata. Each superseded CDLI section is retained locally with its source locator and the reason for precedence. This is the source choice required for overlapping editions; we perform no grouping of repeated legends, shared dies or objects.

ORACC words, GDL sign units and source references follow the earlier normaliser. The reproduction check reconstructs every original field before the schema extension, including the earlier broad `gold` categories. New records keep the actual ORACC POS codes in `gold`. Tokens lacking usable GDL receive units parsed from their printed word and remain marked as dirty in the source parser. This fallback cannot admit a previously excluded record into the clean legacy reproduction.

CDLI words retain ATF spelling and damage marks. Sign units remove editorial brackets and query marks, separate determinatives and preserve compound sign notation. ASCII sign readings are mapped to the Unicode letters and subscript indices used by ORACC. Numeric quantities retain ordinary digits and receive the `NUM:` prefix. ATF hyphens separate sign readings within a word; they do not establish lexical compound boundaries. Each cuneiform word therefore contributes one lexical member. Arrays of members, tags and tag sources are nested by word.

ORACC proper-name and numeral POS codes determine the specified tags. For other words the lexicon first matches citation form and guide word, then a whole-word spelling. The lexicon also declares the POS fallback rules for common nouns, adjectives, verbs and unclassified words. CDLI words absent from the lexicon receive `NAME` and `default-name`, as required. The review table lists every lexical or default assignment and its frequency. Inflected relational spellings are explicit entries; we remove no productive suffix by guesswork.

An unreadable legend is `illegible`. An unreadable, broken or missing first or last sign makes a legend `damaged-edge`, including an editorially supplied edge sign. Damage inside an otherwise bounded legend is `damaged-internal`; complete internal restorations with intact edges are `restored`. A question mark sets `reading_doubtful`. These categories retain the source's physical damage separately from whether an editor supplied a readable restoration.

A blank, uncertain or unknown provenience gives a null site and a reason. Archive labels do not locate an archaeological site, and a spelling containing replacement characters is left unresolved. Certain proveniences retain their regional wording when it is present. The source spelling is stored beside the normalised value, and every null site is copied to the local site review file.

Counts describe all records before eligibility decisions. Distinct legends at both length thresholds are counted by sign-unit sequence; a separate field reports distinct word spellings. Empty sequences and damaged records remain in the census. No statistic from protocol section 7 is computed here. The ten-record checks compare the archived digital editions with the output; the permitted network URLs do not include separate live object pages.
