Paths beginning `src-` are under `data/raw/2026-10-21-head-direction-corpora/`. The held normalised records are used only for the specified Sumerian seal reproduction. We read the earlier normaliser before copying its ORACC functions; we imported no code from it or from a download.

The three CDLI files came through the shared `fetch_source.py` with certificate verification. The catalogue and ATF are full payloads from the media URLs, not Git LFS pointers. Their acquisition records carry the supplied rights note: CDLI permits copying, aggregation and reuse with credit according to common and fair academic practice, with [CDLI terms of use](https://cdli.earth/terms-of-use) as the locator. We made no additional network request to inspect those terms or the repository licence inventory. The project's coordinator supplied that rights decision, and the derived corpus remains local.

Seven authenticated ORACC acquisition attempts failed. OpenSSL could not build the certificate chain even with the installed trust bundles; WinHTTP and both native curl executables also failed. The successful eighth attempt used the shared fetcher through `fetch_recorded_tls.py`, with TLS peer authentication unavailable and that limitation recorded in `acquisition/oracc-oakk-archive-v8.json`. We verified the acquired file's byte count and hash, its ZIP structure, member paths and embedded licence declaration. The hash identifies these bytes; independent transport authentication remains a coordinator check.

The archives are read through the standard library. Every member name is checked for absolute paths, Windows drive prefixes and `..` before reading. Extracted catalogues and review samples are written only into new data directories under `codex-b/`; source archives are never changed and no acquired code is executed.
