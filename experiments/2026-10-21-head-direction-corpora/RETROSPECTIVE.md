# Retrospective: head-direction comparison corpora, 21 October 2026

## Approach

We started from the retry condition of STRUCTURAL-TYPOLOGY-2026-10-12, which asks for two head-initial and two head-final seal-legend corpora measured with the same code. Before writing the protocol we checked the sources on the Internet Archive and in their OCR text. That check moved the Bhita source: Marshall's list of 120 seals and sealings is in the *Annual Report 1911–12*, and the 1909–10 report prints only a summary of the season. It also showed that the Basarh legends are Gupta Sanskrit rather than Prakrit, which leaves them in the head-final group. A complete scan of Mahadevan 2003 is on the Internet Archive as a third-party upload without a licence; we used it locally and tracked only counts and locators. We found no lawful digital copy of Avigad and Sass 1997. The CDLI terms permit re-use under fair academic practice, not CC BY, and we recorded them as such.

We wrote and hashed the protocol, the record schema and the transcription brief before any source was transcribed. We then ran four Codex jobs in parallel: two transcribed the printed lists from page images, one built the cuneiform corpora, and one wrote the statistics code. Their briefs, timings and results are in [RETROSPECTIVE-CODEX.md](RETROSPECTIVE-CODEX.md). While they ran, we reviewed each output as it arrived and wrote the amendments the evidence required, each hashed before any statistic was computed.

## Problems and repairs

Most of the work after the transcriptions arrived went into reading the editors' marks the way each editor meant them. Bloch uses square brackets for restorations, asterisks for letters the engraver omitted and round brackets only for doubt. Marshall uses round brackets for his corrections of what the die carries, sets brackets apart from the words they belong to, and uses an asterisk also for conjectural restorations at the edge of a legend. Spooner uses round brackets for letters he restores on a worn impression. Bloch writes the Indological transliteration and Marshall and Spooner the Survey's older one. We checked each convention against the page images (Bhita pp. 50, 52 and 53; Bloch pp. 108 and 117; Spooner p. 145), wrote the rules in [edition_rules.py](edition_rules.py) with tests on invented strings, and recorded them in Amendments 2 and 3. The same review found an order-dependent defect in the transliteration mapping that our own brief had specified, words that run across printed lines, lumps carrying two different impressions, dates written in words, and one misread Spooner legend. Thirteen coordinator corrections and an extended tag lexicon are recorded.

The cuneiform side needed two decisions. Twenty-five CDLI records are composite editions, legends reconstructed from several impressions without a physical carrier, and one is a cast; we excluded them as non-objects. Run B's records and run C's validator disagreed on three points of form, and a small adapter maps them without changing any reading.

The machine caused two delays. The C: drive filled during the runs, which made writes fail for this session and the Codex jobs; we deleted only our own scratch files and told the coordinating session. ORACC's certificate chain could not be verified from this machine, and we confirmed the archive by a second, verified download with the same hash. A launch line for the two discrimination runs put the shell variables inside the first background job, so the second run did not start; it wrote nothing and was relaunched with explicit paths. A patching step wrote literal backspace characters into two regular expressions; we found them by a byte check of every code file before the runs, replaced them, and re-ran the tests.

## Outcome

Both runs completed and, as Amendment 4 recorded beforehand, kept no statistic: at either entry threshold only one head-initial corpus enters, because the Old Akkadian legends in Akkadian give 11 eligible legends. The entering corpora separate cleanly at sign level (Indo-Aryan edge asymmetry +0.250 against −0.219 for Ur III at a matched size of 196, with Tamil-Brahmi at +0.363 in the second panel), but the 11 Akkadian legends also end in few signs (+0.134, beyond their shuffle range), which points to the closing formula rather than head direction. An exploratory count after the runs found titles before names in Indo-Aryan legends and after names in Sumerian and Akkadian ones. [REPORT.md](REPORT.md) gives the values and the test that would decide between the two readings.

## What remains

The discrimination test needs a second head-initial corpus and a larger Tamil-Brahmi corpus before it can keep or reject any statistic; [FEASIBILITY.md](FEASIBILITY.md) gives the routes. The transcriptions have had one agent's reading with a ten-entry re-read and the coordinator's checks of fourteen entries; an independent second reading of the flagged entries would raise them to verified records. The Basarh and Bhita transcriptions are public domain by age and could be tracked after a maintainer's review.
