# Proposed buried-surface pilot, revised after review

Unrun proposal, 11 October 2026. This workload supplies no power calculation, partner agreement or field authorization.

The proposed pilot compares stable older alluvium, younger cover over older margins, young channel belts where scour is plausible, and older ground altered by leveling or building. A local geoarchaeologist defines these strata before archaeological outcomes are inspected. A proposed 24-block workload, six per stratum, is an operational example with no power claim. Blocks are sampled from a recorded accessible frame, including a matched low-prior-effort subset wherever the archive review supports that classification. Inaccessible selections and replacement rules are retained.

A block begins as a one-square-kilometre indexing cell, clipped to its eligible landform and access area. Transect orientation, core count, depth limit and dating budget must be locked by the partner's sediment pilot before the main comparison; the present literature does not justify universal spacing or reachable depth. A known-context calibration set is separate from inference blocks. Eight of the 24 sampled blocks, two per stratum, are held aside to evaluate any subsequently fitted prediction rule; correlated landform units stay together. Candidate/map status is concealed from surface observers and sample assessors where practical.

The report first counts attempted transects and those adequately resolving the target-age interval. Adequacy requires the relevant sediment unit or its missing interval to be bracketed, sufficient dating resolution, and lateral coverage suited to the proposed surface or feature. Among adequate profiles it estimates the proportion retaining the predicted terrestrial surface, with depth, continuity and scour evidence. Inadequate, inaccessible and undatable profiles are reported separately because their exclusion can bias a survival proportion. Inference is restricted to the sampled accessible strata. Archaeological encounter rates are a later outcome. A preservation premise is demoted in a sampled stratum when adequate profiles demonstrate erosion or absence of the predicted terrestrial horizon. A short core that does not reach it is unresolved. A cap age, substrate age or redeposited charcoal date alone cannot make that decision.

Only where a relevant surface survives should geophysical or small subsurface tests evaluate coherent occupation. Positive contexts require phase assessment and disturbance controls. Failure of a method to discriminate buried materials at the required depth calls for a changed method or an unresolved result. Expansion to excavation depends on the question, permits, archive capacity and conservation resources. The pilot's report includes every negative, positive, inaccessible and undatable outcome, permitting the agenda to change even if it finds no new settlement.
