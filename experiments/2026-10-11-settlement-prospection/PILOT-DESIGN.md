# A pilot for buried river-margin archaeology

Proposed design,11 October2026. This is an unrun field protocol with an illustrative workload, rather than an approved budget, power calculation or access agreement. It concentrates on P08, the Harappa-Khanewal/Old Beas-Ganji Bar comparison. The current evidence supports different preservation histories, while the relative contributions of occupation, burial and erosion remain unresolved.

## Decision and estimand

The decision is whether a mapped preservation stratum contains surviving, target-age terrestrial surfaces suitable for a subsequent archaeological survey. The first estimand is the proportion of inspected subsurface transects that reach such a surface, with depth, continuity and erosion evidence recorded. The archaeological estimand at the next stage is the encounter rate of in-situ, phase-supported occupation evidence under a declared intervention effort. Neither rate estimates total settlement density without further sampling and detectability assumptions.

Compare four strata: stable older alluvium; margins where younger sediment may cover older surfaces; young active or abandoned channel belts where scour is plausible; and older surfaces altered by leveling or building. A geoarchaeologist must establish the landform definitions locally before sampling. Historical mound symbols and current remote candidates are recorded as additional attributes. They do not determine every selected location.

## Sampling and blinding

An illustrative first workload is24blocks, six per stratum, chosen at random from a locally feasible frame after exclusion rules are recorded. Six is a workload choice with no claimed statistical power. Block dimensions, transect orientation and core spacing depend on the local sediment units and the minimal target width. A geologist should set them before inspecting candidate archaeology, record their relation to deposit thickness and report the searchable volume. If permits or ownership make a block inaccessible, retain that outcome and select a replacement under a predeclared rule; the resulting accessible-frame inference remains geographically limited.

Keep one third of eligible blocks aside for an evaluation phase. Freeze the strata, map joins and prediction rule before revealing those blocks' archaeological outcomes. Known positive locations calibrate the methods on accessible deposits but do not estimate regional recall. Natural clay, gravel and younger channel features provide negative material/process controls. A map-negative parcel remains unvisited or unresolved until tested; it is never used as an authenticated archaeological absence simply because it lacks a symbol.

Surface teams should record effort, crop cover, weather, visibility and the origin of location leads. Phase attribution should be assessed by specialists using coded samples where practical, before candidate class and expected result are opened. Sampling nearby features from one mound or one channel lobe supplies correlated observations. Analyses must use the block and landform as units rather than treating every sherd or image pixel as an independent test.

## Stage gates

1. Complete the gazetteer/map concordance and consult existing regional projects. Stop a proposed discovery claim when it is already a documented locality; retain it for the preservation question if appropriate.
2. Establish sediment and surface chronology through sections and permitted cores. Stop settlement testing in a sampled profile if the target-age surface is absent through scour, too young, or outside the temporal/environmental premise. Record deep/inaccessible surfaces as unresolved. A sterile short core does not reject occupation beneath its base.
3. Where a relevant surface survives, pilot appropriate geophysics or limited subsurface tests with known-context and natural controls. If the method cannot discriminate the material contrasts at the required depth, change method or retain unresolved status. Repeating the failed sensor does not create absence evidence.
4. Investigate coherent occupation signals, retaining short-lived dating material, sediment samples, coarse and fine finds, and disturbance records. Stop the Indus affiliation claim when dates or diagnostic context support a different phase or local culture; preserve that result within regional archaeology.
5. Expand only where the question, integrity, maintenance resources and permits justify excavation. Conservation and archives receive a budget line before exposure expands.

The pilot can falsify a preservation premise even without a new settlement. For example, a mapped young-channel stratum may lack surviving target-age soil, while covered margins retain it. This would change which surfaces deserve further work. The opposite pattern or uniformly poor preservation would also be informative. Archaeological finds must be reported separately from this geological result.

## Data and calibration for any future artifact forecast

Record planned and reached depth, sampled cross-sectional area, excavated archaeological volume, deposit class, screening/flotation mesh, collection fraction, fragment/weight counts and join rules. Fit any density/recovery model within deposit classes using a separate calibration sample; hold out blocks for evaluation. Report uncertainty and method failures before extrapolation. The present paper's5/50/500fragments per cubic metre examples are synthetic and supply no calibration.

Record occupation/layer probability separately from cultural attribution and information potential. A method may detect an archaeological surface while failing to date it; a dated deposit may contain no coherent occupation. Controls for redeposition should include abrasion, sediment fabric and associations between artifacts and features. Organic samples require appropriate pretreatment and contamination checks. Dates on residual charcoal or transported objects are evidence about those specimens, with their relationship to the occupation explicitly assessed.

## Outputs and stopping record

Deliver the sampling frame and exclusions; negative, positive, inaccessible and undatable outcomes; sediment logs and dates; effort and volume; phase decisions with source observations; any classifier evaluated on the held-aside blocks; and a preservation/expansion decision. A regional absence claim requires a larger design capable of detecting the proposed target. This pilot is intended to determine whether such a design is feasible and scientifically useful.
