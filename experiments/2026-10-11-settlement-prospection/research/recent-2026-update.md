# Recent Mohenjo-daro and Lothal source update

Scoped source check completed 11 October 2026. The search traced the reported Mohenjo-daro chronology change to a named preliminary excavation report and recovered two 2024 Lothal method precedents. The original Mohenjo-daro radiocarbon report and laboratory table were not retrieved. This record separates material read directly from results attributed to that report.

## Mohenjo-daro: what the current evidence supports

J. Mark Kenoyer's institutional lecture abstract, dated 11 September 2025, reports that new Mohenjo-daro excavations identify the city's roots in the Kot Diji Phase. The abstract supplies no sample identifiers, calibrated ranges or construction dates. It establishes that an Early Harappan occupation interpretation was being presented by the excavator before the April 2026 news coverage. [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://southasia.wisc.edu/lecture-series-archive-fall-2025-present/2025-fall-lecture-series/mark-kenoyer/).

A first-person account by field participant Sheikh Javed Ali Sindhi identifies *The Preliminary Report of Mohenjo-Daro Excavations 2025-26*, attributed to Jonathan Mark Kenoyer, Asma Ibrahim and Ali Lashari and reportedly submitted on 16 March 2026. It attributes 2708–2576 cal BCE, at a stated 50% confidence level, to charcoal MD25-262 from Core 5 at 1.8–2.0 m in the lower wall. Its quoted 3300–2800 BCE range rests on pottery comparison. It also reports that the underlying occupation had not yet been radiocarbon dated. The report itself remains uninspected. [Participant account](https://thefridaytimes.com/13-May-2026/discovering-mohenjo-daro-s-ancient-defences).

The study should retain c. 2600–1900 BCE as its working regional Mature Harappan reference and acknowledge a reported earlier local occupation at Mohenjo-daro. A regional period convention, a ceramic attribution and a charcoal date answer different chronological questions. A dated charcoal fragment constrains construction only through its documented association with the building event; reused wood or redeposited charcoal can be older than that event. Testing the reported extension requires the full calibration distribution, laboratory identifiers, sample material and pretreatment, stratigraphic relationships, and treatment of residual material. Converting a phase's earliest conventional boundary into the city's foundation date would bypass those checks.

The accessible 2026 paper *Inequality declined in the Bronze Age city of Mohenjo-daro* is by Adam S. Green, Iqtedar Alam and Cameron Petrie, published online 18 May, DOI 10.15184/aqy.2026.10359. Its methods digitise early excavation plans and analyse residence areas. It uses c. 2600–1900 BCE for the Indus Civilisation and does not supply the new wall-date dataset. It is useful evidence that existing excavation archives can support further analysis. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2026.10359).

## Lothal: established methods and a specific sampling extension

Ekta Gupta, V. N. Prabhakar and Vikrant Jain published *Sabarmati and its connection with the Harappan port Lothal and the Nal corridor: A study using multi-sensor data, cloud-computing and multi-platforms* in *Journal of Archaeological Science* 170 (October 2024), article 106046, DOI 10.1016/j.jas.2024.106046. The publisher preview describes historical maps, multispectral and multitemporal satellite data, and elevation models, and interprets a former Sabarmati course linked to Lothal. This is a direct precedent for the proposed landscape workflow. The preview does not expose an independently dated channel sequence. [Primary preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305440324001146).

Gupta and Prabhakar's *A Bronze Age Inland Water Network and Its Role in the Maritime Trade Network of the Harappan (Indus) Civilization*, *Journal of Maritime Archaeology* 19:327–350, published 15 July 2024, DOI 10.1007/s11457-024-09403-9, identifies proposed Nal Corridor, Sabarmati and Gulf of Khambhat routes from satellite observations, field observations and literature. Its accessible abstract and bibliography were inspected; full text was unavailable. [Primary preview](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11457-024-09403-9).

These publications justify adding the Lothal–Nalsarovar–Little Rann corridor as a source-led survey window. A useful extension would compare mapped channel margins with interfluve controls, recover sediment cores across suspected junctions, date channel activity and exposure surfaces, and test for phase-diagnostic occupation evidence. The combined observations must establish whether connected segments were usable at the same time. Present channel shape, historical maps and a route's geometric continuity alone leave that question open. This would test an existing reconstruction and its settlement implications.

## Verification and preservation

Searches combined Mohenjo-daro name variants with 2026, radiocarbon, Kenoyer, the report title and sample MD25-262, followed by searches of the author's institution and Sindh antiquities domains. The reported preliminary report has no verified public DOI or download in this check. The participant account's prose and image caption give different years for one June excavation event, so its event date was excluded. Crossref confirms the JAS title, authors, October issue and article number; its 14 August 2024 deposit date was not treated as a publication date. New local witnesses use the `RECENT_` prefix; retrieval details and access failures are recorded in [the source register](recent-update-sources.json).
