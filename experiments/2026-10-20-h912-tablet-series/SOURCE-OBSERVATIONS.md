# Source observations for the four-unit fish series

These are targeted observations by one visual reader. Complete scans, source crops and contact sheets remain local. [Inspection inputs and hashes](inspection-v1.json) identify the original CISI witnesses; [acquisition records](sources-v1.json) identify the author publications. The excavation continuation records its primary Vats page inspections separately.

## The four entries and their neighbours

H-302 B on CISI 1 printed p. 225, PDF p. 261, shows four short strokes, an open V-shaped sign and a fish-shaped sign. H-302 A has a longer inscription containing a fish form, an open form with an upper extension, and other signs. The photograph and caption were inspected on the complete page as well as in the local crop. H-912 A and B are the separate CISI 2 witnesses already inspected in the fish-context study, printed p. 339, PDF p. 374. The B faces share the same three-sign construction. Their A-face transcriptions place W740 and W760 in different orders. The small photographs support similar sign inventories; we leave the fine transcription difference unresolved and change neither sequence.

H-1191 and H-1192 have no linked photograph in the held image index. Their complete two-face records remain in the local register. H-1191's transcriptions are identical to H-912's, and the export assigns both the mixed reference 8650c448. This supplies an identity-review candidate, not a physical merge. H-1192 has four–fish–V on its short face and a related longer face. The original Vats drawing numbered 452 is the source lead for its reversed placement; the excavation package audits that drawing. The common prefix 8650c denotes a broad collection series and cannot identify a single physical object.

Parpola's 1994 *Deciphering the Indus script*, printed p. 194, Figure 10.21, reproduces H-302 and a drawing labelled 3452. Its caption points to Vats 1940 volume II, 452 B. The second drawing visibly puts fish between four strokes and V. The acquired PDF is the complete 392-page book, although the web search indexed its fish chapter. The inspected page is PDF p. 212. Parpola's 2012 *Indus Civilization*, proof p. 12, PDF p. 12, states that three tablets have fish after four-and-V and one has fish between four and V. This matches the four catalogue constructions recovered here; it does not establish four independent contexts.

H-246 A, CISI 1 printed p. 217, PDF p. 253, contains a comparable numeral–V construction with an anthropomorphic form in the following slot. Its corpus code is W125, while the strokes are coded as three tall strokes. The complete page and crop support the human-like shape. H-1921's transcription has the same three-sign sequence, with no held image. A post-unit sign can therefore be compared with a human-like category as well as with fish. That observation gives the recipient or account reading a concrete structural rival to the contents reading. It does not identify the human as a recipient independently.

M-494 A and M-495 A, CISI 1 printed pp. 121–122, PDF pp. 157–158, show repeated open V-like forms inside longer inscriptions. We inspected the complete pages and the crops. Parpola uses their repetition as a quantity comparison. The repetition supports a countable-sign hypothesis but supplies no measured vessel capacity. M-478's person-and-vessel scene remains the source of the pictorial vessel comparison, rather than a container associated with H-912.

M-928 A, CISI 2 printed p. 89, PDF p. 124, is a square animal seal with a long stroke, an internally marked V-like form and a modified fish-like form in the transcription. Its held crop was inspected. It differs in carrier, stroke family and fine V and fish forms from the four-tablet series. Its value-one transcription is retained in the broad sensitivity register; it cannot be silently included as a plain-fish tablet or excluded from a claim about every V–fish combination.

## Physical context and quantity

The excavation continuation checks Vats 1940's narrative pp. 58–60 and tabulation p. 348. They place H-912's 8650c collection in squares H12/21 and I12/1, across 4–7 feet below the surface. The narrative describes collection with knives and sieves over three months. The source's range replaces the apparent precision of the export's midpoint, 5.5 feet. Nearby jars, beads and weights need their own identity and deposit relations; the collection is not a sealed tablet-and-fish assemblage. The source also has conflicting stratum labels for some beads, preserved by that continuation.

Belcher 1991's fish-resource contexts on Mound E and the newly acquired Belcher 2018 chapter establish fish use at Harappa and a route to fish-assemblage observations. Neither creates a registration crosswalk from those fish deposits to H-912. The primary title of the 2018 chapter is *Fish Symbolism and Fish Remains in Ancient South Asia*, pp. 33 onward. The initial acquisition record used a differently indexed title; this note records the corrected title without replacing that record.

Rao 2018 proposes ration tokens and repeats a roughly forty-litre vessel-unit estimate from Wells. The project's existing [capacity audit](../../research/literature/metrology/README.md) has already traced that estimate to selected vessels, long strokes, geometrically reconstructed capacities and unresolved measurement-to-object labels. It finds no independently measured absolute short-stroke unit. We reuse that audit and its primary-source pointers; we do not turn four short strokes on H-912 into 160 litres. Quantity calibration and literal fish contents both remain open physical predictions.

## Reading and discriminator

The source-supported graphic gloss is four, V-unit, fish category. A fixed four-unit allocation indexed to the fish category explains the same written forms as four vessels containing fish. The additional premise in a delivery reading is a physical relation to delivered contents; the additional premise in an offering reading is independently established religious use. The next deciding record is an individually registered tablet linked to a container, measured allotment or documented use event, with the unit and relevant tolerances fixed before the outcome is inspected.
