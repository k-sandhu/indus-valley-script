# Atlas and parallel-study retrospective

We divided the work between geographic mapping, architectural models, changing-marker evidence and town histories. Each contributor owned separate paths and an attempt entry. We read the returned reports, checked their scope and integrated the final records into a map and a readable research page. Existing working-tree changes, earlier maps and scientific endpoints remain intact.

The first map imported the earlier register's 115 entities and preserved its three unlocated labels. The architectural register supplied later coordinate assertions, including two selected harmonizations. Reviewing those assertions showed why a newer record cannot simply replace every old point: Allahdino retains a material latitude disagreement, and other entries have source or locator warnings. We exposed the competing evidence and kept the original values.

Natural Earth's first acquired river file was too coarse for this task. Its thirteen rivers omitted both the Indus and Ganges. We kept that witness and acquired the 1:50 million file at the same pinned repository commit. The final map uses contemporary river geometry with its scale and temporal scope visible. Optional OpenStreetMap street tiles loaded in the browser with their attribution; no tile archive was made.

The initial interval-overlap implementation treated a shared period boundary as sufficient. That would put a city whose only range ended at 1900 BCE into the Late comparison window. We required positive overlap between two intervals and retained inclusive containment for point dates and the single-year control. Browser checks now exercise that distinction and show Mature-only Mohenjo-daro dropping out of the Late window while Harappa remains.

The first integrated map snapshot used a draft numeric Mathura endpoint of 400 BCE. Its textual label already said the closing fourth century. The town author corrected the numeric value to 300 BCE and clarified a source's access depth after our first snapshot. We preserved `mapping/run-v1` and selected a new `mapping/run-v2` built from the corrected final register. Both the corrected town data and their source hashes are explicit. A delivery hash check then caught a later citation-label edit in the town report. We preserved the first reader and generated `reader/run-v2` from the final report. The rendered research page preserves the reports' wording and resolves their relative links.

The architecture contributor's first rendering method let large ground faces hide structures. The later native WebGL renderer resolved visibility with a depth buffer. All five model endpoints remain available, and the stable entry selects run-v5. We inspected the integrated Great Bath scene and checked that city selections and Plan fragments survive the redirect and that its return link reaches the atlas. The architectural retrospective records its geometry, source-locator and interface repairs in detail.

Source review also changed a historical conclusion. Dholavira's source-assigned Stage VI spans about 1950–1800 BCE and records inscribed seals and cubical weights in a reduced settlement. A phase spanning 1900 cannot independently date every object after that boundary or exclude residual finds. The continuity report now states the source's persistence claim and the missing context-specific dates together. The living-town register similarly keeps early occupation, spatial overlap and uninterrupted habitation as distinct fields.

The first in-app browser request timed out. A loopback preview and Playwright CLI supplied the browser checks. Desktop and 390 px layouts, source panels, search, filters, offline layer changes, exports, report rendering and model links were exercised. Generated screenshots and downloaded test exports are local under D:. The repository's global downloaded-HTML exclusion also applied to the new authored viewers; an owned `.gitignore` permits only this package's original viewers, geometry and explicitly licensed map assets.

The final map passes 767 source and packaging checks and 27 browser checks. The separate architecture, continuity and living-town packages retain their own verification records. Knowledge, prose and broader repository results are collected in `qa/check-summary-v2.json` and the delivery record. Existing repository defects are counted separately from owned paths. We do not regenerate shared knowledge maps or add publication scaffolding.

The result is a usable atlas and a source register that makes new archaeological work easier to specify. Published dimensions support detailed water-work and building diagrams; surveyed plans and phase joins would replace schematic city placement. Harappa's overlapping modern settlement is the clearest local target for the continuity question. Dated occupation surfaces after about 1300 BCE, linked across accessible sections and construction archives, would decide whether its later gap reflects a missing record, relocation or interruption.
