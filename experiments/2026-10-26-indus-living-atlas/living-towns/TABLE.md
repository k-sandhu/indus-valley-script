# Evidence table: living towns and Indus-period occupation

Sixteen candidates and two abandoned-city comparisons. Dates apply to the named evidence place. A nearby older settlement is recorded separately. The map points are approximate; the table supplies no excavation boundaries.

| Place | Earliest evidence adopted | Mature / Late overlap | Continuity and spatial evidence | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peshawar, Pakistan | c. fourth–third century BCE | no / no | Gor Khatri preserves a long urban sequence inside Peshawar. The reported sequence begins over a millennium after the Late Harappan comparison interval. | [LT-S01](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/7035/) |
| Multan, Pakistan | No archaeological onset adopted | uncertain / uncertain | Multan is a living historic city. A date for a nearby Harappan mound cannot date its fort or establish uninterrupted occupation beneath the present city. | [LT-S02](https://multan.punjab.gov.pk/fort_places), [LT-S03](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40494-023-00985-6) |
| Taxila, Pakistan | Sixth century BCE for Bhir | no / no | Taxila records successive towns in one valley. Its Neolithic and Bronze Age evidence belongs to Saraikala; Bhir's historic-city date cannot be extended backwards with that separate sequence. | [LT-S04](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/139/) |
| Harappa, Pakistan | Ravi phase c. 3700–2800 BCE | yes / yes | Harappa joins an Indus-period sequence with a modern settlement over part of the mound. The dated sequence reviewed here reaches about 1300 BCE; the intervening history beneath living streets remains the deciding gap. | [LT-S05](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2024.196), [LT-S06](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Harappa1986-90_04_Kenoyer-UrbanProcess_1.pdf) |
| Varanasi, India | c. 800 BCE at Rajghat in the cited excavation chronology | no / uncertain | Rajghat anchors ancient Varanasi in excavated deposits. A proposed earlier date around 1300–1200 BCE needs source-specific review, and early neighbouring settlements require their own footprint links. | [LT-S07](https://asi.nic.in/admin/publications/ePublicationReportsDetails/download/51) |
| Ujjain, India | Period I c. 700–500 BCE | no / no | Ujjain has an excavated early historic urban sequence. Earlier Chalcolithic settlements in the Malwa region do not extend the city's own demonstrated occupation into the Indus period. | [LT-S08](https://nmma.nic.in/nmma/NAS1/nmma_doc/IAR/Indian%20Archaeology%201957-58%20A%20Review.pdf) |
| Mathura, India | Period I, sixth to closing fourth century BCE | no / no | Mathura's reviewed excavation report begins with a small settlement in its sixth–fourth-century BCE Period I. Its date comes from that city's sequence, independently of older dates assigned to the wider ceramic culture. | [LT-S09](https://zenodo.org/records/3386794) |
| Patna / Pataliputra, India | c. sixth century BCE in the reported city cuttings | no / no | Patna occupies the Pataliputra area. The annual report records a major CE 600–1600 gap in its sampled sequence, which makes an uninterrupted-city claim require evidence from other parts of the city. | [LT-S10](https://asiaurangabadcircle.com/assets/files/publications/1955-1956.pdf) |
| Rajgir / Rajagriha, India | Early Historic pre-rampart horizon; calendar onset unresolved | uncertain / uncertain | Rajgir's retrieved excavation section establishes an Early Historic sequence. Its old radiocarbon result needs recalibration and context review before it can supply a calendar onset or a continuous bridge to the present town. | [LT-S11](https://nmma.nic.in/nmma/NAS1/nmma_doc/IAR/Indian%20Archaeology%201962-63%20A%20Review.pdf) |
| Srinagar and Burzahom, India | No Bronze Age onset for the present urban core adopted | uncertain / uncertain | Burzahom demonstrates habitation during the Indus period in the Srinagar region. A dated chain from its Neolithic settlement to the present city was not obtained. | [LT-S12](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5917/), [LT-S24](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/6306.pdf) |
| Rupnagar / Ropar, India | Period I c. 2000–1400 BCE in the ASI site card | yes / yes | Rupnagar supplies a direct Harappan-to-later-period comparison in a living town. The ASI card explicitly records a break after Period I, followed by Painted Grey Ware occupation around 1000–700 BCE. | [LT-S13](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/84884.pdf) |
| Vadnagar, India | c. 800–750 BCE | no / no | Vadnagar has the clearest published long-continuity claim in this register, with seven cultural stages beneath a living town. The supported onset is early first millennium BCE; a press-reported 1400 BCE lead still needs its primary dating record. | [LT-S14](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6629/), [LT-S15](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2023.108470) |
| Lumbini, Nepal | Sixth century BCE timber shrine | no / no | Lumbini preserves an early sacred-place sequence. Its nearby c. 1300 BCE village is an older habitation lead at the edge of the Late Harappan comparison interval, with no demonstrated village-to-modern-town bridge. | [LT-S16](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003598X00049899), [LT-S17](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/earliest-buddhist-shrine-south-asia-discovered-lumbini-buddhas-birthplace-nepal) |
| Tilaurakot / Kapilavastu, Nepal | Eighth century BCE timber settlement | no / no | Tilaurakot developed from an eighth-century BCE settlement into a sixth-century BCE urban form. The evaluation records gradual abandonment beginning in the first–second century CE and keeps its Kapilavastu identification under assessment. | [LT-S18](https://whc.unesco.org/document/221034) |
| Anuradhapura, Sri Lanka | ASW2 Period K, 840–460 BCE | no / no | Anuradhapura preserves a long Iron Age-to-medieval sequence and a living sacred landscape. ASW2's early settled horizon dates to 840–460 BCE; reported c. 4000 BCE hunter-gatherer evidence elsewhere on the mound leaves the intervening occupation unresolved. | [LT-S19](https://www.discoveringbuddha.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/1996-Passage-to-India-Anuradhapura-and-the-Early-Use-of-the-Brahmi-Script-team-of-4-1996.pdf), [LT-S20](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/200/) |
| Mahasthangarh / Mahasthan, Bangladesh | Late fourth–third century BCE urban settlement | no / no | Mahasthan combines an excavated ancient urban core with later habitation and sacred use. Its eastern-rampart table and landscape history support long persistence with changing occupied areas, beginning after the Indus period. | [LT-S21](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6671/) |
| Dholavira, India | c. 3000 BCE; source occupation range c. 3000–1500 BCE | yes / yes | Dholavira's archaeological occupation extends into the second millennium BCE. The ancient city is an abandoned-city comparison for living-town claims, with modern village habitation requiring a separate history. | [LT-S22](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1645/) |
| Mohenjo-daro, Pakistan | Third millennium BCE; deeper foundation unresolved | yes / uncertain | Mohenjo-daro is an abandoned urban centre preserved as archaeological ruins. The source summary supports Mature Indus occupation; precise last-use and post-urban chronology need dedicated phase evidence. | [LT-S23](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/138/) |

The `no` overlap label means the reviewed local sequence begins later; it is not proof that all earlier deposits are absent. `uncertain` marks an unresolved date or spatial join. The two Indus intervals end at 1900 and 1300 BCE; approximate dates near a boundary require their own dating review.

The JSON register carries the civic identity, dated place, urban-development assertion, source access depth, known gaps and prospective evidence for every row.
