# Proposed settlement coverage update

This package adds eighteen source-linked place records: sixteen living-town or modern-settlement candidates and two abandoned Indus comparisons. Twenty-four bibliographic records identify the archaeological basis, source locator, access depth and rights for their dates and spatial claims. Maintainers can add this register to the settlement and context-data section of `knowledge/DATA.md` and `data/MANIFEST.json` after review. The shared files were left unchanged.

The new fields are `modern_civic_identity`, `evidence_place`, `earliest_evidence_bce`, `urban_foundation`, `earlier_regional_evidence`, `occupation_spans`, `known_hiatuses`, `continuity_status`, `indus_to_present_demonstrated`, `spatial_relation`, `coordinate_precision`, `source_ids` and `gaps`. [towns.schema.json](towns.schema.json) specifies the structural contract. [validate.py](validate.py) checks the dates, joins, required gaps and source references. [TABLE.md](TABLE.md) presents the same eighteen observations for readers.

The coverage gain is an explicit relation between an excavated place and a present settlement. Harappa retains a modern-town overlap over part of the ancient site. Rupnagar retains the published break between Harappan and later occupation. Taxila, Srinagar, Lumbini, Anuradhapura and Mahasthan each keep separately located evidence from being assigned one municipal founding date. Unknown dates remain null, with their acquisition or chronology gap. No new inscribed objects, faces, images of inscriptions, accessions or sample determinations enter the object corpus, so its coverage percentages are unchanged.

Sources include primary excavation chapters and annual reports, peer-reviewed work by excavation teams, an independent ICOMOS evaluation and official archaeology syntheses. A State Party's tentative-list account is labelled as that source type. Unknown source years remain null. The year of a report's fieldwork, its original publication and the acquired reprint are distinguished where the title pages permit it.

Raw source responses and derived page renders remain local under logical path `data/raw/2026-10-26-indus-living-atlas/living-towns/`, physically `D:/projects/indus_valley_script/data/raw/2026-10-26-indus-living-atlas/living-towns/`. D: was mounted with about 920 GB free and the checkout junction resolved to its D: target before acquisition. [acquisition-v1.json](acquisition-v1.json) and [acquisition-v2.json](acquisition-v2.json) record URLs, retrieval times, failures, bytes, SHA-256 values and both paths. Twenty-eight responses were saved across thirty-two requests; a saved HTML response can be a redirect page, so the source register records the actual access depth separately.

[witness-inspection-v2.json](witness-inspection-v2.json) records the seven bounded primary-page checks, local extractions and the visually inspected Anuradhapura pages. Its predecessor retains a one-page locator failure at Tilaurakot. Source PDFs, downloaded pages, extracted text and rendered pages are excluded from Git under the existing rules. The tracked package contains our observations, code, bibliographic pointers, manifests and reports. James and colleagues' article is CC BY 4.0; source-specific terms still apply, and no source image is reproduced in the atlas by this contribution.

The next coverage work is a phase-and-location register for Harappa after c. 1300 BCE and Rupnagar during its recorded gaps, followed by source-specific dating reviews for Rajghat, Multan and the village south of Lumbini. A new broad list of old town names would not close those gaps.
