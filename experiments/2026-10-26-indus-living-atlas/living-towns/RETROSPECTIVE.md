# Retrospective: joining old settlements to living towns

Contributor: GPT-6 in the Codex API workspace, exact runtime build unavailable. Date: 26 October 2026. This is one owned contribution to the parallel atlas package. We owned the living-towns directory and its one attempt entry, leaving the geographic atlas, 3D work, continuity study and other contributors' files unchanged.

We read the repository entry guides, data gaps, registry, writing rules and relevant settlement, town-axis and cultural-transmission retrospectives. The existing studies already preserved archaeological phases and warned about chronological and geographic joins. We wrote a protocol for a bounded sixteen-candidate comparison with two abandoned-city controls, then searched primary excavation reports, team publications and official archaeology records. The new work adds modern-place relations and explicit missing intervals.

The strongest useful source check concerned Harappa. Kenoyer's original excavation chapter directly describes the modern town over part of the ancient site. This changed its spatial classification from a possible nearby village to a documented overlapping settlement. The same observation leaves the intervening dated history open. The 2025 crop study's broad continuous-occupation wording was kept separate from its analysed chronology ending around 1300 BCE.

Primary sources also changed chronological summaries. The Rajghat catalogue contains both an older c. 800 BCE chronology and a proposed revision back by several centuries. Mathura's annual report dates the local earliest phase later than the full range sometimes assigned to Painted Grey Ware. At Anuradhapura, visual inspection of printed p. 79 distinguishes an ASW2 occupation range of 840–460 BCE from separately cited hunter-gatherer presence on the broader mound around 4000 BCE. We retained that older evidence with its actual scope. Rajgir's old radiocarbon result remains uncalibrated.

Two government PDF routes timed out. Two IGNCA routes failed legacy TLS renegotiation, and the web reader rejected several PDFs for size. We retained the failures and used public ASIA research deposits for the three annual reports. Certificate checking was unchanged. The Ropar and Srinagar records remain at search-indexed primary-text depth. DOI routes for two research sources returned redirect or login HTML; the institutional bibliography and abstract supplied the supported metadata and access depth.

The acquired title pages corrected source descriptions. The Patna review copy is a 1993 reprint of an original published in 1956. The Rajgir review's original title and copyright pages read 1965, despite a deposit date of 1963. The Mathura volume was published in 1979, after the 1975–76 season in its title. ICAC's institutional bibliography verified the three authors and their order for the 2023 landscape paper.

The first witness check selected the ICOMOS chronology one PDF page early. It found the city summary on printed p. 133 while expecting the eighth-century timber settlement on p. 134. We preserved that failed output and its code, inspected the next page, and wrote a successor with the correct page 178 locator. All seven fragment checks then passed. No archaeological date or source byte changed in that repair.

The resulting register contains eighteen places in five countries and twenty-four sources, with structured gaps for every row. The mapped points have 5 km stated precision and supply regional indexing only. The validator checks internal dates, joins and local source hashes. A final comparison with the primary phase wording corrected Mathura's rounded younger endpoint from 400 to 300 BCE, matching its already stated closing fourth-century date. Earlier validations remain records of their input hashes; the attempt entry identifies the final validation.

The knowledge check passes with no errors or warnings, leaving the two expected generated-file maintainer notes. The repository integrity snapshot reports sixty errors, all outside this contribution, including the existing distributed-path check for data/MANIFEST.json. The prose review's generic citation label was corrected. Its two remaining flags are repository path strings in the frozen protocol and are retained. The checks and their command output are preserved in checks-v1.json and checks-v2.json. No statistical benchmark evaluates this metadata review; no script reading or new historical conjecture was submitted, and no confirmation data were opened.

The next deciding evidence is a dated and mapped series through the later occupation beneath Harappa's modern streets, together with occupation evidence outside Rupnagar's excavated mound during its recorded breaks. Those sources could turn a broad continuity question into a bounded settlement-history test.
