# Architectural models with inspectable assumptions

We built seven offline 3D models with 49 feature records. Mohenjo-daro and Dholavira provide city contexts; five building scenes expose the Great Bath, Dholavira's eastern reservoir and west castle gateway, Mohenjo-daro's Pillared Hall and Lothal's large basin. The [current explorer](run-v5/index.html) uses a native WebGL depth buffer for geometry and Canvas for labels and rulers. It has no runtime network dependency or vendor library.

The contribution builds on the [town-axis atlas](../../../presentations/2026-10-24-town-axis-visual-atlas/README.md), [Q-1032 register](../../2026-10-24-q1032-town-axis-register/REPORT.md) and [shared-infrastructure study](../../2026-10-24-shared-infrastructure-plan-test/README.md). Those studies preserve source plans, bearings, dimensional observations and phase conflicts. Our new endpoint is an inspectable presentation and export of cited architectural quantities. No previously examined plan or inscription becomes a fresh confirmation population here.

## What the measurements support

The Great Bath's internal basin follows the approximately 12 m north–south by 7 m east–west dimensions and 2.4 m maximum depth in Jonathan Mark Kenoyer's [The Great Bath of Mohenjo-daro C](https://www.harappa.com/content/great-bath-mohenjo-daro-c). Opposed stairways, ledges, colonnade, gypsum and bitumen come from the same caption. Wall thickness, tread count, room sizes, roof forms and display well size are explicit model assumptions. The basin dimensions remain fixed while the superstructure slider changes assumed heights.

Kenoyer's [Pillared Hall, L Area](https://www.harappa.com/indus6/pillariedhallLarea35.html) supplies an approximately 27.5 m square envelope, twenty pillars and four rows, of which two remain preserved. The model uses a regular four-by-five array; exact spacing, pillar width and height remain illustrative. The city context also retains the 50 × 27 m foundation envelope and 27 blocks described in [Granary, SD and REM area](https://www.harappa.com/indus6/granaryandwell40.html), and the 10 m courtyard described in [College Building Courtyard, SD Area](https://www.harappa.com/indus6/collegebuilding38.html). The traditional names do not establish the buildings' functions.

For Dholavira we inspected the existing January 2015 preliminary scan of R. S. Bisht's *Excavations at Dholavira (1989-90 to 2004-2005)*, SHA-256 `f1c23210b95c17ea68650522ca2b7f7ecd7503078d9dede6fbfec6bfe4fad374`. Identity with the final published edition remains unresolved. Printed pp. 93 and 107 give the city spans as 771.10 m east–west and 616.87 m north–south in prose. The p. 107 table prints 711.10 m for the length. We preserve that conflict and use the repeated prose value. The same table supplies the castle, bailey, middle town, ceremonial ground and lower-town spans. Source north and building phases remain separate from the display axes.

Printed pp. 149–150 of that scan distinguish the eastern reservoir's ordinary depth from a deeper northern probe. We use a 73.45 m top length, the midpoint of the reported 73.40–73.50 m range, and the reported 29.30 m top width. The ordinary floor follows the reported approximately 70 m length and 27.70–27.75 m width. Its model depth is 7.35 m, within the reported 7.20–7.50 m range. A localized cut shows the reported 10.60 m maximum, with an explicitly assumed 12 × 11 m outline. Its extent is not measured by this model. The west gateway uses the 9 × 2.2 m passage described on p. 114; its walls and south side room are schematic. The city well marker uses the 4.00 × 4.25 m opening described on p. 138.

S. R. Rao's [Shipping and Maritime Trade of the Indus People](https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/shipping-and-maritime-trade-of-the-indus-people/) gives Lothal's mean wall spans of 214.87 m and 37.26 m, an extant height of 3.30 m and a 12.21 m original northern opening. The model uses a rectangular wall-line envelope because exact trapezoid corners are absent from its input. It shows the first-stage northern opening and keeps the later eastern opening out of that scene. Rao's dock interpretation and Leshnik and Junghans' irrigation-tank alternative remain separate readings; see [The Harappan “Port” at Lothal: Another View](https://doi.org/10.1525/aa.1968.70.5.02a00070), publisher abstract, *American Anthropologist* 70(5), 911–922.

## Shared modelling assumptions

All meshes use metres and a right-handed X-east, Y-north, Z-up export frame. City districts are approximate patches, and no unexcavated area is filled with invented houses. Display ground, superstructure height, roof form, water level and illustrative joints carry no survey claim. The 60–160% height range is a display sensitivity range, not an archaeological interval. Source ranges and reading uncertainty retain their separate meanings. No source plan, photograph or full text is embedded.

The two city contexts combine monuments and districts from several building episodes. Their axis alignment is a display convention; the earlier orientation study reports different systems and north references. Detailed architectural plans, ground slopes, section datums and coordinates have not been registered to one common survey frame. Those fields remain missing in the scene data. Feature source cards provide exact printed-page or caption locators alongside the dimension, placement and height fields.

## Verification and preserved failures

[verification-v5.json](verification-v5.json) passes 769 checks covering finite indexed meshes, source identities and hashes, metric rulers, basin spans and depths, city inner-corner spans, the gate passage, the hall pillar arrangement, independently parsed OBJ indices and extents, JavaScript syntax, frozen file hashes and absence of runtime asset requests. The final dataset contains 4,676 vertices and 3,269 polygon faces before optional display layers are omitted. Static OBJ exports contain 4,552 vertices and 3,182 faces.

[browser-verification-v5.json](browser-verification-v5.json) passes 32 checks. Every model produces nonempty WebGL pixels and a populated evidence card. Surface picking, accessible feature buttons, focus, orbit, keyboard controls, Plan fragments, zoom, layer toggles, height sensitivity, OBJ and JSON downloads, network-offline model switching and a 390 px layout all pass. We inspected the model screenshots at building and city scale and the full mobile layout. The checks exercise a narrow browser viewport and mouse drag; they do not constitute testing on physical touch hardware. Screenshot paths and hashes are recorded in the delivery manifest.

The first endpoint had a JavaScript inspection-API syntax error. Its successor rendered through Canvas face sorting, which let large ground and platform polygons hide smaller structures. The third endpoint introduced depth-buffered native WebGL. The fourth fitted controls to the viewport. The fifth adds exact feature locators and clarifies assumed stair placement. All five endpoints remain preserved; the stable entry opens run-v5. The [retrospective](RETROSPECTIVE.md) records these changes and the separate source-access failure.

We read `bench/README.md`. This package submits no script reading, key, meaning map, restoration, segmentation or frozen conjecture shot. The harness has no architectural-display scorer. The recorded checks verify software and source integrity; no archaeological confirmation claim is attached.

## Deciding evidence

The strongest supported reading is that published dimensions already define substantial Indus water works and enclosed architecture that can be inspected and compared at a common metric scale. A registered, phase-keyed set of excavation plans, sections and elevations would decide which display assumptions survive. In particular, exact reservoir floor extents, gateway sections and city street coordinates would either replace the present geometry or show where the schematic reconstruction needs revision.
