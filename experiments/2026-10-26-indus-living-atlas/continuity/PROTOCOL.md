# INDUS-MARKER-CONTINUITY-2026-10-26

26 October 2026. Source review with a prospective archaeological design. Author: GPT-6, Codex API workspace harness; exact model build unavailable. Verification tier: submitted.

We ask whether the loss of the material features by which we recognize urban Indus society can coexist with continued people, settlements and practical traditions after about 1900 BCE. Our unit is a dated observation in a named archaeological sequence. The endpoint is a source-linked regional account and a test design. We will not estimate a probability of historical continuity from an invented model.

## Extension and prior exposure

We have read the project's 11 October early Indian continuity, societal persistence and settlement-prospection studies, the 25 October crop audit and continuity synthesis, their registry records and retrospectives. Selective practical inheritance is already the project's working explanation. The crop package already records dated post-urban plant remains, Bahola's changing crop composition and a break at Burj. Those familiar observations are exposed material.

This study adds an explicit audit of how distinctive markers change across regions and how preservation and survey affect their detection. It separates population persistence, local practice, movement to another settlement, occupation of one plot, script use, language and institutional persistence. It will not count a second publication of an excavation as independent evidence. No previously failed reading or numerical continuity simulation is rerun.

## Source selection before new extraction

We will review a bounded set of primary archaeological publications and original excavator accounts with verifiable bibliographic metadata. Coverage targets are Punjab and Haryana, Sindh, Gujarat and the upper Gangetic connection. We will seek dated intermediate sequences, contrasts in writing and seals, standardized weights, building forms and ordinary subsistence or craft, and at least two original studies of burial, erosion, relocation or survey visibility. Publisher and author repositories have priority. Institutional summaries may locate a report but will be distinguished from excavation evidence. A source with abstract-only access supports only an abstract-level observation.

We will stop after approximately twelve substantive sources or once every target region and the preservation mechanism have an inspectable source. Inaccessible sources remain acquisition leads. Chronologies will preserve the authors' phase ranges and their uncertainty. A regional date range cannot supply the age of an undated object.

## Competing histories and expected observations

Depopulation predicts a substantial reduction in adequately dated ordinary occupation as well as urban markers. Deurbanization and regionalization predict continued or redistributed domestic activity with declining standardization and city-scale infrastructure. Burial, erosion and survey omission predict that detection varies with landform, depth, later cover and method. Later occupation over an earlier site predicts stratigraphically separated deposits; modern occupation by itself cannot establish an unbroken sequence. These histories can occur together in different places.

Every evidence row will retain the source, title, authors, year, URL, page or section, observed statement, interpretation, chronology, geography, confidence and dependence. We will code direct observation, author inference and our prospective prediction separately. No script reading, meaning map or new conjecture card is submitted. After reading `bench/README.md`, we judge that its sign-level tasks do not score this source review. A future conjecture test must register its own bench shots workflow and exposure record before confirmation material opens.

## Prospective design and rejection rules

The design will compare ordinary occupation with diagnostic marker occurrence in dated deposits, retaining excavation effort, screened sediment or excavated volume, depth reached, recovery method and preservation. Entire sites and excavation lineages will form independent groups. Known sequences reviewed here are discovery material. A later study must acquire and reserve independent sites, freeze marker definitions and chronology rules before opening the confirmation labels, and evaluate detection using known-positive deposits.

The marker-loss explanation fails for a specified region and horizon if adequate calibrated observation finds a long occupation break across ordinary domestic evidence as well as diagnostic markers, with displacement searched in the same defensible catchment. A diagnostic institution-specific continuity claim fails if the complete operational chain disappears despite suitable recovery, or if independently dated later examples use a different operational chain. These are local rejection conditions, not a claim that one negative trench can settle the subcontinent's history.
