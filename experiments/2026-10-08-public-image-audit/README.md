# Public images, relational visual reasoning and recent literature

Reviewed 8 October 2026. The user asked about Bonta's side-by-side reasoning over inscription groups, images outside academic publications and the recent papers used in this project. This package audits prior work and adds a bounded public-source search. It does not run a new model benchmark or claim a complete web image census.

## What visual reasoning has been done

Bonta compares whole inscription groups, locates fixed and substitutable components, proposes boundaries and functions, then uses those constraints to develop candidate readings. The [Bonta first-pass review](../../research/literature/2026-10-08-bonta-first-pass/README.md) records that chain. Our earlier answer described visual transcription and chart comparison; those tasks cover only part of the user's intended method.

The project has visually inspected selected paired surfaces, copper-tablet families, fish modifiers and archaeological object images. The [corpus report](../../research/corpus/CORPUS_REPORT.md) records a 25-object plate audit and disagreements in segmentation, allographs and damage. The [copper variant adjudication](../../research/literature/copper_variant_adjudication.md) preserves relational comparisons of graphic variants. The [candidate-pair audit](../../research/literature/candidate_pair_novelty_audit.md) checks which substitutions already appeared in published comparisons. These are relevant precedents within the project.

The larger [1979 digitization](../2026-10-07-helsinki1979-digitization/REPORT.md) and [two-reader verification](../2026-10-08-helsinki1979-row-verification/REPORT.md) tasks used visual models to identify drawing units and candidate codes. Statistical field and substitution studies used transcribed sequences. No completed benchmark was located that gives models sets of source photographs, asks them to infer boundaries and functions, and measures those predictions on fresh inscription groups. That broader task remains a gap.

A future study should preserve seal versus impression orientation and the identity of every object and face. It could ask a model to mark repeated groups and changes with image coordinates, predict which substitutions or boundaries will recur, and test those predictions on withheld object families. Bonta's examples would form a labelled comparison condition; a second condition would omit his proposed values. Shuffled groups and matched short nonphonetic systems would supply controls. This is a proposed design, not a completed result.

There was no documented systematic collection and object-matching pass over visitor photographs, Flickr/Wikimedia holdings, social-media photographs or private collection listings. The current search begins that inventory. It does not establish how much material lies outside all existing catalogues.

The corpus gaps are measurable even within older publications: across nineteen selected CISI 1 sections, 166 of 493 catalogue identifiers were absent from the local export's identifier field. Seventy-five were in pottery/graffiti sections and 91 in other sections. Eligibility and aliases still require review. [Exact section counts](../2026-10-06-evidence-census/synthesis/catalogue-section-split.csv). The local derivative, the 1977 concordance, the 2010 CISI material and the 2023 ICIT summary have different coverage; a recent digital export can still depend on older selection decisions.

| [Harappa faience tablet H2001-5082/2920-02](https://www.harappa.com/slide/faience-tablet) | The image shows obverse and reverse. Focus varies across the display and no accession is legible at the inspected view. Commons records a 2015 photograph and CC0 dedication. | A visitor photograph can improve or supplement a view. Object-level joins were not made here; no new-object claim follows from finding the photograph. |
| [Rakhigarhi elephant sealing](https://www.livehistoryindia.com/story/monuments/rakhigarhi-excavations) | Disha Ahluwalia's 21 May 2022 public article identifies her as an excavation supervisor and credits the image to ASI. The photograph shows a damaged rounded clay-like support, an upper band of worn impressions and part of an elephant below. The caption identifies RGR 1 and Harappan script. | This is a useful public first-person field source. An accession, locus and official object record are needed to determine corpus membership and discovery date. It remains an unresolved acquisition lead. |

The [literal identifier check](identifier-check.json) is exploratory. It counts matching face-record lines, not unique objects, and is narrower than a normalized concordance. Mayig is a Mohenjo-daro annotation subset, so absence of the Harappa prefixes there is expected. No corpus was edited.

The search also located [Outlook's February 2025 Rakhigarhi photo gallery](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/rakhigarhis-treasures-in-photos), explicitly presented as archive photographs. Its metadata was checked, without inspecting all fourteen photographs. A recent [Reddit epigraphy post](https://www.reddit.com/r/IndianHistory/comments/1wqhqp9/select_masterpieces_of_epigraphy_national_museum/) points to a commercial book record and shows a later inscribed hand in the page preview. Its broad Harappan wording does not identify a newly excavated Indus inscription. These are separate date and object questions.

For further collection, each entry should distinguish the object, face, photograph and repost. Record the earliest retrievable image, photographer, caption, excavation/accession identifier, capture and posting dates, seal or impression orientation, crop/rotation history and reuse terms. Compare against our corpus and available later catalogues before classifying an object as absent. A public source's venue should not decide whether its pixels are useful; the traceable observation and object identification should.

The relevant 2024 full-text reviews include M. V. Other recent comparative and methods sources include Contreras's 2026 *Structural Pattern Mining in Inka Khipus: Unsupervised Clustering, Provenance Classification, and a Computational Validation of the Santa Valley Match*, reviewed at abstract level in the contribution survey, and Lago, Cardarelli and Ialongo's 2024 *CQARCHAEO: A PYTHON PACKAGE FOR COSINE QUANTOGRAM ANALYSIS AND MONTE CARLO SIMULATIONS*, whose article was inspected for the metrology study. Their links and access records are in `papers.json`.

Recent sources that are not ordinary papers were also used: the June 2025 RMRL bulletin, the eight-page 2025 Rajan–Sivanantham morphological-study booklet, selected pages of their 2026 *Inscribed Potsherds of Tamil Nadu: Graffiti and Tamiḻi* volumes, Vahia's 2025 slides, Frenez's October 2026 lecture and Bonta's 2025 lectures. The resemblance study records the booklet separately from the longer edition. The volumes concern later Tamil Nadu material and comparisons, not a newly verified Harappan inscription corpus.
