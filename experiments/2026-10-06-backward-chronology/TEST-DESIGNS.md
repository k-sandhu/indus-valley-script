# Tests of transmission and convergence

Status: Proposed. None of these tests was run in this package. A transmission hypothesis predicts the persistence of selected graphic conventions along a dated route. Common simple configurations can arise repeatedly, so shape, context and chronology must be measured together. The four designs below share one acquisition requirement: each observation needs an image, object registration, site, deposit, marking stage, and a separate date assessment with the sample association and calibration. Multiple impressions of the same seal and drawings copied between publications count as one witness.

Before choosing features, freeze the route, temporal windows, eligible collections and missing-data rules. Two annotators should work without knowing the proposed cross-corpus matches, retain disagreements, and validate a held-out sample. Later annotations and newly recovered specimens receive separate dated evaluation directories. Dating exclusions and poor image resolution remain visible in the test population.

## 1. Rare compound signs and positional rules across intermediate deposits

Start with one late Indus compound or positional pattern selected in a discovery subset. A compound must preserve a specified arrangement of constituent features, such as enclosure, an asymmetric branch and a particular junction order. Define rarity before the test: occurrence below 1% in an independently assembled comparison inventory and at least four strokes with two distinguishable junction relationships. These thresholds are proposed selection rules; they have not been estimated from the present table. Freeze permitted reflection, rotation and damage tolerances. Treat a reading direction inferred after seeing a match as a separate exploratory result.

The required data are registered late Indus specimens and marked objects from at least three intervening dated deposits at two sites along a stated route, followed by a later endpoint. The intervening dates need calibrated ranges or independent historical bounds; occupation dates without specimen association are ineligible. Search all eligible objects for the fixed feature and record nonmatches. Compare the route with pottery/mark collections matched on incision stage, object class, image resolution and date uncertainty, including collections outside the proposed route. Use at least 9999 inventory permutations and a maximum-statistic correction across every candidate feature searched.

A result supports this particular transmission model if the frozen feature occurs in the independently dated intermediates, the temporal order survives date uncertainty, and enrichment remains significant at family-wise 0.05 in held-out collections. Reject the declared route if reliable dates reverse the proposed sequence, the feature is equally frequent in matched controls, or a powered acquisition of the prespecified intermediates fails to find it. With sparse collections, report an inconclusive result and the detectable occurrence rate; do not convert missing access into absence.

The repository has Indus transcriptions, Harappa literature, the Tamil catalogue images and the reviewed [stroke membership outputs](../2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/south-india/stroke-membership-v2/results.json). It lacks a joined, dated intermediate specimen series. [Daimabad](evidence-table.json), rows `daimabad-II`/`daimabad-III`, and Rangpur's compound group are acquisition targets. Sulur's four-mark dish, discussed by [Mahadevan 2001](https://journal.fi/store/article/download/43982/11036), pp. 382–384, can nominate an exploratory pattern, but its first-century BCE endpoint and selected reading directions cannot supply the required intervening deposits.

## 2. Feature comparison with complexity-matched null inventories

Encode each mark as a graph of strokes, junction types, endpoints, loops, enclosures, asymmetries and component order. Keep a geometry channel for relative lengths and angles and a topology channel for connections. Record uncertainty from broken surfaces and drawing reconstruction. Use the same annotation procedure on Indus, Chalcolithic, megalithic, early historic and control inventories. Match null inventories by repertoire size, stroke count, junction count, symmetry, writing/marking medium and incidence of simple one- or two-stroke marks.

Changizi, Zhang, Ye and Shimojo's [2006 paper](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/502806), sections “A Topological Notion of Shape: Configuration Types” and “Human Visual Signs Possess a Characteristic Signature Configuration Distribution,” reports shared configuration distributions across writing and other visual signs. Its catalogue contains 36 types formed from up to three segments; the main comparisons use 32 three-segment types. This motivates a baseline in which common junctions are expected. Extend the encoding for larger Indus/graffiti compounds instead of assuming that three-segment configurations capture every diagnostic feature.

Freeze a graph-edit distance and permissible transformations on training data. On held-out sites, compare the observed nearest-neighbor similarity, rare-feature enrichment and full distance distribution with 9999 complexity-matched resamples. The unit is a repertoire or independent object, according to the frozen estimand; repeated occurrences cannot inflate the number of independent shape types. Report results separately for topology, geometry and compound structure, with a multiple-test correction.

Support for transmission requires excess similarity beyond matched controls that also survives removal of simple symmetric signs and uncertain identifications. Reject the stated shape-transmission prediction if matched independent inventories give equivalent similarity with adequate power. A positive shape result alone constrains graphic sharing; the dated-route test is needed to identify a transmission history.

Available inputs include both 2026 PDF volumes and the earlier digital decomposition. The [October 2 reviewed report](../2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/south-india/REPORT-reviewed-v1.md) records printed/digital membership differences, including 4.19C. Those discrepancies require an image-based annotation audit. Comparable unrelated graffiti inventories and consistent graph annotations are missing. No new shape test should reuse the old stroke counts as independently dated observations.

## 3. Seriation of dated repertoires

Assemble at least 12 dated assemblages with at least 30 independently registered marked objects each, distributed among at least three sites. These are proposed minimum acquisition targets; conduct power analysis before freezing evaluation. Each assemblage needs counts of observed objects, mark types, ware, marking stage and context. Use calibrated age distributions when available, retaining laboratories and stratigraphic order. Do not assign every site-wide sherd to the oldest charcoal sample.

Fit a transmission model in which a repertoire changes through losses, innovations and inherited compound features along prespecified neighboring sites. Compare it with independent invention models conditioned on geometric complexity, pottery production and local mark frequencies. Hold out complete sites and temporal blocks, then measure predictive log score for their feature frequencies and repertoire distances. Permute date labels within the allowed stratigraphic constraints and rerun the seriation; compare the observed chronological fit with that distribution. Publish results under alternative dating assessments rather than averaging conflicting methods.

The model receives support if it predicts held-out repertoires better than the declared convergence baselines and recovers the independently known temporal order under date uncertainty. Reject its route or inheritance-rate prediction if the advantage disappears in held-out sites or complexity controls explain the ordered similarity. Broad undated repertoires cannot produce a decisive test.

The repository holds pooled Tamil families and site-level aggregate occurrence data, not the required mark-by-context-by-date frequency matrix. Harappa subphases and the Rangpur/Prakash published sequences are useful starting points. Prakash's ten illustrated types, [Thapar 1967](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/45764.pdf), pp. 66–68, have insufficient counts for the proposed minimum. Recovering their original catalogue/field registrations and calibrating the legacy chronology precedes seriation.

## 4. Positional and combinatorial persistence

Use only objects with at least two reliably segmented marks. Preserve image orientation, physical sign order, line breaks, incision stage and whether signs share one surface. Define competing reading directions before evaluation and correct for their search. A pair on opposite tablet faces and adjacent pottery marks are separate arrangements. Lal's reported Sanur examples, where three signs change order, can test whether the corpus actually preserves a fixed sequence.

Acquire at least 200 independent multi-mark objects per compared time slice, or use a prespecified power calculation to set a lower feasible target. Estimate initial/terminal distributions, adjacent-pair frequencies and compound placement on a discovery set. Freeze candidate rules and score held-out sites. Compare with within-corpus shuffles preserving text length, sign marginals, object class and site, and with independently matched inventories. Run at least 9999 permutations, correcting across all sign mappings, directions and rules considered. Include positive controls drawn from securely known related scripts and negative controls with similar repertoire complexity.

A result supports a transmitted rule if a frozen positional or pair association recurs above its conditioned null in multiple dated intermediate slices, with compatible feature identifications. Reject the particular shared-rule prediction if the association is absent at adequate power, requires incompatible directions between selected examples, or is explained by object/production conventions. Single-sign pottery cannot test adjacency; a failed data threshold is an acquisition result.

The repository holds Indus sequences and the 2026 illustrated Tamil catalogue, with prior work showing that letters and graffiti sometimes occur on one object. The [October 2 source-recovery observations](../2026-10-02-source-recovery/south-india/observations.json) preserve specific examples and missing joins. A comprehensive, dated, direction-annotated southern multi-mark corpus is still missing. The 2026 positional claims on p. 265 are hypotheses to preregister; they are not already independent evaluation results.
