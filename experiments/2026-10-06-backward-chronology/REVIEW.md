## Verdict

Both parts reproduce exactly (32 of 33 rerun file comparisons are byte-identical; the exception is the explained search log), and every headline count in the READMEs and REPORTs matches the saved outputs. The rows I opened are accurate on dates, laboratory numbers and most counts. The main result does not stand as written. The 600-year gap of 1300–700 BCE is the longest empty run of the strictest of three series, and between 1900 and 650 BCE that series holds a single row, two seals from Pirak, so the run is the part of the window that the row's interval leaves uncovered. The row's 1800–1300 BCE interval comes from one sentence of a secondary source and equals the span of Pirak Period I in a published review of the excavation report, whereas the seals are assigned to the later Periods II and IIIA. On intervals that start between 1500 and 1200 BCE, where the sources put the end of Period I, and end between 1100 and 760 BCE, the longest empty run is 400 to 700 years and starts at 1900 BCE, and with the row removed it is 1,200 years. The late edge is set by one row, Perumbalai, and the two less strict series have no empty run longer than a century. The table also omits contexts that its own sources describe: a Jorwe phase at Daimabad (conventional 1400–1000 BC, three radiocarbon determinations near 1100 BC, marked pottery) in the report it already uses for two other phases, and the Iron Age habitation at Thulukkarpatti, the largest graffiti assemblage in the Tamil Nadu volume (4,797 documented sherds). The supported statement is that the table holds no securely dated marked assemblage for a stretch of several centuries between 1900 and 650 BCE, whose position depends on the Pirak interval; the table does not show that marks were absent. The other required fixes concern the claim and source records, count and locator errors in the Tamil Nadu rows, an inconsistent rule for site-wide totals and lost spaces in the amendment's JSON, including its registry entry.

## The 1300–700 BCE gap

The packages report 1300–700 BCE (six bins, 600 years) as the longest internal run of centuries with no assemblage of grade A or B and high or moderate calendar strength. The arithmetic is right. The position and length of that run depend on the rating rule, on one row, and on contexts the table leaves out.

**The rating rule.** The same table gives three answers (the first-pass and amended runs agree on these bins).

| Series | Empty internal runs |
|---|---|
| All grades A–D, any strength | 800–700 BCE (100 years) |
| A+B, any strength | 800–700 and 400–300 BCE (100 years each) |
| A+B, high or moderate strength (the headline) | 1900–1800, 1300–700 (600 years) and 400–300 BCE |

Rangpur (222 marked sherds, B/low) covers 2000–800 BCE in the second series. Its dates come from Rao's 1963 estimate, which reasons from deposit thickness ("about four centuries" for 12 ft of deposit) and rests on no laboratory determination, so the low rating is well founded. Rojdi (one bowl, C/low) is dated by comparison with Rangpur.

**One row carries the headline series.** Between 1900 and 650 BCE the headline series holds a single row, Pirak (two seals), and Perumbalai (649–499 BCE) follows it. The longest empty run is therefore the part of that window that the Pirak interval leaves uncovered. `review/gap-sensitivity/gap-sensitivity.json` recomputes the series with one change at a time, using the package's own functions.

| Change | Longest internal empty run (headline series) |
|---|---|
| none | 1300–700 BCE (600 years) |
| remove Pirak, or rate it C/low | 1900–700 (1,200 years) |
| Pirak seals at 1500–760 BCE | 1900–1500 (400 years) |
| Pirak seals at 1400–760 BCE | 1900–1400 (500 years) |
| Pirak seals at 1300–760 BCE | 1900–1300 (600 years) |
| Pirak seals at 1200–760 BCE | 1900–1200 (700 years) |
| Pirak seals at 1500–1100 BCE | 1900–1500 and 1100–700 (400 years each) |
| remove Perumbalai | 1300–300 (1,000 years) |
| rate Rangpur moderate | 800–700 (100 years) |
| rate Rojdi moderate | 1100–700 (400 years) |
| add Daimabad Phase V (1400–1000 BCE) as B/low, like Phases II and III | 1300–700 (600 years), unchanged |
| add Daimabad Phase V as B/moderate | 1000–700 (300 years) |

For each of 20 intervals with a start of 1500, 1400, 1300 or 1200 BCE and an end of 1100, 1000, 900, 800 or 760 BCE (the grid is in the JSON), the longest empty run is 400 to 700 years and starts at 1900 BCE, where the mature Harappan phase ends. Its length is the distance from 1900 BCE to the start of the seals' interval, which the row's own start at 1800 BCE reduces to 100 years. A long stretch without a securely dated marked assemblage survives these Pirak intervals. Its position at 1300–700 BCE does not.

**The Pirak interval.** The row `pirak` (two illustrated seals) takes 1800–1300 BCE, B/moderate, from one sentence of Kenoyer 2009 (p. 10) that dates the Pirak examples "around 1800–1300 BC". The package did not open the excavation report ("Full original article not recovered"). Kenoyer's figure captions assign the bronze compartmented seal to Period IIIA and the terracotta button seal to Period II. A review of the report (Dumarçay 1980, *Bulletin de l'École française d'Extrême-Orient* 67(1):341–343, on Persée) says that the report divides the levels into three periods and that the first, in two sub-periods, runs from the 18th to the 13th century BC. The row's interval is therefore the span of Period I in the report's chronology, and both seals are later in it. Petrie and Magee (2012, *Iranian Journal of Archaeological Studies* 2(1), PDF pp. 2, 4, 11), summarizing Magee and Petrie (2010), put the start of Period III "probably between c. 1460 and 1150 BC" and its end, on recalibrated radiocarbon dates, between about 1300/1200 and 760 BC. A checking pass also found uncalibrated radiocarbon means of about 1460±140, 1200±150 and 1020±140 BC for Periods I–III in a snippet of the 1979 report. The review puts the end of Period I in the 13th century BC, and a snippet of a survey chapter (author unconfirmed) puts it at about 1500 BC, so the opened sources place the start of Period II between about 1500 and 1200 BCE and the two seals between about 1500 and 760 BCE, without narrowing the range. Neither the package nor the reviewer opened Jarrige and Santoni (1979), Magee and Petrie (2010) or the corpus entries (*Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions* 2, p. 381, Pk-15 to Pk-17) that Kenoyer and Meadow (2010, p. 12) cite for the Pirak seals, so the intervals in the table illustrate sensitivity and are not dates. On what the package opened, grade C and low strength fit the row.

**Rows excluded from coverage.** Thirty of the 54 rows are outside the coverage count: 16 have no usable calendar interval and 14 are pooled parents, duplicates or comparators ([rows-and-the-1300-700-interval.csv](review/rows-and-the-1300-700-interval.csv) lists all 54). Excluding them does not create the gap. Only three rows store an assessment that overlaps 1300–700 BCE: Rangpur and Rojdi, which are included and rated low, and Ibbankatuwa (grade D, 750–400 BCE), which is excluded because "a dated cemetery alone does not establish dated graffiti". Five more of the 16 rows without an interval have source dates that reach the interval, and each record gives its reason:

- Prakash I: assessment 1700–1300 BCE, C/low, "transferred from other sites"; Thapar dates Jorwe Ware, which carries three of its ten marks, to about 1600–1000 BC.
- Navdatoli: the radiocarbon dates belong to phases, not to Lal's sherds; Rao's summaries extend to 1200 and 1000 BCE.
- Sivagalai habitation: Beta-600727, calibrated midpoint 685 BCE, with "no uncertainty interval invented".
- Bet Dwarka: settlement about 1400 BCE, with no dated context for the marked objects.
- Painted Grey Ware: levels dated about 1025–335 BC, with no graffiti noted by Lal.

The other ten add no marked context in the interval: Lothal late ends in 1400, NBPW begins about 500, OCP, Copper Hoards and Sanur are undated, Porunthal lies in the fifth century, Ahar–Banas has no marked object in the sources, the Kilnamandi habitation is dated by comparison with graves of about 1769–1615 BCE, and the Rehman Dheri pendant belongs to Period IA or IB (3300–2850 BCE). Inamgaon is included but stores only a "circa 1500" bin (1500–1400 BCE), although its source gives Early Jorwe as 1600–700 or 1500–1050 BC. None of the rows named in this paragraph would rate above C/low under the package's rules, since their dates are transferred from other sites or not joined to marked objects, so intervals for them would add coverage to the all-grade series only.

**Contexts the table omits.** Volume I of the Tamil Nadu catalogue, which supplies the package's southern rows, and the Daimabad report describe further contexts in or near the interval. Thulukkarpatti, the site with the most documented graffiti sherds in Volume I (4,797 of 15,184), has no row of its own.

Under the package's rules the first six would rate B/low or C/low, since their dates rest on analogy, inconsistent, uncalibrated or rejected determinations, or a sample not joined to the marks. They matter for the all-grade series, where a Thulukkarpatti or Sivagalai Period II row alone fills 800–700 BCE (`review/gap-sensitivity/gap-sensitivity.json`, `whatif_added_rows`), and for what the table says about marked material in the interval.

**What the sources say about absence.** Kenoyer (2006, Table 1) summarizes 1900–1300 BC in the Indus Valley as "disappearance or low incidence of writing". Deraniyagala states that 1000–500 BC in Sri Lanka is protohistoric because there is no evidence of writing. Volume I (p. 295) reports that urns opened at Adichanallur, Sivagalai and Keeladi-Kondagai, and early Iron Age graves at Mayiladumparai, had no graffiti on grave goods, and suggests that the habit of engraving graffiti on grave goods "might have come into practice later", citing Kilnamandi (1692 BCE); on p. 201 the authors add that the Adichanallur and Sivagalai excavations concentrated on graves, which prevented the recording of habitation graffiti. Lal (1960, p. 22) recorded that no graffiti had so far been noted on Painted Grey Ware. Salomon (1998, p. 10) calls the graphic record of India after the Indus decline "virtually a total blank" until Ashoka, while naming Lal's graffiti as proposed missing links. The Dumarçay review of the Pirak report says that the excavations fill in part the archaeological silence at the end of the second millennium BC. These statements support reading the interval as sparse in marked material in the Indus Valley, in Sri Lanka and in southern Indian graves. They do not cover the habitation phases and the Jorwe phase in the table above.

**Reading.** Between the end of the mature Harappan phase (1900 BCE) and the Perumbalai graves (649 BCE) the table holds two Pirak seals and no other assemblage of grade A or B with high or moderate strength. The seals' interval decides where the empty stretch lies and how long it is: 600 years at 1300–700 BCE on the package's interval, 400 to 700 years starting at 1900 BCE on intervals that begin between 1500 and 1200 BCE, and 1,200 years if the row is rated on the evidence the package opened. The omitted contexts would enter as B/low or C/low rows, and a Daimabad Phase V rated moderate would shorten the run at 1300–700 BCE to 300 years. The statement the sources support is that no securely dated marked assemblage was recovered for a stretch of several centuries between 1900 and 650 BCE. The table does not show that marks were absent.

## Attribution of the continuity claims

All 17 claims were read against the cited sources ([attribution-checks.md](review/attribution-checks.md) has the table). Eight contain a discrepancy or an unsupported attribution and nine are confirmed. Two matter most.

- `bet-dwarka-relation` names "Vibha Tripathi" as the scholar who challenged the seal's integrity. The paper cites "Tripathi, A. 1996. Dvaraka in Literature and Archaeology" (*Man and Environment* XXI(2):49–58), which the journal's page attributes to Alok Tripathi.
- `brahmi-distribution` says the Anuradhapura authors suggest transmission "associated with merchants". The Cambridge abstract refers only to "a tentative mechanism for Brahmi's development and spread", and Deraniyagala's overview names migrations and "Semitic trading scripts" without merchants as carriers. Two third-party summaries say the full 1996 article links early Brahmi to traders; neither the package nor the reviewer opened it. The amendment REPORT relies on this attribution ("already attributed in the parent `brahmi-distribution` claim").

The other six are errors of wording, omission or page range. `tn-morphology` states a weaker position than Rajan and Sivanantham: they say graffiti precede Tamiḻi and might have evolved into it (Vol. I p. 11), call the matches "not accidental" (brochure p. 5), and note that Iron Age graffiti run left to right while Indus pot texts run right to left (p. 265). `lal-bridge` omits that Lal's Harappan comparanda include seals and sealings, and omits the bridge sites Maheswar, Brahmagiri–Maski and Sonpur/Rajghat; Prakash, which the claim lists, is not among the bridge sites Lal names on pp. 21–23 (he illustrates Prakash examples elsewhere). `harappa-development` calls the Kot Diji sequence "pre-urban" where Kenoyer describes it as the site's first urban phase, and the REPORT's "earlier 2006 Ravi bracket starts at 3300 BCE" mistakes the date of the earliest graffiti (Table 1) for a phase bracket (Table 2 gives >3700–2800). `mahadevan-pairs` omits p. 381, where the four identifications are. `brahmi-origins` says "aspects of Sircar" where Salomon (p. 20) names Sircar as the most significant later supporter of the Indus derivation. `kharosthi-aramaic` cites pp. 46, 54–55 where the derivation argument is at pp. 51–54.

Lal 1960 and 1962 are one work. The cover of *Ancient India* 16 reads 1960 and the imprint 1962 (a postscript on p. 3 explains that no. 16 was delayed two years), and Lal's article was received on 20 October 1960. The key `lal1960` is acceptable, but the note in `sources.json` ("use the original issue date") should record both years, and the package should use one convention for nominal and printed years; it uses the printed year for Thapar (1967) and the nominal year for Lal. ## Early marks, contact seals and the fifth-century statement (amendment)

[amendment-checks.md](review/amendment-checks.md) gives the detail. The primary-source rows hold up: Quivron 1980 (about 850 marks, 147 variants, 50 categories), Coningham 2002 (1,265 marks; room CXVIII with 100 vessels, 33 incised, 16 signs), Durrani and colleagues 1995 (314 registered and 190 stratified sherds; 12 radiocarbon dates) and the Harappa gallery's dating of the hearth sealing to "circa 2800 BCE", which names no laboratory determination, so the package is right not to grade it A. The correction of "Rakandere" to Rehman Dheri is better supported than the package says. Frenez 2018 (PDF p. 12) describes an ivory button seal from Rehman Dheri whose signs were traced on pots before firing, which is the lecture's claim in his own earlier words.

Four corrections to the early-marks record are needed. The 27 difference at Rehman Dheri (190 stratified signs against phase totals that sum to 163) equals 41 − 14, so the printed "14" for Period II is probably a transposition, and the five-phase matrix that TEST-DESIGNS calls blocked may be buildable from the catalogue. The "Period II" alternative for object 54 appears to misread a trench section, since BIV/25 Layer 6 is Level 19, Period IA. The REPORT attributes "142 surviving jars" to Miller, although the figure is Quivron's (Miller gives about 140), and says that Quivron p. 279 compares "selected pottery shapes with Indus signs", where the page compares incised marks with later writing characters. The 193 added specimens include a script-free elephant seal and a geometric bone seal.

The five contact records are accurate on identity, locators, dates and access and need seven small corrections: the venue of `frenez2023-caption`, the merchant attribution, the era of "fifth century" (the coordinator note gives none), the date locator of `ur-bm120573` (p. 5), a dropped clause of the lecture about seal names, inconsistent `images_reviewed` and grade fields in `contact-items.json`, and a REPORT sentence that says the dated record "constrains when writing occurs" although four of its five cited rows are C/low. The record omits items that the repository's own audit carries, such as Marchesi's reading of Gadd no. 1. In the fifth-century table two entries read as more secure than the sources allow. Anuradhapura's two assessments fall "on different sides" of a fifth-century event only if the abstract's "beginning of the fourth century" means 400–300 BCE, while Allchin (1995) gives radiocarbon 450–350 BC. The package says the Porunthal calibration was not recovered, although Volume II gives the authors' calibrated values (about 580–410 BCE). The table's conclusion, that chronology constrains when writing is attested without identifying agents, stands.

## Test designs

The first pass proposes four designs and the amendment seven: its designs 1–4 adapt the parent designs to the early material and designs 5–7 (firing technology and mark frequency, the hearth-sealing date, contact legends) are new. Each states a data requirement, a comparison, a support rule and a rejection rule, and labels its thresholds as proposals.

**Falsifiability.** The designs are falsifiable in structure. Design 1 fixes a feature, a route, a family-wise error rate (0.05 over at least 9,999 permutations) and a condition on date order. Designs 5 and 6 give numerical decision rules: a decrease of at least 10 percentage points with a 95% interval excluding zero, and at least 95% of calendar probability before or after 2800 BCE, otherwise inconclusive. In designs 1–4 and 7 the rejection rules depend on "adequate power", a "powered acquisition" or a "prespecified detectable effect", and neither document states a detectable effect or a power calculation, so those clauses cannot be applied yet (design 1's date-reversal and equal-frequency clauses can). Design 3 compares a transmission model with an independence model and specifies neither.

**Data held.** The repository holds local-only copies of the Tamil Nadu volumes, Quivron 1980, Durrani 1988 and 1995, the Harappa reports, and the October 2 stroke-membership outputs; the referenced `stroke-membership-v2/results.json`, `REPORT-reviewed-v1.md` (which contains the 4.19C discrepancy design 2 cites) and `observations.json` exist. It holds no dated intermediate specimen series, no mark-by-object-by-deposit matrix and no complexity-matched control inventories, and the documents say so. Measured against the package's own table, design 3's minimum (12 dated assemblages of at least 30 independently registered objects at three or more sites) is far from met: seven non-pooled rows have an exact count of 30 or more and a selected interval (Kot Diji 95, Rangpur 222, Rehman Dheri 190, Kilnamandi burials 43, Perumbalai 1,028, Kodumanal 2,632, Keeladi 3,782), only two of them rated B with moderate strength, and none has specimen-level dates.

**Resemblance and transmission.** No design treats resemblance as evidence of transmission. Design 2 uses shape similarity as a convergence baseline (Changizi and colleagues 2006: 36 configuration types, 32 with three segments, both section titles correct) and says that a positive result constrains graphic sharing only. Every transmission claim requires a dated route (design 1) or dated seriation (design 3). The sentence "Support for transmission requires excess similarity beyond matched controls" (design 2) states a necessary condition and can be misread as sufficient; "support graphic sharing beyond chance" would be clearer. Design 7 keeps stylistic sharing and text alignment apart and assigns no phonetic values.

**Specific points.** The first-pass design 4 cites "Lal's reported Sanur examples, where three signs change order"; Lal (1960, p. 23) writes that the three symbols "interchange their positions on different pots, producing all possible combinations", so the example is accurate. Design 1's rarity threshold (below 1% in an independent inventory, at least four strokes and two junction relationships) is labelled a proposal and was not estimated from the table.

## Method and scope

**Protocol before outcomes.** The first-pass protocol and acquisition script were committed at 00:02:54 on 7 October (commit 3183b4d), before any table or run. The calendar-strength paragraph was added after that commit and before the first coverage computation: the preserved protocol snapshot `inputs-preserved-2026-10-07-01/PROTOCOL.md` was last modified at 00:18:08 and contains it, and run 01 was written at 00:20:57. That refinement produces the headline series; the committed protocol defined an A-only series and an A+B series, which give 32 empty bins (none internal) and two internal empty bins of 100 years each. The amendment protocol (01:07) precedes `curated-inputs-01` (01:12), `curated-inputs-02` (01:15) and `run-01` (01:16); the amendment has no earlier commit, so that order rests on file modification times. Revisions 02 and 03 are dated in `PROTOCOL.md`, leave the earlier inputs and runs in place (`inputs-preserved-2026-10-07-01` and `-02`, `run-2026-10-07-01` and `-02`) and are labelled exploratory. Between run 01 and the final inputs one selected interval changed, the start of `ashoka-brahmi` (268 to 272 BCE); one image flag changed (28 to 27 rows). Documentary rows are labelled Exploratory and arithmetic Verified computation throughout. The package does no resampling.

**Counts and their bases.** Each row carries `count_kind`, `count_unit` and `count_basis`, and the century table keeps lower bounds, upper bounds, unknowns and unaligned counts apart. Six exceptions: (a) the protocol keeps exact site-wide counts without temporal alignment outside the numeric bounds, yet Perumbalai's 1,028 is excluded as "exact, temporally unaligned" while Keeladi's 3,782 and Kodumanal's 2,632, site-wide totals of the same kind (the REPORT says neither "supplies the distribution of marked objects among the dated layers"), are included, so the upper bounds 3,782 and 6,414 for 700–200 BCE contain them. The package tracks 160 files, all `.md`, `.json`, `.csv` or `.py`, and no PDF or image.
