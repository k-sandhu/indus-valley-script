# Tests added by the chronology amendment

Status: Proposed. These tests were not run. The parent [four designs](../TEST-DESIGNS.md) retain their definitions, controls and rejection rules. The newly recovered publications add an early production-to-sealing sequence and several acquisition targets. They do not supply the dated post-urban intermediate series required to connect Indus marks with early historic writing.

Every test needs registered objects, images, site/deposit, marking stage, context associations and method-specific dates. Treat repeated drawings, multiple impressions of one seal and cited republications as the same witness. Freeze the candidate features, allowed rotations/reflections, archaeological route and exclusions before evaluating matches. Two annotators should work without seeing the proposed cross-corpus correspondence and retain disagreements. A failed access threshold is an acquisition outcome; adequate-power rejection needs observed eligible material.

## 1. Rare compounds from pottery to seals

Use the Mehrgarh charts, Rehman Dheri incised sherds, Harappa Ravi/Kot Diji pottery and early seal/sealing images to test the lecture's proposed transfer of a repertoire. Select a diagnostic compound in a discovery subset, requiring at least four strokes and two specified junction relationships, with occurrence below 1% in an independently assembled comparison inventory. Freeze its component order, enclosure and asymmetry. These are proposed thresholds inherited from the parent design; they have not been measured in this amendment.

Require the feature on independently registered objects from at least three dated intermediate deposits at two sites. Include pre-firing pottery, post-firing graffiti and seals as separate classes. Compare the nominated route with controls matched on stroke/junction complexity, symmetry, ware, object class and date uncertainty. Use 9,999 inventory permutations and a maximum-statistic correction over all features and transformations searched. Evaluate matches on held-out sites and temporal blocks.

Support this transmission model if the fixed feature recurs in the dated intermediates, retains its structural arrangement and temporal order under date uncertainty, and exceeds the matched null at family-wise 0.05. Reject the declared feature/route if reliable dates reverse it or comparable controls explain the recurrence with adequate power. A complete repertoire-transfer claim also requires consistent excess similarity over the full eligible population.

The repository now holds Quivron's complete 1980 images, Durrani's 1988/1995 reports and Harappa sources, listed in [sources.json](sources.json). The Rehman rectangular object remains ineligible for a strict ordered route until its conflicting registrations are resolved. Quivron's distinction between seal and pottery repertoires, p.275, is a comparison to test. The primary 1997 chapter and consistently dated specimen series remain missing. [Rows](evidence-table.json): `mehrgarh-pottery1980`, `rehman-dheri-stratified`, `rehman-dheri-pendant54` and the Harappa early rows.

## 2. Complexity-matched feature comparison

Encode topology and geometry separately: stroke count, endpoints, junction types, loops, enclosures, symmetry, relative angles and component placement. Base-fragment orientation uncertainty must remain explicit. Compare Mehrgarh's 147 variants/50 categories and Rehman Dheri's numbered entries with a freshly annotated seal repertoire. Neither published numbering scheme defines normalized sign identity, so do not equate numbered entries across publications.

Changizi, Zhang, Ye and Shimojo's 2006 *The Structures of Letters and Symbols throughout Human History Are Selected to Match Those Found in Objects in Natural Scenes*, *American Naturalist* 167(5):E117–E139, reports shared shape-configuration distributions. The parent [source record](../sources.json), `changizi2006`, and [design 2](../TEST-DESIGNS.md) specify the recovered sections. Use its configuration framework as a convergence baseline, extending it for larger compounds. [Primary paper](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/502806).

Freeze a graph-edit distance on training data. Compare held-out nearest-neighbour similarity, rare-feature enrichment and distance distributions with 9,999 resamples matched on repertoire size, stroke/junction complexity, symmetry and marking medium. Correct for all candidate mappings, and repeat after removing simple symmetric marks. Support graphic transmission if excess similarity survives these controls and replicates in independent dated assemblages. Reject the specified shape prediction if matched independent inventories yield equivalent similarity at the prespecified detectable effect. A positive shape result still needs the dated route from design 1.

The PDF/image inputs and previous South India decomposition are local, but graph annotations and independently matched control inventories are missing. Reuse the [October 2 membership audit](../../2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/south-india/REPORT-reviewed-v1.md) to find annotation problems. Do not rerun its failed date-free stroke comparison as a chronology test.

## 3. Seriation with repaired context counts

Recover a mark-by-object-by-deposit matrix from Durrani's registration records, Quivron's primary corpora and Harappa's subphases. Store sign variants, ware, marking stage and full object identifiers. Require at least 12 dated assemblages with 30 independent marked objects each across three sites, or replace these proposed thresholds with a preregistered power calculation. Keep original laboratory distributions, stratigraphic constraints and alternative calendar assessments.

Compare an inheritance model with independent invention conditioned on shape complexity, production context and local frequencies. Hold out whole sites and temporal blocks; measure predictive log score for their feature distributions. Permute dates only within stratigraphically admissible orderings. Support the model if it predicts held-out repertoires better and recovers independent chronology under dating uncertainty. Reject the stated route or inheritance-rate prediction if its advantage disappears after complexity/context controls or it orders well-dated deposits incorrectly.

The new Rehman report contains 190 stratified sherds, but its printed phase sign totals sum to 163. The unit discrepancy and 27 arithmetic difference prevent filling a five-phase object matrix. The 94 catalogue indices also require variant normalization. [Count audit](dating-details.json), `count_audit`, and the raw primary pages provide the starting observations. The full matrix and modern calibration metadata remain unavailable.

## 4. Positional and combinatorial transfer

Use multi-mark objects only, preserving surface, physical order, incision stage and damage. Annotate seal faces and impressions with their mirrored relationship before testing directions. Rehman Dheri's proposed right-to-left tendency is a hypothesis; Mehrgarh base fragments can lack a recoverable orientation. Do not infer direction from a selected resemblance and then count that resemblance as an independent directional match.

Acquire 200 independent multi-mark objects per time slice, or a preregistered lower target justified by power. Freeze candidate initial/terminal distributions, adjacent pairs and compound positions in a discovery set. Score held-out sites against within-corpus shuffles preserving object length, sign marginals, site and object class, plus complexity-matched external inventories. Use 9,999 permutations with correction for mappings, directions and rules.

Support transmission if a fixed rule recurs in multiple dated intermediate slices beyond its conditioned null. Reject the prediction if it is absent at adequate power or follows only a vessel/production convention. The repository holds Indus sequences and illustrated early/southern marks, but lacks a complete dated, direction-annotated intermediate corpus. The new sherd total does not establish the multi-mark sample size. [Claim](continuity-claims.json), `rehman-continuity-followup`; parent design 4 supplies the later southern comparison.

## 5. Firing technology and mark frequency

The kiln-change statement predicts a decrease in marks associated with a production change. Recover every marked and unmarked vessel from comparable firing/workshop deposits, with kiln type, vessel form, ware, breakage, incision stage and independently dated context. Include contemporary firing methods at Mehrgarh and Nausharo. Petrographic or chemical groups can test whether mark families track production groups, with batch identity kept separate from vessel type.

Freeze the proposed replacement date before measuring frequency. Compare the fraction of vessels with pre-firing marks across methods and periods, using a binomial mixed model with workshop/site effects and a matched difference-in-differences comparison where contemporary controls exist. Test sensitivity to vessel reconstruction, ware, uneven recovery and centralized production. Choose an effect target before acquisition; a proposed starting target is a decrease of at least 10 percentage points, with power calculated using plausible batch counts.

Support the proposed mechanism if the decline follows the independently dated change, exceeds the target with a 95% interval excluding zero, persists within comparable wares and is absent from unchanged-method controls. Reject that effect-size claim if adequate sampling excludes a decrease of that magnitude, or if the decline precedes the alleged replacement. An association with production groups can support a manufacturing function without authenticating the identity of an owner.

The opened sources give a 142-jar failed batch, an estimated original load near 200, and a separately cited 100-vessel/33-marked storage room. Their populations and dates are not interchangeable. Exact batch-to-mark registrations and frequencies by kiln method are missing; Quivron 1997 and the original excavation reports are acquisition targets. [Rows](evidence-table.json), `mehrgarh-failed-firing`, `mehrgarh-nausharo1997`, `nausharo-kiln-II`; [lecture audit](lecture-claim-audit.json), `kiln-change`.

## 6. The claimed circa-2800 hearth date

Join registration **H98-3485/8437-01** to its exact feature, excavation unit, deposit history, carbon sample and laboratory determination. Obtain the original calibration output and sample material; test intrusive/residual charcoal and whether the sealing lay in the dated deposit. Prefer multiple short-lived samples and an explicit stratigraphic model where the field record permits it. Site-phase tables are contextual information until that join is established.

For the specific before-2800 assertion, preregister the decision: support an earlier context if a defensible model places at least 95% of its calendar probability before 2800 BCE and the specimen association survives the depositional audit. Reject that endpoint if a securely joined context places at least 95% after 2800 BCE. An interval straddling 2800 or an unresolved association is inconclusive. The test dates the context, not the incision directly.

The repository holds the registered 1999 figure, the 2006 description, the 2005 site sample table and excavation-gallery captions. It lacks the feature/sample link needed for grade A. Recovering that link could refine an early endpoint; it would leave the separate post-urban bridge question to its own evidence. [Dating details](dating-details.json), `harappa`.

## 7. Contact imagery, legends and an external alignment

For each published foreign-legend object, acquire museum/field registration, high-resolution obverse/reverse/impression images, provenance, a current epigraphic edition and method-specific dates. Verify whether an Indus sign block occurs on that physical object. Deduplicate editions and casts. Keep stylistic sharing and textual alignment as separate observations.

A contact-transmission test can compare unusual bovid composition, carving conventions and seal construction across dated local/foreign groups, using complexity- and medium-matched controls and held-out provenance groups. A stronger text-alignment test requires repeated paired Indus/known-script blocks on at least two independent provenanced objects. Freeze candidate correspondences on one subset, then predict held-out blocks against sign-marginal and length-matched shuffles, correcting for all searched alignments.

Support the graphic-contact prediction if a diagnostic convention is enriched beyond controls and has a compatible dated route. Support a proposed text alignment only if it replicates on independent held-out objects. Reject an asserted bilingual classification when inspected objects have only one script; reject the predictive mapping if it fails the held-out/null comparison. Neither test begins by assigning phonetic values to the Indus signs.

Four illustrated cases and a three-token abstract-level cohort are recorded in [contact-items.json](contact-items.json). None supplies an authenticated aligned pair. The Linear Elamite item is one seal across several reproductions; Shu-ilishu's cylinder has a cuneiform interpreter legend. The BnF inventory, Winkelmann originals, Bahrain plates and final forthcoming study remain acquisition targets. The [external-anchor audit](../../../research/literature/external_phonetic_anchor_audit.md) supplies the prior object checks.

The lecture's merchant-reintroduction account requires a separate historical comparison: dated early Brahmi find contexts and decoded donor/profession evidence along a declared route, compared with non-trade contexts under equal recovery. Early pottery dates alone cannot identify transmitters. [Existing assessments](evidence-table.json), rows `anuradhapura`, `porunthal-I`, `kodumanal`, `keeladi` and `ashoka-brahmi`, show which chronological associations first need recovery.
