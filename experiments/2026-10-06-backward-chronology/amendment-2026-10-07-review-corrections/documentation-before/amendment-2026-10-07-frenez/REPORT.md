# Early marks, early Brahmi and western contact examples

This amendment adds primary evidence for early Indus-region marks, but leaves the recovered late Indus-to-early historic gap unchanged. The inventory grows from **42 to 54 rows** and from **19 to 24 dated additive assemblages**. Of the 24, two have dating-link grade A, 14 B and eight C. The longest internal gap for A/B links with high or moderate calendar strength remains **1300–700 BCE**, six century bins. [Summary](run-01/summary.json), fields `window_dating_grades` and `gaps`, and [comparison](comparison-01/comparison.json) contain the calculations. Additional early material changes possible assemblage and exact-object upper counts in **16 of 33 bins**; it supplies no new A association. The documentary assessments are Exploratory.

The coordinator's [caption check](../../2026-10-06-research-program/frenez-talk-check.md) supplied the lecture statements and timestamps. This package did not independently watch the video or recover its transcript. [Eight claim records](lecture-claim-audit.json) separate the statements, recovered observations and missing joins. Earlier Package E outputs remain unchanged, including all 42 source-assessed rows. The [477-file preservation check](comparison-01/baseline-verification.json) and [build check](build-verification.json) verify this preservation.

## Mehrgarh and Nausharo

Gonzague Quivron's 1980 *Les marques incisées sur les poteries de Mehrgarh au Baluchistan, du milieu du IVe millénaire à la première moitié du IIIe millénaire*, *Paléorient* 6:269–280, supplies the primary mark study. Page 269 reports **nearly 850 marks, 147 variants and 50 categories**. Pages 274–276 describe pre-firing incisions and place the studied phase sequence from the middle fourth millennium into the first half of the third. This is a mark-observation total; the distinct marked-object total is unavailable. Row `mehrgarh-pottery1980` therefore has an unknown object count and an approximate 3500–2500 BCE phase interval, B with low calendar strength. All 12 page images were recovered and the critical count/date passages visually checked. [Primary paper](https://www.persee.fr/doc/paleo_0153-9345_1980_num_6_1_4281), pp.269,274–276; [evidence table](evidence-table.json), row `mehrgarh-pottery1980`.

The firing interpretation has an identifiable literature history. Quivron 1980, pp.275 and 278, observes marks across different vessel forms and proposes workshop identification. His 1997 *Incised and Painted Marks on the Pottery of Mehrgarh and Nausharo-Baluchistan*, in F. R. and B. Allchin's *South Asian Archaeology 1995*, pp.45–62, was not recovered in full. R. A. E. Coningham's opened 2002 *Deciphering the Indus Script*, in *Indian Archaeology in Retrospect*, volume II, pp.81–103, discusses it at pp.93–94. That discussion reports **1,265 marks over 3600–2500 BCE**, a failed Period VI firing near 2700 BCE, and **33 marked vessels among 100 in room CXVIII, with 16 sign types**. It attributes an ownership-in-communal-firing suggestion to Quivron's p.61 endnote. The pooled row is excluded because it overlaps the Mehrgarh study and cannot isolate a Nausharo denominator. [Coningham primary chapter](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Deciphering_the_Indus_Script.pdf), pp.93–94,102; [source access records](sources.json), `quivron1997` and `coningham2002`.

The lecture's kiln replacement mechanism remains unverified. Quivron 1980 p.278 describes a decline around 2400 BCE; Coningham reports a peak near 2700 followed by decline. Heather M.-L. Miller's 1997 *Pottery Firing Structures (Kilns) of the Indus Civilization During the Third Millennium B.C.*, in Rice and Kingery's *Prehistory and History of Ceramic Kilns*, pp.41–71, documents several contemporary firing methods at pp.42–43. Her Mehrgarh discussion records **142 surviving jars** from a failed firing, with an estimated original load near 200; these are firing-batch denominators, not marked-object counts. Open-air and updraft firing coexist at Nausharo Period II, about 2500–2400 BCE, pp.48–49. No recovered comparison joins kiln type, exact marked/unmarked denominator and calibrated date. [Miller chapter](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Miller_PotteryFiringStructuresKilns.pdf), pp.42–43,47–49; [evidence rows](evidence-table.json), `mehrgarh-failed-firing` and `nausharo-kiln-II`.

Two primary acquisition targets are identified through that literature: Audouze and Catherine Jarrige's 1979 *A Third Millennium Pottery-Firing Structure at Mehrgarh and Its Economic Implications*, and Catherine Jarrige, Jean-François Jarrige, Richard H. Meadow and Gonzague Quivron's 1995 *Mehrgarh: Field Reports 1974–1985 from Neolithic Times to the Indus Civilization*. Their original mark/batch registers were not opened. Miller's bibliography gives the 1979 chapter pp.213–221, while Quivron's p.275 footnote prints pp.213–231; that pagination conflict needs the original chapter. [Sources](sources.json), `audouze-jarrige1979` and `jarrige1995-report`.

The proposed movement of a repertoire onto seals needs a narrower test. Quivron 1980 p.275 says the contemporary clay/bone seals have a different, restricted range of geometric or animal designs; p.279 compares selected pottery shapes with Indus signs. These observations support a feature-level comparison. They do not establish that a complete pottery repertoire was transferred to seals. [Primary paper](https://www.persee.fr/doc/paleo_0153-9345_1980_num_6_1_4281), pp.275,279; [claim](continuity-claims.json), `preseal-repertoire-followup`.

## Rehman Dheri

The likely correction of the caption's “Rakandere” is **Rehman Dheri**, in the Gomal Valley near Dera Ismail Khan, historically North-West Frontier Province, now Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. F. A. Durrani's 1988 *Excavations in the Gomal Valley (Rehman Dheri Excavation Report No. 1)*, *Ancient Pakistan* VI:1–232, pp.17–18, locates the site there. This remains a publication-based identification: the lecture image was not independently matched. [University publication archive](https://sites.google.com/uop.edu.pk/ancientpakistan/volumes/volume-vi); [lecture audit](lecture-claim-audit.json), `site-correction`.

Durrani, Ihsan Ali and George Erdosy's *Seals and Inscribed Sherds of Rehman Dheri*, *Ancient Pakistan* X (1994–95):198–233, p.208, reports **314 registered inscribed sherds, 190 stratified**, from 1976–82 and 1991. The catalogue's numbered main entries run from 1 to 94; letter variants prevent treating that index as a normalized repertoire. Pages 216–217 report phase sign totals **7, 12, 14, 19 and 111**, summing to **163**, 27 below 190. Because the totals use different wording and do not reconcile, this amendment does not allocate 190 objects to individual phases. The row keeps a broad 3300–1900 BCE phase bracket from the volume's *Introduction*, p.vii. [University volume X archive](https://sites.google.com/uop.edu.pk/ancientpakistan/volumes/volume-x); [dating details](dating-details.json), `count_audit`; [evidence row](evidence-table.json), `rehman-dheri-stratified`.

The site's calendar uses 12 radiocarbon determinations listed in Durrani 1988 Figure D, p.139, including PRL-675/676, WIS-1697 through 1702, PRL-674/673 and BM-2062/2063. The source prints BP measurements and separate calibrated ranges using the Libby half-life. It does not identify the curve, interval probability or each sample's material/context in that figure. The phase link is B; its calendar strength is low. No dates were recalibrated or combined. The exact measurement transcription remains [local raw](../../../data/raw/2026-10-06-backward-chronology/amendment-2026-10-07-frenez/radiocarbon-source-rows.json). [Dating assessment](dating-details.json), `rehman_dheri`, links the phase, laboratory and source-image records.

The rectangular object is more problematic than a single early date suggests. The 1988 report calls it ivory and assigns it to Period IB, about 3100–2850 BCE, p.28. The 1995 catalogue calls it bone and records item 54 as **BIV/25, local Layer 6, Level 19, 31×28×4.5 mm**, p.201. Level 19 belongs to IA in the volume's *Introduction*, p.vii. Yet the 1988 layer discussion correlates local Layer 6 with main Layer 12, Period II, p.25. Kenoyer's 2020 comparative Table 2 places the Rehman pendant in 2800–2600 BCE. All four assessments remain separate and unselected. The authors also question whether early seal-like objects functioned as seals or amulets without associated impressions. [Durrani publications](https://sites.google.com/uop.edu.pk/ancientpakistan/volumes/volume-x), pp.198–201; [Kenoyer 2020 chapter](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer2020%20Origin%20of%20Indus%20Script.pdf), pp.221,225–226; [row](evidence-table.json), `rehman-dheri-pendant54`.

Durrani and colleagues proposed antecedents of Indus signs and a right-to-left tendency from mark layout. Their 1995 conclusion also reports no clear progression from simple to composite signs, p.217. The recovered drawings and context entries permit a new annotation study, but the count and orientation problems must be resolved before seriation or positional statistics. [Attributed claim](continuity-claims.json), `rehman-continuity-followup`; [test designs](TEST-DESIGNS.md), sections 1–4.

## Harappa's early seals and hearth sealing

The pre-2800 record includes pottery marks and a carved bone fragment. The parent Ravi row retains **13 post-firing and four pre-firing sherds**, a 2006 excavation snapshot. The new pre-firing subset row repeats those four without adding coverage. The carved bone fragment **H1521**, illustrated as H98-3503/8514-07 in the 1999 report, is a different object class and contributes one object. Its relief design is weathered/burned and stamping use is unresolved. [Kenoyer and Meadow 2010 published chapter](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer-Meadow-2010-HARP.pdf), Table 1 p.xlv and p.li; [rows](evidence-table.json), `harappa-ravi-prefire-subset` and `harappa-ravi-bone1521`.

The first intaglio seals and seal-impressed clay belong to the **Kot Diji phase, about 2800–2600 BCE**, in the 2010 chapter p.li. Its unfinished elephant seal **H1533** has a sealed context and ceramic association; the upper part is missing, so no written signs are preserved there. A separate clay sealing, **H98-3485/8437-01**, is listed in Kenoyer and Meadow's 1999 *Harappa: New Discoveries on its Origins and Growth*, *Lahore Museum Bulletin* XII(1):1–12, Figure 4 item 6, caption p.6. Kenoyer's 2006 Figure 5:9 and text describe the sealing's hearth find and script-like signs. [1999 report](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer1999_Harappa%20New%20Discoveries%20on%20its%20origins%20and%20growt.pdf); [2006 report](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer2006_The%20Origin%2C%20Context%20and%20Function%20of%20the%20Indus%20Sc.pdf); [evidence rows](evidence-table.json), `harappa-kot-diji-elephant1533` and `harappa-kot-diji-hearth-sealing`.

The excavation gallery confirms a sealing found in a hearth and labels it circa 2800 BCE. Neither gallery caption supplies a lab determination. Meadow and Kenoyer's 2005 *Excavations at Harappa 2000–2001: New Insights on Chronology and City Organization*, *South Asian Archaeology 2001*, pp.207–225, p.209/Table 2, presents additional calibrated samples and says the beginning of Period 2 remains largely undated. A table of site samples cannot authenticate the lecture's particular fireplace-to-sealing association without a field-register join. The new sealing and elephant rows therefore have B/moderate phase dates, with no new A endpoint or authenticated before-2800 intaglio date. [Hearth gallery](https://www.harappa.com/slide/sealing-find), [object gallery](https://www.harappa.com/indus2/137.html), [2005 report](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer2005_Excavations%20at%20Harappa%202000-2001%20New%20insights%20on.pdf); [dating details](dating-details.json), `harappa`.

## Contact-period seals

The separate contact row records **four illustrated examples**: the Ur stamp **BM120573/U7683**, a BnF stamp lacking a recovered inventory number, the round Linear Elamite stamp reproduced by Winkelmann/Laursen/Frenez, and the Shu-ilishu cylinder **Louvre AO22310** as an interpreter-contact comparator. The row is excluded from South Asian additive coverage. [Case audit](contact-items.json), `cases`; [existing external-anchor audit](../../../research/literature/external_phonetic_anchor_audit.md).

C. J. Gadd's 1932 *Seals of Ancient Indian Style Found at Ur*, *Proceedings of the British Academy* 18, reprint pp.3–22, describes **no.1**, BM120573, at pp.5–6. Its inscription is foreign cuneiform; its worn condition and find circumstances give no secure contextual date. The old pre-Sargonic/before-2500 epigraphic estimate and museum's circa-2500 label remain distinct. Frenez's 2018 *Private Person or Public Persona? Use and Significance of Standard Indus Seals as Markers of Formal Socio-Economic Identities*, pp.166–193, Figure 10 p.177, illustrates different objects with cuneiform, Linear Elamite and Indus legends. Its caption supplies no paired-script alignment on one object. [Gadd publication](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/33779.pdf), pp.5–6, Plate I; [Frenez chapter](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/private-person.pdf), Figure 10; [cases](contact-items.json), `ur-bm120573` and `bnf-cuneiform-stamp`.

Laursen's 2010 *The westward transmission of Indus Valley sealing technology: origin and development of the ‘Gulf Type’ seal and other administrative technologies in Early Dilmun, c.2100–2000 BC*, *Arabian Archaeology and Epigraphy* 21:96–134, identifies the Linear Elamite example as **no.28**, Table 1 p.100 and Figure 9, caption p.110, panel p.111. Page 104 note 4 traces collection ownership and rejects a mistaken Bahrain find attribution. Frenez's 2023 Figure 3O caption cites Winkelmann 2004 catalogue **II.1-02**. Laursen's proposed 2112–2095 BCE date depends on a restricted historical/script-horizon assumption; no independently dated deposit is supplied. The original Winkelmann article/book remain unopened. [Laursen paper](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/The_westward_transmission_of_Indus_Valle.pdf), pp.100,104,110–111,119,121; [Frenez caption](https://www.harappa.com/content/indus-valley-early-commercial-connections-central-and-western-asia); [case](contact-items.json), `linear-elamite-round-stamp`.

Gregory L. Possehl's 2006 *Shu-ilishu’s Cylinder Seal*, *Expedition* 48(1):42–43, documents the interpreter legend and rollout. It gives competing Late Akkadian/Ur III stylistic dates; the Louvre currently gives an Akkad 2340–2200 BCE attribution by style. Those dates are preserved separately. The cylinder contains no documented Indus text. A separate published Gulf comparator, Laursen, Al Shaikh, Reeler, Lombard and Marchesi's 2026 *Stamped Tokens with Cuneiform Inscriptions from Bahrain: New Light on Kingship and the Gods in Early 2nd Millennium BC Dilmun*, *Journal of Near Eastern Studies* 85(1):115–145, reports **three tokens impressed by two cuneiform seals** in its institutional abstract. The abstract establishes neither Indus-style imagery nor aligned Indus text; its repository plates remain embargoed until 1 April 2027. [Possehl paper](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/201402/Shu-ilishus-Cylinder-Seal.pdf), pp.42–43; [Louvre catalogue](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010147038); [2026 institutional abstract](https://cris.unibo.it/handle/11585/1059790); [cases](contact-items.json), `shu-ilishu-cylinder` and `bahrain-tokens2026`.

The lecture says a group study is forthcoming. The related *Hybrid identities* title appears under papers in preparation in Frenez's CV dated 10 July 2024, PDF p.7. Live searches recovered no final published article or artifact catalogue under that title. The CV establishes its 2024 status; the lecture supplies a later statement, with access limited to the coordinator's caption audit. The contact selection authenticates **zero aligned Indus/known-script pairs**.
