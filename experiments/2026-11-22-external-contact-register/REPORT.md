# Edition-linked register of contemporary Meluhha, Susa, and Dilmun contact records

We built a deterministic, edition-linked register of 196 records from held local primary catalogues and cuneiform archives, separating 193 contemporary third-millennium and early second-millennium BCE contact records (182 physical objects or physical object groups and 11 composite text editions) from 3 later dated comparative controls. The complete register is in [register-v1.json](register-v1.json), aggregate counts and input digests are in [summary-v1.json](summary-v1.json), the tabular Meluhha attestation extract is in [meluhha-commodities-titles-v1.csv](meluhha-commodities-titles-v1.csv), and independent verification checks are in [verification-v1.json](verification-v1.json). Every record preserves its exact edition, physical or composite status, source dependence, and SHA-256 input hashes under [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md).

## Register composition and chronological separation

Strict chronological separation governs the register. Cohort 1 contains only third-millennium and early second-millennium BCE witnesses (roughly 2350 to 1600 BCE), when Mesopotamian scribes used the name Meluhha for the eastern maritime trading partner linked to Magan and Dilmun. Our scan of the CDLI transliteration dump also recovered 83 later or undated Meluhha hits (73 Neo-Assyrian, 2 Neo-Babylonian, 1 Middle Babylonian from Alalakh, 6 undated medical tablets from the `BAM` series, and 1 undated mathematical tablet `P217674`), along with 2 Middle Babylonian Failaka seals (`P511661`, `P511663`). We excluded all of those later records from Cohort 1 because first-millennium and Late Bronze Age scribes reassigned Meluhha to Nubia and Kush, and we logged every excluded entry locally in `data/raw/2026-11-22-external-contact-register/excluded-records-audit-v1.json`. We also excluded three verified lexical or onomastic false matches in the third-millennium and second-millennium scan: `P257207` (`[me-luh-luh]-ha`, purification rites in the *Lament for Nippur*), `P346234` (`u3-me-luh-luh`, a verbal form of "to wash"), and `P135796` (the personal name `e-lum-me-luh`).

Across all 193 Cohort 1 records, deterministic extraction from CDLI transliterations, ORACC lemmatized archives, and audited primary catalogues recovered 47 unique commodity and metrological unit classes, 32 unique titles and institutional roles, 543 unique personal and royal names, and 63 records with linked seals or seal impressions. Twelve CDLI composite seal entries for Ur III Susa (`P454287` to `P458708`) and four CDLI composite entries for Early Dilmun (`P507624` to `P507627`) are linked under `associated_seals` and `source_dependence` rather than counted as separate physical objects. Likewise, the duplicate Lipi witness `6000.1` is merged into Ur seal `3898.1` (`U.17649`, `BM 123208`, Gadd no. 16, Laursen no. 21).

## Contemporary Meluhha cuneiform records

The 135 contemporary Meluhha records (`124` physical carriers and `11` composite royal or literary editions) come from the union of CDLI (`cdli_cat.csv` and `cdliatf_unblocked.atf`), ORACC (`oakk.zip` and `epsd2-admin-ur3.zip`), and the Louvre and CDLI records for Shu-ilishu's cylinder seal (`P525331`, `AO 22310`). Five Ur III administrative texts (`P331231`, `P512784`, `P516138`, `P516366`, and `P518135`) appeared only in the ORACC Ur III JSON archive and were absent from the 2022 CDLI transliteration dump.

By period, Ur III tablets form the largest group with 61 records, followed by Old Babylonian texts (43 definite and 1 uncertain, mostly Nippur `Hh` lexical lists preserving third-millennium commodity terms alongside Old Babylonian copies of Old Akkadian and Ur III royal inscriptions and Sumerian literary compositions), Old Akkadian texts (15 definite and 2 uncertain), Lagash II texts (10, including Gudea's Statue B, Statue D, Cylinder A, and Cylinder B physical exemplars `P232275`, `P232277`, `P232300`, and `P232301`), Early Old Babylonian Isin craft tablets (2: `P236658` and `P390701`), and Shu-ilishu's cylinder seal (`P525331`, `AO 22310`, where we retain the Louvre's 2340 to 2200 BCE Akkadian date, CDLI's Ur III attribution, and Possehl's late Akkadian or Ur III stylistic alternatives separately). By region, Girsu leads with 48 records, followed by Nippur (34), Ur (20), unprovenanced Mesopotamian texts (11 including `AO 22310`), Adab (7), Umma (4), Irisagrig (4), Isin (2), and one record each from Susa (`P215605`, Old Akkadian `MDP 14, 062-126 42`), Uruk, Larsa, Sippar-Yahrurum, and Puzriš-Dagan.

Three distinct historical layers appear inside these 135 texts:

1. **Maritime traffic, diplomacy, and officials.** Old Akkadian tablets from Adab and royal copies name crews and officers directly tied to Meluhha vessels: `nu-banda3 ma2 me-luh-ha` (captain or overseer of the Meluhha boat, `P326408`), `lu2-tukul ma2 me-luh-ha` (armed attendant of the Meluhha boat, `P212842`), `gurusz ma2 me-luh-ha` (workers of the Meluhha boat, `P217502`), `ugula me-luh-ha` (overseer of Meluhha, `P115261`), and `ra-gaba me-luh-ha` (mounted messenger of Meluhha, `P213104`). In `P253274` (`CUSAS 35, 288` from Adab), `i3-li2-a-hi`, titled `lu2 me-luh` ("man of Meluhha"), appears on the same Old Akkadian tablet as `gal-zu`, titled `maszkim ma2 dilmun` ("commissioner of the Dilmun boat"). In the Ur III period, `P320486` (`CM 26, 136`, Umma) records provisions for the royal messenger (`sukkal`) Utu-illat on the occasion of his journey to Meluhha (`u4 me-luh-ha{ki}-sze3 i3-gen-na`), while Louvre cylinder seal `AO 22310` (`P525331`, `S014339`) identifies `su-i-li-su` as `eme-bal me-luh-ha` ("interpreter of Meluhha").
2. **Resident Meluhhan communities in Sumer.** Girsu administrative records document a permanent Meluhhan settlement attached to the Lagash state near the seaport of Gu'abba (`gu2-ab-ba{ki}`, 3 records): the village of Meluhha (`e2-duru5 me-luh-ha`, 7 records), the granary of Meluhha (`i3-dub me-luh-ha`, 11 records), and an orchard of Meluhha (`{gesz}kiri6 me-luh-ha`, `P130369`). Six of these Girsu tablets carry cylinder seal impressions (`P114609` with seal `S004667`, `P116633` with `S001662`, `P206053` with `S014336`, `P235705` with `S004976`, `P374962` with `S003079`, and `P492732` with `S004883`). Resident individuals bearing Sumerian personal names carry the qualifier `dumu me-luh-ha` ("son of Meluhha", 10 records, notably `ur-{d}lamma dumu me-luh-ha` in `P102519`, `P108448`, `P136196`, `P207485`, and `P318542`, and `ur-{d}ig-alim dumu me-luh-ha` in `P124739`). At Irisagrig, four Ur III ration lists (`P453801`, `P454137`, `P516138`, and `P516366`) issue oil and barley to groups labelled `lu2 me-luh-ha{ki}-me` ("men of Meluhha").
3. **Imported goods and craft materials.** Across the 135 records we extracted 45 commodity and metrological unit categories. The most frequent Meluhha-qualified imports are exotic woods (`{gesz}ab-ba me-luh-ha` in 22 texts and `{gesz}mes me-luh-ha` in 19 texts, worked into thrones `{gesz}gu-za`, footstools `{gesz}giri3-gub`, and tables `{gesz}banszur`), metals (copper `uruda` in 18 texts including 6 minas of Meluhha copper in `P136689`, gold `ku3-sig17` in 18, bronze `zabar` in 15, silver `ku3-babbar` in 14, tin `nagga` in 9), precious stones (lapis lazuli `za-gin3` in 16, carnelian `{na4}gug` in 12), ivory figurines or birds of Meluhha (`dar{muszen} me-luh-ha` in 6 texts, including Ur `UET 3` workshop accounts `P137081`, `P137085`, `P137088`, `P137092`, and `P137094`), ebony (`{gesz}esi`, including the bilingual Old Babylonian royal copy `P251861`), and a spotted dog of Meluhha (`ur gun3-a me-luh-ha`, `P226318`). Staples and local rations (barley `sze` in 53 texts, bread `ninda` in 20, wool `siki` in 18, oil `i3` in 18, sheep `udu` in 17) measured in standard units (`gur`, `sila3`, `barig`, `ban2`, `gin2`, `ma-na`) track the local provisioning of crews, messengers, and Meluhhan villagers.

## Ur III Susa sealed administrative transactions

The CDLI catalogue contains 166 Ur III records from Susa, of which 21 are physical sealed objects (19 tablets, 1 bulla `P127653`, and 1 miscellaneous sealed carrier `P200397`) and 12 are dependent composite seal editions (`P454287` to `P458708`). Twenty of the 21 physical carriers have full local CDLI transliterations with `@seal` sections; one (`P520278`, `NMI BK 01176`) is catalogued as unpublished without text. Sixteen belong to De Graef's `MDP 54` archive (`P332428` to `P332462`), including `P332451` (`BDTNS 157897`, `MDP 54, 029`, `TS.B 140`, `NMI BK 00696`), which records a delivery of 1 `gur` of lentils (`gu2-tur`) to `ur-{d}suen` authenticated by seal `S005998` (composite `P458708`) naming King Shu-Suen and `be-li2-a-ri-ik`, cup-bearer (`sagi`) and governor of Susa (`ensi2 szuszin{ki}`). Together with `P200397` (naming Governor `i-da-du` of Susa) and `P112925` (`szu-{d}ba-ba6`, servant of `e-ba-ra-ad`), these 21 physical records show how Ur III Susa paired short commodity disbursements (barley, lentils, sheep, flour, oil, silver) with official seals naming ruler, office, and city.

## Gulf Indus-style seals and Early Dilmun named inscriptions

Cohort 1 includes 29 physical Indus-style and Gulf Type seals found outside South Asia, drawn from [joins.csv](../2026-10-12-round-seal-tradition/joins.csv), Gadd (1932), Laursen (2010), and [external_phonetic_anchor_audit.md](../../research/literature/external_phonetic_anchor_audit.md): 22 West Asian and unprovenanced Gulf Type seals from Laursen's Table 1 (nos. 6 to 27), Karzakan no. 56 (`BBM 20362`), Kalba `K4`, Ra's al-Junayz `RJ 12803`, the Susa cylinder seal (`Louvre SB 2425`), and Gadd's Ur seals nos. 1 (`BM 120573`, `U.7683`), 6 (`BM 122947`, `U.16220`), and 7 (`U.11958`). As established in the anchor audit, none of these 29 objects bears both a legible cuneiform sequence and a transcribed Indus script sequence; Gadd no. 1 pairs a bison motif with archaic cuneiform (provisionally read *Ka-lu-lim* or *Ka-lu-si* by Marchesi) without Indus writing, and Gadd no. 6 is a distinct uninscribed cylinder.

Alongside the Gulf Type seals, Cohort 1 records 8 Early Dilmun cuneiform witnesses from Bahrain (`dilmun_named_seal`). Seven are physical stone vessel inscriptions in CDLI from the Royal Mounds of A'ali and Qal'at al-Bahrain (`P507617` to `P507623`, linked to 4 dependent composite texts `P507624` to `P507627`) naming the Dilmun rulers `Yagli-El` (son of `Rimum`) and `Rimum` with the palace formula `e2-gal` and the epithet `ARAD2 {d}in-za-ak` ("servant of Inzak"), plus two capacity inscriptions (`P507620` and `P507621`). The eighth entry (`ECR-DLM-EXT05`) records the institutional abstract of Laursen and colleagues (2026, *Journal of Near Eastern Studies* 85(1): 115–145, DOI `10.1086/739820`), reporting three stamped clay tokens from Bahrain carrying impressions of two cuneiform seals naming `Yagli-El` and a divine servant epithet; its full PDF remains embargoed until 1 April 2027, so no object accessions or plates are inferred.

## Cohort 2 comparative controls and independent verification

Cohort 2 retains three later dated controls in isolation (`indus_contact_evidence_class = "later_control"`): Hallock's 1969 *Persepolis Fortification Tablets* (Elamite cuneiform, approximately 509 to 493 BCE, `ECR-CTL-EXT06`), Early Historic South Indian Tamil-Brahmi personal seals and rings (third century BCE to third century CE, `ECR-CTL-EXT07`, including the Karur silver ring naming `Peravatan` and pointing to the 16 frozen Tamil-Brahmi seals and rings in the 24 October recovery [REPORT.md](../2026-10-24-readable-seal-controls/REPORT.md)), and Sidebotham and colleagues' 2025 Roman-period Berenike, Myos Hormos, and Sumhuram Tamil-Brahmi pottery overview (first century CE, `ECR-CTL-EXT08`, indexed excerpt tier).

Independent verification in [verify.py](code/verify.py) ran without importing [build_register.py](code/build_register.py) and passed 7 of 7 checks ([verification-v1.json](verification-v1.json)): exact SHA-256 and byte-size recomputation across all 9 source files and every record hash map; 19-field schema completeness and unique IDs across all 196 entries; zero conflation between Cohort 1 and Cohort 2 classes or forbidden later periods; lookup of all 163 unique CDLI P-numbers directly in `cdli_cat.csv` with zero missing IDs and zero provenience or period mismatches; zero leakage of the 3 excluded false positives, later-period Meluhha samples, 16 dependent Susa and Dilmun composites, or duplicate Lipi witness `6000.1`; exact recomputation of all summary counts and CSV row parity (`135` rows); and verification of all three local derivative files on `D:`.

## Boldest supported reading and deciding external-anchor test

The contemporary register supports a concrete historical picture of Indus-Mesopotamian contact in the late third millennium BCE: Meluhhan trade was not an anonymous relay of prestige stones, but an administered network with named interpreters (`eme-bal me-luh-ha`), specialized Meluhha-ship captains and crews (`nu-banda3 ma2 me-luh-ha`, `lu2-tukul ma2 me-luh-ha`), royal envoys traveling to Meluhha (`P320486`), and a settled Meluhhan village with its own granary and orchard inside the Lagash state near Gu'abba whose residents took Sumerian names and sealed routine barley receipts. Yet across all 193 contemporary records, the cuneiform administrative stream and the Indus-inscribed Gulf seal stream remain completely disjoint at the level of individual objects: the readable tablets tell us what offices, names, and commodities circulated, while the 29 Gulf and Mesopotamian Indus-style seals still carry no paired readable legend.

The deciding external-anchor test that would connect a contemporary record in this register to a specific Indus inscription is an excavated sealed clay tablet, envelope, or bulla from an Ur III or Old Akkadian administrative locus (such as Girsu's Gu'abba archive, Ur, Adab, Susa, or Early Dilmun Bahrain) that bears an Indus-script or Gulf Type seal impression directly alongside a readable cuneiform text naming the sealing party, office, or commodity (for example, matching an Indus-inscribed seal impression to a named `lu2 me-luh-ha`, `eme-bal me-luh-ha`, or `dam-gar3` recorded in the tablet's `kiszib3` sealing clause). Inspecting the embargoed plates of Laursen and colleagues (2026) after 1 April 2027 and systematically imaging the unphotographed Ur III Girsu Meluhha-village bullae and sealed tablets (`P114609`, `P116633`, `P206053`, `P235705`, `P374962`, `P453801`, `P492732`) provide the most direct empirical targets to search for such an object-level bridge.
