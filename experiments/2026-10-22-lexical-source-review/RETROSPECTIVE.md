# Retrospective

We began with the input audit's 24 exact queries and its three known Winslow failures. Before reading new sample outcomes, we froze forty source-review rows by deterministic ranking within six strata. The frozen record pins the full derived dictionary, protocol, selector and exact selection. We then inspected original Tamil Lexicon and Winslow HTML blocks and the held proposal pages. The source decisions and later guard are post-outcome judgments; the frozen component is sample selection.

The first packet builder located Winslow blocks by a headword substring. That returned extra nearby entries, especially for short forms. We preserved that packet and wrote a second version that parses each original block and requires its headword, gloss and parent to match the selected row. Every one of the 43 rows then resolved to one original block. Tamil Lexicon selections used stable identifiers from the entire existing record and resolved all 56 exact candidates to original HTML.

The first dictionary match often had the wrong role. Reading the homographs supplied explicit pole, cattle, fish, star and ornament selections. The numeral compounds supplied direct evidence relating aṟu to āṟu, eḻu to ēḻu and iru to iraṇṭu in specific contexts. We recorded those relations without collapsing written forms. The source pages also revealed two errors in the proposal extraction: āvu from Kannada and Telugu and tōḷ from Toda had entered the Tamil alternatives. We inspected the corresponding PDF pages visually to confirm the language labels.

The Winslow audit exposed both a boundary error and missing metadata. Aintaru had been cut into aint plus aru, whereas the dictionary analysis identifies taru trees. Two rejected compounds have boundaries printed in the Tamil Lexicon. Several other rejected entries are grammatical derivatives or phrases, so a rejected spelling alone does not establish a missing lexical compound. Nine rejected rows remain unresolved. Five subentries had marked parents but no origin flag in their derived row; a parent mark needs its own field because it does not settle the history of the complete compound.

The known jamīṉ failure came from a general grammatical-header omission. The source describes ja as a letter using a sentence beginning “is a letter,” which the old recognizer missed. The new wrapper handles that construction and keeps origin metadata attached to the source parent. It deliberately leaves missing increment forms for a general morphological review. We did not substitute selected words into a result table or recompute the rebus comparison.

Some terminal printing initially failed under Windows' default legacy encoding, and a broad source search encountered an unrelated locked dependency file. We narrowed the search to held source directories and used UTF-8 for Tamil diagnostics. A diagnostic test exposed resource warnings in the frozen selector's convenience reader; the verification code uses a context-managed reader without changing the frozen selector. Thirteen focused tests and the synthetic shots guard pass. The second verification record adds deterministic replay of all sample selections; the first record remains preserved.

All judgments are provisional AI source review. A Dravidianist still needs to assess the unresolved member identities, phrase eligibility, historical phonology and textual dates. The project now has source-cited corrections for the reviewed inputs and a reproducible sample exposing concrete extraction failures. A complete revised extraction tested against an independently adjudicated sample is the next test of whether those repairs support a reliable comparative evaluation.
