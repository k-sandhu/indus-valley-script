# Harappa seal contexts require separate deposit and style dates

We recovered nine source-qualified seal records from Kenoyer and Meadow's *Inscribed Objects from Harappa Excavations 1986–2007*. Three carry explicit excavation keys, and six supply individual context examples. The new ledger closes a source-to-record gap: it preserves the exact basis of each temporal assertion, including a contradictory lot and two dates reassigned by seal style. It adds no independently dated confirmation seal.

The registered block contains H-1657, H-2587 and H-2588. On author-manuscript page 3, footnote 4 gives H-1657 as H99-4064/8796-1 and explains that the excavation unit links the object to field context. This passage supplies an identity key, without an individual period or deposit. H-2588 appears in the page 10 Figure 2 caption as H98-3493 8314-0001. Page 12 says that it and H-1537 came from mixed contexts and were reassigned from Period 2 to Period 3C through carving technique and glaze. Those assignments cannot provide the independent clock required for a test of changes in seal traits.

H-2587 has an unresolved contradiction within the same primary source. Page 9's final paragraph gives H96/7158-01; page 10's Figure 2 caption gives H96/7458-0001. Both statements identify the circular button seal. We preserve both assertions and leave its lot blank in the normalized record. The missing registration number is not an error: the authors explain on page 3 that year, unit and item can uniquely identify an unregistered recorded object.

## Which seals could enter a later cohort

The six context examples were added through a protocol amendment after the first source inspection. They are source-selected cases, with no selection by legend, sign or predicted outcome. [The records](../../data/raw/2026-10-24-harappa-context-extension/context-records-v1.json) retain the primary-source pages and the qualification beside every date.

| Object | Published recovery evidence | Later disposition |
|---|---|---|
| H-1688 | Mound E street deposits, Period 3A, ca. 2600 BCE, p. 10 | Early contextual candidate; broken seal, lot and sample link absent |
| H-1689 | Western Mound E deposit containing Early Harappan and Harappan pottery, p. 10 | Boundary uncertain; proposed late 3A or early 3B also uses carving style |
| H-1682 | Period 3B context in a trench on eastern Mound E, p. 10 | Early contextual candidate; obtain the lot and phase argument |
| H-1683 | Northeastern Mound E slope, unfinished seal, p. 10 | No independent period; unsuitable for completed-legend comparison |
| H-1692 | Dustbin outside a Period 3B house at northeastern Mound E, p. 11 | Early contextual candidate; establish dustbin relation to the house |
| H-1537 | Mixed context; former Period 2 attribution revised to 3C, pp. 4, 12 | Style-based date; exclude from strict independent clock |

The three early contextual candidates remain a queue, not an eligible analytical cohort. None has a directly linked radiocarbon sample or a manufacture date in the inspected source. Page 2's chronology gives 3A as ca. 2600–2450 BCE, 3B as ca. 2450–2200 BCE and 3C as ca. 2200–1900 BCE, based on the site's radiocarbon and comparative sequence. A phase interval applied to a deposit does not date the carving event.

## Source coverage and exposure

We extracted author-manuscript pages 1–6 and 9–15. We opened no figures, corpus text rows or reserved confirmation files. The prose on pages 10–11 describes some graphic forms and counts; those descriptions count as exposure. H-1688, H-1689 and H-1692 must therefore carry this exposure history into any later design. The present source work cannot certify their signs as unseen.

The 2010 author manuscript is a held primary synthesis by the excavators. Agreement between its pages does not add an independent witness. Its registration explanation and revised chronology are distinct assertions from one evidence lineage. Existing source layers were consulted through their coverage manifests, without opening object observations or sign payloads. We make no claim that these values were absent from every prior project ledger and no global coverage increase.

A search for H99-4064 located the 2009 chapter indexed as `Kenoyer2009 Indus Seals_0.pdf`, with a search snippet assigning it to late Period 3C (2000–1900 BCE). Ordinary HTTPS returned 403; the web reader could not open the 28,208,521-byte PDF. A filename search of held research sources found no matching 2009 chapter. We retain the snippet as an unresolved lead and do not promote its date into the record. [Access and source manifest](source-manifest.json).

[Author checks](validation-v1.json) pass uniqueness, exact-key counts, retained lot conflict, absent manufacture dates and sample links, and exclusion of text fields. These checks validate the ledger's structure. They do not independently verify the archaeology. [Versioned summary](summary-v1.json).

The boldest supported reading is that Harappa's reported periods combine several kinds of evidence, and their independence must be established object by object before testing script change. The decisive next evidence is the field lot and stratigraphic relation for the three early candidates, paired with independently contextualized Period 3C seals from Mound E or AB. Only after those records are qualified should a joint frozen design score the existing predictions about closing stock, interior length and signs 806/817.
