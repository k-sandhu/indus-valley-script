# Original Gonur tokens and Altyn Depe impressed object

The original Gonur figure is recovered. Sarianidi reports a pile of 26 clay tokens near the palace's central northern entrance, while Fig. 41 contains 23 separate photographed representations and 23 schematic drawings. Two source-informed model reads agree on these panel counts. The figure gives a group-level link between tokens and their marks, without individual object identifiers. The original Altyn Depe Plate 94.2 is also recovered: three linked drawings depict one terracotta object from Excavation 5, horizon 11, room 18, captioned “imprint of a seal (?)”.

## Acquisition and reading

The [preread protocol](PROTOCOL.md) was saved before inspecting the recovered figures. The earlier Bonora et al. account had already supplied the reported 26-token total and the proposed Altyn Depe interpretation. The readings are therefore source-informed. The root agent independently counted the same native Gonur page after the first count. That checks the separation of pictured representations; it supplies no independent excavation witness or human epigraphic assessment.

The Margiana expedition's [official bibliography](https://margiana.su/publikaczii/rabotyi-v.i.-sarianidi/?page=3) links Victor Sarianidi's *Margiana and Protozoroastrism* (Athens: Kapon Editions, 1998, ISBN 9607254619) to a [public PDF share](https://cloud.mail.ru/public/YRbv/maWz94TRz). Its public page exposed the ordinary download route. The acquired file contains 191 PDF pages. The Institute for the History of Material Culture RAS [hosts the 2008 Altyn Depe monograph](https://www.old.archeo.ru/izdaniya-1/vagnejshije-izdanija/pdf/Altyn_technical_2008.pdf/view), with a [direct download](https://www.old.archeo.ru/izdaniya-1/vagnejshije-izdanija/pdf/Altyn_technical_2008.pdf/at_download/file) of 373 PDF pages. The book's English contents identifies Kircho's chapter on manufacturing and typology of clay objects, the chapter referenced as Kircho 2008b by Bonora et al.

The expedition also hosts the [2005 palace volume](https://margiana.su/publication/book/Gonur-Depe_City_of_Kings_and_Gods_2005.pdf). Its bibliography link initially resolved to a nested path returning 404; resolving the same relative path against the domain root recovered the 165 PDF spreads. All three source files are pinned in the [source manifest](source-manifest.json). Downloaded sources, OCR and images remain local-only. The 1998 book carries an all-rights-reserved notice; the other sources carry copyright notices without a verified open license.

## Gonur: the pile and the illustrated sample

Sarianidi's description on p. 89 says the tokens were piled together in one chamber by the palace's central northern entrance. He describes clay cones or cakes bearing scratched marks and proposes that they may have been held in a small bag. His administrative interpretation is that counters could have helped check food and cattle entering the citadel. The passage provides neither an individual inventory nor a numbered room, floor, find layer, excavation year or token-associated date. Adjacent architectural passages discuss named rooms and multiple palace phases; those identifiers are not attached to the token find.

The [photograph panel](../../../data/raw/2026-10-02-source-recovery/oxus/gonur-fig41-photo-panel.png) has 23 isolated representations. Most outlines are rounded or oval; several are tapered or irregular. The [drawing panel](../../../data/raw/2026-10-02-source-recovery/oxus/gonur-fig41-drawing-panel.png) has 23 isolated schematic representations. Many contain crossed straight lines forming grids, while some contain curves or rounded outlines. The caption links the panels as tokens and marks on them. It supplies no per-object numbering, and the panels have different row layouts. Counts of photographed representations, drawing representations, distinct physical tokens and distinct sign types are therefore separate fields in the [observations](observations.json).

Each panel contains three fewer representations than the reported hoard total. The publication leaves selection and repeated-view rules unspecified. Adding both panel counts would give 46 representations, with no corresponding physical-object total. This matters for a future comparison of mark frequencies: its denominator must follow a documented object inventory or an explicitly bounded picture sample. The source now supplies a real photograph/drawing/group connection, while a quantitative coding dataset still needs specimen identifiers and all views of each token.

The chronology chapter lists a Gonur I (Palace) sample, Beta 33563, with a measurement of 3630 ± 60 and a tabulated 1790 B.C. value (p. 77 / PDF p. 76). No link to the token pile is given. The historical table is preserved as a locator, with no recalibration or transfer of its date to these tokens.

The closest illustrated sealing passage inspected in the 1998 book is Fig. 28 and its text on p. 65 / PDF p. 64. It identifies bullae and ceramic boxes from household rooms of the Gonur temenos. Positions 1–5 and 7 are bullae; position 6 is a modern impression from a cylinder seal in a Togolok-1 tomb. The published contexts differ from the palace-entrance pile. In the later 2005 book, a search of all 165 spreads using extracted text and normalized line-break hyphens found no matching token-hoard description with the recorded English and Russian terms. That search bounds the additional check; it is not a complete visual collation of the later volume.

## Altyn Depe: three views and a secure room locator

Plate 94 groups items 1–17 as terracotta objects from Excavation 5, horizon 11, room 18. Item 2 has three views joined by short dashes. The [recovered crop](../../../data/raw/2026-10-02-source-recovery/oxus/kircho-plate94-2-crop.png) shows an irregular rounded marked face, a tapering profile that is thicker toward its lower end, and a largely plain opposite face. Two main horizontal bands contain narrow elongated impressions, and partial impressions reach the top edge. Where strokes meet or reach the margin, the boundaries of individual cavities are ambiguous. The observation records two main bands and leaves the individual impression count unresolved.

The source provides a line drawing and the tentative classification “imprint of a seal (?)”. An object photograph and a high-detail surface record are the useful next witnesses for comparing independent impressions, overlapping seal contacts and impressions of woven material. The recovered illustration establishes the target and its view relationships. Its marks supply no checked quantity or arithmetic operation.

The monograph attributes Excavation 5 horizons 13–9 to the Late Eneolithic and horizons 11–9 to later phases of the Geoksyur-type complex (p. 48 / PDF p. 49). Its broad regional Namazga III range is 3200/3100–2800/2700 BCE (p. 6). The room description distinguishes multiple floors and fill/construction deposits (pp. 34–36); item 2's caption gives no matching subdeposit. Items 3 and 6 on the same plate are called figurines-dibs. Those room-level associations are retained at the level supplied by the caption.

A separate passage enumerates ten clay bulla fragments with seal impressions from Excavation 5, horizons 10–8 (p. 104 / PDF p. 105). Its footnotes place most near the horizon 9 granary and describe one as reused in a horizon 8 kiln. This later group provides a documented sealing comparison with cord, cloth and container-surface contacts. It has its own stratigraphic scope, distinct from Plate 94.2 in horizon 11.

## What this recovery adds

The new result is a checked source packet: the Gonur pile's narrative total can now be compared with its two original illustration panels, the Altyn Depe object has an exact original classification and context, and the Gonur elephant seal has an original-publication object/impression/context join. The administrative and numerical proposals were already in the literature. The next test should recover the Gonur token inventory and object photographs with reverse views, then connect each specimen to a mark drawing and a defined deposit. For Altyn Depe, an original object photograph or measured surface image would allow reproducible impression counts and contact geometry. These are specific missing observations that could support a future quantity hypothesis.
