# New source coverage for the Harappa and Lothal tests

The source recovery adds 19 newly consulted publication panels for the eight fixed Lothal objects. Fifteen come from the original CISI1 catalogue and four are colour panels in Frenez's publications. The catalogue supplies 14 distinct labelled object-face codes. L193 has two panels of its A face; the colour panels revisit L190. These are views of eight existing objects. No new individually registered photograph of the four Harappa targets was acquired.

The most useful additions are L190's labelled reverse and side photographs, L163 and L168's upper-side photographs, and each Lothal object's museum registration and photograph archive key. L190's additional photographs still leave the contact topology unresolved. The branch therefore preserves a specific acquisition packet for the next physical observation.

CISI1 printed XXIV, PDF 28, defines A as obverse, B as reverse, C as upper side, D as right side and F as left side for ordinary objects. Numbered impressions and repeated photographs stay separate in the [observation table](observations-v2.json). Each crop records its physical-photo source, integer bounds and native image hash.

L190 D follows a narrow elongated recess between projecting margins. F exposes parallel oblique relief, while B adds a reverse view. Neither reader could trace two adjoining contact planes through one continuous junction or follow a single cylindrical section. The [second-model reading](second-reading.json) used the same native D and F crops and knew the question. L190 consequently remains unresolved under the earlier contact rule. L206 remains the one resolved curved-contact example in the [prior test](../../2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/lothal-contact/REPORT.md).

The G02 fronts expose additional cut zones. At least three separated curved depressions are visible on the selected L161, L162 and L163 upper margins; at least two are visible on L168. The narrow C views locate the adjoining upper surfaces of L163 and L168. Coarse pixels, damaged margins and hidden faces leave complete object totals unavailable for all eight targets. These views identify surfaces for a standardized recording series; their lower counts are saved by selected zone.

Frenez's [*Private Person or Public Persona?*](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/private-person.pdf), Figure 6, PDF 14 / printed 173, provides a labelled L190 colour front. Its original montage is 1949 × 2064 pixels. The view makes the three stamp regions, intervening ridges and split lower projection easier to follow. Frenez's [*Mirrored signs*](https://zenodo.org/records/5513821), Figure 9, PDF 13 / printed 33, adds three small labelled L190 colour views. That complete montage is only 481 × 681 pixels. The following prose calls it Figure 8, so both coordinates are retained. Archive photographs may recur across these publications.

## Object and archive locators

CISI1 printed 373, PDF 409, supplies all eight target rows. The key on printed 365–366, PDF 401–402, identifies their LTH SRG registrations with the ASI Lothal Site Museum at Saragwala. It also supplies Finnish Concordance keys, HU photograph numbers or ASI photograph sources. The detailed eight-row transcription remains local; its hash and route are in [record-locators.json](record-locators.json).

Frenez's university-hosted CV, PDF 2 / printed 2, records documentation of Lothal sealings at ASI Lothal in January 2005 and the National Museum in New Delhi in February 2006. The [original 2024 article](https://doi.org/10.1080/02666030.2024.2401206) names L190, L206 and L163 contact photographs in Figure 9 on printed 225 and identifies the corresponding research licences in its acknowledgements. The author-deposit text was readable; its PDF and figure pixels were not acquired. Its caption's cage, door-peg and wooden-box assignments remain published interpretations in the manifest.

For Harappa, [Kenoyer and Meadow's 2010 introduction](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/KenoyerMeadow%202010%20Inscribed%20Objects%20from%20Harappa.pdf), standalone PDF 3 / displayed page 3, footnote 4, places registration-number objects in the Harappa Museum reserve collection, subject to loans. It explains how the excavation unit links each object to its field record. All four targets and the four comparison objects have these registrations. The request therefore names the 1995, 1996 or 1997 registration register and specific field unit, while asking for current location confirmation.

The new 1997 excavation chapter leads to H95-2614/6560-02, Figure 10.21, PDF 21 / printed 159. It is adjacent to the requested H95-2613/6560-01 and contributes no target photograph. CISI3.1 remains the specific missing plate/basic-data witness for the modern Harappa catalogue joins.

## Acquisition record and next test

The [alternative CISI1 scan](https://rarebooksocietyofindia.org/book_archive/CorpusVol1.pdf) has 431 pages rather than the IGNCA file's 862. All seven checked target/data page images have identical embedded bytes. Different PDF hashes therefore add no detail or independent image reading. Five PDFs were acquired through ordinary public requests. Failed publisher, author-deposit, catalogue and institutional routes remain in the access log.

The unsent [observation-request packet record](request-packet.json) names the Harappa exceptions, same-production controls, Lothal museum registrations, photograph archives and required views. For Harappa, adjoining-face views would test whether the count exception occupies a complete face and whether the C-form variant belongs to the same carving process. For Lothal, a calibrated contact junction on L190 or L193 would add the second resolved G01 object; complete upper, rear and lateral views of G02 would permit comparable whole-surface cut counts. A quantity interpretation would additionally need an independently measured referent that predicts those counts.

The [passing verification](verification-v2.json) records 436 passed checks covering source bytes, registrations, crop pixels, target membership, native dimensions and panel accounting. The [first verification](verification.json) preserves two links checked before their output file existed. Third-party witnesses and the detailed source table stay local. The branch contributes registered source coverage and a usable acquisition design, with the earlier numerical endpoints preserved.
