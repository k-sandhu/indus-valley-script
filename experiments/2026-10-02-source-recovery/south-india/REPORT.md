# South Indian numerical examples with source records

The original Alagankulam excavation report joins a proposed numeral to a trench, depth, stratum and drawing. Mahadevan's original Tondur chapter joins a reported numeral to a named cave with three beds. The 2026 catalogue also supplies a registered sherd with letters and a separate graphic. These recoveries give us specific objects to examine when testing how numerical marks relate to archaeological quantities.

## Alagankulam

The government report *Alagankulam: An Ancient Roman Port City of Tamil Nadu* (2005, publication 458) describes a probable value of 408 on printed page 42. Its inscription table on page 44 identifies serial 2 as rouletted ware from AGM-2, locus `0’-I’`, depth 1.40 m, stratum 2. Drawing 2 on page 48 depicts the rim sherd and its three incised graphics. The table gives the interpretation `8/100/4=408`. We visually checked the table and drawing against the acquired original scan. [Government source](https://tamildigitallibrary.in/assets/docs/uploads/primary_files/book/TVA_BOK_0010617_Alagankulam.pdf), [observations](observations.json).

The excavation report supplies the inscription's context but no measured contents, capacity or counted deposit corresponding to 408. Its general chronology and later site-wide radiocarbon dates remain separate from this individual sherd. A useful next observation would be its museum registration, modern photograph and any original field record identifying what the number counted.

The same report describes a four-sided object fashioned from a rouletted-ware rim at AGM-3/A1, depth 0.45 m. Three sides bear graphics and one is plain. The authors consider a die or weight and propose values 1, 2 and 3 if the die identification is confirmed. Plate 15 shows two sides. We retain this object as a candidate operation control, with its function and values attached to the authors' hypothesis. [Government source, printed pp. 15, 62, 118](https://tamildigitallibrary.in/assets/docs/uploads/primary_files/book/TVA_BOK_0010617_Alagankulam.pdf).

## Tondur

We recovered the original scan of Iravatham Mahadevan's *Recent Discoveries of Jaina Cave Inscriptions in Tamilnadu*, in *Rishabh Saurabh*, printed pages 116–135 including the reading list. The Tondur section names Panchanampadi cave, reports three rock beds there and reads a terminal numeral as three horizontal strokes. The inscription describes beds made by Moci. Two other caves on the hill have seven and two beds; they are separate groups. Mahadevan dates the inscription to roughly the third–fourth centuries CE on palaeographic grounds. [Original book scan](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.428160), [authored text mirror](https://www.jainsamaj.org/content.php?url=DiscoveriesCaveInscriptionsInTamilnadu).

The count, lexical referent and numeral agree in this published account. Original Plate 8 is now locally available, although its dark reproduction does not permit a fresh stroke count. Plate 7 shows the cave's relief rather than all three beds. This provides a source-reported comparison within Tamil-Brahmi. A new independent check needs a readable inscription image and a documented census of the named cave's beds.

## Registered writing and graffiti

The 2026 volume II table, printed page 1258, and Appendix Plate 80, page 1356, identify **Alagankulam_044**. Its labelled photograph shows a letter sequence and a separate complex graphic at the right. The catalogue reading ends in `#`; the table gives I-II, depth 380 and rouletted ware. The table header leaves the depth unit unspecified. This is a direct specimen join between the catalogue record and a shared surface. Its separate graffiti registration and numerical referent remain to be recovered. [Catalogue acquisition](../../2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/south-india/volume2-acquisition.json), [source coordinates](observations.json).

**Alagankulam_004**, table serial 910 and Appendix Plate 77, supplies a photographed broken sherd with four visible long strokes, I-II, depth 205 and RW. The table calls its reading “Graffiti?” and the plate calls it undeciphered. Its context makes it a practical comparison target for stroke arrays; the fragment gives no complete inscription or measured quantity.

Two cross-edition matches are candidates. The old serial 1 and Alagankulam_048 share locus and ware, with depths 4.10 and 410; old serial 4 and Alagankulam_010 share locus, ware, a ka reading and a cross-shaped graphic, with depths 1.45 and 145. We preserve the original readings and unconverted depth fields. Exact museum or field-register concordances would settle these identities. The 408 sherd's modern catalogue identity remains unresolved.
