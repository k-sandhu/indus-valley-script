# Susa source audit

The recovered 1911 catalogue changes the evidence behind the close duck-weight comparison. It records AS 13820 and AS 13821 as unmarked, gives AS 13820 a mass of 2769 g, and identifies both materials as veined marble. Ascalone 2022 joins those numbers to SB 9340 and SB 9342, describes six and five strokes, gives SB 9340 a mass of 3067 g, and calls both objects limestone. The mass, mark and material records need reconciliation before the modern duck group can carry a historical numerical interpretation.

We also recovered a clear museum photograph of the four-mark weight, its original 1911 drawing, and the publication that dates the deposition of an imported Indus weight to the twelfth century BCE. These sources supply concrete identity and context checks for the next analysis.

## The four round marks

The [Louvre SB 13677 record](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010186254) joins it to AS 2615. Its 1500 × 1197 pixel photograph shows four cup-like depressions in one row. Soutzo's p. 9 / Fig. 8 depicts the same four round marks. The publication establishes their presence by 1911; the creation date remains unprovided.

Soutzo interpreted the object as four decashekel units using his 345 g measurement. He placed AS 13855, a veined-marble ovoid with four strokes and a mass of 335 g, under the same value. AS 13855 supplies a historical comparison between round and stroke marks. A literal search of the acquired Ascalone 2022 text did not locate that number. Its modern inventory and dated context are recovery targets. This interpretation predates Ascalone's later proposed Harappan-related equivalence. Both interpretations should be evaluated against independently dated objects and readable unit records.

## Imported weight SB 17774

The [Louvre record](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010188675) identifies SB 17774 / AS 5724, assigns creation to 2400–2100 BCE, and records discovery in 1904 at the Inshushinak temple funerary deposit. Kenoyer's entry 292b in the Met catalogue explicitly places its deposition in a prince's funerary repository of the twelfth century BCE. The weight was therefore retained for centuries after its production period. [Met catalogue, pp. 401–402](https://www.metmuseum.org/met-publications/art-of-the-first-cities-the-third-millennium-bc-from-the-mediterranean-to-the-indus).

The Met p. 401 heading calls the find a foundation deposit, while p. 402 and the Louvre record use funerary wording. The excavation inventory is needed to resolve that context label. The museum and Met record a 2.8 cm dimension and a 1.9 cm dimension; Ascalone gives 2.40 × 2.39 × 1.95 cm. We retain the measurements with their source axes. One museum view supplies no identified script inscription on its visible faces. This object cannot date the use of nearby numerical marks solely through its own production date.

## Cross-marked group and remaining targets

Ascalone's SB 13177 entry cites Soutzo p. 11 no. 12295. The original page gives no. 12995, a 4.40 g blackish-schist ovoid with a cross, illustrated in Fig. 12. Its half-shekel value is Soutzo's interpretation. The original-to-modern link stays provisional until an accession concordance resolves the differing number. SB 13552, SB 13561, SB 13564 and SB 15535 still need independent museum dates, marked-face photographs and find context.

All eleven original targets and the additional AS 13855 control appear in [observations.json](observations.json); [observations.csv](observations.csv) supplies a compact view. The earlier SB 13514 and SB 13747 accession collisions remain unresolved in the frozen census. No new mass fit was run.

## Access and contribution

Soutzo 1911 and the Met 2003 catalogue were acquired from public library and publisher sources. The exact Ascalone 2023 institutional record and author-upload page were located. The institutional download redirects to a login page, and the author preview remains unavailable. Full text was not acquired. The site's generated FAQ text was excluded from archaeological evidence.

The contribution of this run is a source audit: three measured discrepancies, conflicting duck-mark descriptions, a verified early four-mark drawing, a recoverable four-stroke comparison, an accession-locator mismatch and separation of an imported object's creation and deposition dates. It supplies a smaller set of archival and imaging tasks that can decide whether the earlier numerical candidates deserve further fitting.
