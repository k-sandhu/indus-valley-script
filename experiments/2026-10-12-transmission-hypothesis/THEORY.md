# How practical inheritance could become literary knowledge

12 October 2026. Exploratory theory. The mechanisms below are proposals to test against the new source comparisons. They do not assign an authenticated Harappan ancestry to a text.

The developed hypothesis is that a practice can survive through working communities and later acquire a place in a different institution of learning. Its operation, terminology, carrier relationships and explanation can therefore have different histories. Early Indian literature could contain inherited local knowledge, inherited Indo-Iranian poetic and ritual conventions, and new reasoning produced when specialists brought these repertoires together. The historical question concerns the route of a particular component and the formation of its later combination.

## The objects of transmission

Practical knowledge includes ways of finding materials, preparing them, performing an operation, judging its outcome and repairing a failure. An occupation name does not contain all that knowledge. Conversely, a practitioner can teach an operation while using words that differ from those used by earlier practitioners. The same useful operation can also be rediscovered. Distinguishing these histories needs observations of the choices made during work and evidence about the available alternative solutions.

An organizing relationship has further elements: who may participate, who supplies inputs, how work is scheduled, who judges performance and how obligations are maintained. Some relationships recur within a household or a local group. Others depend on distant supply, a patron's resources or coordination over a large area. Their requirements can change even if a recognizable product continues to be made.

A literary account selects and organizes what it represents. A hymn can recognize a ploughing operation, a specialist role or trained speech without preserving a complete manual or naming the practice's donor. A later prose tradition can make a coherent explanation from components whose histories were different. The transmission objects are consequently the operation, its organizing relationship, the words used to represent it and the explanation connecting it to the world. They should be coded separately.

The new fixed passage frame supplies a concrete incorporation example. RV 10.71.2 compares fashioning speech with sifting meal and connects speech with companion recognition. A practical process becomes a model for an intellectual activity. This is a directly represented association at the textual endpoint. Its earlier history remains to be compared. The [refinement record](coordinator/REFINEMENT-analogy.md) labels the development after reading that stanza.

This extends the earlier proposal about practical memory and changing attribution. The new question is where knowledge crosses from a working institution into a verbal teaching institution, and which parts change during that crossing.

## Survival through repeated work

Knowledge can remain available when people repeatedly use it and recruit new participants. Work supplies some checks: a material reacts, a tool wears, a crop grows or a vessel withstands use. The feedback can preserve useful capabilities while allowing changes in particular techniques. The October 11 persistence model made repair and learning joint in one expressly invented variant. Here that relationship becomes an empirical question about who learned during which actual operations.

A local practice can survive a change in settlement scale if participants retain its inputs, opportunities to work and means of learning. A practice can be lost despite surviving buildings or finished products if its replacement tools, resources or competent participants become unavailable. A surviving artifact is therefore evidence about an object; a production sequence with waste and tools can provide a better observation of capability. Imported or curated objects have different implications for local know-how.

The proposal predicts uneven retention. Repeated household or local work with available materials may provide many occasions for learning. A dependent specialist operation may require particular inputs or a network large enough to support it. Retention should be tested against these measured differences, rather than assumed from a hierarchy of simple and complex occupations. Specialist traditions can be portable, and households can depend strongly on exchange.

Material visibility complicates that test. A technique's absence from a recovered sample becomes evidence of loss only when the recovery process, sample size and preservation make the missing traces informative. Continuation of a settlement, continuation of a productive capability and continuation of a particular sequence remain different outcomes.

## Transfer during work and language change

A practice can pass between communities through co-work, household recruitment, apprenticeship, exchange or patronage. These channels require contact that concerns the relevant activity. Demographic mixture identifies an opportunity for social interaction at an appropriate scale; it does not show which activity was taught. The [population control](coordinator/observations-v2.json) records the sampled and modeled scope of that distinction.

The proposed relationship with language change is asymmetric. A recipient may learn a procedure and describe it in an already prestigious or widely used register. It may keep a donor word for one unfamiliar material while translating the remaining steps. It may also borrow a word without learning the practice. Word history and operational history can therefore constrain one another without being identical.

The hypothesis generates a comparison rather than a diagnosis from a single word. If a linked practice was adopted while its terminology changed, operational choices could align with the proposed donor while ordinary vocabulary aligns with the recipient's repertoire. If both the operations and specialized terminology align with an independently established comparative tradition, that alternative becomes stronger. If only a generic purpose aligns, the comparison remains weak. A particular donor society requires geographical, chronological and feature-specific evidence beyond the fact of contact.

This mechanism could explain why practical local contributions are unevenly visible in later Sanskrit. It also predicts cases where no distinctive local contribution can be recovered. The comparison must allow that outcome.

## Where practical knowledge enters organized speech

Working communities and verbal specialists can interact around provisioning, commissioning, teaching and public performance. A patron can support people who perform different kinds of work; a teacher can compare their tasks; a poet can use technical actions to express relationships. These interactions provide possible settings in which knowledge about materials and cooperation becomes part of a learned repertoire.

The proposed contribution of such interaction is conceptual organization. Participants already know that one task supplies another, that inputs must be available at the right time and that roles depend on other roles. A teaching tradition can articulate those relations, compare them across activities and connect them to explanations of social or cosmic order. The resulting intellectual work belongs partly to that tradition's own setting. Its maturity can reflect a long inherited repertoire and active new combination at once.

The sifting image suggests one specific form of this process: material actions can provide models for selecting, refining or coordinating knowledge. Its historical study should code the practical action, the conceptual use and the verbal form separately. A familiar operation and a distinctive inherited poetic construction can contribute to the same image. Neither component's history determines the whole combination by itself.

This does not require a surviving centralized Harappan school. The institutional claim to test is more specific: a working relationship supplies knowledge to a later teaching or performance relationship. Evidence of each endpoint would still leave the intervening transfer to be established. An early textual description of specialist learning is positive evidence for organized knowledge in that textual setting, even when its remoter ancestry is unresolved.

The strongest rival for poetic and ritual organization is inherited Indo-Iranian or Indo-European learning. Exact verbal, role and patronage comparisons can support that explanation under comparative philological premises. It can coexist with local knowledge acquired in South Asia. A shared poetic institution and a particular agricultural operation need separate tests.

## Distinguishing transmission from constrained reinvention

A linked sequence can carry more information than a finished object's broad appearance, but its components can be dependent. Material properties, tool geometry and the purpose of the task may force several choices to occur together. Counting those choices as independent matches would exaggerate the evidence. [Diachenko, Rivers and Sobkowiak-Tabaka](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10816-023-09623-8) discuss connected-component constraints; this study uses that methodological caution without adopting their quantitative convergence model.

[Shennan's chapter](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10107913/) distinguishes learned production attributes from assemblage patterns shaped by use, environment and formation processes. It provides a direct predecessor for the comparison proposed here. Functional adaptation and transmission can both contribute to a useful practice. A functional trait should not be classified as independent invention before assessing its history.

A better test identifies choices for which other workable solutions existed, records their co-occurrence in dated production contexts, and compares controls matched for material and task. Distinctive ways of correcting a failure or making a replaceable tool may be especially useful observations, provided their alternatives and preservation are assessed. A new technical measurement should be validated on assemblages with known histories before it is applied to disputed ancient routes.

## Predictions and rejection conditions

The selective-retention hypothesis predicts capability persisting in some working contexts despite changes in settlement size or organization. It is weakened for a proposed case when apparent continuation consists entirely of stored or imported objects, or when necessary production inputs and operations cease without a documented replacement route. Recovery gaps alone leave it unresolved.

The recoding hypothesis predicts a mismatch between some operational and lexical histories. It is weakened for a case where the proposed donor's distinctive operations are absent, the recipient already had the same sequence under an independently supported inherited route, or the candidate word never accompanies the proposed operation. A terminology mismatch by itself neither supports nor rejects an older practice.

The integration hypothesis predicts observable teaching, performance or patronage settings that relate different kinds of knowledge. It gains direct support for a textual stage where a later account demonstrably reorganizes a received repertoire. It remains a hypothesis for a Harappan-to-literary transition until feature-specific evidence joins the relevant working and teaching settings.

These tests permit partial results. A craft may have an intermediate production history without a recognizable literary endpoint. A poetic institution may have a well-supported comparative ancestry without identifying every technical contribution to its hymns. The developed explanation of early literature's maturity is therefore a component history of survival, contact and intellectual combination, with the unresolved transitions retained for new collection.
