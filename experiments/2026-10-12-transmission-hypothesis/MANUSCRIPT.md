# Practical inheritance and the formation of early Indian literary knowledge

12 October 2026. Exploratory historical study with source-based rival comparisons and independent review.

## Argument

Some knowledge could outlive the Indus cities because people continued to work, obtain materials and learn in changing communities. Later verbal specialists could represent that knowledge, combine it with inherited poetic and ritual conventions, and use practical operations to think about speech, competence and social relations. This gives a component explanation of early Indian literature's maturity: its operations, organizing relationships, terminology and explanations can have different histories.

The present study makes that explanation more specific. Intermediate working-stage objects and crop specimens give practical transmission concrete endpoints to investigate. A complete Rigvedic passage frame depicts differentiated work and competent collective speech. Comparative Iranian evidence supplies an independently motivated account for part of the professional verbal repertoire. One stanza directly uses sifting meal to describe shaping speech. Later non-Vedic classification supplies another form of intellectual organization. These observations support different steps in the proposed explanation; a particular Harappan-to-literary route remains untraced.

The stronger positive finding concerns incorporation. RV 10.71.2 represents a practical filtering action as a model for fashioning speech. Practical experience can be a conceptual resource within a sophisticated poem, even when the source of that experience is uncertain. Incorporation into a text and descent from a named civilization are separate historical claims.

## Sources and method

The October 11 continuity study proposed selective inheritance and intellectual recombination, with no diagnostic material-to-textual join in its selected evidence. The persistence study added the distinction between current service and replacement expertise, including an explicitly invented shared repair/learning variant. This continuation uses new material loci and a new complete-hymn frame. It does not repeat the earlier rice reassignment, eligibility filter or retention models.

The material frame covers hard-stone manufacture, ceramic operations and crop-processing or ecological practices. Eight source records provide 26 observations at declared reading scopes, including excavation reports, an author-hosted collection description and a 2026 archaeobotanical study. The textual frame covers the complete RV 9.112, 10.71 and 10.101, totaling 27 stanzas, with bounded Iranian, Greek and Pali controls. Its 36 passage-observation records include nine control summaries; seven separately recorded lexical-source observations bring its authored total to 43. The independent reviewer reads six scoped primary works in seven target records, with one unavailable target retained. Three coordinator controls provide population-history and archaeological-method context. These frames overlap; their record totals are not a combined independent sample.

Five routes are tested component by component: local post-Harappan transmission; inherited Indo-Iranian or Indo-European repertoires; wider regional contact; independent reconstruction under recurring practical constraints; and later literary or institutional organization. They can combine in one component. A comparison that challenges a uniqueness claim need not reject a possible local contribution.

The unit is a named operation or relationship. Excavation observation, source-author interpretation, edited textual depiction and comparative reconstruction retain separate status. Manufacture and deposition, archaeological phase and a directly dated specimen, composition and collection, physical witness and narrated origin also retain separate dates. Unknown intermediates are recorded as unknown. The [seven comparison records](coordinator/comparisons-v1.json) state predictions, observations, dependencies, missing discriminators and proposed transitions.

The program checks source IDs, schema and declared-record completeness. Historical relevance and rival discrimination are manually assessed and independently reviewed. The reviewer demonstrated that an observation about population history, deliberately mislabeled as a teaching edge, can pass the validator. That fixture defines the software's boundary; it is excluded from the historical sample. [Audit amendment](coordinator/AUDIT-AMENDMENT.md).

## Material knowledge that could continue

### Hard-stone work and replacement knowledge

Bhagwanpura supplies a stronger intermediate candidate than completed ornaments alone. The [excavation report](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/80282.pdf) catalogues BPR-36 as a roughly faceted unfinished black-jasper bead in late IB, on printed page 120. BPR-208 is a single mid-IB accession described as quartz on page 118 and banded agate on page 122. Its material conflict remains unresolved. Page 30 reports unfinished beads and raw material and infers bead making. The objects represent working stages; associated waste, tools and the precise manufacture history would strengthen the local-production interpretation. [MO12–MO14](material/observations.json).

The Harappa bead-pot candidate requires a different decision. The [collection description](https://www.harappa.com/blog/single-bead-pot-collection) states that its contents belong to several occupation phases. An unfinished tubular-drilled bead shows an operation on an object, but deposition in that pot cannot independently date the operation or locate its workshop. Curated older objects and traded roughouts remain alternatives. The two captions describe the same context and do not replicate it. [MO01–MO05](material/observations.json).

Local practitioners, transferred craft specialists and imported working-stage objects can all contribute to these assemblages. A proposed teaching lineage should follow preparation, tools, drilling or shaping, correction and replacement in dated production contexts. The selected hymns describe artisans and material imagery but no bead-making sequence. The present material result is an intermediate working-stage candidate, rather than a bead-to-text ancestry. [TC02](coordinator/comparisons-v1.json).

### Ceramic sequences and local contact

The [Hulas report](https://asi.nic.in/pdf/EXCAVATIONS-AT-HULAS.pdf), printed page 72, describes striations, neck/body joining and trimming. These observations permit an operational reconstruction beyond vessel names. Firing details and precise succession remain unresolved. Bhagwanpura adds pottery dabbers, related forms and a scientific comparison of differently colored sherds. Bulk similarity does not independently identify geological source or a dated recipe succession. [MO06, MO14–MO19](material/observations.json).

Learning between neighboring makers is already a proposal in the Bhagwanpura report. Its page 30 society discussion suggests that some users learned particular terracotta and design practices from neighboring Late Harappans. This is a source-author transmission hypothesis, rather than an observed teaching event. The new contribution here is to identify what production and chronology evidence would discriminate that hypothesis from movement of vessels or makers and functionally constrained convergence. It is not a priority claim for the idea of ceramic transmission.

Ceramics could carry clay preparation, joining, finishing, firing judgments and correction routines. Such knowledge can be useful in smaller settlements. A particular lineage needs comparable operational observations in successive contexts. Sanghol's proposed later bead/terracotta factory concerns Kushana buildings and cannot bridge the earlier interval. [TC03](coordinator/comparisons-v1.json).

### Agricultural and ecological practice

The new [Jognakhera study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00334-026-01140-4) provides directly dated crop specimens. Its source-calibrated 95.4% ranges are 2471–2298 cal BCE for barley, 1880–1691 for Vigna and 1517–1428 for rice. The inspected supplementary date table does not supply each specimen's individual layer join. Its recovery and processing residues provide further endpoints, while rotation in the same fields and a migration route are author interpretations needing separate observations. [MO22–MO25](material/observations.json).

Hulas rice impressions also require a formation comparison. The report discusses the difficulty of identifying wild and cultivated rice from impressions and raises rice-bearing marsh clay in comparative Gujarat cases. Applying that alternative to Hulas is a prospective control, rather than a finding that its clay contained wild rice. Recovery and chronological differences within the report constrain a continuous local farming argument. Bhagwanpura grinding tools on a hut floor supply a working-place candidate, while unrecovered grains leave their specific use unresolved. [MO07–MO11, MO17](material/observations.json).

These observations motivate knowledge of crop handling, material preparation, storage and locally useful ecological relations. RV 10.101.3–7 represents ploughing, sowing and water-related fittings within a ritual hymn. Its later well-without-digging image and invocation of Soma belong to the same complete frame. A crop specimen, a grinding tool and that poetic sequence do not yet specify the same operation. [TC01](coordinator/comparisons-v1.json).

## Institutions represented in the early texts

RV 9.112 depicts workers seeking corresponding opportunities and a household image with a bard, a healer father and a mother using grinding stones. The complete hymn also compares animal and human desires through a Soma refrain. It supports a picture of differentiated work and shared resource concerns. It supplies neither a household census nor an apprenticeship or hereditary occupational rule. A Greek list of invited specialists in Odyssey 17.382–386 offers a component comparison; shared broad roles alone cannot establish the ancestry of an entire institutional package. [TC04](coordinator/comparisons-v1.json).

RV 10.71 is more directly concerned with knowledge. Hearing can coexist with failure to grasp speech; companionship, differentiated attainments, collective performance and assembly success recur. Its final stanza distributes recitation, melodic performance, speaking knowledge and measurement of sacrifice among participants. A peer-qualification mechanism is a testable interpretation of this picture: competence and affiliation could support the retention and development of specialized knowledge. The poem depicts those relations without providing a formal school's curriculum or an observed admission procedure. [TX-RV10.71.4–11](texts/OBSERVATIONS.json).

The inherited rival has specific support. Jamison and Brereton's introduction attributes poet–patron relationships and underlying poetic formulae to Indo-Iranian or Indo-European traditions. The independently read Old Avestan controls include addressed listeners, a ritual-specialist term and a translated song/support relationship. These are edited textual and comparative observations, with their own philological premises. They give part of the professional repertoire an available ancestral explanation beyond a necessary post-Harappan local origin. They leave the histories of particular agricultural or craft contributions open. [Review observations O-R01–O-R03 and O-R07](review/observations-v2.json); [Old Avestan teaching edition](https://www.lexicons.ru/old/a/avestan/_pdf/old-avestan-exercises.pdf).

The appropriate institutional candidates therefore differ by channel. Households and recurring working relationships could carry practical skills. Apprenticeship, co-work or the movement of practitioners could transfer them. Professional verbal and ritual traditions could preserve, evaluate and reorganize representations. The present evidence identifies some working and textual endpoints; actual carrier histories require activity-specific evidence. These institutions can persist or be rebuilt without retaining a city's architecture or every former office.

## Practical knowledge as a conceptual resource

RV 10.71.2 contains an observable incorporation relation. Its source-language comparison associates sifting meal with shaping speech and companion recognition. The practical action becomes a model for an intellectual activity. Griffith's older “corn-flour” wording means grain meal; it cannot identify maize or another particular crop. His language about creation also does not date the origin of human speech. [Exact stanza record](texts/OBSERVATIONS.json), `TX-RV10.71.2`.

This example sharpens the transmission hypothesis. An operation could influence a later text by supplying an analogy, organizing relation or judgment of competence, rather than by appearing as a complete manual. A received poetic construction, a locally familiar act and a composer's present reasoning could all contribute to the same stanza. The directly supported claim concerns the represented comparison. Whether a Harappan working tradition supplied it remains unresolved. A quern indicates grinding and cannot substitute for a sifting observation in that join. [TC06](coordinator/comparisons-v1.json).

The formulation is an openly post-reading refinement within the fixed poetic-learning family. The motivating verse is discovery evidence. A new comparison should freeze complete passage sets and code operation, conceptual use, verbal construction and historical attribution separately. Exact Iranian or other inherited image controls, dated material operations and documented recoding cases would help separate transmitted imagery from conceptual reconstruction. [Refinement](coordinator/REFINEMENT-analogy.md), [prospective tests](EMPIRICAL-TESTS.md).

The lexical control gives a related distinction. The selected *takṣ* forms and the historical dictionary include physical and mental fashioning, while the selected *bījam* means seed. The dictionary marks the latter's origin doubtful, and Witzel places it in a non-Indo-Aryan agricultural list without settling its donor at that locus. The seed comparison was an explicitly marked post-selection addition. Shared action vocabulary supports represented conceptual resources; proposed contact vocabulary leaves the donor and the practice's route unresolved. [Lexical-source observations](texts/LEXICAL-SOURCE-OBSERVATIONS.json), `TX-LEX-TAKSH` and `TX-LEX-BIJA`.

## Later organization and the histories of components

DN 1's selected major morality sections group prediction, ritual, calculation, treatment and other arts as livelihoods from which Gotama abstains while supported by donated food. This directly represents a developed normative classification at its own textual stage. It is not an occupational census or a demonstrated rewrite of a surviving Rigvedic passage. The arts' component histories and the grouping's history require separate study. [TC07](coordinator/comparisons-v1.json).

The canon-control sources provide another type of account. Witzel's [school and canon study](https://michaelwitzel.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/canon.pdf) distinguishes regional traditions, collection and later consolidation, with dates and mechanisms treated as historical reconstructions. Skjærvø's [role comparison](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kayanian-i/) argues that related poet/priest vocabulary underwent changes of evaluation in the Iranian tradition. The examples support separate histories of wording, role and explanation; they do not date every technical contribution to a collection event. [O-R05 and O-R07](review/observations-v2.json).

Population history can constrain opportunity at another scale. The inspected [Narasimhan study](https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/AAAS/Narasimhan_Formation_Science_2019_3166350.pdf) combines sampled ancient people and modeled populations, and its ancestry map explicitly leaves exact routes unresolved. A demographic relationship does not identify a taught recipe or literary transfer. Its contemporary group comparisons also retain selection and classification limits. The same captured PDF is used by two readers, rather than counted as two scientific works.

## Interpretation and next measurements

The developed theory is selective practical inheritance followed by component-specific transfer and conceptual organization. Repeated work can sustain skills; changing contacts can move practitioners or inputs; a verbal tradition can recode and combine what it encounters. Sophisticated literature can consequently preserve old verbal conventions, represent useful practices and reason about their relations in its own setting. The earliest surviving collection need not preserve a single source history for all these contributions.

The source-based tests give unequal results. Intermediate working-stage objects and plant-use dates strengthen particular material candidates. A curated collection, a conflicting accession description, uncertain recovery and unmatched operations weaken narrower proposed bridges. Inherited verbal and role comparisons supply a positive rival for parts of the professional repertoire. The sifting analogy and DN 1 classification directly show conceptual organization at their textual endpoints. None of the seven cases asserts a traced Harappan literary descent.

Further progress requires sharper observations. Reconstruct a distinctive operation with dated production, correction and replacement traces. Assess alternative workable techniques under matched materials. Identify recipient learning or a specific comparative verbal construction. Preserve genre and temporal axes while comparing later organization. The [test designs](EMPIRICAL-TESTS.md), [claim map](CLAIM-EVIDENCE.json) and [review response](REVIEW-RESPONSE.md) define those continuations. This study supplies source-qualified transitions to investigate and a concrete incorporation mechanism; archaeological ancestry remains a component-by-component question.
