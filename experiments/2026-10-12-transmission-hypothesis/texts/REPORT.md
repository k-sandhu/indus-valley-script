# Practical repertoires and their textual organization

12 October 2026. New passage comparison following the earlier continuity and persistence studies.

The selected Rigvedic passages portray specialized work, differing capacities among ritual peers and coordinated practical operations. They also show an explicit conceptual use of ordinary work: RV 10.71.2 compares fashioning speech with sifting meal. This supplies a concrete mechanism to develop. A recipient intellectual tradition can use a practical operation as a model for evaluating knowledge and organizing participation, while the operation, its terminology and its explanation follow different histories.

The new comparison does not identify a particular Harappan donor. Inherited poetic roles and the Soma/Haoma repertoire supply specific external explanations for components. The material sources provide narrower observations of tools, plant handling and production. Their present specificity does not match a complete plow, sowing or well-fitting sequence. The [six component comparisons](COMPARISONS.json) therefore retain several routes and distinguish what each observation can explain.

## The frame and what it measures

The protocol and [selection](selection-v1.json) were hashed before new acquisition and coding. The main frame contains the complete hymns RV 9.112, RV 10.71 and RV 10.101, totaling 27 stanzas. It targets occupations, speech/learning and agricultural coordination. The prior RV 2.1, RV 4.57, DN 27, flood and rice endpoints were not repeated.

Sanskrit comes from GRETIL's unaccented witnesses based on Aufrecht's 1877 edition, paired with Griffith's translations. The controls comprise Younger Avestan Yasna 9.1–8 and Vendidad 3.23–33, Greek Odyssey 17.382–386, selected independently reviewed Old Avestan loci, and Pali DN 1.1.21–27. Complete language/translation pairs remain in ignored local records. [SOURCES-v2.json](SOURCES-v2.json) gives ten source groups, their editions, actual reading scopes, access failures, transport caveats and rights. These groups include related editions and translations and do not count independent ancient witnesses.

The unit is what a stanza or paragraph depicts. A poetic household, an ideal ritual participant and a normative list are different observations from a surveyed population or excavated workplace. Composition, collection/redaction, narrated setting, historical manuscripts and modern witnesses remain separate. Jamison and Brereton describe book IX as an anthology containing earlier and younger compositions, and book X as a later major addition. Neither placement dates the first existence of every represented practice. Their selected introduction is a comparative scholarly account, not a new ancient witness. [Jamison and Brereton 2014, selected introduction](https://vedisk.sites.ku.dk/files/2021/09/13-22-Stephanie_W_Jamison_Joel_P_Brereton_The.pdf)

The [retained result](run-v1/RESULTS.json) joins 36 stanza/control observations, seven lexical-source observations, six rival comparisons and four material comparisons. Categories describe support for a component or an unresolved route. They are not numerical likelihoods. The five routes can combine: local post-Harappan practitioners, inherited Indo-Iranian/Indo-European repertoire, wider contact, practical convergence/reconstruction and later specialist organization.

## Occupations and resources

RV 9.112.1 relates different workers to corresponding opportunities: a woodworker to repair, a healer to illness and a ritual specialist to a Soma-presser. Verse 2 places an artisan's material imagery beside the search for a wealthy person. Verse 3 portrays a bard, a healer father and a mother working with grinding stones. The different tasks within this depicted family make it unsuitable as proof that each profession was inherited through one fixed caste. The final verse broadens the poem to bodily and animal desires. The Soma refrain belongs to the complete comparison. [RV 9.112, Griffith witness](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/rigveda/rv09112.htm) [TX-RV9.112.1–4; TC01]

The Greek control lists an invited seer, healer, woodworker and singer. It gives a comparison outside a proposed local Indian route, while its context differs: the speaker discusses which strangers are invited. Independently reviewed comparative scholarship also attributes the poet's patronage role to Indo-Iranian and Indo-European inheritance. These observations weaken an argument that differentiated specialists require a uniquely Harappan origin. They leave particular local skills and contributions open. [Murray's Odyssey, pinned Greek and English witnesses](https://github.com/PerseusDL/canonical-greekLit/tree/01b725d835e6e733062ffd79e0efdbae1ba06e5c/data/tlg0012/tlg002) [TX-OD17-ROLES; review/O-R01-1]

The Younger Avestan Haoma passage represents preparation, praise, a ritual performer and rewards. Its mythical genealogies are not dates of institutions. The specific value of this control is the available related ritual repertoire. A Soma refrain cannot establish where the occupational examples originated. [Yasna, Mills witness](https://www.avesta.org/yasna/yasna.htm) [TX-Y9-PRESS/MYTH]

## From an operation to a model for knowledge

RV 10.71.2 uses the meal-sifting comparison while describing the mental fashioning of speech and companions recognizing their affiliation. The Sanskrit comparison marker connects the two domains directly. Griffith's older English “corn-flour” does not identify maize or a particular ancient crop; “cribble” means a sieve. The source depicts an analogy, with no named tool design, processing location or donor. [Sanskrit, RV 10.71.2](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/1_veda/1_sam/1_rv/rv_hn10u.htm), [Griffith's complete hymn](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/rigveda/rv10071.htm) [TX-RV10.71.2; TC02]

The surrounding hymn makes the institutional setting more specific. Hearing words and understanding them can diverge in verse 4. Losing a knowledgeable companion is linked to losing a share in speech in verse 6. Verse 7 depicts unequal mental capacities; verse 8 compares performances among ritual peers. Verse 10 welcomes a successful assembly participant associated with support for companions. Verse 11 differentiates recitation, melody, speaking knowledge and measuring the sacrifice. These are depicted relations among competence, affiliation, recognition and work. They motivate a mechanism of peer participation and qualification, with a formal school or examination remaining a further hypothesis. [TX-RV10.71.4–11]

The independent Old Avestan controls represent addressed speech and a ritual specialist, and the scholarly introduction discusses shared poetic formulae with creative variation. Their selected scope does not supply the exact sifting/affiliation relation or its first invention. They establish why verbal sophistication and ritual expertise have available explanations beyond a local urban inheritance. [Skjærvø 2006 teaching witness](https://www.lexicons.ru/old/a/avestan/_pdf/old-avestan-exercises.pdf) [review/O-R02-1–2; review/O-R01-3]

The [developed mechanism](MECHANISM.md) proposes that recipient specialists can retain an operation, abstract a relation from it and make that relation serve a new conceptual or institutional purpose. It is an exploratory refinement after reading, inside the fixed complete hymn. The sifting image is the directly observed relation; a processor-to-poet apprenticeship and the route from a Harappan household are prospective historical questions.

## Agricultural operations in a ritual setting

RV 10.101.3–4 invokes plows/yokes, sowing, ripeness and a sickle, with song and divine favor. Verses 5–7 name well receptacles, straps and stone-wheel terminology. These are more specific than a general statement that farming existed. The complete hymn also calls for sacrifice, employs chariot and protection imagery, describes a well sunk without digging and concludes with Indra drinking Soma. The apparatus cannot be reconstructed as one uniquely determined literal machine or a complete farming manual. [RV 10.101, Griffith witness](https://sacred-texts.com/hin/rigveda/rv10101.htm) [TX-RV10.101.1–12; TC03–TC04]

The Sanskrit gloss controls refine the English. The selected plow term is *sīra*, supported by the dictionary's plow entry. The adjective behind Griffith's “iron forts” establishes metallic imagery here, rather than an authenticated iron composition. Verse 9 uses a pasture/fodder comparison; a substring search would incorrectly classify it as the grain lemma *yava*. Such decisions matter when a modern translated word is being joined to archaeology. [MW sīra display, printed page 1219](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MWScan/2020/web/webtc/getword.php?key=sIra&filter=roman&transLit=slp1) [TX-LEX-SIRA; LEXICAL-RESULTS.json]

Vendidad 3.23–33 supplies a qualified comparison. It values sowing, watering dry ground, draining wet ground and sustained cultivation, then associates grain production with righteousness and food with strength for work. These are directly represented agricultural and conceptual relations in a Younger Avestan normative text. The selected Old Avestan cow/forager phrases provide a pastoral comparator, with no matched plow or lifting sequence. [Darmesteter's Vendidad 3 witness](https://www.avesta.org/vendidad/vd3sbe.htm) [TX-V3-CULTIVATION/REGISTER; review/O-R02-3]

The new material records include Hulas plant impressions and recovery limits, Bhagwanpura grinding tools on a hut floor without recovered grains, and Jognakhera crop specimens and a husk-residue candidate. They establish narrower material endpoints. A complete matching sequence, specific equipment arrangement and carrier relationship remain absent from the supplied observations. For beads, artisan materials and stone implements supply no drilling/polishing counterpart. For ceramics, a wooden vessel or cup supplies no forming/firing counterpart. [Text-side material comparisons](MATERIAL-JOINS.json) [TJ01–TJ04; material/MO07–10, MO17, MO22–25]

## Vocabulary has its own history

The fixed lexical checks find *sīra* in two selected stanzas, fashioning forms associated with *takṣ* in three, healer forms in two, brahman-related forms in four and speech forms in seven. The selected grain-*yava*, *lāṅgala* and *kṛṣ* checks have no matches. These are stanza-presence checks in the fixed frame, not a complete lemmatized Rigvedic inventory. A mental fashioning form does not count as an additional carpenter workplace. [LEXICAL-RESULTS.json](LEXICAL-RESULTS.json)

The seed word *bīja* was added after inspection and is marked separately. The historical dictionary calls its origin doubtful; Witzel's agricultural list attributes it to non-Indo-Aryan vocabulary without resolving its donor at that locus. Witzel discusses competing proposals for *lāṅgala* and wider substrates, but that plow word is outside this new passage frame. His inferred regional language labels are scholarly hypotheses. Contact can contribute a word while leaving the donor, technique and institutional route unresolved. [MW bīja, printed page 732](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MWScan/2020/web/webtc/getword.php?key=bIja&filter=roman&transLit=slp1), [Witzel's selected article](https://www.michaelwitzel.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/ejvs0501article.pdf) [TX-LEX-BIJA/PLOW-LEAD/WIDER-CONTACT; TC05]

## Later classifications and permission

DN 1.1.21–27 groups divination, prediction, calculation, ritual and treatment under livelihood from which Gotama abstains while supported by donated food. The list gives an institutional relation among available arts, permission and support. Its gem-mark passage concerns interpretation, with no bead manufacturing. Its medical passage includes procedures within the same normative classification. [Pinned Pali/English DN 1 witnesses](https://github.com/suttacentral/bilara-data/tree/795983655cc7e5c5694184750426b06a6604be21) [TX-DN1-NORM/MARKS/CALC/TREAT; TC06]

This directly attests literary organization at the passage's own stage. It does not show a particular earlier surviving text being rewritten. No new redaction history or manuscript date for this list was established here. The distinctions let later literature illuminate possible incorporation and classification mechanisms while its dates remain separate from Harappan material contexts.

## What would discriminate the routes

[TESTS.md](TESTS.md) sets out prospective comparisons. A local contribution needs a distinctive operation/role relation, independently dated intermediate practice and carrier evidence capable of distinguishing exchange, inherited repertoire and reconstruction. A conceptual-incorporation test should compare complete practical images and their conceptual functions across matched textual genres and traditions. The motivating sifting verse can define the hypothesis; a held-out sample must test its broader predictions.

The current result supports separate histories for operations, organizing relations, terminology and explanation. It supplies concrete observations and a mechanism through which mature intellectual organization could incorporate practical knowledge. Its historical donor remains a feature-specific test, rather than a consequence of sophistication alone.
