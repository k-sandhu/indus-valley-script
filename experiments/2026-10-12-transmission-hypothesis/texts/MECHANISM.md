# Incorporation through practice, analogy and peer recognition

12 October 2026. Developed hypothesis after the fixed passage comparison.

RV 10.71 supplies a direct practical-to-conceptual analogy: sifting meal is used to portray fashioning speech. The same poem connects comprehension, affiliation, unequal competence, recognition in an assembly and differentiated ritual work. These observations support a concrete question about transmission. How can a recipient tradition use knowledge of an operation while giving it a different conceptual function and social setting?

The proposed mechanism has four relations whose histories can differ. A useful operation is available through work, observation, recruitment or exchange. A speaker abstracts a relation from it, such as distinguishing usable output from what should be removed. Peers use the relation to discuss competence and participation. Performance and support help sustain the resulting intellectual repertoire. The operation's name and its explanation can change at any of these steps.

Only some of these relations are directly represented in the selected texts. RV 9.112 portrays different household tasks and specialists seeking resources. RV 10.71.2 gives the analogy; verses 6, 8, 10 and 11 give affiliation, peer comparison, recognition and work distinctions. Independently reviewed scholarship supplies comparative poet/patron inheritance. A specific apprenticeship connecting a grain processor with this poet, the initial abstraction event and a post-Harappan carrier are unobserved. The mechanism can explain conceptual development without requiring each participant to know the complete history of the operation. A remembered action can provide a stable model while equipment changes. A competent newcomer can introduce another example. A specialist group can combine inherited formulae with practical images and its own criteria for successful performance. This permits contributions from local practitioners, wider contact, inherited repertoires and new reasoning within one recipient tradition.

The organizing relation can also change the use of retained knowledge. DN 1 classifies a broad repertoire by permitted livelihood and its connection with donated support. This is direct endpoint organization. It does not establish that the list rewrites RV 9.112 or that its therapies originated with the depicted physician. It offers a later comparative case for a recipient institution selecting which available activities its members should undertake. [TC06](COMPARISONS.json)

Three predictions make the hypothesis testable. First, a retained operation can have changed terminology while preserving an unusual dependency or action relation. Second, practical-to-conceptual analogies should preserve some operation structure, with selective changes explained by their new intellectual purpose. Third, changes in permission or patronage should affect which parts of a repertoire are emphasized, even where people still know the underlying operations.

The alternative explanations have distinct consequences. A conventional inherited metaphor can account for the conceptual relation before the proposed local contact. Practical convergence can produce the same useful comparison independently. Regional diffusion can supply an operation through a route without an Indus-city intermediary. Later classification can organize components of several origins. Demonstrating the incorporation mechanism in a text therefore does not select its historical donor.

The next study should measure the relation at a finer level than a list of occupations or a word origin. For a sifting analogy, record the input, transformation, retained output and criterion of success in both domains. For a water-lifting image, record the fittings and dependencies, allowing several physical arrangements. Material traces then test whether the same unusual relations are present in independently dated practice. This is a prospective source and operation comparison; no new historical probability or simulated transmission endpoint is reported here.
