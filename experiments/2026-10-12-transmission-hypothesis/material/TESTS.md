# A prospective test of operation transmission

12 October 2026. Designed after the documentary audit; no collection reanalysis, laboratory work or new excavation was performed.

The observational unit is an operation or linked technical relation within a dated working context. The endpoint is whether a proposed route explains that relation better than its specified alternatives. Keep production at a site, transmission between practitioners and later textual representation as separate outcomes. The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) and [comparisons](comparisons.json) supply the frozen families and current decisions.

## Verify intermediate work before comparing ancestry

Begin with BPR-36 and BPR-208 because the catalogue records their unfinished condition and subperiod. Resolve the conflicting material labels for BPR-208 against the collection and drawings before sourcing. Include all relevant finished, unfinished and failed pieces from the fixed excavation/context frame, including surface finds with their separate status. Do not select only specimens resembling an earlier product.

Use microscopic working traces to distinguish roughing, drilling, abrasion and polishing; record the basis for inferred order. Seek matching chips, powders, roughouts, drill pieces and wear in associated screened deposits. Compare imported roughouts, curated older pieces, repair of older ornaments and local manufacture. A local last-stage operation should not be reported as the complete local production chain. Directly constrain the deposit with short-lived material and retain disturbance and residual-object alternatives.

The following outcomes would discriminate different scopes:

| Observation | Consequence for the comparison |
|---|---|
| Matching manufacture waste and tools in a dated working context | Strengthens local execution over an exclusively imported-completed-product account |
| Imported raw or partly worked material with local finishing traces | Supports a mixed supply/production route; leaves the source of instruction unresolved |
| Old manufacture signature in a curated later assemblage without working association | Supports retention or exchange of an object rather than a contemporaneous full workshop |
| A rare linked technical choice repeated in successive local contexts | Makes local transmission more discriminating once rarity, chronology and alternative contacts are assessed |
| Same choices occurring across several connected regions | Supports a wider transmission network and weakens exclusive attribution to one city or lineage |
| Independent makers reproduce the same bundle under matched goals and materials | Reduces that bundle's ability to distinguish inherited instruction from reconstruction |

The early Ravi association [MO26](observations.json) is a recovery/context control, not the missing intermediate. Use an equivalent recovery protocol to compare later waste, because absence in old collections can reflect the recovery method.

## Separate ceramic form, preparation and forming choices

Use a dated local series with paste, joining orientation, forming marks, finishing sequence and thermal signatures. Include ordinary products and failures rather than only decorated vessels. The twelve Bhagwanpura specimens do not supply this full source/phase frame. New analysis needs geological reference clays and independent findspot joins before treating bulk similarity as local provenance.

Compare choices across earlier and later contexts while masking their period to the technical coders where feasible. Keep transmitted shaping habits separate from copied-looking vessel forms. A pot can transport a technological signature; a workshop and appropriate waste provide a different observation. Published proposals of potters learning from neighbors, including Joshi and Bala's p.30 account, become hypotheses tested against these relations.

Calibrate convergence using modern makers assigned the same material, performance goal and available tools. Include a taught recipe, an alternative taught recipe and independent solution attempts. Measure operation order and trace patterns in addition to successful output. These experiments supply observed reconstruction frequencies in their declared setting; they do not produce a historical probability of Harappan ancestry. Ancient transfer needs its own chronology and contact comparison.

## Test the plant-processing/clay interface

The post-audit mechanism proposes that deliberately prepared crop by-products could connect repeated household operations. At Hulas, distinguish selected husk temper from natural inclusions in local clay. Compare inclusion frequency, size, orientation, cuticle morphology, breakage and heat alteration with clay-source samples and associated processing refuse. Match sample volume, sieve size and context coverage. Preserve ambiguous wild/cultivated identification.

For deliberate selection, expect patterned preparation or addition that differs from unsorted local clay and occurs in compatible construction/ceramic contexts. Naturally included plants should follow clay-source composition more closely. Imported husk, imported crop awaiting processing and local plant collection remain available controls. Residues do not identify their donor household automatically.

Then test whether processing and construction/clay preparation co-occur in an independently dated working setting. Directly date suitable short-lived material and record the relation between a dated specimen and every claimed context. Jognakhera's supplementary site-level date entries cannot be silently attached to each botanical sampling layer. One grain date does not date the full aggregate crop mixture or establish continuous occupation between dated endpoints.

Rotation and ecological management need further observations: crop-growing conditions, weed associations and depositional/processing stage, with field or spatial evidence where available. Summer/winter crops can arise from separate fields, imported food or aggregated occupations. Seasonal names and legume abundance alone do not identify rotation on the same land. No earlier rice percentage, uncertainty assignment or eligibility endpoint is rerun here.

## Observe a carrier and preserve the weaker joins

Household or workshop continuity needs evidence beyond the repeated place name. Seek overlapping work settings, repeated technique choices, novice/expert error patterns calibrated against material failure, and evidence for recruited or mobile practitioners. A stratigraphic sequence and an accessible shared task may locate an opportunity for learning; neither identifies a teacher by itself.

An institutional carrier is a more specific claim. Kinship, patronage, workshop membership, seasonal exchange and traveling specialists predict different access and succession patterns. Define their predicted observations before coding a favored chain. Include interrupted or transformed production settings as controls. Changes of decoration or terminology can coexist with retained operations, while unchanged decoration can coexist with replacement of practitioners.

The material-to-textual step requires an independently assessed operation relation in the text and a material signature that distinguishes its routes. Whole-hymn context, ritual/poetic usage, strata and Iranian controls remain necessary. If the selected text supplies only a generic working word, keep it as a contact or representation question. Do not search a large corpus for a favorable match and then treat that match as the prespecified endpoint.

Stop the route-identification test when chronology cannot locate the proposed transition, operation order is unidentifiable, reference materials are absent or competing contacts remain unbounded. Report which endpoint survives: local working, plant use, a production configuration or an opportunity for transfer. This leaves a concrete next measurement without promoting compatibility to a traced historical route.
