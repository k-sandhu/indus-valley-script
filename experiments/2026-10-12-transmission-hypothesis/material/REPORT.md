# Practical knowledge in intermediate material contexts

12 October 2026. New documentary comparisons following the October 11 continuity and persistence studies.

The inspected sources add concrete material candidates for practical transmission: unfinished stone objects in Bhagwanpura's later assemblage, forming and joining traces in Hulas pottery, plant material incorporated into clay, and directly dated second-millennium crop specimens from Jognakhera. These locate working stages and plant use more closely than a list of finished ornaments or crops. The observations leave the route between earlier knowledge, later practitioners and a particular textual operation unresolved. The [frozen ledger](observations.json) and [rival comparisons](comparisons.json) specify that distinction for each case.

The study contains eight source/work records, 26 observations and three candidate families fixed before new coding. The twelve broad rival comparisons retain ten `compatible` and two `unresolved` decisions; no broad historical route is excluded. Three narrower controls identify claims that require more explanation, including an account in which every bead arrived already complete. These labels describe the comparisons, rather than numerical plausibility or evidence-count voting. The [frozen output](run-v1/summary.json) records the exact sizes and source joins.

## Scope of the comparison

The candidates are hard-stone working, ceramic preparation and manufacture, and plant processing or ecological management. The rivals are local transmission, imported/curated material, regional contact or movement of practitioners, and independent reconstruction under similar constraints. A transmitted method can also arrive through regional contact; these routes can contribute to different parts of one practice. Indo-Iranian inheritance and later literary organization are assessed in the independently owned textual comparison.

Eight work/page records cover five sites and shared source lineages. Harappa captions, collections and field papers overlap; a chapter, mirror or later chronology is not an additional excavation. The material records keep object manufacture, deposition, field season, publication and phase dating separate. Cultural pottery labels do not identify language or ethnicity. A household location can identify a setting for work without identifying its teachers or an unchanged resident population. These assumptions govern the comparisons below.

## Stone working: an intermediate object and a curated collection

Bhagwanpura supplies an accession-level working-stage candidate. The catalogue describes BPR-36 as a roughly faceted unfinished black-jasper bead from a late level of Sub-period IB. BPR-208 is also unfinished and assigned to mid-IB, but the two lists call it quartz and banded agate respectively. It remains one unresolved accession. The adjacent craft discussion reports unfinished beads and raw material and proposes local bead making. These observations warrant investigating an intermediate working context; they do not yet provide its complete waste/tool association or a transferred technical sequence. [MS04; MO12–MO14, pp.30, 118, 120, 122](sources.json), [primary report](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/80282.pdf).

The Harappa bead pot makes the transport and curation rival substantive. One caption describes a tubular perforation in an unfinished bead and infers later manufacture. Another excavation description says the pot contains objects from all Harappan occupation phases. The collection's approximate deposition date therefore cannot date every object's working. An imported or retained roughout remains possible even when the object is unfinished. The color-based material-source claim and supply-loss explanation also require their own tests. [MS01–MS02; MO01–MO03](observations.json), [drilling caption](https://www.harappa.com/slide/tubular-drill-hole), [collection description](https://www.harappa.com/blog/single-bead-pot-collection).

These cases weaken a completed-ornaments-only description while preserving several explanations for the working stage. Local manufacture is stronger when roughouts, tools and matching waste occupy a dated working context. Meadow and Kenoyer's screened early Ravi deposits provide a source control for that association, with drills, flakes and unfinished beads related to living surfaces. That example is preurban and cannot fill the posturban gap. The later phase dates in the same paper likewise date their carbon contexts rather than every bead's manufacture. [MS07; MO04, MO26, p.209](sources.json), [field paper](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer2005_Excavations%20at%20Harappa%202000-2001%20New%20insights%20on.pdf).

The next useful stone observation is a linked operation signature: roughing choices, drilling marks, abrasion and polishing order joined to replacement tools and waste. Shared generic facets or holes can arise through contact, imports and similar material constraints. A signature's discriminating value needs comparative production evidence, including alternative techniques that achieve the same product.

## Ceramic traces and an existing learning proposal

Hulas pottery preserves a described relation between separately formed parts, joining and later trimming. The report also discusses temper, striations and finishing while leaving its firing arrangement unclear. These are technical observations beyond shape resemblance. The botanical appendix records husk impressions in potsherds and burnt plaster. Their intentional addition, source and processing history remain questions; rice-bearing clay and wild plant use supply formation controls. [MS03; MO06–MO08, pp.72, 223–224, 232–233](observations.json), [official report](https://asi.nic.in/pdf/EXCAVATIONS-AT-HULAS.pdf).

Bhagwanpura adds a pottery-tool inference and a twelve-specimen material analysis. Similar bulk clay chemistry is an author interpretation requiring geological controls, and the inspected analysis lacks a precise phase/findspot comparison for all specimens. Grey-ware shapes resembling earlier vessels can be compared with preparation and forming choices, but those observations do not by themselves identify a teaching direction. Joshi and Bala already proposed that later users learned wheeled terracotta and geometric pottery designs from neighboring Late Harappans in the `F. Society` paragraph on printed p.30. This study develops tests of that attributed hypothesis rather than claiming priority for learning through contact. [MS04; MO14–MO16, MO18](sources.json), [report pp.20, 30, 209–215](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/80282.pdf).

An inherited recipe predicts continuity in particular preparation and forming choices even where decoration changes. Regional adoption predicts local execution of a transferred method; incoming specialists can produce the same material pattern. Reinvention predicts recurrence of useful operations under comparable clay and vessel constraints. Finished-pot exchange can carry both shape and manufacturing traces without transmitting their production locally. The present records do not choose among these routes. A dated series of workshop failures, joins, paste and tools would give the comparison more specific observations.

## Plant handling and the date of an endpoint

The July 2026 Jognakhera study supplies three direct crop-specimen dates, with published 95.4% ranges of 2471–2298 cal BCE for barley, 1880–1691 for Vigna and 1517–1428 for rice. Eight botanical samples came from specified layers and were recovered through flotation. The supplement gives the dated specimens' site, without their individual sampled-layer joins. A named husk mass is another processing-residue candidate. Seasonal legumes and grains support several possible land-use regimes; they do not alone identify rotation on one field, continuous household teaching or migration. [MS06; MO22–MO25, methods/results and supplementary Tables S1–S3](sources.json), [published study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00334-026-01140-4).

The Hulas appendix and Bhagwanpura provide different kinds of context. The former used one season's plant sample and records loss through burning and identification; its typological chronology differs from the main report's older physical dates. The latter places querns and pestles on a hut floor while explicitly reporting no recovered grains. A grinding household is an excavator hypothesis. Residues and wear could test the worked material. These are bounded candidates for processing and domestic knowledge; they do not recover a plough–yoke–sowing–irrigation programme. [MO09–MO10, MO17](observations.json).

Imported crops can be processed locally, while local processing does not establish local cultivation. Seed movement, harvested-food exchange and movement of farmers may leave overlapping crop occurrences. The Hulas discussion of temperate nuts gives a source-specific example of proposed distant supply. The crop comparison therefore needs growing-regime and sourcing evidence alongside dated plant use. [MO11, MO22–MO25](observations.json).

## Repeated work as a possible carrier

The following mechanism was developed after the documentary coding. It is a prospective hypothesis, informed by the observed settings and the earlier proposal of learning during work.

Practical knowledge can persist through recurring tasks that expose learners to both a method and its consequences. Clay joining reveals cracking or separation; sharpening reveals cutting performance; grinding reveals the condition of the worked material. A household, workshop or mobile group may reproduce a useful operation while changing products, language or ceremonial explanation. The carrier needed for a particular operation can be smaller than the urban institutions that once organized its supply. Its existence and teaching relation still require evidence at the chosen place and time.

Plant residues in building material suggest a particularly useful interface to test. If cleaned husk was deliberately selected as temper, plant processing supplied another craft's input. Repeated exchange of that by-product could carry knowledge of its preparation and behavior between people doing different jobs. A learner might encounter both activities through ordinary work, without a separate instruction establishment. If impressions instead came mainly from naturally plant-bearing clay, the proposed link between processing and deliberate temper choice would lose support. The present observations motivate this distinction without resolving it.

Selective survival then concerns an operation or relation, not an entire inherited culture. A maker could retain joining and trimming while adopting new vessel forms. A bead worker could change the drill or material while retaining other stages. Successful output provides feedback, but several different recipes can succeed. Therefore practical effectiveness alone cannot establish ancestry. A persistent technical signature needs to include choices that competing routes would reproduce less readily, assessed with actual comparative controls.

## What would change the result

The first empirical test should join collection verification to a technical comparison. Reconcile BPR-208 and inspect the unfinished Bhagwanpura objects with microscopy, sourcing and associated screened waste. Record which stages occurred locally and which remain compatible with imported roughouts. Constrain context dates using short-lived material and an explicit stratigraphic model. Compare the resulting signature with earlier and contemporary regional workshops before inferring a direction of transfer. [Test design](TESTS.md).

A second test should distinguish deliberate plant-temper selection from raw-clay inclusion. Sample husk-bearing plaster, pots, local clays and associated processing deposits under matched recovery. Compare inclusion size, breakage, concentration, distribution and thermal history. Then test whether independently reconstructed plant handling and clay preparation belong to the same dated working setting. This can evaluate a material learning interface while preserving imported crop and regional-contact alternatives.

The [operation-level join table](operation-joins.json) keeps the weakest transition visible. The new records do not supply the particular plough/yoke or irrigation relations selected for RV10.101, and no bead or pottery technique has a fixed counterpart in the textual frame. The text's ritual and poetic use of working language needs its own interpretation. Material working stages and dated plants improve the historical question, but a specific technical transmission into a particular textual representation remains untraced.
