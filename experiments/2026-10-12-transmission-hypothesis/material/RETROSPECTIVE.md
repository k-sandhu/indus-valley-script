# Material transmission study: retrospective

12 October 2026. This record documents observable work, source problems and the completed endpoint.

## Approach and scope

The new assignment asked for practical operations and possible carriers after Indus urban contraction. Earlier studies had already tested rice eligibility, chronology, continuity and service renewal. I read the repository navigation, full relevant registry records, earlier retrospectives, editorial rules and source-handling policy, then preserved those endpoints. The new material study used production stages and processing contexts rather than repeating their numerical analyses.

The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) fixed hard-stone work, ceramic preparation/manufacture and crop-processing/ecological knowledge before acquiring and coding the new witnesses. Prior packages and discovery snippets had exposed broad claims about these cases. The protocol therefore describes a purposive documentary comparison. It does not claim outcome-blind selection. Four broad rivals were retained for each family: local transfer, imported/curated material, regional contact or movement of makers, and independent reconstruction. New source loci could inform a fixed family; any new family would have required an exploratory label.

## Source acquisition and reading

Eleven normal acquisition requests produced valid Hulas, Bhagwanpura and Sanghol report witnesses, Harappa pages and papers, and a Jognakhera supplement. Two Jognakhera publisher responses were small HTML challenges despite an HTTP-success status. A normally requested author PDF returned HTTP 403. I kept these failures, inspected selected main-article loci through the web tool, and identified the saved text as a conversion rather than original PDF bytes. TLS verification remained enabled.

The scanned Bhagwanpura, Sanghol and Harappa reports required page rendering and OCR. The reusable OCR utility saved parent, render and text hashes and engine/exit status. The 142 OCR page derivatives include navigation pages and search coverage; source reading scopes in the ledger identify what was inspected. Selected page images were read directly to check catalogue entries, chronology tables and context claims. Ordinary HTML extraction missed substantive image-alt captions in the Harappa collection page. A separate local derivative retained those captions with the source hash, revealing the all-phase curation statement.

The twelve [manual source controls](source-checks.json) address identity, locus and interpretation. They complement the automated checks of hashes, joins and coding vocabulary. The article or report's own interpretive claims remain attributed to its authors.

## Corrections and counterevidence

The Harappa pot initially offered an unfinished-object candidate in a later context. The collection caption's all-phase contents made curation a necessary explanation to consider. I kept the unfinished drilling stage and the mixed collection in the same comparison instead of assigning the pot's deposition date to every object.

Bhagwanpura provides a stronger intermediate working-stage lead, but BPR-208 is described as quartz on printed p.118 and banded agate on p.122. Both entries assign mid-Sub-period IB. I preserved one accession and its material conflict. BPR-36's rough, unfinished condition and late-IB label remain a separate observation. The p.30 local-production inference is useful, while complete tool/waste association and precise physical dates for those objects remain missing.

The `F. Society` paragraph on the same printed p.30 already proposes that later users learned wheeled terracotta and geometric pottery designs from neighboring Late Harappans. The present mechanism and tests develop that attributed proposal. Learning during contact is not a new claim established by this package. The contribution is a candidate-specific comparison that keeps technical work, object transport, regional transfer and identified teaching distinct.

Hulas joining and trimming provide operation traces beyond shape resemblance. Husk impressions motivate a plant-processing/clay interface, but the appendix's natural rice-bearing-clay example comes initially from comparative Gujarat cases. It supplies a formation control for Hulas rather than an observed Hulas clay source. Its crop chronology and the main report's older physical-date discussion also differ. Later PGW occupation is described in a spatially separate part of the site. None of these observations was converted into a continuous household sequence.

Bhagwanpura's grinding tools occur on a hut floor, while the report explicitly says grains were not recovered. The proposed grinder household therefore needs residue/wear tests. Sanghol's bead/terracotta factory suggestion belongs to a Kushana context; it cannot supply the requested Late Harappan intermediate. I retained Bara domestic features with their own phase and overlap qualifications.

The July 2026 Jognakhera study and supplement add three directly dated crop specimens and eight sampled layers. The supplement identifies the dated specimens at site level without individual sampled-layer joins. Crop occurrence and a husk mass are usable observations; rotation, unchanged teaching and migration require other relations. Those author interpretations were kept in separate fields. No earlier rice percentages or eligibility output was rerun.

## Result and continuation

The frozen endpoint has eight work/page records, 26 observations, three candidate families and twelve broad rival decisions, of which ten are compatible and two unresolved. Three narrower claim controls specify less adequate descriptions. The seven operation-join rows supply no diagnostic material-to-textual sequence, and no teaching relationship is observed. These are scoped documentary results. Shared Harappa publications, mirrors and field lineages remain dependent records.

The [post-coding mechanism](REPORT.md) proposes repeated task feedback and transfer of prepared by-products as possible small-scale carriers. The [next tests](TESTS.md) begin with accessions, waste, sourcing and dates, then examine technical signature rarity and deliberate husk selection. New data should identify which operation was executed at a site before inferring the source of instruction. A useful continuation needs dated working contexts and a comparative operation signature; another list of finished products or crop names would leave the present route comparison unresolved.

The package passes 300 automated integrity checks, including the 142 OCR derivatives. These checks preserve the documentary endpoint; they are not archaeological confirmation of transmission. Raw source witnesses and derivatives remain ignored and local. Only the owned dated package and raw directory were changed. Earlier endpoints, other contributors' files, global navigation and registry entries were left intact.
