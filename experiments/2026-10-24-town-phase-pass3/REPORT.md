# The nomination plan locates reservoirs but leaves restoration faces unjoined

We acquired the ASI's 2020 nomination Volume I and its January 2020 site plan through UNESCO. Twelve pages were inspected. The plan supplies named reservoir components and the dossier supplies conservation photographs, but neither inspected source identifies the numbered stones or maps the 2005–06 southeast Castle intervention onto ancient wall phases. The exact source hashes, page bounds and local paths are in [source-manifest.json](source-manifest.json). Q-1032 remains open.

The [nomination dossier](https://whc.unesco.org/document/181028), printed p. 388 (PDF index 411), repeats the management plan's statement that southern reservoir and southeast citadel masonry was dismantled before resetting in 2005–06. This corroborates the publication relation already recorded in pass2; it adds no excavation observation. Printed p. 386 describes numbering stones before resetting the veneer from the east gate toward the northern corner in 2000–01. That location must remain separate from the southeast episode. Printed p. 387, Figs. 4.2–4.4, shows Lower Town wall consolidation, eastern reservoir work and reconstruction of Stage VII pillars after the 2001 earthquake. These captions identify other places and episodes. They supply no numbered-stone crosswalk for the target wall.

The [ASI site plan](https://whc.unesco.org/document/189006), first sheet, labels five southern reservoirs spatially, together with Castle gates and southwest corner. Its legend repeats SR4 for the fifth reservoir while the drawing labels SR5. We retain that inconsistency in the local observation record. The sheet has a scale, north symbol and grid numbers, but no restoration footprint or wall-by-wall stage key. It gives a location framework for requesting the relevant records, with no completed join between conservation work and ancient faces. The second sheet maps property and quarry boundaries; it does not provide the missing join.

The dossier's cover states 2020. It is a nomination publication, separate from the preliminary January 2015 excavation report. Its conservation tables recur in Chapters 4 and 6 and depend on the same institutional account; repeated entries are not independent interventions. The inspected pages give no collection locus, material or laboratory documentation for DHR93/TL/8 or /4. Term screening found neither exact TL label in the accessible text layer, which does not establish absence from images or other archives.

Nine bounded queries also pursued official Lothal volumes and the 2005–06 Indian Archaeology conservation review. The annual PDF request returned HTTP 403. The Lothal search produced bibliographic records, the previously known 286-page text edition and later reproductions citing plate XXXVIII. No original plate or SRG11 locus 5B field 6 section was admitted. The TF-19 depth datum, exact sample position against the damaged western dock embankment, and Dholavira TL collection contexts remain unknown. Search results are leads, preserved locally where complete responses were available.

Author validation checks hashes, twelve-page accounting, four local observation records and the frozen protocol. The package adds two source copies and a plan locator, with zero construction-sample joins or newly documented excavation observations. No bearing, calibration or confirmation test was run. A stylized inscription graphic in the dossier footer was incidentally exposed during the visual page check and is logged without transcription.

The strongest supported reading is that the surviving Dholavira masonry records modern rebuilding as well as ancient construction. The deciding evidence is a numbered-stone photographic register and restoration footprint tied to the southeast Castle face and southern reservoir, joined to phase-keyed excavation coordinates. At Lothal, the original sample section with its datum would decide the deposit relation; the inherited depth alone cannot complete that join.
