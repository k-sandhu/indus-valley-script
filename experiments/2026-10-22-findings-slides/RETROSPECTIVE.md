# Retrospective

We turned the preserved research and the 22 October literature audit into an illustrated explanation for a reader without specialist training. Each main finding gives its observation, a plain-language interpretation, the social implication if its interpretation holds, and the specific contribution to reading the script. We kept possible meanings beside their evidence requirements. The publication slides distinguish prior broad ideas from our narrower additions.

The actual photographs come from the existing catalogue crop collection and were inspected before use. The cover is an explicitly labelled generated scene. We used native HTML charts for numerical results and ordinary English examples for segmentation, vowel flexibility, rebus readings and restoration. No photograph was redrawn, and the existing research outputs were not changed.

The first browser pass found the novelty table too close to its footer. We reduced the table's cell padding and rechecked the result. We also expanded the birth-star definition and identified Brāhmī as a later readable script. Browser checks cover every slide at 1440 × 958, all slides at phone width, keyboard and button navigation, overview selection and 24-slide print output. The validation record gives the final outcome.

There are no new archaeological sources, object identities or metadata fields in this package, and no change in research-data coverage. The HTML remains local because the embedded catalogue photographs have unresolved redistribution rights. The code and explanation follow the repository's reuse terms.

The strongest interpretation presented is that some seal texts identify a holder within a shared set of roles or formulas. A fixed inventory that predicts unseen seals, followed by independent evidence tying those units to holders or uses, would decide whether this model advances beyond structural resemblance.
