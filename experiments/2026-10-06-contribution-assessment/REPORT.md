Review of 6 October 2026. A literature subagent then searched for precedents the drafts do not cite, with each source marked as opened or seen only in a search snippet. Two consequential claims were checked again directly: Yadav et al.'s 2010 PLoS ONE methods and Raghavendra's August 2026 preprint. No predictor, simulation or frozen endpoint was rerun.

## Measured effect of the alias repair

Paper 001 and the October 2 review identify the alias-preserving transitive split as the defensible new element of the prediction study. The preserved endpoints show that the repair changed the measured results very little. The unique-string gain moved from 0.84807 bits per noninitial sign in [`nested_unique`](../../research/methods/results/nested_unique/nested_results.json) to 0.84836 in [`nested_unique_preserved_aliases`](../../research/methods/results/nested_unique_preserved_aliases/nested_results.json). Local-model restoration top-one moved from 33.66% in the [superseded benchmark](../../results/restoration_superseded_lost_aliases/benchmark.json) to 33.81% in the [current benchmark](../../results/restoration/benchmark.json); the paired gain over the baseline moved from 16.71 to 16.82 percentage points.

The repair was necessary for a correct split. Its outcome is a robustness result: on this transcription population, discarded duplicate aliases did not inflate the sequential gain or restoration accuracy.

## Contributions with specialist value

The Fuls audit is the most direct correction of published work. Conditional on the chapter's two equalities, the [exact enumeration](../../research/methods/NUMERICAL_CONSTRAINT_AUDIT.md) finds four positive-integer solutions; the stated ranges select 10/8/6. The printed-page-30 footnote infers an even short-stroke multiplier from a relation that fixes only parity agreement. Vidale's 1987 chapter, the inspected source behind the bangle range, supplies no M1383 join and no 15–20 total. The historical ICIT 3786 identifier does not match the current corpus record with that number. These points fit a short commentary, ideally shared with the author before submission.

The source audits supply checkable catalogue discrepancies. Soutzo's 1911 Susa catalogue records AS 13820 and AS 13821 as unmarked veined marble, with AS 13820 at 2769 g; Ascalone 2022 joins them to SB 9340 and SB 9342 as limestone with six and five strokes, and SB 9340 at 3067 g. The Lothal pixel table counts 133 impressions against a prose total of 130. Tamil Nadu compound 4.19C, specimen SGI(PPT)-G-0137, has six components in the printed concordance and five in the web array. H2238 carries the only stroke-count exception among the 22 Harappa tablets. Gonur's report gives 26 tokens while Figure 41 shows 23. Each can be resolved by a named archive or museum request. ## Open problems the project is positioned to address

The subagent found no openly licensed, complete, machine-readable Indus corpus with object identifiers. ICIT is available on request without a stated license; Lipi has no detected license; the project's pinned Mayig subset has 179 faces; the CC BY epachayan repository covers Mohenjo-daro catalogues only and was unreviewed. The Helsinki repository marks Koskenniemi and Parpola's 1979 *Corpus of Texts in the Indus Script* as CC BY ([handle](http://hdl.handle.net/10138/342677)); the project's [acquisition record](../../research/corpus/primary/1979_extraction_assessment.md) also records CC BY 4.0 for the 1982 concordance, which the subagent could not locate through repository search. The earlier font-transcription pilot failed its frozen endpoint mainly through crop geometry. After clean vertical bounds, a post-reference development rerun matched 18 of 20 row counts, with 6 whole-compatible rows at top one and 16 at top five. A digitization would need a new frozen crop and matcher, fresh reserved rows and independent review. Its source is an interim 1979 edition that omits later finds.

Rajan and Sivanantham's 2025 *Indus Signs and Graffiti Marks of Tamil Nadu: A Morphological Study* was reported by PTI as finding Indus parallels for about 90% of graffiti marks; the subagent opened that report in The Week of 6 January 2025, and its exact URL was not retained. The subagent found no published matching criteria, chance baseline or formal critique. Changizi, Zhang, Ye and Shimojo, *American Naturalist* 167(5), 2006 (snippet), documents shape configurations shared across writing systems; no study was found using it as a null model for cross-script resemblance. The project holds both 2026 *Inscribed Potsherds* volumes and a pinned web catalogue. A test would apply one declared matching rule to the graffiti and to unrelated sign inventories of comparable complexity and compare the match rates. The study's own criteria would have to be reconstructed, so the test would assess a declared rule rather than the authors' unstated procedure.
