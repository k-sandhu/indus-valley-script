## Tamil source coverage

The current CICT PDF URL returned 404. A source inventory located the same URL's held 2 October copy, which we restored by hash. The title page identifies Mahadevan's *Early Tamil Epigraphy: From the Earliest Times to the Sixth Century C. E.*, revised and enlarged second edition, Volume I, with a 2020 imprint. It is a 57-page preview, not the full revised edition. Its figure list on printed p. xxxii names nine rings and three seals. The nine ring captions and first two seal captions have candidate joins to the 2003 records; this is dependent editorial evidence for existing objects. The third seal caption, “Vil Atiṉa...”, supplies an additional acquisition target. Its full catalogue page 67, physical object identifier, site and complete text are absent. We leave sign units, orthographic words and compound members null rather than turn the abbreviated caption into a complete legend. Full ring catalogue p. 70 is also outside the preview.

The Tamil Nadu institutional page timed out in the acquisition request. Its search snippet adds no object to the ledger. The 1998 Urkesh courtier PDF was acquired but has no text layer; it remains a source requiring page-image reading. No Tell Brak or Kültepe edition was acquired in this subset.

## Validation and next acquisition

The author check verifies all acquired source hashes and byte counts, unique candidate identifiers, source links, duplicate-join fields and the required fields for any admitted record. It reports zero admissions. It checks the structure and preservation of the ledger; it does not independently verify historical readings. A machine extraction of every acquired PDF and the exact displayed page coverage are recorded locally in `exposure-v1.json`. The old source files and frozen outputs are unchanged.

The next bounded acquisition should obtain revised catalogue pp. 67–70 and the associated seal and ring plates, establish whether the third seal has a documented findsite, and then transcribe any complete eligible legend. Akkadian growth needs the inscribed physical carriers in a full Tell Brak edition or a separately declared Old Assyrian period extension. Those sources must be joined against existing dies before counting them.

The strongest result is that the sources recovered here strengthen object identity without increasing the comparison population. The deciding test is a complete source-backed legend with a site and a physical carrier whose aliases and sign sequence do not join an existing legend.
