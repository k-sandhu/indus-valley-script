# Comparing proposed meanings with understood signs

The user requested comparisons between the signs assigned meanings in the earlier exercise and signs expressing the same meanings in writing traditions of comparable development. This source-led study uses the October 11 run-v6 assignments for all 713 Wells codes and records the October 12 visual-family map as a separate working extension. Its meanings remain proposals. Neither the older assignment nor the newer family description silently replaces the other.

The main historical comparison is developed Sumerian cuneiform and Egyptian hieroglyphic writing in the third millennium BCE. Old Babylonian Akkadian provides a later use of the cuneiform tradition. Mycenaean Greek Linear B provides a later administrative comparison. These are operational comparisons of developed writing and record use; their societies, media and inscription lengths differ. Sumerian and Akkadian share a writing tradition and do not count as two independent inventions of each compared shape.

Freeze the input bytes and their hashes before constructing the crosswalk. Cover every previously assigned code in an inventory audit, retaining tiers, alternative meanings and missing or conflicted drawings. Use the new visual-family definitions to organize candidate object meanings. Give the nine reviewed codes explicit entries, including cases where the assigned account function has no verified equivalent sign. Search the comparison sources by meaning before inspecting shapes. Record unsuccessful searches as bounded source gaps rather than absence from a language.

For each counterpart, distinguish the pictured referent, documented lexical value, and role in writing. A phonetic sign in a word, a classifier, a logogram and a complete multi-sign spelling require separate labels. A comb-shaped IVS closer and an understood comb word answer different questions. A plough is a related agricultural implement, not an exact harrow match. Carrying a staff does not identify a particular office.

Use primary institutional sign lists, lexica and editions. Preserve exact URLs, retrieval times, source hashes, dictionary IDs, sign IDs and text/token locators where available. Canonical Unicode glyphs are modern display forms. Their resemblance does not measure resemblance to a dated archaeological ductus. Record period-attested lexical examples separately from source-wide dictionary forms. Keep acquired pages, fonts, photographs and exhaustive token extracts in the ignored local companion, with source-specific rights recorded. Reuse existing local corpora by hash instead of acquiring them again.

The endpoint is a descriptive semantic crosswalk and a side-by-side catalogue. It estimates no probability of a proposed IVS meaning and conducts no shape-similarity significance test. The earlier unblinded resemblance and lexical-fit failures remain applicable; a quantitative graphic test would need dated glyph exemplars, independent encoding and same-complexity negative controls. Revisions after inspecting sources receive a separate amendment or new run. Preserve the older catalogues and all source witnesses.
