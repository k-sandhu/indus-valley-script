# Dholavira photographs and corpus bridge decisions

We inspected accession-labelled photographs for all ten frozen Dholavira cases and compared their source records with 62 candidate corpus families across 274 retained relationships. Four cases have an exact match to a candidate's published length, width and body thickness; a fifth matches length and width, with the corpus retaining rounded thickness values. All ten family bridges remain unresolved under the frozen admission rule: the candidate families have no linked corpus photograph in the held image index, and none carries the target's complete accession. The pass adds ten primary photograph records and specific measurement evidence without merging physical objects.

The block is PCF-DHV-005–014, frozen in [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) before graphics were displayed. We reused the held preliminary *Dholavira Report (preliminary, Jan 2015)* and the actual normalized and original corpus metadata, rather than treating the earlier register's candidate flags as established identities. The [source manifest](source-manifest-v2.json) records exact bytes, hashes, page coordinates, source routes and D: paths. Catalogue images cover nine PDF pages, listed in that manifest; the measurement table is on PDF pages 264–265, printed pages 247–248. Detailed comparisons remain local in `data/raw/2026-10-24-object-identity-pass2/bridge-decisions-v1.json`.

Seven targets have measurements in the primary table. Four have exact numerical agreement in all three comparable fields with one candidate family each. One further case has matching length and width, while its raw corpus thickness field is blank and another field records a rounded thickness range. These comparisons preserve extended dimensions and the separate body and maximum thickness fields. The measurements are evidence for prioritizing accession recovery. Their possible derivation from the same excavation tables means that the two appearances do not provide independent archaeological corroboration. The comparison rule for physical identity remains the one frozen before display.

Two source differences deserve explicit retention. One of the four dimensional candidates has seven encoded corpus tokens while the catalogue describes five signs. Counting conventions and the damaged source inscription need review before these quantities can be compared. Another dimensional candidate carries a different export excavation number from the accession printed beside the source photograph; the namespace relation is unknown. We preserved these alternatives and all 274 inherited relationships. The detailed source notes distinguish observed outlines and damage from descriptions of reverse features that the front photographs cannot verify.

## Access outcome

The public ICIT menu was recovered. It directs readers to request database access from its administrator. Both HTTP and HTTPS versions of its linked database entry returned HTTP 401; a version without the linked `www` prefix failed DNS resolution. The old ASI full-report URL returned 404. The official ASI cover PDF was acquired and inspected, but it has only two pages and supplies no final-edition catalogue concordance. The acquisition records retain the URLs, response failures, byte counts, timestamps and hashes. We made no contact or purchase.

The held image index contains no photograph link for any of the 62 candidate families. The compact face register agrees with that gap for this subset. A filename-prefix check alone would have been insufficient, so we checked the index's family identifiers directly. The inspected older-export metadata supplied no resolving accession. This bounded negative source result identifies the missing material precisely: corpus photographs or a concordance connecting the report's accession labels with the corpus family identifiers.
