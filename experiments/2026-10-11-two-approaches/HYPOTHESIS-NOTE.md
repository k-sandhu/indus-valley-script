# A typed record carried across an object's faces

Status: proposed theoretical extension, written before the two packages' results. The idea is to infer the organization of a record before selecting words for its signs. Learned sign classes can suggest which substitutions preserve a field. The faces of a single carrier can then show which fields belong together. This combines Rao's grammar recommendation with Mukhopadhyay's semantic-scope recommendation.

Consider an object with a longer inscription on one face and a short, repeated module on another. A candidate record schema places an operation or entitlement on the first face and a grade, quota or validity class on the second. A terminal sign could close a field or state its class. This permits a concrete administrative hypothesis: some tablets could carry a standing entitlement whose scope and limit were encoded separately. The entitlement might concern labour, a commodity, access or an issuer's responsibilities. Those alternatives require different contextual evidence even when they share the same graphic schema.

## Two linked levels of inference

A sign-class grammar predicts admissible order and substitution. For example, an abstract production `QUALIFIER* CLASS CLOSURE` permits several qualifiers followed by a class and a closing marker. These labels describe roles in a proposed model. They are not readings of particular Indus signs. The grammar package will test whether learned classes predict withheld texts better than positional and local transition models.

Carrier-level binding concerns relations between complete face modules. If a class on one face strongly restricts the module on another, the carrier may implement a typed relation. The administrative package will test that restriction against rewiring of partners within matched archaeological and graphic populations. Mould copying can create the same statistical restriction. Distinct object bundles and source-defined production groups therefore matter when estimating its reach.

The relation resembles a database constraint: one field limits valid values in another. A grammar can describe each field while the carrier binds the fields into one record. This is an analytical connection to formal records and type systems; the proposed ancient functions remain hypotheses. Full face sequences should not be concatenated into an invented reading order to obtain the relation.

## Competing mechanisms

| Mechanism | What the carrier could hold | Observation that would favour this mechanism over the other listed models |
|---|---|---|
| Standing entitlement | Allowed activity plus grade, quota or validity class | A bearer-linked object reused across several dated acts while the authorization module remains stable; independent indications of controlled access or occupational authorization |
| Transaction receipt | Event category plus quantity or settlement category | Independently counted deliveries or payments vary with the short module; dated event records change together with those counts |
| Issuer or bearer identity | A name, title, affiliation or corporate emblem plus an associated rank/category | Objects independently linked to the same person or corporate group retain a module across different commodities and operations |
| Commodity or ration specification | Goods or labour class plus quantity/measure or allocation category | Independently identified contents or recipient activities predict the class and measured amounts predict the short module |

The first experiments may favour a typed relation while leaving all four functions viable. Replacing the names of unobserved classes in a graphical model can leave every probability on observed signs unchanged. The administrative package will exhibit this equivalence explicitly. Resolving it requires an observation outside the inscription patterns used to fit the classes.

## Predictions to carry forward

The standing-entitlement hypothesis predicts more persistent class modules than the event-specific receipt hypothesis when one bearer can be followed across separate uses. The identity hypothesis predicts transfer of its stable module across goods and places associated with the same independently identified bearer. The commodity hypothesis predicts transfer across different bearers handling the same goods. These comparisons need bearer and goods labels obtained from the physical or archaeological context.

A qualifier that merely changes the drawing style should preserve both grammatical placement and its partner distribution after site, medium and copying controls. A qualifier that changes a functional subtype can preserve placement while shifting the distribution of partner modules or independently measured quantities. This separates allographic and functional hypotheses without choosing a language.

The proposal also predicts that some apparent exceptions to a single linear grammar will become regular when face role is included. That requires reliable surface identity, orientation and within-object relations. An improvement obtained by selecting face roles after observing the exceptions must be evaluated on new carrier families.

## Required witnesses

The useful next witnesses are registered objects connecting a face module with an independently observed operation, content, quantity, bearer or repeated use. Seal and impression photographs are different views of one object. Two transcriptions of the same source are different annotations of that source. Their contribution is improved observation rather than an increase in independent contextual cases.

One resolved example would establish a local constraint. Generalizing a class or scale requires withheld objects, alternative functions, archaeological dating and a source-checked sign concordance. The two packages will supply the measured constraints and failed predictions that determine which part of this proposed schema is worth pursuing.
