# Two extensions of published Indus research proposals

Started 11 October 2026. Status: exploratory research program. The user requested two approaches beyond the existing Steven Bonta extension, parallel work, imaginative hypotheses and adversarial review. The earlier Bonta endpoints and their semantic alternatives remain preserved.

The program develops two named prescriptions. Rao and colleagues propose inferring a grammar from recurring sign classes and ordering rules (*A Markov model of the Indus script*, 2009, Discussion, p. 13690). Mukhopadhyay proposes narrowing the inscriptions' semantic scope before assigning individual sign values (*Semantic scope of Indus inscriptions comprising taxation, trade and craft licensing, commodity control and access control: archaeological and script-internal evidence*, 2023, Introduction). The second approach also uses Frenez's request to test his branding assumptions and Rao's labour/ration model as competing explanations. Our extensions and those authors' prescriptions will be recorded separately.

## Questions and outputs

The grammar package asks whether a learned finite-state class grammar transfers across previously unseen inscription families and lengths. It compares predictive probability with tuned positional and Markov models. Class interpretation follows the measured behaviour; grammatical labels are hypotheses. Constructed administrative and compositional systems test the specificity of the assay.

The administrative package asks whether inscriptions on faces of the same physical carrier constrain one another beyond site, object type and selected sign frequencies. Competing models describe portable permission, identity, commodity/quantity and transaction records. Pairing, aliases, repeated templates and orientation must be audited before a coupling result is interpreted. A source-defined class of stroke marks can be used as a graphic variable without assigning a numerical scale.

The coordinator combines the results into explicit hypotheses and acquisition priorities. A useful result may be an improved grammar, a measurable binding between object faces, a failed prediction, or a demonstrated equivalence between semantic models. An attractive meaning that the observables cannot distinguish remains a hypothesis.

## Review and interpretation

Adversarial review checks probability normalization, leakage, reference-population matching, multiple selection, alias closure, reconstruction risks, source attribution and semantic identifiability. Reviewers may recompute outcomes and construct counterexamples. Repairs receive new output directories; earlier outcomes remain discoverable. The report connects each claim to exact analysis outputs and source observations.

All contributors follow `knowledge/editorial/WRITING.md`: maximize reader comprehension, explain ideas in connected prose, keep necessary shared assumptions together, and avoid repeated corrective comparisons or warnings that interrupt the argument. The report distinguishes source observations, fitted results, simulations and imaginative extensions where that distinction changes the claim.
