# Image review for the anchor expansion

The visual pass reviewed all **713 exact-code drawing witnesses** and assigned **673 codes to seventy working component families**. This includes **533 of the 567 codes previously marked unreviewed**. Forty codes remain outside the new family map, including thirty-four previously unreviewed codes. The number 533 measures codes supplied with a component-based comparison; it does not count 533 distinct lexical meanings. [Frozen family map](families.json), [freeze record](freeze-manifest.json).

The review used fifteen newly rendered, local-only font sheets, with fifty codes per sheet except the final sheet. Each code was rendered through Lipi’s documented U+E000-plus-decimal-code encoding. Two licensed chart contact sheets supplied sixty-seven family representatives; W142 and W272 were also viewed as separate cells. A further fourteen paired sheets compared every one of the **498 codes with an existing chart pointer** against its exact-font witness. The other 215 codes have no chart pointer in the starting inventory. The manifests give the actual code groups, image hashes and source paths seen. [First image review](image-review-manifest.json), [paired review](crosswalk-image-review-manifest.json).

The font supplies exact code distinctions in this project’s drawing witness. It is not an artifact photograph. The public chart comes from Koskenniemi and Parpola’s *Corpus of Texts in the Indus Script* (1979), University of Helsinki edition, under CC BY 4.0. Its graphic classes and printed identifiers differ from Wells codes. The paired sheets, which include the unresolved-rights font, remain local. Public records contain our observations, group memberships and source hashes. [Source and licence record](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/corpus-of-texts-in-the-indus-script/), [project redistribution policy](../../../knowledge/provenance/REDISTRIBUTION.md).

## Shared components and proposed functions

The new anchors extend a small set of visible operations: support a load, suspend an object, join units, contain material, divide a bounded space, repeat a row, or attach teeth to a stem. Each code receives one primary family, while its particular qualifiers and earlier inherited proposal remain separately discoverable. Added head marks, bars, inner grids and surrounding strokes do not automatically receive separate words.

| Family | Visible component and working interpretation | Codes / previously unreviewed |
|---|---|---|
| Carrier and sling-load | Shoulder-level support with paired suspended loads; extend *vivadha*, *śikya*, *śikyapāśa* and *yuga* to transport apparatus and service | 12 / 7 |
| Toothed-tool bearer | A legged figure holds forked or toothed extensions; compare *matya*, *kaṅkata* and cultivation service | 7 / 6 |
| Staff bearer | A figure holds one or two long uprights; compare *daṇḍin* and responsible-agent *adhikārin* | 4 / 3 |
| Flag or divided panel | A side panel on a staff has inner bars or exposed teeth; compare *koṣṭha*, *kośa* and *kaṅkata* | 5 / 4 |
| Bound stalks | Repeated upright stems share a lower band or rectangle; compare *stamba*, *śūka* and *kāṇḍa* | 11 / 10 |
| Animal or herd product | A horizontal body carries legs and a head/tail qualification; compare *paśu* and *go*, while retaining rack and composite-form rivals | 11 / 11 |
| Wheel or divided transport unit | A closed oval has radial sectors or crossbars; compare *cakra* and *yāna*, with round measures and lattice rivals | 7 / 5 |
| Woven or netted material | Diagonal lines close into repeated cells; compare *jāla* and *tantu*, with leaf venation and tool-hatching rivals | 2 / 1 |

Other families cover vessels, gateway frames, enclosed allotments, leaf forms, fish, birds, grouped creatures, pointed shafts, paired loops, joined diagonals, repeated curves and quantities. Several bounded or repeated shapes support only a broad material or record-function comparison. Their particular contents and qualifier meanings remain open. The exact Sanskrit senses, dictionary locators and cited literary scope belong to the separate [anchor ledger](../anchors/senses.json) and [family links](../anchors/family-links-v2.json).

The map declares a positional or contextual role before the new calculation: twenty-seven families predict a counted entity, twelve predict a closing category, ten predict a nonfinal entity and four predict an internal connector. Seventeen remain unconstrained because their pictures do not supply a defensible order prediction. Those seventeen cannot gain positional support merely because an unconstrained rule admits every slot. Whole-record counts can still expose which parts of a proposed material interpretation remain distinguishable.

The family map was fixed without reading the new complete-context result tables. Earlier project outcomes, including the closing carrier and panel examples, were already known. The new roles therefore support an exploratory compatibility test rather than a fresh blinded semantic validation. Context integration must retain each code’s own support and contradictions; a rare variant does not inherit a frequently occurring sibling’s evidence.

## Source conflicts and the closer-image correction

Pairing every available chart class with its font witness exposed eleven cases where the public mapped class should be suppressed as that code’s glyph: **W167, W168, W309, W354, W374, W375, W796, W817, W850, W860 and W930**. The chart can still be shown separately as the mapped source class with its differing observation. For example, W850’s font is a diamond while P355 is an oval; W930’s font has a peaked cover while P339 is a radial oval; W374’s font has crossing loops on a stem while P103 has lateral leaf-like branches. The [crosswalk audit](chart-crosswalk-audit.json) gives every reviewed code a class status and records the conflicting observations and source hashes.

Closer paired inspection also corrected three initial readings. W353 has a small U-shaped lower return, which the first broad view missed; its shared P091 prong/rail/loop class is usable with the ordinary variant limit. W354 has separate lower ends and carries the stronger P091 mismatch. W700 is one U-shaped outline, matching P310; the first repetition note came from reading a neighbouring column. W940–943 combine fish-like and segmented or arthropod-like creatures within curved sides, so the initial two-fish description was too narrow. W940’s initial suppression flag is withdrawn.

These corrections are preserved in [image-corrections-v1.json](image-corrections-v1.json) and the [integration supplement](integration-supplement-v1.json). The frozen family membership and positional roles remain unchanged. The supplement supplies corrected observations, broader competing anchor vocabulary and authoritative public-glyph dispositions. This keeps the initial map visible without allowing its mistaken picture description to determine the final viewer.

W272 is a further source challenge. Its segmented center and appendages support a small-creature comparison, while P070 includes an upward curved tail-like feature. Scorpion or insect competes directly with the earlier bird/messenger wording. W264–271 also retain segmented-creature, insect/scorpion and humanoid alternatives beside bird. Several upper terminals in W776–786 can be read as headed people in the chart or as looped/branching stems in the font. The common enclosure and terminal structure supports a compound family; it does not determine a plant species, messenger office or commodity.
