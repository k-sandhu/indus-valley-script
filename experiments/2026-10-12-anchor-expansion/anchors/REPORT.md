# Sanskrit comparisons for the October 12 extension

The ledger adds **forty selected senses** to the fifty-six used in run-v6. The strongest additions describe components that can be checked directly on a drawing: a carrying sling, a vessel-suspension cord, a bow, a grain awn, a jointed stalk, a thorn, a horn and a ladder. Other additions distinguish competing objects, such as a house, fenced plot, portable box, cooking-pot and ordinary vessel. The [ledger](senses.json) records each exact headword, sense ID, print page and literary citation scope. The [family links](family-links.json) connect those senses to the visual contributor’s proposed component families.

The six anchors carried forward from the external review are *vivadha*, *matya*, *kaṅkata*, *yuga*, *kośa* and *śreṇi*. Their extension proceeds through shared visible components. A carrying-pole design can supply a suspension hypothesis for other paired-load graphics; teeth and a spine can supply tool and ordered-row hypotheses; a bounded panel can supply enclosure, access and container alternatives. This procedure expands the comparisons available for whole-inscription tests. It supplies no particular Sanskrit pronunciation for an Indus sign.

## Exact source checks

Every selected sense was checked against the pinned Cologne digitization of Monier-Williams’s 1899 *A Sanskrit-English Dictionary* and the project’s existing dictionary index. The pin is `55e8addbd96e8d9001b8789026b026763f4049c1`; the local `mw.txt` SHA256 is `555ecf5aadaaf21e5965d6b3b419f428fc9ab9237f563627001ceca468d0cff8`. The [verification record](verification.json) checks headword, entry ID and page/column for all ninety-six senses. Each ledger row also gives an exact line pointer to the pinned source. Selected source bodies remain in the ignored local companion. [Pinned dictionary](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-orig/blob/55e8addbd96e8d9001b8789026b026763f4049c1/v02/mw/mw.txt).

Internet checks used Cologne’s scan index and original dictionary page URLs. The page records for the carrying sling, granary, plant clump, grain awn, ladder, bow, jointed stalk and small-animal rivals were located. Requests to retrieve the complete scan index directly returned HTTP 500 and 403. Browser screenshots of pages 1011 and 771 timed out. Consequently, the gloss and citation selections below rest on exact digitized records; this package does not claim a new visual verification of all printed entries. [Official scan index](https://sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MWScan/form.php?sfx=pdf).

The dictionary’s introduction explains the scope of its citation abbreviations: an RV label places a cited use in the Rigvedic-and-later literature, while L. identifies native lexicon evidence in its editorial convention. A citation dates the use cited by the lexicographer; it does not supply a first-attestation study or an Indus date. The ledger therefore keeps the selected sense’s own sources beside its meaning. [1899 introduction, p. xviii](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MWScan/MWScanpdf/mw010022.pdf).

The carrying-sling and suspended-vessel records provide a closer account of hanging components than another general synonym for porter. Their neighbouring *śikya* sense 216504 means balance-string and cites Wilson. That rival is useful when a beam and two suspended objects are present, but its separate citation should remain visible. [Print page 1069](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MWScan/MWScanpdf/mw1069-zAsanadUSaka.pdf).

Three spelling issues matter for exact lookup. MW indexes **sampuṭa** under SLP1 `sampuwa`; the ledger displays the nasalized spelling **saṃpuṭa** and retains `sampuṭa` as its comparison headword. The bow entry is **dhanus**, while **dhanuṣ** is a separately indexed compound form (99604). The granary’s canonical key is **kusūla** (53680); **kuśūla** is an attested variant and a cross-reference. Searching the first matching spelling without these distinctions would select the wrong entry or an unresolved pointer.

## Object and action competitors

The additional vessel senses are *droṇa* (98002), *kalaśa* (45762), *sthālī* (255299) and *pātra* (120799). They distinguish a trough/bucket, a jar, a cooking vessel and a general receptacle. *Saṃpuṭa* (236160) offers a covered box or casket, with a later selected citation. Separate capacity records for *droṇa*, *kalaśa* and *pātra* exist, but they are not selected here. Complete inscriptions can test whether a global vessel family behaves as a counted entity, a commodity-bearing category or a closing container class. They cannot identify its contents from a bare vessel outline.

For bounded designs, *gṛha* (66501) supplies house/habitation with RV/AV citations, *kakṣā* (41532) supplies wall/courtyard/room with later citations, and *vṛti* (203992) supplies fence or enclosed cultivation with Manu/MBh citations. *Kusūla* (53680) supplies granary with a Bhāgavata citation. The nearby AV citation belongs to a different goblin sense, 53683; it must not be used to make the granary sense Vedic. Existing *kośa*, *koṣṭha*, *kṣetra* and *dvāra* keep portable-container, compartment, plot and access alternatives available. [Granary print page 298](https://sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MWScan/MWScanpdf/mw0298-kuSThacikitsita.pdf).

*Bandha* (142631), *pāśa* (123319) and *sūtra* (251185) distinguish tying/binding, a loop or noose, and a cord/thread. A loop chain may depict an object, an operation or a relation. To prefer an internal connector, the fixed mapping must recur between independently classified entities across complete strings and show fewer conflicts than an object/name interpretation. The dictionary’s later deposit, credit and abstract-rule senses should not enter that test silently.

The plant additions *sasya*, *aṅkura*, *puṣpa*, *pattra* and *pallava* separate crop, shoot, blossom, leaf and bud. Several pictured designs can fit more than one of these. A round body on a stalk can be fruit, seed, bud or an implement; a branched form can be vegetation, tally or tool. Source chronology also differs: the selected *sasya* and *puṣpa* records cite AV; *aṅkura* cites Suśruta; *pallava* cites epic/classical sources. [Grain-awn print page 1085](https://sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MWScan/MWScanpdf/mw1085-zuSkavraNa.pdf), [plant-clump print page 1257](https://sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MWScan/MWScanpdf/mw1257-skabdha.pdf).

## Small-animal challenge

The earlier W272 bird/messenger comparison prompted a separate graphic challenge during this extension. The Sanskrit contributor has not inspected the new full-context tables or settled the drawing identity. The ledger supplies **vṛścika** (204836, p. 1011,3), scorpion, and **makṣikā** (154017, p. 771,3), fly/bee, alongside *śakuni* and *pakṣin*. The exact scorpion record supplies no literary citation. Its neighbour’s Varāhamihira/Purāṇa references belong to the zodiacal Scorpio sense, 204837. The selected *makṣikā* record cites RV but includes both fly and bee, so it cannot select a species or a honey commodity. [Scorpion print page 1011](https://sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MWScan/MWScanpdf/mw1011-vRddhayogazataka.pdf), [fly/bee print page 771](https://sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MWScan/MWScanpdf/mw0771-bhRkuTi.pdf).

The useful graphic test counts leg-like projections, identifies the head and any curved terminal tail, and checks whether supposed wings are paired limbs or geometric additions. Its family link remains conditional on the visual reviewer’s classification. If insect or scorpion features survive that check, the old messenger metaphor loses its pictorial basis. If the drawing remains ambiguous, the complete semantic walk should preserve the generic small-animal slot and its competing classes.

## Integration scope and observed repairs

The contributor fixed these dictionary choices without reading the new complete-context outcome tables. Family links describe graphic hypotheses and their rivals; they do not add a corpus support tier. Root integration can use the new exact vocabulary while the complete-record contributor calculates support, split stability, substitutions and contradictions with a globally fixed sign map.

The build initially used a repeated prefix newline count to create source line pointers. It was stopped before writing outputs and replaced with a single-pass counter; the successful ledger build completed in five seconds. The build refuses to overwrite completed outputs. The forty additions exclude two initially considered generic hand/handle comparisons to make room for the explicit W272 small-animal challenge. Historical run-v6 senses and files remain unchanged.

An independent parser also checks all ninety-six source line pointers, comparison-headword transliterations and citation extractions, and confirms that every original field of the fifty-six reused senses remains unchanged. Its [separate verification](verification-independent.json) uses the same pinned witness and adds no independent historical attestation.
