# Layout dates: the coordinator's judgement

Written on 21 October 2026 before either measurer had started, so before any bearing of this package existed, and before any output of the other package was opened. The tables and this note are hashed in `DATES.sha256`.

The dating run ([dating-v1/](../dating-v1/), Codex `gpt-6.1-sol`, prompt [CODEX-PROMPT-DATING.md](../CODEX-PROMPT-DATING.md)) assembled the phase sequences, layout phases, candidate dates and 238 radiocarbon determinations for 33 towns and 53 parts, with page locators. It applied the three levels of evidence of the other package's rule strictly: a candidate needs a published Bayesian start boundary, a published calibrated start for the layout phase based on radiocarbon from the site, or an excavator's start from cross-dating without radiocarbon. Seventeen parts in eleven towns have such a candidate. Several towns that every synthesis places in the Mature Harappan period have none under this reading, because no inspected source states a calibrated start for the phase in which their exposed layout was built: Mohenjo-daro, Lothal, Surkotada, Banawali and Rakhigarhi among the required towns.

We keep two tables. The strict table [dates-strict-v1.csv](dates-strict-v1.csv) holds the dating run's seventeen candidates unchanged; it is our primary date table, the one used in variant V2 of the protocol, because it applies the frozen rule as written. Two of its entries carry a flag. Dholavira's castle is dated by the revised start of Stage I, 3551 BCE (Sengupta and colleagues 2020), the nucleus that the later castle grew from, while its exposed walls were built later. Bhirrana's planned settlement is dated to 4551 BCE from a published chronology (Sarkar and colleagues 2016) that is much earlier than the conventional sequence of the region.

The liberal table [dates-liberal-v1.csv](dates-liberal-v1.csv) adds a date for parts without a strict candidate wherever the dating record attributes radiocarbon determinations to the layout phase, or, where the layout phase is described only as the site's Mature Harappan occupation, to that occupation. Each determination is calibrated with IntCal20 by our own [calibrate.py](../analysis/calibrate.py), and the part takes the median calendar year of the oldest determination, the sensitivity rule of the other package, with σD equal to the larger of 150 years and half the 68.3% interval of that determination ([calibrations-v1.json](calibrations-v1.json), code [build_dates.py](../coordinator/build_dates.py)). Mohenjo-daro has no listed determination for an early layout, so it takes the midpoint of the calibrated result for a core from its first mud-brick perimeter wall, 2708–2576 cal BC, reported from the 2025–26 excavations and quoted in a 2026 article; we have not seen the preliminary report itself. The liberal table is a sensitivity variant: it dates nineteen more parts, including Mohenjo-daro (2642 BCE), Lothal (2156 BCE), Surkotada (2706 BCE) and Rakhigarhi (2591 BCE), and it rests on determinations whose link to the first construction of the measured walls is weaker than the rule asks.

Banawali, Juni Kuran, Desalpur, Kot Diji, Sutkagen-dor, Kunal, Mitathal, Navinal, Nausharo, Ganweriwala, Karanpura and Pabumath have no date in either table. Dholavira's lower town, Harappa's mound beneath the modern town, Shikarpur's and Gola Dhoro's extramural areas and Rakhigarhi's middle town have no layout phase established.

A town's date is the date of the part that supplies its deviation, matched by the part name the measurers record. Where a measurer's part does not correspond to one of these parts, the coordinator assigns it to the part whose area contains the measured lines, by the plans, and records the assignment beside the result.
