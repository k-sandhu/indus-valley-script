# Measurer B log, share H (Haryana, Rajasthan, Indian Punjab)

This log covers the eight towns of share H: Kalibangan, Banawali, Rakhigarhi, Farmana, Bhirrana, Kunal, Mitathal and Karanpura, worked in that order, and one town we added under the brief's provision for further towns, Tarkhanewala-Dera (District Ganganagar, Rajasthan). The measurer is Claude Opus 5.5 (model ID claude-opus-5-5) running as a Claude Code subagent; no further subagents were used. We read `AGENTS.md`, `knowledge/editorial/WRITING.md`, the measurement rules, our brief, `tools/README.md`, the tool code, the infrastructure run notes and `infra-v1/validation-v1/`. The validation gives the empirical scale of imagery errors: runway segments of 100, 200, 300 and 400 m have RMS residuals of 4.27, 1.76, 0.99 and 0.75 degrees, and stated errors understate observed residuals by a factor of about 1.5. We keep the tool's stated sigma in every record and treat it as a lower bound.

## Working method and helper scripts

Renders and crops go to `data/raw/2026-10-21-town-orientations-verification/measure-B/<slug>/`, picks and the `measure_lines.py` results to `measure-B/picks/<slug>/`, town inputs to `measure-B/town-inputs-H/` and town records to `measure-B/towns/`. All Python that reads imagery, plans or downloads runs with the prepared environment and `-I`. The package tools worked as documented and were not modified. We wrote reading and checking aids in `measure-B/scripts-H/`, none of which computes a recorded value:

- `pxgrid.py` copies a render, optionally cropped and enlarged, with a labelled grid in the original render's pixel coordinates, so picks can be read from zoomed views.
- `probe.py` lists the positions of dark runs along chosen rows or columns of a plan render; we used it to place picks on dashed and hand-drawn lines where a zoomed image was too narrow to read.
- `coords.py` converts between imagery render pixels, UTM and WGS84 with a render sidecar.
- `overlay.py` draws the line work of a plan over an imagery render with a trial similarity, as a visual aid for identification only.
- `trylines.py` runs `measure_lines.refine` on each pick separately without writing, to find a failing pick before the recorded run (the tool stops at the first failure).
- `drawlines.py` draws picked (yellow) and refined (red) lines over a fresh copy of a render, for the required visual check of every fit.
- `build_town.py` assembles a town input for `systems.py` from the `measure_lines.py` results, adding `source_id`, a source locator and the files used, and applying the grouping labels chosen at the systems stage.

One property of the line fit matters for low-resolution plan scans. When a 1-bit bitmap is rendered at several render pixels per scan pixel, an almost axis-parallel edge becomes a staircase, and the fit's one-pixel consensus threshold locks onto a single step, returning an exactly axis-parallel line with a near-zero residual. We therefore render bitmap plans at one render pixel per scan pixel (72 dpi for a figure placed at one pixel per point, 300 dpi for a 300 dpi page scan), where neighbouring steps fall inside the threshold and the fit follows the mean slope.

## Kalibangan

The Sentinel-2 composite (nine scenes, 2019-2026) shows the site as a bare, bright area about 630 m across centred near UTM 43N E 415600 N 3260750 (29.47338 N, 74.12950 E), which contains the Wikidata starting point and is surrounded by canal-colony fields laid out on the cadastral grid; a dense village lies to the south-east. We rendered it at 3 km, 1.5 km, 800 m and 500 m in RGB, NIR, SWIR and brightness, with and without edge enhancement and with stretches tightened to the mound. The mound surface is mottled at the 10 m pixel scale in every layer, and no straight feature inside it corresponds to a wall, street or platform of the plans. The straight north and east edges of the bright area follow the surrounding field grid and are field boundaries. A trial overlay of the ASI plan (scaled from its scale bar and turned by its arrow) fits the KLB-1, KLB-2 and KLB-3 layout inside the bright area, but it can be shifted by more than 100 m without any feature confirming or refuting a position, so no registration was attempted. The imagery record for Kalibangan is "inspected, not measurable": features not resolvable at 10 m.

Two plans were measured. The held ASI Memoir 110, *Excavations at Kalibangan: The Harappans (1960-69), Part II* (2020), has no settlement plan in its architectural sense, but Fig. 22.2 on PDF p. 590 (printed p. 1288), "Location of Harappan Cemetery (KLB-8)", is a 1960-69 survey plan of the whole site showing the fortifications of KLB-1 and KLB-2, the KLB-3 ritual area, the cemetery and the Ghaggar bank. It is printed rotated on the page and stored as a 626 by 448 pixel JBIG2 bitmap. Its north symbol is a needle with one filled half, a circled N and a split tail; nothing on the plan or caption says true or magnetic, so we classed it unspecified and note that the needle form may follow a magnetic-needle convention. The scale bar's labels are barely legible; we read it as 100-0-100 m, which makes KLB-2 about 360 by 240 m. A first set of picks on a 300 dpi render produced exactly axis-parallel fits locked to bitmap steps (picks v1, no recorded output); picks v2 on a 72 dpi render gave fits that follow the drawn walls. The arrow is only 32 scan pixels long, and with a one-pixel endpoint uncertainty its error is 2.5 degrees, which dominates every line of this source. We measured eight wall lines: the four KLB-1 walls lie at theta -6.7 to -11.1 degrees from the arrow, the KLB-2 north and south walls at -9.9 and -9.8, and the long KLB-2 east and west walls at -2.8 and -0.9. The lower-city enclosure is therefore drawn as a parallelogram skewed by about 8 degrees, and the walls running across both mounds share one direction. The interior streets of KLB-2 on this plan are drawn among dense contours and dashed trench lines that we could not separate reliably, so we did not measure them.

The second plan is Fig. 3.7 of an unattributed University of Lucknow chapter (PDF p. 11, printed p. 49, "The site plan of Kalibangan (courtesy ASI)"), a schematic redrawing at 300 dpi with a long arrow through a circled triangle and a 25-0-100 m scale bar. Its first picks (v1) placed corridors over both lines of the double-dashed streets, and several fits jumped between lines; in v2 every street pick follows the western line of its pair with a 3-pixel corridor, the two cross lanes were dropped because their dashed lines wander by up to 15 pixels, and the KLB-1 south wall is fitted on its inner face. On this drawing the KLB-2 north and south walls and four streets lie at -11.9 to -13.7 degrees from its arrow, the KLB-1 cross and south walls at -13.1 and -13.8, and the KLB-2 east and west walls at -0.6 and +2.5. Parallel streets on the drawing differ among themselves by up to about 2 degrees, a drafting error not covered by the fit sigma.

We grouped lines by mound and direction; the picks carried provisional system labels, and the town input records the original label wherever we changed it. The rule selects `klb1-walls` (4 lines, 427 m, theta -9.55, SE 1.28) as the main system of Fig. 22.2 and `klb2-grid` (6 lines, 933 m, theta -12.56, SE 0.30) for Fig. 3.7. On Fig. 22.2 we regard the cross-wall direction shared by KLB-1 and KLB-2 (-9.6 to -9.8) as the better summary of the town layout. The SE of Fig. 22.2 systems comes from the rule's propagation formula, which treats the line errors as independent although all eight share the same 2.5-degree arrow error. Both arrows are of unknown reference, so neither plan value is a true bearing. Relative to the cross-wall direction, the two drawings' arrows differ by about 3 degrees, within the Fig. 22.2 arrow error.

The Lucknow chapter text on the same page says that Harappan towns consist of walled mounds "oriented in different directions"; it gives no figure and was not used. We searched for the 2015 Part I volume with the architectural plans; it is sold but not openly available. A University of Lucknow teaching note on Kalibangan (`downloads-H/lko-durgesh-kalibangan/source.pdf`, 265,410 bytes, SHA-256 17449cc2a13edbf954d66f4997c5fc42b9bbc099d0ea44b9562c8fb0f7421d0d, retrieved 2026-10-21T06:21:39Z from https://lkouniv.ac.in/site/writereaddata/siteContent/202003281454237730durgesh_Kalibangan.pdf, no licence notice observed) proved to be text only.

## Banawali

The composite (five scenes, 2019-2023) shows the protected mound as a bare, roughly round area about 220 m across centred at UTM 43N E 537960 N 3274365 (29.59853 N, 75.39201 E), 1 km south-west of the village. We rendered it at 2.5 km, 700 m and 600 m in all four layers, with edge enhancement and tight stretches. The mound has a curved brighter rim on its north and west, which matches the form of the curved acropolis wall on the plans but is not a straight feature. The straight outer fortification of the plans, about 300 by 280 m, lies largely under fields and leaves no trace at 10 m; a straight bright north-south line east of the mound is a modern track. The imagery record is "inspected, not measurable".

The plan-library entry, R. S. Bisht's Fig. 1 in *Puratattva* 29 (1998-99), PDF p. 47 of the IGNCA scan, printed p. 33, is a 100 dpi greyscale scan with a compass-rose north symbol on a stem and the letter N, and a "50 0 50 meters" scale bar. The held *Indian Archaeology 1987-88 - A Review* prints the same drawing as its Fig. 1 (PDF p. 30, printed p. 22), also at 100 dpi, with a plain arrow and N instead of the compass rose. Both arrows are unspecified. We measured both prints as separate sources because their north symbols were drawn separately. On the first pass we read the Puratattva scale bar's 0 at the fourth subdivision tick, which made lengths 23 per cent too long; the IAR print, at the same page scale, has an unambiguous 0-50 m bar of 60.5 pixels, and a second look at the Puratattva bar showed five 10 m subdivisions ending at the 0 tick at x 311. Picks v2 correct the scale and leave every direction unchanged.

On each print we measured the outer southern wall, the two straight stretches of the outer western wall (which bends near its middle), the northern wall, the eastern wall through its excavated black stretches, and the double-dashed street running south from the bastion in the middle of the northern wall. The enclosure is an irregular quadrilateral. Its southern wall and the southern stretch of the western wall lie within 1 degree of the arrow (theta 0.2 and 0.3 on Puratattva, 0.9 and 0.7 on IAR); the northern wall and the northern stretch of the western wall lie at +8.8 to +11.9; the eastern wall at -9.7 to -12.3; and the street at +2.7 and +3.9. The rule picks `sw-walls` as the main system on both prints (Puratattva 459 m, theta 0.21, SE 0.30; IAR 463 m, theta 0.80, SE 0.30). The north and east walls diverge from it by about 10 degrees in opposite senses, so the main system describes the southern and western sides of the town rather than a common direction of the whole circuit. Relative to the same drawn walls, the two north symbols differ by 0.85 degrees.

IAR 1987-88 Fig. 2 (PDF p. 32), a section across the defences in trench TrC2, is labelled NNE and SSW at its ends; we did not use it. No other Banawali plan was found in the held IAR volumes for 1976-77, 1982-83 and 1999-2000.

## Farmana

The site was levelled for agriculture, and the composite (five scenes, 2018-2022) shows only fields around the excavators' coordinate (29 deg 02' 22" N, 76 deg 18' 21" E); the backfilled mud-brick houses are far below 10 m. The imagery record is "inspected, not measurable", and the located point is the published coordinate.

The plans are good. The plan library lists four vector drawings in the 2008 report (Shinde, Osada, Uesugi and Kumar, *A Report on Excavations at Farmana 2007-08*), and the held 2011 final report (Shinde, Osada and Kumar, eds., *Excavations at Farmana, District Rohtak, Haryana, India, 2006-2008*) adds the Central Area plan, Fig. 5.68 (PDF p. 111, 1:500, a 100 dpi image), which has no north arrow. We tied it to north through its base map: Fig. 3.2 shows the same contours and 5 m trench grid, the report's methodology chapter (PDF p. 34) says "All the trenches in the main habitation area were oriented in cardinal directions (Figures 3.1 and 3.2)", and Fig. 3.1 shows the grid with a compass star marked N parallel to it. On Fig. 5.68 we therefore used a north-south grid line as the north line, with class unspecified because the report does not say whether its cardinal directions are true or magnetic. The 2008 Fig. 19, "Main Trenches: Plan of Structures and Features", is a vector drawing of the same Central Area with a compass star, and we measured it as an independent drawing of the same walls. Figs. 11, 14 and 17 of the 2008 report are trench plans with their own arrows in other parts of the site: Trench 3X and Trench 3A3 in Locality 3 and Structure no. 1 in Trench 2.

On the vector plans the wall bands are coloured fills with black outlines and brick hatching. We fitted wall faces as edges, placing picks from dark-run probes across each band, and checked every fit on a drawn render. Several faces turned out not to be single straight lines: on Fig. 19 the west face of Complex No. 2 steps near its middle, the green wall south of Lane no. 1 jogs, the north wall of Complex No. 2 steps, and the south face of the courtyard wall bends at a junction. The v1 fits across those jogs followed brick lines inside the bands, so later picks restrict each line to one straight stretch (Fig. 19 picks v2 to v4). On Fig. 5.68 the two Lane no. 1 picks first ran past the wall ends (v1, corrected in v2), and the v3 picks correct two identification texts after comparison with the room labels of Fig. 19; no direction changed in that step.

The Central Area has one dominant direction. On Fig. 5.68 the house blocks, both sides of Main Street and both sides of Lane no. 1 form `central-main` (7 lines, 106 m, theta -21.52, SE 0.54) relative to the grid; on Fig. 19 the same walls give `central-main` (9 lines, 59 m, theta -22.66, SE 0.52) relative to its compass star. The long wall closing the Central Courtyard on the south, with two shorter walls, is turned about 6 degrees less on both plans (theta -15.35 and -15.79) and forms a second system. Trench 3A3 (theta -20.84) follows the Central Area direction, Structure no. 1 in Trench 2 is turned a little further (-25.97, SE 1.04), and the houses in Trench 3X lie on the grid's cardinal directions (-1.24). All five sources come from one excavation team on one grid, so their north references are not independent. The notes text in the town record quotes the two Central Area means as about -21.6 and -22.2; it was written before the systems run, and the computed values above govern.

We met stated orientations of the Farmana structures in the report texts and used none of them. The paragraph above Fig. 5.68 (2011 report, PDF p. 111) gives a numerical orientation for Structure Complex No. 2, which we read before we had measured anything on that page. The 2008 report gives numerical orientations for Structure Complex No. 1 (PDF p. 34) and for the town as a whole (PDF p. 36), legible in the page thumbnails we used to locate the figures, and its text on PDF p. 28 describes the Trench 3X walls as running north-south. Our values come only from lines fitted on the drawings against the drawn north reference.

## Mitathal

The composite (five scenes, 2018-2022) shows the modern village south-east of the starting point and fields, a canal and a bright bare patch to the west; the report places the twin mounds 1.5 km west of the village, but the excavated cuttings were small (25 x 6 m and 10 x 6 m) and backfilled, and nothing at 10 m can be tied to them. The imagery record is "inspected, not measurable".

The held report is Suraj Bhan, *Excavation at Mitathal (1968) and Other Explorations in the Sutlej-Yamuna Divide* (Kurukshetra University, 1975; IGNCA scan 60032). It has two fold-out plans at 100 dpi. Plan MTL 2 (Fig. 4, PDF p. 34) covers the 10 x 6 m cutting on the western mound and draws the Period IIA (Harappan) and IIB (Late Harappan) walls brick by brick, with a needle-like north symbol (a line with a loop at its upper end, a cross-bar and the letter N) and a metre scale whose ticks agree with the trench pegs 1 m apart. We measured six faces of Period IIA walls, located with face traces. The walls of Structures 10, 15, 16 and 19 share one direction (system `mtl2-iia`, 4 lines, 4.8 m, theta +17.09, SE 0.57); the walls of Structure 12 (+30.5) and Structure 9 (+3.3) depart from it. The faces are only 0.7-1.6 m long, and the brick-by-brick drawing makes them irregular at the scale of one brick. Plan MTL 1 (PDF p. 32), the 25 x 6 m cutting on the eastern mound, was not measured: its only candidate north symbol is a narrow notched arrowhead at the edge of the fold-out without a letter N, partly lost at the page edge, and its axis is ambiguous by about 8 degrees depending on whether its straight edge or its bisector is taken.

While reading the site description to identify the trenches we met three direction statements and used none: trench MTL-1 is described as running "from south-east to north-west" and Mitathal 2 as having its longer axis north-south (p. 4), and the Period IIA streets are said to be "laid east-west and north-south" (p. 7).

## Karanpura

The site was levelled for cultivation, and the composite (five scenes, 2019-2023) shows sandy fields north-east of the village with no mound; the imagery record is "inspected, not measurable". The plan-library entry (Prabhakar and Korisettar 2017, *Current Science*, Fig. 10) is a contour and trench layout without walls. The held *Indian Archaeology 2013-14 - A Review* reports the 2012-13 excavation with an Early Harappan structural plan of Area B (Figs. 11-12, PDF p. 95), a smaller one of Area D (Fig. 13, PDF p. 97, short wall stubs, not measured) and a photograph of a Mature Harappan house (Plate 38); there is no plan of Mature Harappan architecture.

We measured the Area B plan and flagged the period in the source id (`plan-iar2013-fig11-early-harappan`) and in every line, so that a comparison restricted to the Mature Harappan period can drop it. Fig. 11 is a 98 dpi raster with an eight-point compass rose whose arrowhead marks north and an excavation grid labelled at 10 m intervals, which we used for the scale because the graphic bar's labels are too blurred to read. The walls of the lower building complex lie at theta -8.5 to -11.6 (system `lower-complex`, 6 lines, 32.2 m, theta -10.10, SE 0.46) and the two long parallel walls crossing the area at -16.2 and -17.5 (system `long-walls`, 2 lines, 32.6 m, theta -16.98, SE 0.77). The rule selects `long-walls` by a margin of 0.4 m in total length; we regard `lower-complex`, with six walls of two rectangular buildings, as the better summary of the Area B layout. The notes field of the town record contains an unfinished phrase about the main system ("the rule's main system by length? see systems"); the values above are the computed ones, and we did not rewrite the record because outputs are never overwritten.

While checking the IAR captions we met text on PDF p. 110 describing Mature Harappan walls as having a "northwest - southeast orientation" and the fortification as running "in a northwest to southeast direction"; it was not used.

## Tarkhanewala-Dera (added town)

Searching the held literature of the region for further plans, we found P. K. Trivedi, *Excavations at Tarkhanewala-Dera and Chak 86 (2003-2004)*, ASI Memoir 99 (2009), an Internet Archive copy among the post-catalogue-finds sources. Tarkhanewala-Dera, in District Ganganagar, Rajasthan, has Mature Harappan houses of mud brick, and the report's fold-out Figs. 4 and 5 (PDF pp. 35 and 37, 300 dpi images) are plans of the excavated Harappan structures, each with a feathered north arrow marked N, unspecified, and a 0-4 m scale bar. We added the town under the slug `tarkhanewala-dera`. It has no composite in the imagery manifest, so it is a plans-only record, located at the report's coordinate (29 deg 14' N, 73 deg 14' E).

The walls are drawn as rows of outlined bricks. Fits across the whole band at lower resolutions (25 and 50 dpi trial renders) gave directions that changed by up to 6 degrees between resolutions, so we fitted edges instead, each on the cleanest outer outline of a wall. To find those outlines we wrote `scripts-H/outline.py`, which traces both outer outlines of a band along perpendicular profiles and reports their straightness; outlines with an RMS of 1.0-2.8 pixels seeded the picks, and `measure_lines.py` made the recorded fits on 150 dpi renders. On Fig. 4 six walls (Str. 7A, 7C, 7E, 8, 10A and 5B) form the main system (19.3 m, theta +1.11, SE 0.91); the long Str. 17 in the eastern trenches lies at -5.0 and Str. 5D at +8.7. On Fig. 5 the walls of Str. 19A-C and 20A-B form one system (15.7 m, theta -5.75, SE 0.56), with Str. 19 and Str. 20 three degrees from perpendicular. The two arrows were drawn separately, and the 6.9-degree difference between the two main systems may lie in the arrows rather than in the walls.

We also examined the Dhalewan report (ASI Memoir 115, Madhu Bala and Vishnu Kant), whose Period IC (Mature Harappan) houses and street lie in Mansa district, Punjab. Its site plan, Fig. 4.8 (PDF p. 70), and its house plans are 72 dpi colour rasters: the period colours and the scale can only just be read, and the outline traces either reached their search limits or scattered by 3-5 pixels. We did not measure Dhalewan and created no town file for it; a better scan of the memoir would make it measurable.

## Tool behaviour and checks

The package tools ran without failure and were not modified. Two behaviours needed care. `measure_lines.py` stops at the first failing pick, so we used `trylines.py` to test picks one by one before each recorded run. Fits on enlarged 1-bit bitmaps lock onto pixel steps, which we avoided by rendering at native resolution, as described above. The validation's calibration applies here as well: the conditional plan sigmas (often 0.05-0.4 degrees for a line) are far smaller than the drafting irregularities visible on the drawings (up to 2 degrees between parallel streets on the Lucknow Kalibangan drawing), and the north reference of every plan in this share is unspecified.

Before finishing we reran `measure_lines.measure` on every picks file used in a town record and compared each line's bearing, theta, sigma and length with the record, reran `systems.town_result` on every town input, and rebuilt every town input from its spec and ran `systems.py` into a scratch directory; all town files, inputs and the summary reproduced exactly (`scripts-H/verify.py` and the scratch comparison). Every line carries a source locator and a positive sigma, and every plan source used for lines carries a north class, its evidence and a survey-year field. Superseded picks and lines files are kept beside the versions named in the records.

## Independence and limits

We did not open the other measurer's directories, `experiments/2026-10-21-town-axes/`, `data/raw/2026-10-21-town-axes/`, the package protocol, the Codex prompt files or the excluded analysis, astronomy, dating and notes directories, and we did not open the *Interim Reports* vol. 2 scan. Listing the package's top level showed the names of those files and directories; their contents were left alone. We used no commercial imagery and no OpenStreetMap geometry, and ran no Git command that changes the repository. Two downloads were made, each into its own directory under `downloads-H/`, with URL, time, size, hash and rights observation recorded in `sources-H.json`. The held literature we read lies under `research/literature/sources/` and under `data/raw/` of other experiments, outside the forbidden directories. The plan-search JSON files under `infra/plan-search/` were not opened, because the brief limits the infrastructure raw directory to its imagery and plans. Free space on C: stayed above 10 GB throughout.

## Boldest reading

Every Harappan town in this share that could be measured from plans has its main walls turned from the plan arrow by a consistent amount within each drawing: about -10 degrees across both mounds at Kalibangan on the 1960-69 survey plan, about -22 degrees for the whole Central Area at Farmana on its excavation grid, about +17 degrees in the Mitathal cutting, and within about 1 degree of the arrow for the south-western circuit at Banawali and the main houses of Tarkhanewala-Dera Fig. 4. Read at full strength, these plans show towns of the Ghaggar plain laid out on house grids that differ from each other by more than 20 degrees, the opposite of a shared north-south rule. The test that would decide it is to fix the north reference of each drawing: register Fig. 22.2 of Kalibangan, the Banawali plan and the Farmana grid to sub-metre imagery or to surveyed control points, and see whether a spread of 20 degrees or more between towns survives once every value is a true bearing.
