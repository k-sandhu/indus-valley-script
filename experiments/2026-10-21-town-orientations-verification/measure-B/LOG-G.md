# Measurer B, Gujarat share: working log

This log records what we looked at, what we could and could not identify, and every judgement made while measuring the Gujarat share (Lothal, Dholavira, Surkotada, Kanmer, Shikarpur, Juni Kuran, Gola Dhoro, Desalpur, Khirsara, Kuntasi, Rojdi, Rangpur, Kotada Bhadli, Pabumath, Navinal). The measurer is Claude Opus 5.5 (model ID claude-opus-5-5) in the Claude Code harness, working as measurer B, share G.

## Working method and shared assumptions

We read `AGENTS.md`, `knowledge/editorial/WRITING.md`, the measurement rules, the brief for share G, the tool guide, the tool code under `tools/`, the infrastructure run notes, the validation summary and the three source indexes in `infra-v1/`. The validation README sets the scale of real errors: modern runway segments of 100 m scatter by 4.3 degrees RMS and 400 m segments by 0.75 degrees, about 1.5 times the conditional errors the tool reports, so a short imagery line deserves less trust than its stated sigma suggests. We keep the tool's sigma unchanged in every record.

Imagery lines are fitted on the median composites named in the imagery manifest, using renders made with `render_image.py` and picks read from labelled pixel grids. We wrote three small helpers in `measure-B/scripts-G/`: `view.py` crops and enlarges a render with a grid in render-pixel coordinates and overlays picked (orange) and fitted (cyan) lines; `coords.py` converts between render pixels, UTM and latitude-longitude through a render sidecar; `build_town.py` assembles a town input for `systems.py` from measured line files, adding the source ID, a source locator and the lines-file path to every line and copying each plan's arrow result into its source entry. `show.py` prints a line table. None of these change a tool; the tools ran unmodified. Every fitted line was checked by drawing it over a render other than the one used for picking, or over the plan render. Working images are named `w*` (grids and zooms) and `c*` (fit checks) in the raw directory of each town.

Plan arrows were measured as manual tail-to-tip picks with a stated pick uncertainty, because most arrows have a broken shaft, a letter N across the shaft or a half arrowhead that a profile fit cannot follow. Where a plan prints both a metre and a feet scale we compared them and used the metre scale. Plans that print walls brick by brick were fitted on one face per wall with `edge` profiles; their inlier fractions are near one half because every brick outline makes a gradient, and the fits were accepted only after the overlay showed them on the face.

A line belongs to one system. When a source's lines did not fit within the 6 degree window we split off the outlying line into its own system rather than accept a failing window, and said so in the town notes. The rule's main system (greatest total length) stands in every case.

## Lothal

The imagery manifest lists a five-scene composite (2018-2022). On the 3 km and 1.2 km renders the mound is a vegetated oval about 450 m across, cut on its west by a modern road. Inside it lies a dark, water-filled rectangle about 40 by 210 m, with bright excavated ground to its west and the museum buildings to the south. The schematic site plan in the plan library (Fig. 3.1 of an unattributed university chapter, "courtesy ASI") puts the "Brick Lined Enclosure", the dock, along the east side of the town with the acropolis and platform west of it, and Rao (1979, Fig. 19) shows the same basin in isometric view. We identified the water body as the dock basin and fitted its two long edges on B08, where water is darkest (render `r05-nir-500.png`, 16 times enlarged). Both fits used all 12 profiles with sub-pixel residuals, and the overlay on the brightness render `r06` follows the edges. They give θ = +0.10 and +0.30 degrees, σ 0.43 degrees each, about 189 m long. The irregular bright patches of the excavated area could not be tied to particular walls, and the road and fence along the mound are modern, so the dock is the only imagery system: θ = 0.20 ± 0.30 degrees.

We measured three plan sources. The library plan (`plan-lothal-01`) has an arrow marked only "N", so it is unspecified. Its dock is drawn as a parallelogram, with long sides 5.3 and 5.6 degrees clockwise of the arrow and short sides that are not square to them, and the acropolis and warehouse outlines on the same sheet lean by between -0.8 and 7.4 degrees. The main plan system is the dock, θ = 5.46 ± 0.42 degrees. We left the dashed "Site Boundary" unmeasured because the plan does not call it a wall.

The held Rao (1979) scan is the "plates missing" part of MASI 78, but it keeps the text figures. Its list of figures (PDF page 6) led us to Fig. 17 (plan of B block, phase IV), Fig. 18 (warehouse plan) and Fig. 19 (the dock, an isometric view with no arrow, not usable). Figs. 17 and 18 carry the same symbol: a compass-rose disc with an arrow through it and no letter or legend, which we class as unspecified. The warehouse plan is printed sideways with its arrow pointing to the page left; its metre scale (0 to 7 m, 75 to 76 pixels per metre at 300 dpi) agrees with its feet scale within 1.2 percent. Thirteen faces of the brick blocks 41e to 41h against the passages range from -2.9 to +4.4 degrees; with the south face of 41h split off, the main system of twelve faces is θ = 0.14 ± 0.39 degrees. The B-block plan gives nine wall faces of house 172.IVa, the verandah and the long southern wall. Eight lie between -12.1 and -8.3 degrees; the thin passage wall, whose fit used under half of its profiles, gave -5.0 and was split off. The main system is θ = -9.83 ± 0.47 degrees. On this sheet the arrow stands 18.7 degrees clockwise of the page vertical while the dash-dot excavation grid is drawn square to the page, so the arrow and the grid disagree with each other as well as with the other plans.

Imagery and plans disagree by more than 2 degrees for the site plan (5.3 degrees) and the B-block plan (10.0 degrees); the warehouse plan agrees with the imagery within 0.1 degrees. We did not register a plan to the imagery: the dock corners are the only shared features and they form a narrow rectangle with blurred ends at 10 m. Rao's Fig. 15 (smithy and Street 1) and Fig. 16 (public drain) carry arrows and remain unmeasured.

## Dholavira

The composite (nine scenes, 2018-2025) shows the site west of the Wikidata point. The library's only plan, Kenoyer's redrawing of Bisht (1989), is small, so we searched the infrastructure records and found that the deleted "preliminary2015" copy of Bisht's 2015 Dholavira report survives at `data/raw/2026-10-11-societal-persistence/evidence/acquisition-v5/bisht2015-preliminary-original.pdf`. Its caption scan (with orientation text suppressed by the tool) listed the plans. We used Fig. 2.2 (north-up, no arrow) and Fig. 6.1 (the same base map with a compass-rose arrow) to identify features, and Fig. 2.4, a schematic map titled "DHOLAVIRA 1990-1998", which draws the ceremonial ground and an east-west street crossing the middle and lower towns. We did not read the report's text pages.

The walls themselves are not resolved at 10 m. What the imagery does show, located against those plans, is a bright excavated block of about 150 by 160 m (the castle), a bright east-west band about 50 m north of it (the north side of the ceremonial ground, where Fig. 6.1 draws a grey dashed line), and a bright straight band about 510 m long, 205 m north of the castle, running from the middle town into the lower town; Fig. 2.4 puts its street about 210 m north of the castle. We first fitted these and two dark lines in an exploratory picks file (`imagery-r12-explore-picks.json`), kept for the record. One exploratory fit failed at the corridor edge, which stopped `measure_lines.py` for the whole file; we wrote `scripts-G/measure_each.py`, a wrapper that calls the unchanged tool once per line and records failures, and used it from here on. The final imagery picks give the street θ = -3.67 ± 0.36 degrees and the ceremonial-ground band θ = -3.20 ± 1.65 degrees, together the main system θ = -3.54 ± 0.52 degrees (704 m). The castle's north and east edges (+1.55 and -1.17 degrees, the latter with σ 6 degrees) form a separate system. We excluded the dark line along the field boundary west of the castle (θ -2.1 from five profiles) because it cannot be told apart from a field edge.

The three plans agree with each other more closely than the Lothal plans. Kenoyer's Fig. 4.4 draws the outer and middle-town walls square to the page with its arrow 5.7 degrees clockwise of vertical, giving θ = -5.51 ± 0.30 degrees from seven walls. The first pick of the middle town's north wall lay between the two lines of the double wall and the fit crossed from one to the other, so we repicked it in `plan-dholavira01-v2-picks.json`. Bisht's Fig. 6.1 gives θ = -6.99 ± 0.35 degrees from seven walls; its middle-town south wall, drawn along the castle, bailey and dimension arrows, gave -1.1 degrees from a third of its profiles and was split off. Bisht's Fig. 2.4 gives θ = -8.00 ± 0.51 degrees; its east-west walls (-8.4 to -9.8) and north-south walls (-6.5 to -7.2) are not drawn square. All three plan arrows are unspecified. The imagery main system differs from the plans by 2.0 to 4.5 degrees. The plan systems combine outer and middle-town walls in one 6 degree window, so `systems.py` flags them as mixing town parts; the window and 200 m conditions themselves pass. We made no registration, because the features located sharply in both imagery and plan, the castle corners, span only about 150 m.

## Surkotada

The nine-scene composite shows fields, tracks and water-filled quarry pits around the Wikidata point. The plans give the fortified settlement as about 120 by 60 m with rubble walls a few metres thick, which is about 12 by 6 composite pixels; we found no mound or rectangle of that size within 1 km in the RGB, NIR or edge-enhanced renders, so the town has no imagery lines and its record keeps the starting point.

Two plans carry arrows, both unspecified. Joshi's Fig. 4 is titled a sketch plan and is printed sideways with its arrow to the page left. We measured only the walls drawn black as exposed rubble, not the hatched walls marked as conjecture. Grouped by wall, the two segments of the south rampart give the main system θ = -13.70 ± 0.78 degrees; the west wall (-9.2) and the cross wall between citadel and residential area (-16.1) are single-line systems. The chapter's Fig. 3.2 ("courtesy ASI") is a cleaner drawing with contours, walls nearly square to the page and an arrow through a circled N standing 10.8 degrees clockwise of vertical; five wall edges give θ = -11.91 ± 0.58 degrees. The fits on its hatched bands use under half of their profiles, as hatching produces many gradients, but the overlay shows each on the intended edge. The two plans' scale bars imply site lengths of about 185 m and 123 m, a disagreement we record without resolving; it affects only the weights within each source.

## Kanmer

The five-scene composite shows Kanmer village with a dark patch about 100 m across at the published point and a bright modern road along its east side. In NIR the patch is very dark, more like a pond or damp hollow than a mound, and no rectangle matching the fort could be outlined; the town has no imagery lines.

The library plan (Kharakwal et al. 2011, Fig. 5) is drawn on graph paper with a lettered and numbered excavation grid square to its arrow, and the fort walls turned about 30 degrees anticlockwise of both. It has no scale bar, only the statement "SCALE = 2CM=5M". We took each lettered grid square as 5 m and calibrated on the outer ticks of the thirty squares from K to NN, which makes the fort sides about 110 and 118 m. Eight exposed wall segments were fitted. Five segments of the outer north-west, north-east and south-east walls give the main system θ = -29.82 ± 0.63 degrees; the short south-west segment (-24.9) was split off for the window, and two inner walls form their own system (-31.0 and -26.4). These values lie near the folding limit, so a reader should keep in mind that a layout turned 60 degrees the other way would fold to the same numbers. We labelled the walls by side, so `systems.py` flags the outer-fort system as mixing named parts although all its lines belong to the fort circuit.

On the same page, the text above the figure makes a qualitative statement that the houses were built at cardinal directions with the fort wall (Kharakwal et al. 2011, printed page 360, PDF page 6). It gives no number and we did not use it.

## Shikarpur

The five-scene composite shows the highway, a pond and a dense tree grove (bright red in NIR) near the site point, which matches the coordinate printed in Bhan and Ajithprasad's 2007-2008 report. The 116 m square fortification of the plan could not be outlined, so there are no imagery lines. That report's last page lists coordinates for eleven Gujarat sites; we used it only to check locations. Its Surkotada entry reads 70 deg 30' E, which we took as a misprint for 70 deg 50' because the other values place it beside the Wikidata point; a render at the corrected point (23.63579, 70.84402) showed no recognisable fort either, and the Surkotada record keeps the Wikidata point.

The library plan is panel B of Abhayan et al. (2025, Fig. 2, CC BY), courtesy MSU Baroda. Its north symbol is a crosshair circle with N above the vertical axis and no arrowhead, measured as a 71-pixel line, so the arrow error of 1.14 degrees dominates. The grey "Plan of fortification" band is drawn square to that symbol: eight inner and outer edges give θ = 0.24 ± 0.41 degrees, all within 0.8 degrees of each other. The band is a drawn reconstruction that includes the excavated parts of the wall. The held 2008-2009 report has only a section drawing with an arrow.

## Juni Kuran

The nine-scene composite was rendered at the Wikidata point and at the coordinate printed for "Juni Kurna" in the Shikarpur 2007-2008 report, about 1.4 km apart. Both show fields, drainage lines and the modern village, and neither shows a rectangle that matches the plan's citadel or its roughly 220 m outer enclosure, so the town has no imagery lines.

The library plan (Pramanik 2003-04, Fig. 2) is a scan of about 100 dpi whose legend and scale labels are illegible. Its north symbol is an eight-rayed compass star with one long ray, which we measured from the tip of the opposite short ray to the tip of the long ray. To set a scale we read the seven label marks under the bar as 10 5 0 10 20 30 40 m, and to check that reading we ran the tool's OCR on PDF pages 55-68 of the report. The tool suppresses any line with orientation wording, and it did so on PDF pages 55, 62 and 64; we did not see those lines. The remaining text gives the outer fortification as 220 m by 225 m, and the scale reading makes the enclosure's south side about 210 m. The rounded line bundles around the citadel look like contours and were not measured. Of the straight lines enclosing the area east of the citadel, the east line of the east double wall failed to fit (fewer than four inliers) and was dropped in a second picks file, which also adds two short north-side segments. The drawn enclosure is not rectangular: its east side gives θ -15.7 degrees and its south and north sides -9.1, -9.9 and -8.5. Because the north segments lie about 236 m from the south wall, no two of the three groups can form one system under the rules, and the single east wall (223 m, θ = -15.72 ± 1.49 degrees) is the main system by length. We prefer the three east-west lines, about -9.2 degrees over 255 m, as the better representative and say so in the town notes.

## Gola Dhoro (Bagasra)

We read the coordinate review first. The site index point agrees within 25 m with the excavators' coordinate, and the 2025 article's longitude, 10 km east, lies outside the held 5 km imagery window. Around the point the composite shows uniform dark scrub between a stream and a salt flat, and the enclosure, about 55 by 45 m on the plan, is not resolved, so there are no imagery lines.

The library plan is panel A of Abhayan et al. (2025, Fig. 2), "Plan and Layout of Structures at Bagasra (Gola-doro)", courtesy MSU Baroda. Its north symbol is a fleur-de-lis under the letter N, and its excavation grid is drawn square to the page and to the symbol, with the fortification turned about 11 to 12 degrees anticlockwise of both. The first pick of the south fortification line was about 4 pixels off at its east end and used only 8 of 22 profiles, so we repicked it in `plan-gola-dhoro01-v2-picks.json`. Five fortification lines, solid where excavated and dashed where traced, give the main system θ = -11.40 ± 0.49 degrees. The walls of the eastern gate and of the Stage 2 and Stage 3 enclosures run at other angles and were not measured.

## Desalpur

By the time we reached the optional towns the infrastructure run had added composites for all of them, so every remaining town had imagery. The Desalpur composite, however, is centred on the Wikidata item "Desalpur", a modern town beside a highway. Indian Archaeology 1963-64 (held in the evidence census, PDF pages 19-20) places the excavated site, Desalpur (Gunthli), in Taluka Nakhatrana on the river Dhrud, and the Shikarpur 2007-2008 report prints a coordinate for "Gunthali (Desalpur)" about 35 km north-west of the composite, outside the held window. The IAR describes a rubble fortification around a site of 130 by 100 m and refers only to photographic plates. With no plan and no imagery of the site, Desalpur is recorded as not measurable. We searched these pages with `scripts-G/find_pages.py`, which withholds any line with orientation or compass wording and flags its page; IAR 1963-64 pages 19 and 20 were flagged and those lines were not read.

## Khirsara

The composite around the excavators' coordinate shows fields bounded by hedges, a stream and the village to the west, with no recognisable fortified rectangle, so there are no imagery lines. The library had no plan. The held PLOS ONE article (Pokharia et al. 2017) has no site plan in its PDF but cites its supplementary S1 Fig, a contour map of the excavated site. We downloaded it from the PLOS server (`measure-B/khirsara/download-plos-s1fig/`, 8,244,346 bytes, SHA-256 b87fcd36...0cc5f, retrieved 06:55:56 UTC, article page states the CC BY licence) and read it only with the prepared environment.

The S1 Fig legend lists contour lines and modern structures only. The fortified area appears as straight bundles of contour lines on a rampart mound, so no wall face is drawn. We judged these bundles worth measuring as a stated proxy and picked the centre of each straight bundle at both ends by hand (manual mode, σ 8 pixels, about 1.8 m), because a profile fit cannot choose consistently among four or five wavering contour lines. The north symbol is a four-pointed star under an N, unspecified; the blurred scale bar was read in the same "10 5 0 10 20 30 40" pattern as at Juni Kuran. The west and south sides (-10.1 and -8.6 degrees) form the main system, θ = -9.33 ± 0.63 degrees over 397 m, and the east side (-1.2) stands alone. Like Juni Kuran's, the drawn enclosure is not rectangular. The record labels every line as a rampart contour proxy.

## Kuntasi

The composite is centred on the modern village. Indian Archaeology 1987-88 (PDF page 25) places the ancient site, Bibino Timbo, 3 km south-east of the village and prints its coordinate, which falls about 0.9 km east of the window; a render of the window's south-east corner shows fields, a canal and a river bend. The file in the library's Kuntasi folder is the conference volume *Recent Advances in Marine Archaeology*, whose Kuntasi paper is one page of text. With no plan in hand we first recorded Kuntasi as not measurable (`towns/kuntasi.json`).

While searching the held chapter by Rawat (2015) in *Port Towns of Gujarat* for Pabumath, we found its Fig. 6, a Kuntasi layout plan redrawn after Dhavalikar et al. (1996), with a north line (a cross bar, a small arrowhead and a sideways N) and a 15 m scale. We therefore redid the town as `towns/kuntasi-v2.json`, keeping the first file, and appended its row to `summary-G-revisions.csv`, because `systems.py` refuses a second row for a town already in `summary-G.csv`. The inner fortification's east wall, the west wall of the northern room complex and the long wall north of room 24 give θ = -12.49 ± 0.51 degrees (three lines, 93 m). The fit on the east wall of the room complex wandered across room interiors and is excluded; the solid south-east stretch of the outer fortification (-25.8 degrees) is not parallel to the inner walls and stands alone.

The same chapter's Fig. 2 is a Lothal site plan after Abha Narain Lambah Associates (2004). Its north symbol is a circle under an N with no line to measure, and it has no scale bar, so we did not measure it.

## Rojdi

The composite shows irrigated fields and a broad bright area of bare ground east of the site point. No Rojdi plan was found in the library, the held Indian Archaeology volumes or the evidence-census sources, and without a plan no imagery feature can be tied to Harappan architecture. Rojdi is not measurable.

## Rangpur

The site point has arcminute precision, and the published architecture lies in cuttings of 7 to 11 m, below the imagery resolution, so Rangpur is measured from plans only. The library lists three cutting plans in Rao (1962-63). The scan has no text layer, so we ran the tool's OCR on PDF pages 42-63 (orientation lines were suppressed on PDF page 42) to confirm that the structures of RGP 3, 4 and 7 belong to the Harappa culture of Sub-period IIA. Each plan carries a compass wheel crossed by an arrow with no letter or legend.

In Figs. 7 and 8 the walls are drawn square to the page and the arrows diagonal, so the wall faces fold to between +41.5 and +44.5 degrees, beside the folding limit. Fig. 7 (RGP 3) gives θ = 43.94 ± 0.43 degrees from five faces of wall 7(II) and platform 2(II). In Fig. 8 (RGP 4) one face folded to -44.25, which is the direction +45.75, and because the 6 degree window is computed on folded values it had to go into its own system; the remaining page-aligned faces give θ = 43.17 ± 0.45 degrees, and the long wall with the north wall of Room 11 forms a second system near +38.6. Fig. 8's arrow has thick ends on both sides; we took the lower-left, arrowhead-like end as the tip, and a 180 degree reversal would leave θ unchanged. Fig. 10 (RGP 7) needed a full-figure render to include its scale bar, so its picks were shifted by 972.2 pixels from the first render, and the first pick of the west edge of platform 14 lay about 35 pixels off the outline and fitted blank ground, so it was repicked in `plan-rangpur03-v2-picks.json`. Fig. 10 gives θ = -34.67 ± 0.63 degrees. The three cuttings disagree by up to about 12 degrees once the folding limit is allowed for, which we read as drafting variation in three hand-drawn arrows.

## Kotada Bhadli, Pabumath and Navinal

All three have composites, and none has a usable plan. The held plans of Kotada Bhadli and Navinal are panels D and E of Abhayan et al. (2025, Fig. 2), drawn over commercial basemaps; the library excludes them and so did we. Pabumath appears in Indian Archaeology 1977-78 and 1980-81 with photographic plates only, and in Rawat (2015) with a coordinate. In the Kotada Bhadli composite a squarish patch of about 150 m lies about 500 m north-west of the point, but without a plan we cannot say whether it is the fortification or a field. The three towns are recorded as not measurable.

We did not search for further Harappan towns in the region beyond those met in the sources above. Nagwada, Babar Kot and Padri appear in the held Indian Archaeology volumes and the evidence census and are candidates for a later pass.

## Tool problems and repairs

The tools ran unmodified. `measure_lines.py` stops at the first failed line in a picks file, so we wrote `scripts-G/measure_each.py`, which imports the tool and calls `measure()` once per line, records failures and writes the same output format; it was used from the Dholavira imagery onward. `systems.py` refuses a second summary row for a town, so the revised Kuntasi row went to `summary-G-revisions.csv`. The 6 degree window is computed on folded deviations, which splits walls lying either side of ±45 degrees (Rangpur Fig. 8); we put such lines in their own systems and said so. Several plan scans had illegible scale labels (Juni Kuran, Khirsara); we read them from the arrangement of tick marks and checked one against stated dimensions in the report text. We did not need the IGRF correction, because no plan states magnetic north. Two shell heredocs failed to parse in the harness; we wrote those files with the editor instead, and no partial file was left.

## Reproduction and checks

`scripts-G/reproduce.py` re-measured every lines file from its picks and render sidecar, rebuilt every town input and recomputed the systems. Every line and every system reproduced exactly; every line in the records has a source locator and an error, and every plan source a north class, evidence and an arrow result (`repro-G-report.json`). We also reran `tools/systems.py` itself on every town input into `repro-G/`; the town files are identical to those in `towns/` apart from their creation time, and the regenerated summaries equal `summary-G.csv` and `summary-G-revisions.csv`.

## Limits and encounters with stated orientations

We wrote only inside the two measure-B directories, opened none of measure-A, the town-axes package or its raw directory, and opened none of the package files the brief excludes. We did not open the excluded pages of the *Interim Reports* volume. We made one download, the PLOS ONE S1 Fig for Khirsara, recorded in its own directory. All Python that read imagery, plans or the download ran with the prepared environment and `-I`, from scripts in `scripts-G/`.

We met qualitative statements about orientation in three places and used none of them: the Kanmer chapter's text above Fig. 5 (Kharakwal et al. 2011, printed page 360) says the houses were built at cardinal directions with the fort wall; the OCR of the Rangpur report showed that cutting RGP 7 ran north-east to south-west (PDF page 61), a description of the trench; and the OCR of Juni Kuran and Rangpur pages and the name search of Indian Archaeology pages withheld orientation lines on the pages listed above, which we did not read. We saw no numerical orientation figure for any town.

## Summary of the Gujarat share

Imagery resolved Harappan architecture at two towns. At Lothal the dock basin gives θ = 0.20 ± 0.30 degrees, and at Dholavira the excavated street and the north edge of the ceremonial ground give θ = -3.54 ± 0.52 degrees. Plans, all with arrows of unspecified meaning, place the walls of most other towns between -9 and -16 degrees (Surkotada, Juni Kuran, Gola Dhoro, Khirsara, Kuntasi and Lothal's B block), Shikarpur and Lothal's warehouse at 0 degrees, Kanmer near -30 degrees and the Rangpur cuttings near the folding limit. Where imagery and plans meet, the Dholavira plans run 2.0 to 4.5 degrees anticlockwise of the imagery and Lothal's B-block plan 10 degrees anticlockwise, the schematic Lothal site plan runs 5.3 degrees clockwise, and Lothal's warehouse plan agrees within 0.1 degrees.

The boldest reading these measurements support is that the Gujarat towns whose architecture we could see from space were laid out on true north to within a few degrees, and that the 5 to 15 degree turns in the plans belong mostly to their unspecified, hand-drawn arrows. Magnetic declination in Gujarat during the excavations was small, so a magnetic reading of the arrows explains a degree or two at most, not ten. The test that would decide it is to register the Dholavira and Lothal plans to sub-metre open imagery or surveyed control points, calibrating each plan's arrow directly, and to measure Kanmer and Kotada Bhadli, whose plans make the strongest claims of a turned layout, in imagery fine enough to resolve a 100 m fort.
