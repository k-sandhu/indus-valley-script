# Measurer B log, share S (Sindh, Baluchistan, Pakistani Punjab)

This log records what measurer B looked at and decided for the ten towns of share S: Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Chanhu-daro, Kot Diji, Nausharo, Sutkagen-dor, Balakot, Allahdino, Lakhanjo-daro and Ganweriwala. The measurer is Claude Opus 5.5 (model ID claude-opus-5-5) running as a Claude Code subagent. All renders are under `data/raw/2026-10-21-town-orientations-verification/measure-B/<slug>/`, picks and line results under `measure-B/picks/<slug>/`, town inputs under `measure-B/town-inputs-S/` and helper scripts under `measure-B/scripts-S/`.

## Setup and shared assumptions

We read `AGENTS.md`, the writing rules, the measurement rules, the tool guide, the brief for share S and the validation README, then the site, imagery-manifest and plan-library indexes for the ten towns. The validation shows that the conditional fit error understates observed runway residuals by a factor of about 1.5 and that 100 m segments scatter by 4.3 degrees RMS; we keep the tools' sigma unchanged in every record and treat short imagery lines with that in mind.

Every plan bearing is relative to the plan's own north arrow, measured from tail to tip with `measure_lines.py`. Where a plan states no meaning for its arrow we classify it `unspecified`, and the true reference stays unresolved. We measured plan lines on gridless renders (rendered with `--grid 0`), because the grid that `render_plan.py` draws overwrites whole pixel rows and columns; the measurement tool re-renders the page without a grid in any case, so the picks are in the same pixel frame. Most plan lines are wall faces along named streets and lanes, fitted as `edge` features with a 5 px corridor; thin single wall lines and thin arrows are fitted as `trough` features. We located wall faces with a small script (`scripts-S/runs.py`) that lists the dark ink runs along rows or columns, and checked every fitted line on an overlay (`scripts-S/zoomgrid.py`, files named `chk-*.png`). Early on we found that reading coordinates by eye from full-page renders was off by up to 40 px, so all endpoints come from ink runs or 4x enlargements.

The IGRF evaluation in `tools/igrf.py` creates a temporary directory beside the coefficient file. To keep every write inside our own directories we copied the coefficient file to `data/raw/.../measure-B/igrf-S/igrf14coeffs.txt` (SHA-256 8f8d8840...0903c, identical to the infrastructure copy) and point magnetic corrections at that copy.

Three helper scripts are used for every town: `scripts-S/build_town.py` merges the line results into a town input for `systems.py` and copies each plan's north record, arrow measurement and scale into its source entry; `scripts-S/contact.py` makes contact sheets for finding plan pages and refuses PDF pages 36-47 of the Jansen and Urban volume; `scripts-S/redgaps.py` lists street gaps inside red-filled excavation areas of colour plans.

## Encounters with stated orientations and excluded material

The held scan `research/literature/sources/jansen_urban1987_interim2_govscan_77275.pdf` contains the whole of *Interim Reports* vol. 2 twice: PDF pages 1-192 and again PDF pages 193-384. The excluded printed pages 33-44 therefore also occur at PDF pages 228-239, where the chapter titled "Axis Systems and Orientation at Mohenjo-daro" begins. Our contact sheet of PDF pages 193-240 showed those pages as thumbnails 170 px wide, at which no text or number is legible; we did not render, enlarge or otherwise open them, and we did not view the contact sheets of PDF pages 289-384. The guard in our contact script and in `tools/plan_support.py` covers only PDF pages 36-47, so a later user of this scan should exclude PDF pages 228-239 as well. On PDF page 33 (printed page 30) a small table sits beside plans of the "Moneer" area; we did not read it. Jansen and Urban's Fig. 8 on PDF page 25 carries a drawn rotated grid over the site plan; it is an interpretation and we did not measure it.

## Mohenjo-daro

The Sentinel-2 composite (nine scenes, 2019-2026) shows the site as a brick-red and bare-soil area of about 1.2 km by 1.2 km south of the Moenjodaro runway, enclosed by modern tracks and embankments. We rendered it at 4 km, 2 km, 1.2 km and 800 m in RGB, NIR, SWIR, brightness and edge-enhanced brightness. Two light, cleared patches lie inside the site 530 m apart and almost due north-south of each other. Mackay 1938 Plate I (site plan) puts the DK-G and HR excavations 600 m apart, nearly due south, with the Stupa mound to the west of DK-G; the western brick-red patch falls where Plate I puts the Stupa mound. We take the northern patch to be DK-G, the southern HR and the western patch the Stupa mound. No straight edge inside the mound could be tied to a wall, street or drain at 10 m: the excavated streets are at most 10 m wide and are lost among trees and spoil, and the only straight boundaries are the edges of the cleared patches, tracks and embankments, which the rules exclude. The imagery source is therefore recorded as located but not measurable.

The plan library holds one Mohenjo-daro plan, Kenoyer 1997 Fig. 4.3, a site plan at 1.2 m per pixel. Its hatched areas are excavation outlines; the one line we could identify as architecture is the thick line along the east side of DKg, continued north by a dotted double line, which matches the position of First Street along the east side of the G Section on Mackay's Plate XIII. The twin lines through HR are too irregular at this scale and were not measured. Its single line gives +4.65 degrees from the figure's arrow.

The held copy of Marshall 1931 (IGNCA 14985) is volume I only; its site plan was issued "in pocket at end of Vol. I" and is not in the scan, and the plates volume of Mackay 1938 was not held (another study's raw directory holds volume I, the text, which has no plates). We downloaded two openly accessible government scans from IGNCA, each into its own directory under `downloads-S/`: Marshall 1931 vol. III (plates) from `https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/48270.pdf`, retrieved 2026-10-21T06:10:45Z, 15,592,632 bytes, SHA-256 9e08f9ee...6a434; and Mackay 1938 vol. II (plates) from `https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/9954.pdf`, retrieved 2026-10-21T06:11:42Z, 14,428,400 bytes, SHA-256 a6254545...3f58f. Neither page states a licence; both are Government of India publications of 1931 and 1937-38. We found them through the Internet Archive search API, which lists the same IGNCA items. We opened only plate lists and plates.

Marshall vol. III Plate XXXIX (HR Area, A and B Sections) is a folding plan scanned at about 100 dpi with a 50 ft scale bar and a plain arrow marked N. We measured 17 street and lane frontages: Street 3, Street 2, First Street, Deadman Lane, High Lane, South Lane, Lane 4 and the drain lane between Blocks 2 and 3. They fall into three orientation groups relative to the arrow. Ten faces on First Street, Street 2, the northern part of Street 3, the drain lane and South Lane lie between +3.6 and +7.1 degrees (main system hr-main, +5.68, SE 0.30). High Lane, Deadman Lane and the southern part of Street 3 lie between -1.3 and +2.6 degrees, and Lane 4 runs at about +10 degrees. The east frontage of Street 3 visibly bends near House XLV, so we measured it as two lines. A crease runs north-south through Section B near x 750-790 px; no measured line crosses it.

Mackay vol. II Plate XIII panel C (DK Area, G Section, northern portion, Late Period Ib and II, 1930-31) is drawn with north to the left of the page and a survey grid parallel to the page edges. We measured nine faces on First Street, West Street and Central Street. First Street and West Street frontages lie between +3.4 and +6.4 degrees and one Central Street face at +2.6, giving the main system dkg-main at +4.23 (SE 0.42); the opposite Central Street face lies at -0.8 degrees. The arrow is only about 78 px long at 200 dpi, so its 0.17 degree error enters every line.

The three plans agree to within 1.5 degrees: HR at +5.68, DK-G at +4.23 and the Kenoyer DK-G line at +4.65, each relative to its own unspecified arrow. The HR and DK-G plans show different quarters under different arrows, so this agreement does not separate a quarter-to-quarter difference from an arrow difference. We inspected and did not measure Jansen and Urban 1987 Fig. 7 (Moneer area; its scale bar has no legible values), Fig. 2 on PDF page 88 (HR, VS and MN; no scale bar) and Mackay Plate I (site plan; street lines too coarse at the scan's resolution, used for identification only).

## Harappa

The composite (nine scenes, 2018-2025) shows the archaeological area as tree-covered, brown and bare ground of about 1 km by 1 km south-west of the dense modern town, with the old Ravi bed to the north, as on Vats 1940 Plate I. We rendered it at 3 km, 1.5 km and 700 m. Trees, paths and spoil break up the mounds, and no straight edge could be tied to the granary, the workmen's quarters or any other wall on the plans, so the imagery is located but not measurable.

The plan library lists Vats 1940 vol. II Plates I and III, both in the held scan `vats_1940_govscan_9842.pdf`. Plate I is the site plan; its arrow is a plain N, and its legend says "Magnetic Declination about 1° 55' East in 1930. (Decreasing by about 3' annually)". That statement suggests the Plate I arrow is true north but does not say so, so we keep it unspecified. Plate I shows excavations as filled rectangles, not architecture, and we used it for identification only. We considered registering Plate I to the imagery through its roads and buildings, but could not confirm that any of them still stands where it stood in the 1930s, and we did not register it.

Plate III (Mound F, Great Granary) is drawn with north to the left of the page. We measured eleven faces: the long outer wall of the Western Block on the Central Aisle side and both faces of five unit long walls in the two blocks. All lie between -1.4 and -2.1 degrees from the arrow and form one system at -1.80 (SE 0.30, 146 m). In a contact sheet of the plates volume we also found Plate XI (PDF page 33, Mound F, Trenches IV and V, the Workmen's Quarters), which is not in the plan library. It carries a 20 ft survey grid parallel to the page, so we placed every corridor on a hatched wall face away from the grid lines. Eight faces of house walls and of the long wall between the two rows of houses lie between -0.7 and -3.1 degrees, one system at -1.66 (SE 0.30, 95 m). The two Mound F plates agree to 0.15 degrees relative to their own arrows. In both, the walls are drawn slightly off the page axes while the arrows lie on them.

## Chanhu-daro

The composite (five scenes, 2018-2023) shows three bright bare patches between a road and a canal-side track. We take the largest, about 130 m by 160 m, as Mound II and the smaller patches to its north-west and south-west as Mounds III and I, which matches their arrangement on Mackay 1943 Plate I; the Wikidata starting point falls on the largest patch. The excavated houses have rooms of 2-3 m, far below the 10 m pixel, and the patch edges are field and track boundaries, so the imagery is located but not measurable. We did not register Plate I to the imagery, because mound outlines give controls no better than 10-20 m over a 300 m baseline.

The held Mackay 1943 report has four plans of the 1935-36 season, each with a compass needle inscribed "MAG. NORTH NOV. 2ND 1935" and S, N, W and E marked. The plan library lists Plates II (Mound I) and III (Mound II, upper Harappa level); we found Plate IV (Mound II, Harappa II level) in a contact sheet of the plates and measured it as well. We classify all three needles `magnetic`, give the survey date as decimal year 1935.836, and let the tool apply the IGRF-14 declination at the mound (+0.871 degrees east), keeping the raw bearing in every line. The report's text gives no other statement about north.

Walls on these plans are solid black bars 6-12 px wide at 200 dpi, many walls are short and the plans are dense, so picking by eye would have been slow and error-prone. We wrote `scripts-S/wallcands.py`, which opens the dark-ink mask with a long thin rectangle at 2 degree steps and returns each surviving bar as a candidate segment, tested it on a synthetic image (it recovered three drawn lines to within 0.5 degrees), and reviewed every candidate on enlarged overlays (`cv-*.png`). We selected by hand only candidates that matched a single drawn wall, dropping diagonals across thick bars, curved walls, duplicates and bars merged across two walls, and refined each with `measure_lines.py`: trough fits on Plates II and IV, and on Plate III, whose bars are 8-12 px wide, edge fits on one face. We checked every fit on the overlays `chk-plII.png`, `chk-plIII.png` and `chk-plIV.png`. On Plate III we dropped cd3-c59 as a second fit of the same face as cd3-c22. The candidate files and selection headers are kept in `picks/chanhu-daro/`.

Within each plate the deviations form a continuum without an architectural break, so we grouped them with `scripts-S/group.py`, which takes the 6 degree window with the greatest total length as the first system and repeats for the remaining lines. On Plate II this rule happens to follow the architecture. Its main system, +5.88 (SE 0.40, 16 lines, 53 m), is the house that occupies Square 12/K, which the text describes on p. 59, together with the walls south of it; the western houses form a second system at -2.60. In the 12/K house the north-south walls run 3-5 degrees more clockwise than its east-west walls, and the text notes that room 44 was "inaccurately built". Plate IV gives a main system of -26.02 (SE 0.41, 21 lines, 93 m) for the northern and eastern buildings and Street 447, and a second system of -19.86 for the building with rooms 139 and 136 and the west side of the street labelled STREET. Plate III gives -25.81 (SE 0.32, 22 lines, 101 m), with a second system at -31.97 along the long wall from 257 to 176. Relative to the same 1935 magnetic north, then, Mound II (Plates III and IV agree within 0.2 degrees) is turned about 32 degrees from Mound I.

For the two earlier towns we grouped by street and feature, before we wrote `group.py`. Applied to Marshall's HR plate, the window rule would take the window from +0.47 to +6.47, which adds both faces of Deadman Lane (+2.55 and +0.47) and drops South Lane (+7.11), and would give +4.70 (11 lines, 213 m) in place of +5.68; we keep the street-based grouping, which separates the near-zero lanes as their own feature.

## Imagery for the optional towns

When we started, the imagery manifest listed Kot Diji, Nausharo, Sutkagen-dor, Balakot, Allahdino, Lakhanjo-daro and Ganweriwala as `missing-space`, although composites for all seven were already on disk. We re-read the manifest before Chanhu-daro; by then (manifest written 02:33 local time) it listed all seven as available, and we used the manifest entries for every town record.

## Kot Diji

The plan library has no Kot Diji plan. The Internet Archive lists a Digital Library of India scan of *Pakistan Archaeology* 2 (1965), which contains F. A. Khan's excavation report; we downloaded it from `https://archive.org/download/dli.ernet.108437/108437-Pakistan%20Archaeology%20No.2.pdf` (retrieved 2026-10-21T07:05:18Z, 13,363,252 bytes, SHA-256 11eeb767...8f5e12; no licence statement in the item metadata) into `downloads-S/pakarch2-1965-dli108437/`. Its Fig. 4, "Kot Diji - Plan of excavated remains" (PDF page 44), is the only plan of architecture, and as scanned it has a title, a 5 ft scale and excavation square labels but no north arrow. Fig. 3 (excavated area on contours) has none either, and Fig. 2 (site and environs) has an N arrow but shows the site only as an outline, so no shared feature carries the Fig. 2 arrow to Fig. 4. The report's text (OCR of PDF page 43 with orientation lines suppressed; none were found) assigns the citadel's fortification wall to the Kot Dijian period and says that "There is no evidence to show that the Harappans also used the fortification wall". In the imagery the fort hill is the white limestone ridge with dark wall lines south-east of the highway, and the prehistoric site lies across the road from it among fields and buildings, where no mound could be delimited. Kot Diji is recorded as not measurable: a plan without a north arrow, and features not resolvable at 10 m.

## Nausharo, Lakhanjo-daro and Ganweriwala

None of the three has a plan in the library, and a search of the Internet Archive found no open scan of a site plan for any of them; a HAL poster about the French mission at Nausharo that a web search returned would not load (the server returned an access-denied page). In the imagery neither the Nausharo mound in the Bolan valley nor Ganweriwala on the Cholistan saline flat could be identified with confidence, and the published Lakhanjo-daro coordinate (Biagi and Vidale 2022) falls inside the Sukkur industrial area. The held Biagi and Vidale article locates Mound D on a Google Earth base map with Maxar imagery, which we did not use, and it has no plan of architecture. All three are recorded as not measurable. The Ganweriwala record's note that published work on the site is a surface survey comes from our general knowledge and was not checked against a source; it should be read as unverified.

## Sutkagen-dor

The composite shows the Dasht River channel and, south-east of it, a dark red-brown patch about 110-120 m wide and 250-300 m long standing out from the pale alluvium, which we take as the rock outcrop that Dales 1962 says carries the citadel; the Wikidata point lies 80 m west of it. Its west edge is roughly straight, but at 10 m it cannot be told apart from the rock and shadow edge of the outcrop, so we did not identify it with the drawn wall, and the imagery is recorded as not measurable.

Dales's plan (plan library entry sutkagen-dor-01) has an unlabelled arrow, which we classify `unspecified`. The scale bar's ticks survive but its numbers do not, so we took the scale from the text on printed page 4, which gives the eastern wall as about 24 ft thick at Operation B, and measured the two outline faces of the hatched east wall just north of trench B; with that scale the bar's major ticks fall 9.75 m apart, consistent with an unlabelled 10 m division. The scale affects only lengths and the 200 m check. Five fortification wall faces form one system at -1.55 (SE 0.47, 272 m): the west and east walls lie at -2.05 to -2.13 and the north and south walls at +0.27 and +0.93, so the drawn citadel is about 2.5 degrees from rectangular. On the same page Dales describes the citadel wall as oriented "closely to the magnetic points of the compass"; we met this qualitative statement while reading the page for the scale and did not use it.

## Balakot

Dales and Kenoyer 1977 print the site coordinates on Fig. 3 (25° 27' 45" N, 66° 42' 30" E), 30 m from the infrastructure starting point. The imagery there shows only irrigated field patches and tracks, so the mound could not be located and the imagery is not measurable. Fig. 4 (plan library entry balakot-02), the plan of the Harappan levels on the high mound, has an arrow marked N and a 0-6 m scale. We measured eighteen wall faces in three building groups: the eastern group lies between 0.0 and +3.0 degrees from the arrow; the central building's long walls between +7.1 and +10.3, with its south wall at +1.7 and +5.6 on its two faces; and the western building is drawn out of square, its north wall at +5.8 and its east wall at -0.8. The window rule gives a main system of +2.37 (SE 0.70, 10 lines, 50 m) that joins the eastern group with the western building's long walls and the central building's south wall, and a second system of +8.18 for the central building and the west wall of the western group. The town record's note gives the central building alone as "about +8.6"; the correct value for its five long-wall faces is +8.47.

## Allahdino

The only coordinate for Allahdino is Fuls's arcminute value, which falls in housing and industrial estates east of Karachi, and we could not locate the site in the imagery. Fairservis 1986 Fig. 2 (plan library entry allahdino-01) draws walls as bands of brick courses inside dashed trench outlines, with a half-arrowhead marked N and no scale. We located the outlines of long wall bands from dark-run profiles of a blurred copy of the render (`scripts-S/bluruns.py`) and fitted eleven of them as edges on the original page. Ten lie between -17.3 and -19.7 degrees from the arrow, in both the long direction and the cross direction, and form the main system at -18.12 (SE 0.32); one cross wall lies at -13.4. We fitted the straight side of the half-arrowhead as its shaft. Its error, 0.32 degrees, dominates every line's sigma, and the drafter's intended axis may differ from the straight side by a few degrees, which is the main uncertainty for this town. Because the plan has no scale we gave it a nominal 0.1 m per render pixel so that the tools run; the lengths and the 200 m check are therefore nominal, while the system mean and its standard error do not depend on the scale.

## Reproduction and checks

`scripts-S/reproduce.py` reruns `measure_lines.py` on every picks file, rebuilds every town input from its spec, reruns `systems.py` into a scratch directory and compares the results with the saved files. It also checks that every line has a source entry with its locator and a positive sigma, and that every plan source has a north class with its evidence. All ten towns reproduced exactly, and every check passed (`reproduce-S-v1.txt`).

## Tool problems and repairs

The tools ran without errors. We worked around three behaviours without changing any tool. The grid that `render_plan.py` draws overwrites pixel rows and columns, so we picked on renders made with `--grid 0`, and the measurement tool re-renders the page without a grid in any case. `igrf.py` writes a temporary directory beside the coefficient file, so we pointed it at our own copy. `measure_lines.py` requires a scale for every plan, so for Allahdino we used a declared nominal scale. One of our own steps failed once: a spec file for Chanhu-daro was written in Windows-1252 by the shell, and we re-encoded it to UTF-8 before use without changing its content. No output was overwritten; where a tool refused an existing destination, the earlier file had already been written and we kept it.

## Independence

We did not open the other measurer's directories, the town-axes package or its raw directory, or any package file outside the measurement rules, the brief for share S, `tools/`, `infra-v1/` and the imagery and plans under `infra/`. While listing the raw package directory we saw the names of `measure-A`, `astro`, `dating`, `codex` and `notes`, and opened none of them. We made no Git command that changes anything, and wrote only inside our two `measure-B/` directories and the session scratchpad. We used no commercial imagery and no OpenStreetMap geometry; the one OSM-derived item is the Balakot starting coordinate from the site index, which we replaced with the coordinate printed on the published plan.

## Summary of results and the boldest reading

No imagery line could be measured in share S. At 10 m resolution, the excavated architecture of these towns is lost among trees, spoil, fields and modern building, so every bearing comes from plans. Five towns have plan systems relative to unspecified arrows: Mohenjo-daro HR +5.68 and DK-G +4.23, Harappa Mound F -1.80 and -1.66, Sutkagen-dor -1.55, Balakot +2.37 and Allahdino -18.12. One town has magnetic arrows that the tools correct to true north with IGRF-14: Chanhu-daro, with Mound I at +5.88 and Mound II at -25.81 and -26.02 relative to true north.

The boldest reading these measurements support is that a Harappan town did not keep one orientation across its mounds. At Chanhu-daro, Mounds I and II, drawn on one 1935 survey under one dated magnetic needle, are turned about 32 degrees from each other. At Mohenjo-daro and Harappa, plans of different quarters agree within 1.5 degrees of their own arrows. The test that would decide it is a check of the arrows on the Mohenjo-daro and Harappa plates: either a registration of Vats Plate III and Marshall Plate XXXIX to dated control (for example the surveyed corners of the Great Granary and the HR streets on a modern survey), or a statement of their north in the reports' text. If both arrows prove to be true north, the HR, DK-G and granary systems become absolute and the Chanhu-daro contrast can be compared with them directly.
