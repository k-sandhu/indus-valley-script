## Astronomy definitions to implement

Thuban (α Draconis, HIP 68756) is used for validation only.

**Positions.** Retrieve ICRS J2000 positions, proper motions (μα* = μα cos δ), parallaxes and radial velocities from SIMBAD (TAP at `https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/sim-tap/sync`), keeping the Hipparcos new reduction (van Leeuwen 2007) values and the radial velocities SIMBAD lists, with their bibcodes. Save the raw response and write `astro-v1/stars-retrieved-v1.json`. Propagate each star rigorously to the epoch by straight-line space motion (position, proper motion, parallax and radial velocity as a space-motion vector, as ERFA `pmsafe` does), written in your own code. Precess from the ICRS to the mean equator and equinox of date with the long-term model of Vondrák, Capitaine and Wallace (2011, *Astronomy & Astrophysics* 534, A22; erratum 2012, 541, C1), coded yourself from the published coefficients: the precession of the equator (ltpequ), of the ecliptic (ltpecl) and the bias-precession matrix (ltpb). You may read the coefficient tables in the paper or in ERFA's BSD-licensed source, but write the implementation yourself. Ignore nutation, aberration and annual parallax. The mean obliquity is the angle between the ecliptic pole from your ltpecl and the equator pole from your ltpequ. The sun's declination is +ε at the June solstice, −ε at the December solstice and 0 at the equinoxes.

**Horizon.** For an observer 1.6 m above the DEM surface at the town's coordinates, compute the apparent horizon altitude at every azimuth from 45° to 135° and from 225° to 315° in 0.1° steps. Use the Copernicus DEM GLO-30 (AWS open bucket `copernicus-dem-30m`) from 2 km to 20 km, sampled every 30 m along each ray, and GLO-90 (`copernicus-dem-90m`) from 20 km to 250 km, sampled every 90 m. Terrain within 2 km is excluded, so the mound, its trees and the modern village do not count. The apparent elevation of a terrain point at distance d with height difference Δh is atan(Δh/d − d/(2R′)), with R′ = 6,371 km / (1 − 0.13). The apparent horizon at an azimuth is the largest of these elevations and of the sea-level dip, which we take as −arccos(R′/(R′ + H)) for the observer's height H above sea level (DEM value plus 1.6 m); state this interpretation in your notes. Tiles that do not exist (open sea) count as sea level. Rights: Copernicus DEM licence; attribution "© DLR e.V. 2010–2014 and © Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014–2018 provided under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA". Write one profile file per town to `astro-v1/horizon/<slug>.json` with the coordinates, observer height, DEM tile hashes and the altitudes. A flat horizon at apparent altitude 0° is the sensitivity variant.

**Refraction and extinction.** Astronomical refraction follows Bennett (1982), R = cot(ha + 7.31/(ha + 4.4)) arcminutes for apparent altitude ha in degrees, scaled by (P/1010)(283/(273 + T)) with T = 20 °C and P = 1010 hPa; the true altitude is ha − R(ha). A star is last seen setting at the apparent altitude hv = max(apparent horizon, e), where its extinction angle e is the apparent altitude at which m + k(X(e) − 1) = 6.0, with k = 0.30 magnitudes per air mass and the air mass of Kasten and Young (1989), X = 1 / (cos z + 0.50572 (96.07995° − z)^−1.6364), z the apparent zenith distance in degrees. Variants: k = 0.20, k = 0.45, and no extinction (hv equal to the apparent horizon). The sun is taken when its upper limb touches the apparent horizon: its centre's apparent altitude is the horizon minus the semi-diameter 0.267°.

**Azimuth and predicted deviation.** For declination δ, latitude φ and true altitude h, cos A = (sin δ − sin φ sin h) / (cos φ cos h), azimuth from north, taking the western solution for setting and the eastern for rising. The horizon depends on azimuth, so iterate until A changes by less than 0.001°. The predicted deviation θc is A − 270° for every setting target and A − 90° for every rising target, so a positive value means a grid turned clockwise. The rate dθc/dD is the central difference over D ± 50 years.

## Scoring scripts

Both scripts read a plain CSV with columns `slug, part, role, lat, lon, theta_deg, sigma_theta_deg, D_year, sigma_D_years`, where `role` is `discovery` for Mohenjo-daro, Harappa and Kalibangan and `confirmation` otherwise, D is in astronomical years and σ values are in degrees and years. They compute θc and its rate with `astronomy/predict.py` at each row's coordinates, unless optional columns `thetac_<target>` and `rate_<target>` are supplied. Each has a selftest on synthetic tables with known outcomes, written to `astro-v1/selftest-analysis-v1.json`.

**`analysis/score_frozen.py`** implements the other study's frozen rules exactly as follows. Stage 1 chooses the target on the discovery towns: for each target c, χ²c = Σ (θt − θc(Dt))² / (σθ,t² + (dθc/dD · σD,t)² + σint²), with σint = 1.0°; the chosen c* has the smallest χ²c (ties by the target order); at least two discovery towns are needed; the choice is flagged as a discovery failure if the smallest χ²c exceeds the 99th percentile of χ² with as many degrees of freedom as discovery towns. A command-line option fixes c* to a supplied target instead (the other study's frozen choice), and the script then also reports the choice it would have made. P0, the kill condition: Pearson's r between θ and D over all towns with a one-sided permutation p-value, enumerating all permutations when n ≤ 9 and drawing 200,000 random permutations otherwise; P0 passes when p < 0.05, Spearman's ρ is positive and r stays positive when any single town is left out; it needs n ≥ 8 and is undecided otherwise; a failure with n ≥ 8 makes the claim dead. Repeat P0 on the confirmation towns alone, and report the weighted least-squares slope of θ on D with weights 1/(σθ² + σint²) and a 95% interval from 20,000 bootstrap resamples of towns, beside 0.5° per century. P1, the Pleiades version: passes when at least 80% of all towns have θ > 0, no town has θ < −2σθ, and the 95% interval of the fitted slope contains the slope predicted by the Alcyone model and excludes zero; compute that predicted slope as the weighted least-squares slope of the Alcyone values θc(Dt) on Dt with the same weights, report also the mean Alcyone rate, and state this interpretation in the output. P2, the Aldebaran version: for each town the Aldebaran model gives a predicted sign; P2 passes when at least 80% of the towns whose predicted deviation differs from zero by more than 0.5° have θ of that sign, with at least eight such towns. P3: over the confirmation towns, χ² of c* with the stage 1 error model against two alternatives, cardinal intent (θ = 0 everywhere, rate 0) and a constant offset equal to the inverse-variance weighted mean deviation of the discovery towns (weights 1/(σθ² + σint²)); P3 passes when χ²c* is smaller than both and the χ² upper-tail probability of χ²c* with as many degrees of freedom as confirmation towns exceeds 0.05; a leave-one-out check reports whether the ordering of the three χ² values survives removing any single confirmation town. Power: 20,000 simulations with the towns' dates and errors, θ drawn as θAlcyone(D + N(0, σD)) + N(0, σθ) + N(0, σint), reporting the probability that P0 passes; inside the simulation you may estimate each p-value from 2,000 random permutations, and say so. Credence: P0 failing with n ≥ 8 gives 0.02 and status dead; P0 and P3 passing give 0.40; P0 passing and P3 failing give 0.15; fewer than eight towns leave 0.10 and status open. D1: report whether Wanzke's two figures for Mohenjo-daro, 7.2° and the interval 1–2°, each lie within 2σθ of the Mohenjo-daro θ. Options select the extinction and horizon variants.

**`analysis/compare.py`** compares two settlement tables in the same CSV format, matched by slug: for deviations, Δ = θ₁ − θ₂, z = Δ / √(σ₁² + σ₂²), the inverse-variance weighted mean of Δ with its standard error, median |Δ|, root mean square, the towns with |z| > 2 and > 3, and the verdict "agree" when the weighted mean lies within two standard errors of zero or within ±0.5° and at least 80% of towns have |z| ≤ 2.5; for dates, ΔD and the verdict "agree" when |ΔD| ≤ max(100 years, σD of the second table) for at least 80% of towns; for astronomy, given two θc tables at the same coordinates and dates, the largest absolute difference per target and the verdict "agree" when it is at most 0.10°.
