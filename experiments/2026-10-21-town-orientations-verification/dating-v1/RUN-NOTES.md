We assembled a source register for town layouts, their construction phases and the evidence used to date those phases. The evidence files cover every town in the assignment. They distinguish a town part, its exposed architecture, an earlier settlement beneath it, and the calendar start proposed for the relevant phase. Every proposal awaits project judgement. This run was produced by GPT-6 in the Codex API harness using command-line source readers and live web research. No independent project review was performed.

We began with the repository entry instructions, writing rules, data gaps, knowledge index, experiment registry, relevant literature retrospective and redistribution rules. We then examined held excavation reports and searched for the original reports, laboratory date lists, later site dating papers and calibrated regional syntheses. We used author or institutional copies where available. The search record in each town file identifies the reports and routes examined. The bibliography distinguishes an inspected local report, a full article read through a web transcription, a search-indexed extract and a bibliographic lead whose full text was unavailable. These routes provide different amounts of evidence; the access route is part of each source record.

The calendar rules are those fixed in the assignment. A calibrated occupation span does not by itself date the construction of every part within that occupation. We selected a candidate only after recording the proposed connection between the architecture and a published phase. We used a published calendar beginning when it qualified, and retained cross-dated excavation estimates at the lower evidence level. We kept older radiocarbon “B.C.” values separate from calibrated calendar dates and retained the reported half-life where it could be established. We did not recalibrate samples or fit new models. Explicit calendar-BCE fields have astronomical counterparts. Conversions from a printed calibrated-BP summary change its epoch only; they do not repair a disputed calibration or sample attribution. The fixed uncertainties belong to the assignment and do not represent new estimates of excavation precision.

Harappa provides the most substantial combination of an architectural sequence, calibrated phase chronology and accessible laboratory tables in this register. Even there, a date for the first enclosure and a date for an exposed mature plan need separate treatment. Review of Kenoyer’s architectural account changed the Mound E entry: it describes a changed Harappan street and wall plan in an excavated sector, alongside continuity elsewhere. The proposal for that exposure therefore uses the mature replanning phase; the earlier enclosure remains an alternative with its own candidate. The mound beneath the modern town remains unresolved. The exact passages and both architectural readings are in [Harappa’s evidence file](evidence/harappa.json).

Kalibangan has an accessible excavator account connecting its new town divisions to the Harappan occupation, and published calibrated phase beginnings. The register retains both the later comparative synthesis and the earlier excavator’s corrected chronology. Its predecessor suite still contains entries read from a transcription, including an anomalously early result. Shikarpur has a direct statement that enclosure construction began with occupation, together with phase-associated calibrated determinations. Kanmer has extensive accessible sample tables, but the earlier site article and later archaeobotanical review give different phase openings. The later review supplies the proposal, while the earlier value remains visible. Most Kanmer samples still lack an explicit individual sample-to-cultural-phase attribution in the inspected tables. The evidence files for [Kalibangan](evidence/kalibangan.json), [Shikarpur](evidence/shikarpur.json) and [Kanmer](evidence/kanmer.json) preserve these distinctions.

Dholavira’s excavation report gives an unusually detailed sequence of town expansion. Its first enclosed nucleus, subsequent castle conversion, bailey, middle town and lower-town addition are separate events. The newer carbonate chronology gives a much earlier beginning than the excavation’s typological table. We joined that published beginning to the first enclosed nucleus, while retaining the excavator’s chronology for the opening of the expansion phase. The lower-town addition has no separate calendar opening in the inspected evidence. Individual conventional ages and sample contexts from the newer study are in its supporting Table S4. Direct publisher access failed, and searches of the author route, repositories and Figshare did not recover that table. No individual determination from it was invented or reconstructed from a plotted envelope. This remains the largest missing required-town determination suite; see [Dholavira’s evidence file](evidence/dholavira.json).

The construction sequence is clearer than the calendar start at several other required sites. Lothal’s report identifies the first elaborate plan, but its numerical chronology mixes comparative estimates with old uncalibrated site determinations. Surkotada’s initial enclosed units are well described, but its report likewise uses an older radiocarbon timescale. We retained those observations without treating their printed “B.C.” estimates as calibrated starts. Primary laboratory lists also expose conflicting transcriptions in the Surkotada report and later compilation. Banawali’s excavator and the ASI inventory place the new internal plan earlier than accounts which simply call the mature occupation its first town. Its accessible selected radiocarbon suite does not date that earlier construction phase. Rakhigarhi has early organized architecture and several dated occupations, but the period labels, structural labels and founding events of the named town divisions have not been synchronized securely. These gaps are stated in the files for [Lothal](evidence/lothal.json), [Surkotada](evidence/surkotada.json), [Banawali](evidence/banawali.json) and [Rakhigarhi](evidence/rakhigarhi.json).

Mohenjo-daro’s recent perimeter-wall observations were available through a field participant’s account quoting a preliminary report. The accessible account gives calibrated sample intervals, but not the full laboratory-code and conventional-age information needed to enter new determination rows. It does not supply a modelled or stated calendar start of the wall phase. The older dated samples belong to later occupation and cannot fill that gap. Chanhu-daro’s modern field dossier reassigns the major old exposures within a revised sequence and describes earlier planned quarters. We retained its comparative first-period beginning, including the alternative opening printed in an artefact caption. Recent report metadata were found, but their full dating tables were unavailable. The proposals and access limits are in [Mohenjo-daro’s](evidence/mohenjo-daro.json) and [Chanhu-daro’s](evidence/chanhu-daro.json) files.

The optional sites have uneven coverage. Farmana has a complete transcription of the prehistoric rows in the inspected final-report dating table, and explicit disagreement between the preliminary and final cross-dated beginnings. The final report itself also questions the cultural attribution of the basal excavated levels. Bhirrana’s first and revised reports supply a useful crosswalk: the earlier planned transitional settlement and the subsequently described exposed enclosure are different architectural targets. Its proposal refers to the earlier planned settlement. Selecting the enclosure as the target would require a different construction-phase decision. Its very early published phase chronology remains a review issue, and the synthesis’s individual phase groupings are identified as such. One ambiguously labelled determination was withheld from the Bhirrana rows pending site-attribution review. See [Farmana](evidence/farmana.json) and [Bhirrana](evidence/bhirrana.json).

Kotada Bhadli has a published Bayesian phase model with a plotted beginning of walled occupation. Its figure does not print the numerical median and start-only range required for the highest level in this assignment. We did not estimate them from pixels. The stated approximate beginning is entered at the next level, with the assignment’s default uncertainty. The complete occupation interval is recorded separately and was not substituted for start uncertainty. Rangpur has an excavator’s cross-dated first built phase, within an older chronological framework; that proposal retains its evidence level and does not become a site radiocarbon result. The original chronology’s dependence on comparisons with other sites is explicit. Both are described in [Kotada Bhadli](evidence/kotada-bhadli.json) and [Rangpur](evidence/rangpur.json).

Bagasra began before construction of its enclosure. Khirsara has an extensive dating table but the inspected article does not assign the founding walls of each named component to a subphase. At Kuntasi and Rojdi the accessible occupation dates have not been joined to the first enclosure construction. Lakhanjo-daro’s dated craft context is not a dated founding layout. Juni Kuran, Desalpur, Kot Diji, Sutkagen-dor, Balakot, Allahdino, Kunal, Mitathal and Navinal retain phase or sample observations without an eligible layout start. Nausharo, Ganweriwala, Karanpura and Pabumath have search records only: a sufficient locatable construction chronology was not recovered. Empty candidate or determination lists indicate the limits of this acquisition, not proof that no relevant work exists. Several reported components and unresolved-unit rows also require review against the definition of a separately planned town part.

The raw directory preserves successful downloads, unexpected HTML responses and a failed empty download. Each network acquisition has its URL, UTC retrieval record, byte count, SHA-256 and observed rights information. Several filenames suggested a report that the contents did not contain. We identified the actual documents from their title pages and recorded the mismatch instead of allowing the filename to determine the bibliography. A disk-full error interrupted an optional-site download; after space became available, a new filename preserved the successful retry and the empty earlier artifact. Scanned tables required OCR, and the Farmana tables were also rendered and checked visually. The acquisition review distinguishes downloaded bytes from locally rendered review images. Downloads were read as untrusted data with isolated Python and were never executed.

For the data manifest, this package adds a town identifier and aliases in the display name; reported and unresolved town-part definitions; published cultural and structural labels; architectural descriptions and crosswalks; the proposed first-layout event and alternative events; short source-word excerpts; candidates at each evidence level; calendar scale, epoch conversion and uncertainty rule; full sample identifiers, conventional ages and errors; material, trench, layer and depth where supplied; individual phase attribution and its evidential status; first-occupation estimates; conflicting transcriptions; retrieval and rights records; and explicit acquisition gaps. New source witnesses include excavation reports, primary laboratory date lists, site dating articles, institutional field dossiers and later calibrated syntheses. The bibliography and acquisition inventory identify them individually. All changes to coverage remain in this owned package; the project data manifest and knowledge summaries were not edited.

The verification script checks the correspondence between evidence files and CSV rows, full locator registration, calendar conversions, duplicate laboratory-code rows, evidence-level selection, uncertainty arithmetic and every acquisition hash. Its result is in `validation.json`; acquisition content checks and review-image hashes are in `acquisition-review.json`. These checks establish internal consistency. They do not certify that every published sample has been found or replace review of the original pages still available only as transcriptions. The final read-only Git status records owned paths separately from concurrent changes elsewhere. No Git mutation, change outside the owned paths, access to the excluded study files, external publication or transcription of a layout bearing was performed.

The strongest conclusion this register supports is that a town’s first occupation is often a different event from the construction of its exposed Harappan plan. Some town parts preserve an earlier organized settlement, while others were added or replanned later. The decisive next evidence is a construction contact, its published phase assignment and short-lived dated material from that contact or its immediate predecessor. A reviewer can then accept, replace or withdraw each proposed calendar beginning without changing the source observations preserved here.
