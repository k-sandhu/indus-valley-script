# Blind verification of the town-axes study: report of 21 October 2026

We repeated the measurements, dating and astronomy of [experiments/2026-10-21-town-axes/](../2026-10-21-town-axes/REPORT.md) independently and blind, with open imagery and our own code, and then applied its frozen rules to our numbers. Its frozen outcome is reproduced: on our measurements, as on its own, the test of whether Harappan towns were laid out on star settings is undecided, the credence stays at 0.10, and the Pleiades version fails because Dholavira is turned anticlockwise. Its astronomy and scoring are reproduced exactly: our separately written code gives the same predicted deviations to within 0.026° at every town, target and date it reports, and the same statistics when run on its tables. Two of its inputs are not reproduced. Open 10 m imagery could be measured at only two towns, Lothal and Dholavira, so its imagery values for Mohenjo-daro, Harappa and Sutkagen-dor, including both discovery towns, rest on one commercial imagery source that we could not check; and at Lothal our value for the dock, +0.19° ± 0.30°, differs from its −1.88° ± 0.30° by more than the stated errors allow. Two of its date choices rank evidence differently from its own rule. Its secondary analysis on plans, which weakened the claim to 0.04, holds on our independent plan readings in the sense that the correlation again fails, but its sign reverses, because the one town that sets it lies on the diagonal where a grid's deviation is ambiguous.

## How the verification was made

The design was frozen at 03:28 UTC on 21 October in [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) and [MEASUREMENT-RULES.md](MEASUREMENT-RULES.md), before any town was measured here (hashes in `PROTOCOL.sha256`). From the other package we read only its protocol, town list and star file before its report existed ([READ-LOG.json](READ-LOG.json)). Our sources were open: Copernicus Sentinel-2 imagery at 10 m, published plans held locally or openly downloadable, the Copernicus elevation model and SIMBAD. No commercial basemap was used; Landsat panchromatic imagery could not be obtained without an account.

Two measurers took every bearing without seeing each other's work, the other package or any hypothesis about stars or dates. Measurer A was the Codex CLI running `gpt-6.1-sol`, measurer B a Claude Opus 5.5 subagent; each worked in three regional shares (Pakistan; Gujarat; Haryana, Rajasthan and Indian Punjab) under the same rules. A separate Codex run built and validated the tools and acquired the imagery and the plan library; another implemented the astronomy and the scoring scripts; a third assembled the dating evidence, from which the coordinating session set the layout dates and froze them at 05:38 UTC, before any measurement existed ([dates-v1/JUDGEMENT.md](dates-v1/JUDGEMENT.md)). All blind outputs, 963 files, were hashed at 07:30 UTC in [BLIND-FREEZE.sha256](BLIND-FREEZE.sha256) before the other package's report, which had appeared at 05:50, was opened.

The deviation of a line is its bearing from true north folded to the nearest cardinal direction, in (−45°, +45°], positive clockwise. A town's deviation follows the other package's rule: it comes from imagery when an imagery system has a standard error of 2° or less, otherwise from plans whose arrow is stated to point to true north (or to a grid), and never from plans with magnetic or unstated arrows.

## What open imagery can measure

The tools were validated on seven airport runways, measured on the same Sentinel-2 composites against official true bearings ([validation summary](infra-v1/validation-v1/summary.json)). Whole runway edges, 2–3 km long, came out with a root mean square error of 0.14° and no bias (mean −0.04°). Cut into independent segments, the error was 4.3° at 100 m, 1.8° at 200 m, 1.0° at 300 m and 0.75° at 400 m, and the tool's stated error was 1.5 times too small on average. A Harappan wall must therefore be several hundred metres long and visible at 10 m before its direction is fixed to a degree, and few are.

Measurer A found measurable architecture in the imagery at one town, the Lothal dock; measurer B at two, the Lothal dock and a street and the edge of the ceremonial ground in Dholavira's middle town. At every other town the mound is backfilled, covered by villages or fields, or its walls are narrower than the pixels. Both measurers searched all 33 towns, and measurer B added Tarkhanewala-Dera. Plans gave relative bearings for 16 towns (measurer A) and 22 towns (measurer B), from 24 and 41 plan sources. No plan our measurers used states that its arrow points to true north. Mackay's Chanhu-daro plates name magnetic north, measurer A also read the arrows of one Kalibangan plan and one Mitathal plan as magnetic, and every other arrow is unstated. So the frozen rule gives our package a primary deviation at two towns ([table-v1/town-values.json](table-v1/town-values.json)).

| Town | Measurer A | Measurer B | Town deviation (rule) | Part |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lothal | +0.10 ± 0.74, 2 lines, 331 m | +0.20 ± 0.30, 2 lines, 378 m | +0.19 ± 0.30 | dock (lower town) |
| Dholavira | no measurable line | −3.54 ± 0.52, 2 lines, 704 m | −3.54 ± 0.52 | middle town |

The two measurers agree at Lothal within 0.10°. They also read the same published figures consistently: on 19 plans that both measured, their main systems differ by a median of 1.3°, 9 within 1° and 11 within 2° ([agreement-v1/plan-agreement.json](agreement-v1/plan-agreement.json)). The large differences come from which walls the main-system rule selected (Banawali, 15°) and from one plan that measurer A read as magnetic and measurer B as unstated (Mitathal, 7°). Seven of the 19 pairs differ by more than twice their stated errors, so the stated plan errors are too small as well.

## Comparison with the town-axes package

**Bearings.** Lothal and Dholavira are the only towns with a primary deviation in both packages ([comparison-v3/comparison.json](comparison-v3/comparison.json), `bearings`).

| Town | This package | Town-axes | Difference | z |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lothal, dock | +0.19 ± 0.30 (A +0.10, B +0.20) | −1.88 ± 0.30 (img-A −1.86, img-B −1.94) | +2.07 | +4.9 |
| Dholavira | −3.54 ± 0.52, middle town | −5.50 ± 1.50, castle (its middle town −4.6 ± 1.6) | +1.96 | +1.2 |

Under the frozen comparison rule the bearings disagree: the weighted mean difference is +2.06° ± 0.41°, and only one of the two towns lies within 2.5 standard errors. Dholavira agrees, and agrees more closely with the other package's own middle-town value (difference about 1°). At Lothal each package's two measurers agree with each other within 0.1°, so the 2° difference lies between the imagery sources, not the measurers. Our stated error is the weak point. The dock edges are 160–190 m long, where the runway validation puts the Sentinel-2 error near 2° per line; with that empirical error the Lothal difference is about 1.5 standard errors (a check made after the outcome was known). The other package measured the dock on Esri World Imagery at about 0.5 m per pixel, twenty times finer, so its value is the more precise one, but no open source available to us can confirm it. Its imagery values for Mohenjo-daro (+0.80 ± 1.84), Harappa (+0.01 ± 0.51) and Sutkagen-dor (−0.38 ± 0.54) could not be repeated at all with open imagery.

**Astronomy.** Our implementation was written without reading the other package's code. At the other package's coordinates and dates it reproduces all 83 predicted deviations that package reports, for all nine targets, within 0.026°, and the three star targets within 0.0001° ([comparison-v3/comparison.json](comparison-v3/comparison.json), `astronomy`). The largest difference is for the December sunset at a town with hills on the horizon, where the two horizon calculations sample the elevation model differently. Our code passed its own validation before use: the obliquity in 2500 BCE is 23.973°, Thuban comes nearest the pole in 2797 BCE at 0.097°, the precession matches the ERFA test vectors to 10⁻¹⁶, and setting azimuths for 2026 match astropy within 0.004° ([astro-v1/validation-v1.json](astro-v1/validation-v1.json)). Aldebaran's geometric setting point at Mohenjo-daro is −2.53° in 2500 BCE and +3.54° in 1500 BCE, against Wanzke's −2.70° and +3.42°.

**Dates.** Under the frozen comparison rule the dates disagree: five of the seven towns dated by both packages under the strict reading agree, short of the required 80% ([comparison-v3/dates-matched.json](comparison-v3/dates-matched.json)). The two disagreements are choices of source. For Chanhu-daro the other package used Mackay's cross-dated start of 3000 BCE, while our dating run found the later excavators' start of 2500 BCE (Didier 2017); the rule prefers the more recent of two sources at the same level. For Dholavira's castle it used Bisht's excavator range, 3000 BCE, where Sengupta and colleagues (2020) give a calibrated start of about 3551 BCE for Stage I, which the rule ranks above a cross-dated start. Our strict reading of the rule dates fewer towns than the other package's: Mohenjo-daro, Lothal and Surkotada have no calibrated or cross-dated start for their layout phase in the sources our dating run inspected, and enter only our liberal table, which calibrates the oldest radiocarbon determination of each layout phase. None of these differences changes a frozen outcome below.

**The frozen rules on our numbers.** Every variant was scored with our independent implementation of the other package's stage 1 and predictions P0 to P3 ([comparison-v3/comparison.json](comparison-v3/comparison.json), `variants`).

V1 gives the other package's outcome: P0 undecided and credence 0.10. V4 and V4x reproduce its reported statistics to the last printed digit, which checks its scoring code and its astronomy at once. P2, the Aldebaran version, reads "undecided" in our scorer and "fails" in the other package's wherever fewer than eight towns are eligible; the protocol states only that a pass needs eight such towns, and either reading leaves P2 without a pass.

## The secondary analysis on plans

The other package's [Amendment 1](../2026-10-21-town-axes/AMENDMENT-1.md) lets towns without imagery take their plan values, whatever the arrow, with an added error for the unknown north, and on eleven towns finds a correlation between deviation and date of r = 0.46 (p = 0.082) that fails P0; it lowers the credence to 0.04. We ran the same rule on our two measurers' plan readings with its dates (variant V5, after its report was read). Ten towns qualify. P0 fails again, but with r = −0.36 (p = 0.84). The difference comes from Rangpur, the latest town in both tables. Its walls run close to the diagonal: one figure puts its long wall at +42° to +44° from the arrow, another figure at −35° to −38°, and both measurers read both. The other package took a line at +40.5°; our rule for picking each measurer's longest plan source took one figure from each measurer, and averaging them on the 90° circle gives −40.1°. For a grid turned about 45°, the folded deviation flips sign with a few degrees of drawing or choice, and with it flips the sign of the whole correlation; without Rangpur the other package reports r = 0.07. Its conclusion that the plans show no date trend therefore survives an independent reading, while any statement about the direction of a trend does not.

The plans are read reproducibly but do not give true bearings. Where both packages measured the same towns from plans, they mostly agree within 3° to 4°: Kanmer −29.8 against −29.3, Gola Dhoro −11.1 to −11.4 against −10.7, Farmana −21.5 to −22.7 against −22.3, Harappa Mound F −1.6 to −1.8 against −1.2, Surkotada −10.8 to −13.7 against −15.1. Chanhu-daro's Mound II, after the same IGRF correction of +0.87°, reads −23.7 to −26.0 here against −19.9 there. Against imagery, unstated arrows are off by 2° to 10°: at Lothal our measurers' plans range from +0.1° (Rao's warehouse plan) to +6.4° (a reproduced site plan) against the imagery's +0.2°, and at Dholavira from −5.5° to −8.0° against −3.5°.

## Verdict

By the protocol, bearings, astronomy and the frozen outcome must all agree for the verification to be recorded as `reproduced`. Astronomy and the frozen outcome agree; bearings and dates do not, so we record a review with discrepancies. Its findings for the record are these. The frozen result of the town-axes package, undecided at 0.10 with the Pleiades version failed, is reproduced on independent measurements and code. Its scoring and astronomy are correct. Its discovery stage rests on imagery values for Mohenjo-daro and Harappa that no open source can repeat, and its Lothal value differs from ours by 2°, which a sub-metre open image or a ground survey would settle. Its Chanhu-daro and Dholavira castle dates should be revisited under its own ranking rule. Its weakened credence of 0.04 is supported, but the positive sign of its plan-based correlation is an artefact of one diagonal town. We propose no change to the credence of its conjecture card [C-0015](../../knowledge/conjectures/C-0015-towns-laid-out-on-star-settings.md) beyond its own 0.04.

## Decision and next evidence

Repeating this test with 10 m imagery or with plans of unstated north will not decide it: the imagery resolves too few walls, and the plans' arrows are uncertain by more than the predicted effect of 0.5° per century. The next measurement is a true-north bearing at the towns whose walls are exposed, from a ground or drone survey, or from published plans registered to surveyed control points. For a public check, sub-metre imagery released under an open licence, if a disaster-response release covers Sindh or Kutch, or declassified photography of the 1960s and 1970s could be registered to the Sentinel-2 grid used here, and the Lothal dock is the first wall to measure on it.

The boldest reading these results support is that Dholavira was laid out on an axis turned about 4° anticlockwise of true north, while Lothal's dock lies within 2° of true north, so the Kutch city and the Gujarat port did not share one astronomical rule. Dholavira's anticlockwise turn is the only clear deviation from the cardinal directions on which two independent imagery sources agree (−3.5° here on Sentinel-2, −4.6° to −5.5° on Esri), and it is the observation that kills the Pleiades version of the star claim, which predicts a clockwise turn there. The test that decides it is a true-north survey of Dholavira's castle, middle town and outer wall and of the Lothal dock, with the prediction that Dholavira's walls lie between −3° and −6° and the Lothal dock between −2° and +1°. If Dholavira's walls lie within 1° of true north, the reading is dead.
