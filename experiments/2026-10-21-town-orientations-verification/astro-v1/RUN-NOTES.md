We implemented the astronomical definitions independently and produced provisional predictions for all eleven required towns. The run contains eleven terrain profiles, 19,899 rows of nine-target predictions at 201 epochs, the catalogue retrieval record, numerical validation, scoring and comparison programs, an IntCal20 calibrator and synthetic checks. We opened none of the study under verification, its raw directory, either measurer's files, the dating files or the infrastructure work. The results here contain no measured town orientations or archaeological layout dates.

The three prediction stars use the positions, proper motions and parallaxes that SIMBAD attributes to van Leeuwen's 2007 Hipparcos reduction, together with SIMBAD's listed radial velocities and their bibcodes. We interpreted the tabulated right-ascension proper motion as mu-alpha times cos-declination. Our code converts the catalogue motion to a Cartesian velocity, includes the relativistic conversion between observed and inertial velocity, and solves the light-travel-time change before precessing the direction. It evaluates the Vondrak, Capitaine and Wallace polynomial and periodic coefficients directly. ERFA and astropy are imported only by the validation program. Downloaded ERFA C files supply coefficients, test literals and licence evidence; we never compiled or executed them.

Thuban is a source exception. SIMBAD's preferred coordinates now come from Gaia, and its TAP schema exposes no history of coordinate values. We recovered its Hipparcos 2007 proper motion and parallax from the measurement tables and retained the Gaia-based J2000 coordinates with their own bibcode. Thuban is used only for validation. A requirement that every Thuban astrometric field come from the Hipparcos new reduction remains unmet. The three stars used for predictions have no such substitution.

The numerical checks pass at their disclosed tolerances. Mean obliquity at -2499 is 23.973239951800 degrees. Thuban's minimum polar distance is 0.097403945654 degrees at astronomical year -2796.354813073, or 2797.354813073 BCE. The largest component error against the ERFA precession vectors is 1.11e-16. The largest difference from pyerfa's propagated and precessed positions is 1.70e-14 degrees. At the modern zero-altitude settings the differences from astropy are between 0.00345 and 0.00377 degrees in magnitude. Bennett's formula agrees with a direct formula evaluation at the four requested altitudes. Kasten and Young's formula differs from their integrated Table II by as much as 0.439 percent at the horizon. We adopted a disclosed 0.5 percent relative table tolerance because the assignment supplies none. validation-v1.json preserves every value, and the fresh-process recheck has identical bytes. The geometric Aldebaran comparison with Wanzke is reported without a pass rule. IGRF correction belongs to the separate measurement work and was not implemented or validated in this assignment.

The terrain calculation uses WGS84 geodesic rays and bilinear DEM sampling. For each of 1,802 azimuths we sampled GLO-30 from 2 to 20 km every 30 m, then GLO-90 from 20 km every 90 m through 249.95 km, with an additional sample at 250 km. The observer is 1.6 m above the DEM. We interpret the sea-level dip height as that total height above sea level, and use the effective Earth radius 6,371,000/(1-0.13) m for both dip and terrestrial refraction. Only tiles absent from the bucket's tile inventory count as sea level. A network error or a nodata cell stops the calculation. The local inventory contains 13 GLO-30 tiles and 103 GLO-90 tiles. The modern DEM supplies the model's terrain surface; it does not reconstruct ancient topography.

Every profile retains its input tile hashes, observer height, apparent altitude at each ray and the distance of the terrain sample that supplies the maximum. The initial PowerShell pipe converted copyright and en dash characters in the attribution to question marks. Numerical profiles and prediction tables were not replaced. The original writer's exact bytes remain in astronomy/history/horizon-v1-original-bytes.py, so its recorded code hashes remain checkable. The scorer implements the target order, discovery failure flag, the positive permutation test and leave-one-town checks, the Pleiades slope test, the Aldebaran sign test, the confirmation chi-squared comparisons, power, credence and the Wanzke compatibility checks. We report the fitted slope for all towns and for confirmation towns. P1's predicted slope is the weighted slope of the Alcyone values at the towns' dates; its mean local rate is recorded separately. The 1 to 2 degree Wanzke interval is compatible when it intersects the measurement's two-standard-deviation interval, and we also report full containment. These two interpretations keep the interval ambiguity visible.

Review found that the first comparison program accepted astronomy tables but did not calculate missing model values from settlement rows. A standalone comparison of two astronomical tables requires the same keys. selftest-analysis-v1.json preserves the first checks, v2 checks the revised comparison, and v3 repeats all checks after the attribution repair. The current result passes 13 scoring checks, 11 comparison checks, eight calibration checks and 15 scalar/vector checks. The largest scalar/vector discrepancy is 0.000017934156 degrees, within the 0.001 degree iteration threshold. The positive synthetic scorer fixture selects Alcyone, passes P0 through P3 and returns credence 0.40; the reversed fixture is dead at 0.02 and the seven-town fixture stays open at 0.10. These are tests of program behavior.

Power uses 20,000 simulations and 2,000 independent random permutations within each simulation. Every random date is evaluated by our astronomical function. The vector evaluator uses the same motion, precession, refraction and horizon definitions as the scalar function. The separate integration check uses the actual eleven coordinate pairs with explicitly synthetic dates and bearings generated from our Alcyone model. It passes all six checks and estimates P0 power as 0.99965 for that synthetic design. This is not an estimate of power for the independently measured towns, whose uncertainties and dates have not been supplied here.

The radiocarbon program reads the IntCal20 curve retrieved from intcal.org. It adds curve and laboratory variance, uses a uniform calendar prior over the published domain and normalizes the annual likelihood. Tests at five curve values return medians within 22 years of the selected curve dates. A phase calculation forms the distribution of the oldest independent calibrated determination by multiplying cumulative distributions of calendar BP. Its output also gives the oldest individual median. Shared curve covariance, reservoir effects and archaeological phase boundaries require a separate model.
