# Protocol: blind verification of the town-axes measurements

Frozen on 21 October 2026 before any town was measured for this package, before any astronomical quantity was computed here and before any output of the package under verification was opened. The SHA-256 of this file and of [MEASUREMENT-RULES.md](MEASUREMENT-RULES.md) is recorded in `PROTOCOL.sha256`. Later changes go into dated amendments.

## What is verified and why blind

The package [experiments/2026-10-21-town-axes/](../2026-10-21-town-axes/) tests study 4 of the [direction note of 20 October](../../knowledge/directions/parpola-forward-2026-10-20.md): Harappan builders aligned the main axes of new towns on the setting point of a bright star or star group, so a town's deviation from the cardinal directions records when it was laid out. It measures the axis bearings of every planned Harappan town it can, dates the layout of each, computes where nine candidate targets set or rose at each town in its layout year, chooses a target on Mohenjo-daro, Harappa and Kalibangan, and scores the other towns once under rules frozen in its [PROTOCOL.md](../2026-10-21-town-axes/PROTOCOL.md) (SHA-256 `625122e4…3df1`).

Every step of that test depends on numbers that a single team produced: bearings read from Esri World Imagery and from plans, layout dates chosen from the literature, and star positions from one implementation of precession, refraction and extinction. We repeat each of these steps independently, before the other package's results are known, so that agreement cannot be produced by knowing the answer. The repeat uses only open sources: Copernicus Sentinel-2 imagery, Landsat where it is available without an account, published plans, the Copernicus digital elevation model and our own code. When the other package publishes its `REPORT.md`, we compare the two sets of numbers and apply its frozen pass and kill rules to ours.

[READ-LOG.json](READ-LOG.json) lists what we read of the other package and when: its protocol, town list and star file, hashed. Until its `REPORT.md` exists we open none of its measurement, dating, validation, astronomy or result files and nothing under `data/raw/2026-10-21-town-axes/`. The coordinating session has read Wanzke's 1987 paper, including the orientations of six other sites that it takes from Jansen (1979); these values are given to no measurer.

## Population

The towns are the eleven required and twenty-two optional towns of the other package's [towns-v1.json](../2026-10-21-town-axes/towns-v1.json) under its inclusion rule: a town enters when a measurer finds at least one straight architectural line of the Harappan period (fortification face, street or lane edge, drain, long house wall or platform edge) whose direction can be measured in imagery or on a published plan. Towns found during the work enter under a new slug. Mohenjo-daro, Harappa and Kalibangan are the discovery towns of the other package; the rest are its confirmation towns.

## Measurement

Two measurers take every bearing independently under [MEASUREMENT-RULES.md](MEASUREMENT-RULES.md). Measurer A is a Codex CLI run of `gpt-6.1-sol` ([CODEX-PROMPT-MEASURE-A.md](CODEX-PROMPT-MEASURE-A.md), written after the tooling exists). Measurer B is a Claude subagent with a separate brief. Neither sees the other's outputs, the other package, this protocol or any hypothesis about stars, the sun, calendars or dates; both receive only the measurement rules, the town list with located coordinates and the documentation of the shared tools. Their outputs go to `measure-A/` and `measure-B/`, with renders and crops under `data/raw/2026-10-21-town-orientations-verification/`.

The tools are built and validated before any town is measured, by a separate Codex run ([CODEX-PROMPT-INFRA.md](CODEX-PROMPT-INFRA.md)). It also acquires the imagery and the plan library. Its validation measures runways whose true bearings are published or computable from surveyed endpoints, on the same Sentinel-2 composites, and synthetic rasters and plans drawn at known angles. The runway residuals give the empirical error of the imagery tool for long, high-contrast lines; a degraded version of the same test, cut to segments of 100–400 m, gives its error for lines of archaeological length.

The rules define the deviation θ of a line as its bearing from true north folded to the nearest cardinal direction, in (−45°, +45°], positive clockwise, as in the other package. Imagery bearings are geodesic azimuths on WGS 84 between the two ends of a fitted line. Plan bearings are taken relative to the plan's north arrow, whose meaning (true, magnetic, grid or unspecified) and survey year the measurer records from the plan and its publication; magnetic arrows with a survey year from 1900 on are corrected with the IGRF-14 declination for that place and year. A measurer groups its lines into systems, sets of lines in one part of a town whose deviations lie within a 6° window, and the rules choose one main system per source by total measured length.

### Town deviation

We apply the other package's town-deviation rule to our two measurers, with our measurer A's imagery value in place of its img-A and our measurer B's in place of its img-B. Two systems lie in the same part of a town when their lines come within 200 m of each other.

1. If both imagery measurers have a main system in the same part, or one covers the whole town, the town deviation θ is their inverse-variance weighted mean. If they lie in different parts, we sum for each part the total measured lengths that both measurers report there, choose the part with the larger sum and combine the two measurers' systems for that part; a measurer with no system there contributes nothing.
2. If only one imagery measurer has a value, it is used alone.
3. If neither has one, θ comes from plan systems whose arrow is classified `true`, or `grid` with 0.5° added in quadrature to the error, combined in the same way. Plans with `magnetic` or `unspecified` arrows never enter the primary value.
4. The town error is σθ = max(propagated standard error, half the difference between the two combined values, 0.3°).
5. Two imagery values that differ by more than three combined standard errors and by more than 1.5° are flagged as discordant; the rule still applies.

An imagery value enters only when its standard error is at most 2.0°, because a 10 m image of a short wall can return a direction without measuring it. A system with a larger error is reported and kept out of the town value. The table also keeps every plan value with its north class, declination-corrected values of magnetic plans and any plan registered to the imagery by control points, for comparison only.

## Layout dates

A separate Codex run with live web search ([CODEX-PROMPT-DATING.md](CODEX-PROMPT-DATING.md)) assembles, for each town and each part of it, the published phase sequence, the phase in which its exposed Harappan walls or street grid were first built, the radiocarbon determinations and models that date that phase, and the date of first occupation, with page references. It is not told what the dates are for. The coordinating session then sets each layout date by the other package's three levels of evidence: a published Bayesian model (median of the start boundary, σD = width of the 95% range / 3.92); a published calibrated start based on radiocarbon from the site (σD = a quarter of the stated range for the start, else 150 years); an excavator's start from cross-dating (σD = 200 years). Where sources at the same level disagree, the most recent is used and the disagreement is recorded. These judgements are written to `dates-v1/` and hashed before the coordinating session opens any output of either measurer. A town's date is the date of the part that supplied its deviation.

## Astronomy

New code in `astronomy/` ([CODEX-PROMPT-ASTRO.md](CODEX-PROMPT-ASTRO.md)) implements the other package's stated definitions without reading its code, so that any difference between the two packages comes from implementation. Its targets are Alcyone (η Tauri) for the Pleiades, Aldebaran and Procyon, setting on the western horizon, and the sun at the equinoxes and solstices, setting and rising. Positions, proper motions, parallaxes and radial velocities come from our own SIMBAD retrieval of the Hipparcos new reduction. Space motion is propagated rigorously to the epoch, and precession follows Vondrák, Capitaine and Wallace (2011, *Astronomy & Astrophysics* 534, A22), coded from the published coefficients; nutation, aberration and annual parallax are ignored. The horizon comes from the Copernicus DEM, GLO-30 to 20 km and GLO-90 from 20 to 250 km, for an observer 1.6 m above the surface, excluding terrain within 2 km, with terrestrial refraction coefficient 0.13. Astronomical refraction follows Bennett (1982) at 20 °C and 1010 hPa. A star is last seen setting at the larger of the apparent horizon and its extinction angle, the apparent altitude at which m + k(X − 1) reaches magnitude 6.0, with k = 0.30 and the Kasten and Young (1989) air mass. The sun is taken when its upper limb meets the apparent horizon. The predicted deviation θc(D) is the azimuth minus 270° for setting targets and minus 90° for the equinoctial sunrise, and the solstitial targets keep the nearest cardinal direction; we tabulate it every 10 years from 3500 to 1500 BCE (astronomical years −3499 to −1499) for every town, with the sensitivity variants k = 0.20, k = 0.45, no extinction and a flat horizon.

Before the code is used it must pass the other package's three gates (mean obliquity in 2500 BCE within 0.02° of 23.97°; least polar distance of Thuban between 2900 and 2700 BCE; Aldebaran's geometric setting azimuth at Mohenjo-daro in 2500 and 1500 BCE reported beside Wanzke's −3.0 and +3.8 gon) and four of ours: the ERFA test vectors for the long-term precession matrix, agreement with `pyerfa` within 0.001° for the precessed positions of the three stars at −2499, agreement with `astropy` within 0.02° for the geometric setting azimuth of each star at Mohenjo-daro on 21 October 2026 (astropy includes nutation and aberration, which move these stars by about 0.01°), and the IGRF-14 declination against published test values.

## Scoring scripts

The same run writes `analysis/score_frozen.py`, our own implementation of the other package's stage 1 choice, predictions P0 to P3, check D1, power simulation and sensitivity hooks, and `analysis/compare.py`, which compares two settlement tables. Both read a plain CSV (town, part, latitude, longitude, θ, σθ, D, σD, with optional θc columns), so that the other package's table enters through a short adapter written after its report. Both are tested on synthetic tables with known outcomes before use.

## Comparisons, in this order

**Agreement between our measurers**, computed once both have finished and our dates are frozen: for every town both measured, the difference of their main imagery systems and of their main plan systems, its z-score, whether the systems lie in the same part, the root mean square difference and the counts within 1° and 2°.

**Agreement with the other package**, computed when its `REPORT.md` exists. For bearings, over towns with a primary value in both tables, Δ = θours − θtheirs, z = Δ/√(σours² + σtheirs²), their weighted mean with its standard error, the median |Δ|, the root mean square and the towns with |z| > 2 and > 3. The bearings agree when the weighted mean of Δ lies within two standard errors of zero or within ±0.5°, and at least 80% of the common towns have |z| ≤ 2.5. For astronomy, at the other package's coordinates and dates, the largest |θc,ours − θc,theirs| over the nine targets agrees when it is at most 0.10°. For dates, they agree when |ΔD| ≤ max(100 years, σD of the other package) for at least 80% of the common towns. Every disagreement is listed with its likely cause: a different part or system, a different source, a plan north class or a different phase.

**The frozen rules applied to our numbers.** V1 uses our town deviations, the other package's dates and our θc, with its frozen choice of target for P3, and also reports the stage 1 choice our deviations would make. V2 uses our deviations, our dates and our θc. V3 uses the other package's deviations with our dates, and V4 its deviations and dates with our θc. Each variant reports P0 to P3, D1, the power of P0 and the credence that the other package's joint rule assigns. The other package's outcome is reproduced when V1 gives the same P0 result (pass, fail or undecided) and the same credence as its report.

We record the verification as `reproduced` when bearings, astronomy and the V1 outcome all agree, and as a review with listed discrepancies otherwise. If the other package's report is not out when our measurements and dates are complete, we write up the blind measurements, the dates, the astronomy, our measurers' agreement and the scoring scripts, hash them, and stop. Anyone can then run `compare.py` and `score_frozen.py` when the report exists.

## Freshness and the bench

The study uses no inscriptions and spends nothing in the corpus. No `python -m bench` task scores settlement orientations; we ran `python -m bench shots --synthetic` to document the freeze workflow ([shots-synthetic-v2.json](shots-synthetic-v2.json); the first attempt, [v1](shots-synthetic-v1.stdout.txt), stopped when a file in the OneDrive-synced package could not be renamed, and v2 wrote its fixtures outside the synced folder). Our scripts follow the bench conventions: versioned JSON with `schema_version`, `task`, `synthetic` and `provenance`, input and code hashes, and refusal to overwrite. Towns that the other package has scored are measured again here; this is verification of the same population and adds no fresh confirmation material.

## Sources, rights and handling

Raw imagery, elevation tiles, plan scans, renders and the Python environment stay local under `data/raw/2026-10-21-town-orientations-verification/` with URLs, retrieval times, sizes, hashes and rights notes. Downloads are untrusted data: scripts live in the package outside the raw directory, paths are passed as arguments, and Python that reads downloaded files runs with `-I`. Commercial basemaps (Esri, Google, Bing, Mapbox) and imagery behind a login are not used; OpenStreetMap and Wikidata supply site coordinates only, never geometry to measure. Tracked files hold code, prompts, protocols, line endpoints and bearings, aggregate tables, manifests and hashes. Every Codex run is recorded with its prompt, launcher, log location, model and harness, and reviewed before its output is used.
