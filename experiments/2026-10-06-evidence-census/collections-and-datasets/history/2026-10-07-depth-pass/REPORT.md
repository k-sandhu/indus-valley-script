# Findings

The evidence census still cannot establish that all findable Indus-script material is indexed. The depth pass queried all **37 collection rows** through site-specific live searches or direct catalogues and added exact HTTP statuses and response bodies for the routes that were retrievable. Only one museum API produced a deduplicable, inscription-specific total: the Cleveland Museum of Art returned **3 distinct seal records** for `q=Indus seal`, object IDs **147416, 140095 and 147417**. The API's broader `q=Indus` returned 10 records, of which these same three were inscribed seals. The other prominent totals use broader result units or remain unmeasured.

This lower bound combines examples identified by accession/object number in sampled records; it is not a count of complete catalogue results. Several pages are gallery or collection highlights rather than query-result objects, so their witness totals do not estimate holdings.

At the collection-row level, an exact direct HTTP status was available for **11 of 37 rows**: seven 200 responses, one 403, one 410 and two 429 responses. The other 26 rows rely on search excerpts or timed-out direct routes with no upstream status. A `null` in a row's HTTP or result-count field means unobservable or unmeasured. It is not a zero. `not_found` is reserved for a concrete accessible query whose results returned no matching object; `not queried` is distinct and is not used for the 37 rows in this pass.

## Metadata and image access

The updated collection portion of the matrix contains **37 rows**. Sampled public records documented accession/object numbers in **15**, findspot in **13**, dates in **15**, dimensions in **14**, material in **12**, photographs in **15**, excavation identifiers in **1**, bibliography in **6**, and explicitly multiple views in **0**. The low excavation-identifier count reflects the specific record fields visible in this sample; it does not mean the museum or excavation archive lacks identifiers. The British Museum record 1947,0416.4 exposes the additional ID `Hr 5635`. Its object type, findspot, material, date and dimensions are also recorded; the museum and corpus excavation strings do not provide an exact join.

The Louvre SB 2425 JSON record gives identifiers SB 2425, AS 41 and CCO S.299, identifies the Indus writing system, gives Susa as the findspot, and exposes date, dimensions, steatite, bibliography and two image URLs with individual credits. The two URLs are recorded as two image assets; the record does not label them as separate views. Cleveland's three object records provide web, print and full-size renditions of one source image per object; those renditions are not counted as separate photographs. Other catalogues commonly expose accession, date, material and dimensions on the sampled pages. Excavation numbers and object-to-CISI references are much less often visible.

Image and data reuse terms are not uniform. The Met identifies qualifying Open Access data and Public Domain images; Cleveland designates its Open Access assets CC0; Brooklyn's search permits filtering for Creative Commons or no-known-copyright images, with rights on each object record. British Museum image rights vary by asset, while its catalogue guide describes search-result downloads. Louvre pages carry per-image photographer/agency credits. No blanket licence was verified for Indian museum, Pakistani, Gulf, or other regional catalogues. Register entries retain the distinction between an API or download route and permission to reuse the returned records or image assets.

## Institutions without an object-level online census

Official searchable routes or selected online records were found for the British Museum, Louvre, Met, Cleveland, CSMVS, Indian Museum Kolkata/JATAN, NMMA, National Museum New Delhi, Ashmolean, Penn, Brooklyn, Berlin State Museums, and Cambridge MAA. A route's existence does not mean an Indus-specific result total was exposed. The National Museum's selected gallery pages show script-bearing objects, but its published **3,500+ Harappan-object** holding figure is not a count of online inscriptions. Lothal's 133 seal/sealing category and British Museum's 724 culture-term results also include objects that were not individually checked for script.

For the site museums at Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira and Rakhigarhi, and for the National Museum of Pakistan, Iraq Museum, Kuwait National Museum/Failaka, Iran, Oman, Turkmenistan and Afghanistan, the live searches did not establish a public item-level count of Indus-script objects. This records a limit of the searched public routes; it is not a statement that these institutions hold no relevant objects. Lahore Museum's official page displays one Mohenjo-daro unicorn-seal highlight but gives no searchable catalogue count. Bahrain's official Hall of Dilmun page confirms a displayed collection of Dilmun stamp seals, without identifying an Indus-script object count. UAE and Turkmenistan results include comparative Gulf/Margush seals, which were not counted as confirmed Indus inscriptions.

## Datasets and archives

The 15-row dataset register carries forward the 2 October landscape without recounting the raw Lipi or Mayig payloads. The original local audit denominators remain **5,680 Lipi records / 4,695 provisional families / 19,946 token positions** and **179 Mayig faces / 1,003 token positions**. Andreas Fuls's 2023 ICIT abstract reports **4,665 objects, 5,650 texts, 17,966 legible-sign occurrences and 16,015 photographs/drawings**; these are historical published counts, not a current export ([abstract book, p. 281](https://2023.caaconference.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2023/04/abstract_book_v13.pdf#page=281)). Helsinki CISI volumes and editions provide published object images and identifiers, but the corpus lacks a unified, current public object-level export verified in this pass. These units overlap and are not summed.

The matrix adds all 15 dataset entries. Its dataset flags are drawn from the existing `metadata_fields` descriptions, not a new independent schema audit. ICIT, Mahadevan's IM77/RMRL, Lipi, Mayig, epachayan, Hugging Face, Kaggle, Zenodo, GitHub, Harappa.com/HARP, Helsinki CISI photographs and editions, and 3D/RTI routes retain their earlier count bases, version and licence qualifications. No new 2024–2026 release was verified as a complete museum-to-corpus register. No image collection was downloaded.

## Paired museum-to-corpus audit

The audit confirmed **0 of 30 pairs (0%)** using the required explicit-key rule. The only near-candidate added is British Museum 1947,0416.4 with additional ID `Hr 5635` and Lipi `record_id` 2478.1, whose `source_cisi` is M-1906 and whose `metadata.excavation-idno` is `HR 5635345`. These strings are not identical and no published concordance linking these specific records was found, so the candidate remains unconfirmed. It was not included in the numerator or field-agreement sample. The remaining 29 slots are marked unfilled; no visual matches were substituted.

## Scope

This is an exploratory discoverability census, not a complete holdings count. The query evidence supports a strong statement that online evidence is dispersed and metadata varies, but it cannot estimate the fraction of all surviving Indus inscriptions that is indexed. The main unresolved work is acquiring stable per-term result sets from regional museum portals, obtaining current catalogue exports where available, and checking museum-to-CISI concordances against identifiers rather than images.
