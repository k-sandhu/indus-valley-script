# Site check D: Susa and Ur

Independent read-only check of two rows of batch D (finds abroad), 7 October 2026. The rows are in `experiments/2026-10-06-evidence-census/sites-deep/d-finds-abroad/site-rows.json`. Susa was drawn at random. Ur was chosen because the synthesis (`synthesis/conflicts.json`, entry `Foreign-find-denominator`) discusses BM 123208, a seal found at Ur that the local export files under Dilmun. No existing file was edited.

## What I opened

- Gadd 1932: the batch's local PDF (`data/raw/2026-10-06-evidence-census/sites-deep/d-finds-abroad/gadd1932.pdf`; 44 image-only pages; sha256 matches the ledger), viewed as page images, with the earlier OCR in `research/literature/sources/gadd1932_ocr.txt` for text search. Gadd page numbers are the reprint's printed numbers (printed p. 14 is PDF p. 25).
- Laursen 2010: batch D keeps no PDF. I read the copy another batch acquired, in place (`data/raw/2026-10-06-backward-chronology/amendment-2026-10-07-frenez/laursen2010.pdf`; 39 pages; sha256 b28d8150...), and compared it with batch D's stored web witness (calls 27, 33, 47, 55 and 64 of `web-witness.json`), which agrees on Fig. 15 and Table 1. Laursen page numbers are printed journal pages (p. 126 is PDF p. 31).
- Louvre record cl010176471 (witness call 66 and a live fetch today), Encyclopaedia Iranica "SUSA i. EXCAVATIONS" (call 51), UNESCO Susa pages (calls 63 and 69), the BM 123208 search snippet (call 80), the CISI 3.3 excerpt (`cisi33-excerpt2022.txt`), Wikipedia "Susa" and "Ur" as an independent gazetteer, and `data/normalized/lipi_observed_faces.jsonl`.
- Fetches of harappa.com, Wiley, Semantic Scholar and the UNESCO pages returned 403, so every UNESCO and Laursen check rests on the stored witness or the other batch's PDF.

Row pointers in the tables continue from `site-rows.json > [site=Susa]` or `[site=Ur]` unless another row is named. Witness call numbers index `web-witness.json` from 0.

## Findings

**Susa.** Both counted objects match their sources, and the minimum of two rests on a script statement for each: the Louvre's script field for the cylinder, and Laursen's Indus-inscribed typing for the round seal, which p. 117 backs with Parpola's finding of a parallel for the full sequence. Gadd p. 16 independently ties the Susa cylinder to Delaporte's number S. 299. The corrections are small. The province is correct but absent from the listed sources, the Louvre date is a period range, the period-or-time count is zero because the coverage file counts only the `time` field, and `susa_unesco_maps` is missing from the row's source lists.

**Ur.** The counts of 16 and 6 and their page locators hold (Gadd p. 14, Laursen p. 126). The row misplaces no. 1 (pp. 5-6, not 3-5) and gives an excavation number, U.7683, that no source in the batch contains. Its note on no. 7 says the sign is disputed where Gadd calls it certain. The holdings and campaign cells understate Gadd: Pennsylvania holds five seals, and the dated seasons begin in 1925-6. The six Gulf-type seals are Laursen's list. Five show signs in Gadd's text and plates; no. 5 shows at most a partial stroke and is counted anyway, which indicates that the minimum reproduces a class list without a per-object sign test. It admits a fragment with a trace of a sign and excludes a cylinder with a certain one, while the Susa row admits a cylinder on a museum script field. This does not change the totals of six and two, and it is worth stating where the minimum is defined.

**BM 123208.** The claim in `conflicts.json` is correct. Gadd no. 16 fixes the findspot at Ur (season 1931-2, filling of a tomb shaft), and the BM snippet agrees on excavator and season while giving Dilmun as production place. The local export holds this seal under two labels, Ur 3898.1 (U17649) and Dilmun 6000.1 (BM-123208), so the Ur and Dilmun families are probably one object with two sign readings. Ur face 3898.1 and the BM date it to the Third Dynasty of Ur, while Gadd gives Woolley's first attribution, the Second Dynasty. Laursen p. 120 explains the difference: Nissen later re-dated shaft grave PG 1847.

**Local export.** Thickness strings on Ur 3897.1, 3898.1, 5225.1 and 5231.1 and Susa 3881.1 equal Laursen's Table 1 disc height and disc-plus-boss height for rows 16, 21, 17, 18 and 14, and several other Gulf-region labels show the same match. I infer that these local records were built from Laursen's table. If so, agreement between the local faces and the published counts for round seals is partly circular, because both come from one source. The five Ur faces would then correspond to five of the six counted seals (nos. 2, 3, 4, 15 and 16), and Gadd no. 5 would be the object without a local face. The row leaves the join open, as the protocol requires, but the arithmetic for it is available. The Susa pairing is Susa 3881.1 with Amiet 1643. Susa 3882.1, a cylinder with a trough motif, is a candidate for SB 2425; the Louvre rates the condition good and Lipi lists poor, so that pairing stays open.

**Source notes.** Laursen's Fig. 15 caption gives per-site numbers that add to 29 against its stated n = 28 [27]. The Ur (6) and Susa (1) entries agree with Table 1, so the rows are unaffected.

**Not found (looked, absent).** U.7683 in Gadd's text and in all batch D witness files. Any dispute of Gadd's no. 7 sign in Gadd or Laursen. A province name for Susa in the Louvre and UNESCO witness text. Drawings in Gadd's plates. A modern state name for Ur in Gadd or Laursen.

**Not checked.** Amiet 1972 (pl. 153, no. 1643) and Delaporte 1920 themselves. Current BM and Pennsylvania records for the Ur seals. Woolley 1934. Parpola 1994a. The kind of image in the Louvre record. The identity of SB 5614. The row statements on ICIT and Mahadevan counts and on the four CISI assignment volumes.
