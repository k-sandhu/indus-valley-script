# Site check C: Chandigarh and Rakhigarhi

Read-only check of two rows of `experiments/2026-10-06-evidence-census/sites-deep/c-north-india/site-rows.json` against the cited sources, carried out on 2026-10-07. Chandigarh was drawn at random. Rakhigarhi was chosen as a major recent excavation with a CISI 1 run (Rgr1–3) and 13 local faces.

## Conventions

Row pointers in the tables read `site-rows.json > rows[name=<site>] > <field>`; only `<field>` is written out. Ledger pointers read `sources.json > sources[id=<id>]`.

CISI 1 page offset, checked on the local `cisi1.pdf` (862 pages): printed page plus 36 gives the PDF page for Arabic-numbered pages (351 at PDF 387, 359 at 395, 360 at 396, 364 at 400, 365 at 401, 366 at 402, 375 at 411). Roman-numbered front matter sits at printed plus 4 (IV at PDF 8, XII at 16, XXIV at 28, XXV at 29). The scan repeats the volume: the image content of PDF 432–862 is identical to PDF 1–431 on every page (image hashes compared).

Other offsets used: Puratattva 6 (`chandigarh_article.pdf`), printed p. 52 at PDF 197, p. 53 at 200, p. 54 at 201, p. 55 at 202, Plate I at 240, volume title page at 122, contents at 123. IAR 1999–2000 (`iar1999.pdf`), printed p. 30 at PDF 38 (printed plus 8). Masudpur 2021 (`masudpur2021.pdf`), printed p. 42 at PDF 56 (printed plus 14). ASI Inventory (`rakhigarhi_inventory.pdf`), printed p. 34 at PDF 53 (printed plus 19).

Mahadevan 1977: the local package has no copy. I fetched the 48-page excerpt at the ledger URL through WebFetch (it matches the 48 pages the package's reader reported) and cite its PDF pages, with printed pages beside them. The tool stored that copy outside the repository; I deleted it at the end. Wikipedia infobox values were read through WebFetch and serve as the independent gazetteer.

Verdicts: confirmed, qualified, discrepancy, unverifiable. Statements marked Inference or Hypothesis are mine and are not source statements.

Four faces (three objects) carry Rgr-1 to Rgr-3 and match the CISI 1 run by site, count and object type. The other nine faces have no CISI identifier, and CISI 1 has no Rakhigarhi entry beyond Rgr-3, so none of them can belong to that run. Seven have no excavation number either. Inference: `RGR 7230` and `RGR6304` follow the RGR site code that IAR 1999–2000 uses for mounds (RGR-1 to RGR-7) and are probably object numbers from the Nath seasons or later work; nothing I opened establishes that.

The family numbers sit above the CISI-derived block (3867–3869) and beside other late additions: the neighbours of 4148–4154 are Dholavira, Gonur Depe and Harappa objects numbered H-1503 to H-2589, beyond CISI 1's Harappa range (H-1 to 382). The indus-website export, which shares this numbering, holds only 3868, 3869 and 4153 of the twelve Rakhigarhi families. The numbering is therefore the ICIT-derived database's own, and no printed catalogue I opened carries it. The exports give no edition label per record. The package's publications register (`sites-and-publications/REPORT.md`, read only in excerpt) lists the titles of CISI 3.2 (Shahr-i Sokhta to Rojdi) and 3.3 (Indo-Iranian Borderlands) without Rakhigarhi, and I opened no CISI 2 or 3 volume. Mahadevan 1977 has one Rakhigadhi object. The source edition of the nine faces therefore stays unidentified. Hypothesis: 4153.1 (period 6, phase 2) echoes the draft report's phrase "period II of phase VI" for square steatite seals (`live-find-01.json`, lines 4384–4386), which would place it in the Nath seasons with the two fields transposed. Family 5468 matches the inventory's cylinder seal (R20).
