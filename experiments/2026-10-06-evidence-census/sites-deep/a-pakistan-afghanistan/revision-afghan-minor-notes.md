# Afghanistan and minor-site revision

The revision covers 11 requested labels. It identifies three published gazetteer coordinate entries, verifies the three pottery sherds catalogued as Nwd1–3, and confirms that CISI2 treats Gharo Bhiro and Nuhato as aliases. The search ledger contains 86 live queries, with at least three for every label. The source ledger contains 30 records and distinguishes inspected bodies, bibliographic records, search excerpts and failed access. These are exploratory census records. ([Rows](revision-afghan-minor.json), `coordinates`, `cisi`, `alias_cross_reference`; [searches](revision-afghan-minor-searches.json); [sources](revision-afghan-minor-sources.json).)

## Counts and site identity

CISI2 publishes Naru-Waro-dharo objects Nwd1–3 on p.406 and their basic data on p.447. Its introduction explicitly describes three pottery sherds. The local export contains three faces, three object families and three distinct Nwd identifiers. The published identifier range is contiguous from 1 to 3, so the local export covers this illustrated inventory. The excavation-wide count remains unresolved because the original Pakistan Archaeology1 report, pp.43–44, was identified through citations but not obtained. ([Rows](revision-afghan-minor.json), Naru-Waro-dharo `reported_counts_by_class`, `publication_metadata`, `local_coverage_judgment`; source RAM-CISI2, pp.xvii–xviii,406,447.)

CISI2 prints “Gharo Bhiro (Nuhato)” in its introduction and catalogues one surface seal, Grb1. The local export has one Gharo Bhiro family and five Nuhato families. The catalogue alias permits a shared site entity, but the six local families need an object identity audit before they can be added together. The one published seal is therefore repeated as an alias cross-reference, with an explicit instruction against adding the two rows. Its 1991 owner code means private collection; present custody has not been established. ([Rows](revision-afghan-minor.json), both alias rows, `alias_cross_reference`, `present_holding`; RAM-CISI2, pp.xv,392,434,447.)

The Shortughai CISI3.3 table of contents lists Sh1–84 on pp.509–523. The preface reports two seals and plentiful graffiti. Those 84 entries include mixed glyptic material and pottery marks, so they cannot serve as an inventory of 84 authenticated inscribed objects. The local Sh69 record covers one seal face and family. The selected public PDF omits the site plates and basic data, preventing an object-level denominator. ([Rows](revision-afghan-minor.json), Shortughai `cisi`, `reported_counts_by_class`, `local_coverage_judgment`; RAM-CISI33, pp.vii–viii,xv.)

Fuls2023 p.498 defines its bracketed regional figures as occurrences of legible signs. This corrects the first pass's object-count interpretation for Bakkar Buthi, Gharo Bhiro, Nuhato, Rajanpur and the smaller Inter-Riverine labels. Bakkar Buthi's actual Fuls2022 body entry, p.30, does contain one ICIT pottery object, ID18; most other assigned site bodies lie outside the public preview. The revision retains those distinctions in `superseded_first_pass_claims` and `icit_count`. ([Rows](revision-afghan-minor.json); RAM-FULS2023, p.498 section5.3; RAM-FULS2022, p.30.)

## Coordinates and publication histories

Uesugi2010 gives Naru-Waro-dharo at 27°25′00″N,68°41′00″E, Mundigak at 31°55′00″N,65°30′00″E and Shortughai at 37°18′00″N,69°30′00″E. The positions are published gazetteer entries, not modern survey fixes. Shortughai is placed under Badakhshan in that gazetteer, while the current DAFA account places it in Takhar. Mundigak's campaign endpoint also differs: DAFA says1959, whereas the gazetteer and CISI3.3 preface say1958. The row records both. ([Rows](revision-afghan-minor.json), `coordinates`, `province`, `excavation_campaigns`; RAM-GAZ-PK p.116; RAM-GAZ-AF pp.136,138; RAM-DAFA-S; RAM-DAFA-M; RAM-CISI33 p.x.)

The Rajanpur article was recovered and visually read. It is Qasim Ali Qasim2001, *Indus Civilization Sites Discovered in Sandal Bar*, pp.38–40, rather than the first pass's Mughal1964 attribution. Page38 locates RajanpurChak585 at 31°09′30″N,73°23′00″E and describes surface ceramics. Its identity with the local tablet remains unresolved; the row records these as candidate coordinates. The modern Rajanpur district is another homonym and has not been substituted. ([Rows](revision-afghan-minor.json), Rajanpur `coordinates`, `publications`, `superseded_first_pass_claims`; RAM-QASIM2001, p.38/PDF74.)

Two final excavation publications are securely identified bibliographically: Francfort1989 for Shortughai and Casal1961 for Mundigak. A two-page1964 Naru excavation account is also identified, but its status as the definitive report remains unresolved. None of these three full report bodies was inspected. The `final_report.status` values are therefore `identified`, with the exact access limitation retained; they are not counted as verified full reports. ([Rows](revision-afghan-minor.json), `final_report`; RAM-FRANCFORT1989; RAM-CASAL1961; RAM-NARU1964.)

## Approaches, access failures and reuse

The first pass's local figures and preserved row structure were reused. New evidence came from the coordinator's full CISI2 acquisition, the selected CISI3.3 PDF, DAFA histories, Uesugi's gazetteer chapters, the primary1972 survey, an excavation photograph and scanned2001 proceedings. Page extraction worked for the gazetteer and CISI2. The proceedings required image rendering because text extraction returned almost no article text. These approaches and their access scopes are recorded in the source ledger.

The Nuhato2019 Google Drive download returned404 through requests and a failed redirect through the web tool. Baloch2004 and the terminal download of the1989 Cholistan appendix returned403. Pleiades showed a bot challenge, and ArchAtlas returned403. Persée's1978 Shortughai PDF requires a captcha; the opening article page was inspected, with no attempt to bypass the download restriction. Failed searches remain in the query ledger. Acquired third-party PDFs, page extractions and renders remain under the local raw directory; no image reuse permission is inferred from public viewing.
