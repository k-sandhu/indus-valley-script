# Retrospective

Status: Exploratory.7 October 2026.

## Revision and reused work

The first pass left many fields unverified and described normalized search strings as though they were an executed search trail. Its ten files were copied before revision, with byte counts and SHA-256 values. The new 130-query record comes from actual live-tool responses. Every site was revisited, including the added Kanewal; shared ICIT queries are excluded from the three-query site minimum.

The coordinator’s local-site-coverage.json was reused unchanged. The earlier sites-and-publications census and October 6 contribution assessment supplied leads, not verified field values. The existing CISI 1 scan and its counting/errata audit avoided repeating a scan acquisition. The Mahadevan 1977 full scan came from batch A’s common-revision witness. Existing Frenez–Tosi 2005 and Rangpur 1963 PDFs, plus the backward-chronology Rangpur OCR, were reused. The Rangpur count was checked visually on the original p. 130 rather than accepted from OCR alone. Acquisition records name every reused path.

## Access problems and fixes

Several NMMA annual-review downloads timed out. Alternate official ASI Aurangabad URLs supplied readable PDFs. Other routes returned 404 and remain in the record. Lothal, Kanmer and Bet Dwarka mirrors returned HTTP200 challenge/HTML pages to requests; web-indexed original report content was readable separately. Transport success was therefore audited apart from content access.

The Nuhato 2019 journal download failed. Its Sindh departmental publication page exposed another public PDF link, which supplied a 121-page 2018 report. That report yielded coordinates, campaign history, two inscribed seals and illustrated graffiti; its 2019 journal pagination is not assumed equivalent. Publisher downloads for Juna Khatiya’s 2021 paper returned 404 while the indexed full PDF remained readable. Rojdi’s CISI 3.2 pages and the cited Nuhato CISI 2 page could not be obtained.

## Corrections and count decisions

The first pass assigned Juna Khatiya’s coordinates and pottery lead to Juni Kuran. The gazetteer and site sources now keep these records separate. Desalpar’s three objects are two seals and a sealing. The verified Somnath monograph is by Nanavati, Mehta and Chowdhary, 1971. Rangpur’s 222 marked sherds, Dholavira’s 225 recovered versus 194 analysed seals, and Lothal’s 91 versus 93 sealings required count bases and disagreements to be preserved explicitly.

CISI contents and plates establish contiguous catalogue ranges for ten sites. Object identifiers were separated from multiple faces and modern impression photographs. The local export also contains later finds, so a single coverage percentage would mix dates and units. The ICIT-related publication search yielded 13 site aggregates totaling 1446 legible signs; these are excluded from object counts. Current files are UTF-8; original archive bytes remain intact, including their damaged characters.

## Limits and follow-up

Six identified final reports remain unread at their object pages. Several sources describe a site without exposing an inscription inventory, so availability assessments are tied to the inspected passage and accompanied by failed-search records. Current museum destinations, exact later-season class totals and several campaign directors remain unresolved. The next useful work is direct inspection of Rojdi’s 2019 catalogue, Nuhato’s 1991 citation and the six remaining final inventories, followed by registration-number matching to the local export. The dataset supports that review through source IDs, locations, count units and preservation hashes.
