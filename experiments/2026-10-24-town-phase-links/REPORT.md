# Named walls have a sequence of building episodes

We added 11 source-qualified relations for the Lothal dock and Dholavira castle and middle town after inspecting 24 pages in two held excavation scans. These relations connect structures to additions, repairs, retained plans and later deposits. None provides an identified construction sample joined to a surveyed wall. The [aggregate output](run-v1/summary.json) records the counts and hashes; the detailed relations and page derivatives remain on D: under `data/raw/2026-10-24-town-phase-links/`. The [source manifest](source-manifest.json) lists exact pages, source identities and inherited retrieval records.

## Dholavira: a fortification grows inward

The held preliminary January 2015 scan gives a physical sequence for the castle's southern defences. On printed p. 95 (PDF index 111), the first settlement lies below the castle and its southern wall is cut near the southwest corner. The text reports an 11 m basal width. Fig. 5.1 on p. 96 shows the early masonry and later additions in a section facing east across the rain gully. On p. 97, a 2.80 m brick wall is added inside the Stage I defence in Stage II; the addition also abuts the Stage I wall segment extending east. Fig. 5.3 on p. 98 names Trench 55X5X2, with its section facing south. On p. 99 the southern wall is widened inward again by about 4.5 m in Stage III, when the earlier enclosure becomes the castle. The prose does not assign that addition separately to IIIA or IIIB.

The middle town belongs to another episode. Fig. 5.2 on p. 97 begins its schematic column at IIIA, while the text on p. 99 puts the foundation of the extensive walled town north of the cleared residential area in Stage III. The schematic supplies a more specific stage attribution, but does not identify each extant enclosure face. Stage III damage and repairs, Stage IV maintenance and Stage VI reuse of some walls (pp. 99-100, 103) require separate treatment when matching a measured line to masonry.

The current plan cannot complete that match. Fig. 6.3 on p. 110 shows the castle with a north symbol and scale, but no visible wall-by-wall stage key. The section and the plan need surveyed common coordinates before an exposed facing can inherit the section's construction attribution. Fig. 6.1 and the descriptions on pp. 109-111 and 113 describe the middle-town geometry and enclosure salients; they do not locate dating samples on those arms. We preserve the scan's preliminary edition status: identity with the final published report is still unresolved.

The chronology table on pp. 104-105 associates TL labels DHR 93/TL/8 and DHR 93/TL/4 with Stages II and III. The discussion questions the consistency of the Stage II determination. These are thermoluminescence entries, not new radiocarbon dates. The selected pages supply no sample material, exact collection locus or construction relationship for either wall. We therefore add a phase-level dating relation and leave the wall-to-sample join empty.

## Lothal: the dock changes while its basin fills

Rao's *Lothal: A Harappan Port Town (1955-62), Volume I* explicitly assigns dock construction to Phase IIa on p. 125 (PDF index 135). The broader Phase II account on p. 29 links earth excavated from the basin with the raised platforms of the Acropolis and Lower Town. The circa 2350 BC figure on p. 125 remains Rao's chronology. His p. 44 derives the sequence using cross-dating and a count of building phases around a radiocarbon anchor; it is not an independently dated dock foundation. That page's inconsistent Phase IIA wording is preserved in the earlier register.

Fig. 19 on p. 124 identifies the first and second inlet sills, spillway and basin floor in an isometric drawing. It supplies structural identity, not a north-referenced bearing plan. The following text assigns the eastern inlet to Phase IV after damage in IIIb and describes closing drains that passed through the western wall. A survey should separate the initial embankment from inlet replacements, breach repairs and drain blocking.

The fill supplies a further ordering relation. On p. 125 occupation debris seals the damaged western embankment. On p. 126 later Period B debris overlies mature Harappan debris in the northern basin, with later black clay above. These descriptions make redeposition explicit. The inherited dating compilation identifies TF-19 with debris sealing the western dock wall in SRG 11; this round did not reread its original laboratory page and does not upgrade that identification. Its deciding missing evidence is the sample's position in a section through the particular damaged wall, with an assessment of residual charcoal and old wood. A later fill cannot supply the original building boundary through a shared site or phase name.

We also retained a source conflict relevant to plan matching. Printed pp. 86 and 87 reverse the stated compass directions of Street 1 and Street 6. A future join must check plate XXXVIII and street labels against a true-north survey. Our source copy lacks the plate volume. We did not select one prose direction as correct.

## Scope and checks

This is an extension of explicit wall relations, not an increase in the register's 34 towns, 1,380 measurements, 118 dated-part assertions or 620 radiocarbon assertions. Some building-phase facts were already in the 21 October compilations. The new contribution brings the original section, illustration and deposition descriptions into a checked relation table and makes the uncompleted joins explicit. No new bearing, determination, calibration, boundary estimate or scientific test was produced. The source selection and 24-page stopping rule were written in the [protocol](PROTOCOL.md); [reproduce.py](reproduce.py) checks locators, hashes, fields and counts. These are author checks, with verification tier submitted. No confirmation cohort was opened and the bench spending ledger was unchanged.

The strongest supported planning reading is that builders preserved an architectural line through substantial changes in masonry and use. Dholavira's castle grew through inward additions, while its middle-town enclosure began in a later episode; Lothal's basin retained an embankment system through changes to inlets and drains. The deciding test is to survey named surviving faces to true north, map each face onto phase-keyed excavation sections and restoration records, then date short-lived material sealed by its construction. Freeze those wall identities and sample relations before evaluating whether bearings were retained across rebuilding. This source review establishes the sequence to test; it has not measured that retention.
