# Amendment 1: locality of opening and closing segments

Recorded 12 October 2026 after the frozen S1–S5 outcomes and before computing anything below. Exploratory.

Whole-text locality cannot separate two readings of the duplicates: titles or categories shared across cities, and common personal names borne by different people in different cities. Both spread identical texts among cities. The texts' internal structure gives a sharper comparison. Indus texts begin with many different signs and end with few. If a seal text combined a personal, family or local element with a general title or category, and the variable element came first, seals sharing an opening segment would tend to come from the same city, while seals sharing a closing segment would not.

**Population.** The eligible seals of the protocol with at least four signs, so that two-sign opening and closing segments are proper parts of the text.

**Statistics.** For each segment type, group seals by the segment and keep groups of two or more seals. Compute the text-weighted same-site share L as in S1 (each group weight one) and its null by 10,000 permutations of site labels among the population. Segment types: opening pair (first two signs in reading order), closing pair (last two), opening sign, closing sign, and the middle pair of texts with at least five signs (signs two and three) as a reference. The primary comparison is the opening pair against the closing pair, reported as each one's ratio L/null and the difference of the two ratios with a bootstrap interval over groups (2,000 draws). A second version weights every pair equally.

**Interpretation rule.** Opening pairs local (ratio above the 97.5th null percentile) while closing pairs are not supports a local-element-plus-general-category structure. Both near their nulls supports texts whose parts are used across cities alike. Local concentration of opening segments could also reflect regional conventions, local deities, places or offices rather than personal names; the result describes where segments occur, not what they denote.
