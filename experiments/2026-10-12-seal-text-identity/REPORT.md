# Where identical Indus seal texts are found

12 October 2026, revised the same day after an [adversarial review](review/REVIEW.md); the [response](review/RESPONSE.md) lists the changes. Frozen [protocol](PROTOCOL.md); exploratory additions after outcomes are [Amendment 1](AMENDMENT-1.md) (segments), [Amendment 2](AMENDMENT-2.md) (merged variants, length-stratified null) and [Amendment 3](AMENDMENT-3.md) (review checks). Numbers come from `run-2026-10-12/`: [summary](run-2026-10-12/summary.json), [segments](run-2026-10-12/segments.json), [sensitivity](run-2026-10-12/sensitivity.json) and [review response](run-2026-10-12/review-response.json). ## Findings

**Under the frozen test, duplicate seal texts are not concentrated in one city.** Among 1,430 complete single-line seals from named South Asian sites, 65 texts occur on two or more seals, covering 174 seals; 41 of these texts occur at more than one site. The share of each shared text's seal pairs found at one site averages 0.473, against 0.448 when site labels are shuffled among all seals (95% range 0.345 to 0.552; exact value 0.4478), so p = 0.31 and the ratio to chance is 1.06. By the frozen rule the duplicates are classed as texts used across cities. Holding text length fixed in the null lowers the expectation to 0.382, because short texts come from a wider spread of sites, and the observed share is then modestly above it (p = 0.032, ratio 1.24); merging graphic variants gives the same picture (0.479 against 0.394, p = 0.023).

**The pooled result hides a difference between Mohenjo-daro and the smaller sites (exploratory).** Same-site pairs at Mohenjo-daro match chance: 59 observed against 60.2 expected under the length-stratified null. Harappa has 10 against 6.0 (p = 0.11). The other sites together have 7 against 1.5 (p = 0.002): three Banawali seals sharing one text and a second Banawali pair, two Dholavira pairs (one of which may be a single seal catalogued twice) and a Lothal pair. Restricted to the 513 seals outside Mohenjo-daro, the same-site share is 0.413 against an exact chance value of 0.286 (p = 0.07). At Mohenjo-daro, duplicates behave like shared labels; at some smaller sites they are local, as one owner's seals or a local institution's would be. The breakdown was chosen after the frozen outcome and rests on small counts. If a fraction f of shared texts were wholly local and the rest followed the length-stratified null, the point estimate is f ≈ 0.15, and values up to about 0.25 remain compatible with the data.

**Long seal texts are almost all unique.** No two of the 445 seals with six or more signs share a text. A bigram model of the corpus, used to estimate how often seals of a given length would coincide by chance, predicts 97.2 identical pairs among two-sign seals against 122 observed, 17.7 against 34 for three signs, 4.1 against 24 for four signs and 1.0 against 3 for five. For four or more signs the observed pairs exceed the model 5.3-fold when it is fitted to all distinct texts, 4.2-fold when fitted to non-seal texts and 3.1-fold when fitted to seal texts, all below the protocol's tenfold threshold for whole-text reuse. Whether refitting on deduplicated texts biases the expectation up or down is not established; a simulation in the review found a slight underestimate.

**Seals with identical texts share the animal only when it is a unicorn.** Of 130 pairs with identical texts where both seals have a field motif, 89 share it (68.5%, against 61.6% under within-site shuffling; p = 0.15); averaged per text, 72.6% against 62.0% (p = 0.033). Eighty-eight of the 89 matching pairs are unicorn pairs, and of the 42 pairs involving any other animal, one shares its motif. Seals coded with no motif are mostly rectangular text-only seals; counting them as a category, pairs share their category 54.1% of the time against 43.0% (p = 0.016). A text recurring on seals with different animals, such as a Banawali text found with four, fits a text that is independent of the emblem.

**Sealings link cities.** Sixteen sealings or tags carry a text found on an eligible seal; they represent six distinct texts. Thirteen of them, carrying four of the texts, match only seals found at other sites. Ten Lothal bale sealings (catalogue numbers L-161 to L-170) carry the four-sign text of Mohenjo-daro seal M-895. On L-161 the photograph shows a U-shaped sign, strokes inside brackets, a heart-shaped sign and a tall stem, in the same order as on M-895. The sealings show an elephant; the catalogue codes M-895 as a unicorn, but the seal is a fragment and its photograph shows only a striated band below the text, so that code may be an inference. If it is right, a different seal carried the same text. A Kalibangan sealing (K-82) matches Mohenjo-daro seals M-1697 and M-712, whose photograph shows the same branched sign, three tall strokes, two short strokes and diamond with an inner loop; another Lothal sealing (L-146) and a Rupar sealing match Mohenjo-daro seals. Three Mohenjo-daro tags match seals at Mohenjo-daro and elsewhere. These matches show texts used at Mohenjo-daro on goods or documents sealed at Lothal, Kalibangan and Rupar; they do not show whether a seal, the goods or only the formula travelled.

**Duplicates at Mohenjo-daro lean contemporaneous.** Among pairs of Mohenjo-daro duplicates with period labels, 13 of 19 share the label (0.684), against 0.458 under within-site shuffling (p = 0.051).

**Shared opening segments are mildly local, but the contrast with closing segments is not established (exploratory).** Among the 957 seals of at least four signs, groups sharing their first two signs average a same-site share of 0.598 against 0.482 (p = 0.004, ratio 1.24), and groups sharing their last two signs 0.534 against 0.481 (p = 0.041, ratio 1.11). Counting pairs equally gives the same ratio for both (1.11 and 1.12), and single opening and closing signs are slightly local by that count (1.10 and 1.07). The amendment's literal rule is met, opening pairs above their 97.5th percentile and closing pairs just below theirs, but the difference between the two ratios (0.13) lies inside its permutation range of −0.21 to 0.22 (p = 0.11). Two-sign segments are a little more regional than chance in general.

## What the results say about the descriptions

| Description | Prediction | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Personal names, duplicates as one owner's seals (frozen) | Duplicates mostly same-site | Not met overall; met for part of the duplicates at smaller sites |
| Personal names, duplicates as common names (added after outcomes) | Duplicates spread; long names rarely repeat | Compatible at Mohenjo-daro |
| Titles, offices or categories across cities (frozen) | Duplicates spread; frequent repeats including long texts | Partly met: spread at Mohenjo-daro, but repeats few and short |
| Clan or guild identities across cities (frozen) | Duplicates spread; reuse above the model; motif concordance | Reuse above the model (3.1 to 5.3-fold); concordance only among unicorn seals |

Seal texts are mostly individual compositions. A small set of short texts recurs, used at Mohenjo-daro as freely as chance would place them, and some duplicates at smaller sites stay in one place. The recurring texts appear with different animals except on unicorn seals. This fits name-like or individually specific legends whose common forms repeat, with local holders accounting for some duplicates outside the largest city, and it fits title-plus-qualifier formulae. It does not fit duplicates that are mostly the seals of single local owners across the corpus.

## Methods

The population is faces with object type beginning `SEAL` in `data/normalized/lipi_observed_faces.jsonl` (Lipi commit `fc8813a5`), complete, single line, without unknown signs, of at least two signs, from named sites in South Asia, one face per object family (lowest record identifier). The one-face rule misses one Harappa duplicate on second faces (H-352, H-354 and H-369 share a text there). Text identity is the exact reading-order Wells/ICIT sequence. The field motif is the `symbol` code before its first colon. The locality statistic averages, over texts carried by two or more seals, the share of their seal pairs at one site; its frozen null shuffles site labels among all eligible seals 10,000 times. Motif concordance uses pairs where both seals have a motif and shuffles motifs within site. The coincidence calculation sums squared text probabilities by dynamic programming over squared transition probabilities and normalizes by the probability of each length.

[verify.py](verify.py) rebuilds the population, the groups and the observed statistics without the analysis code, checks the permutation null mean against its exact hypergeometric value, and checks the coincidence calculation by enumeration for one- and two-sign texts; it reuses the analysis module's model-fitting function and constants. The protocol hash file was written in the same command immediately before the first analysis run, and later appends for the amendments changed its timestamp; the package is not yet committed, so file times are the only record of order.

Mohenjo-daro supplies 917 of the 1,430 seals, which sets the chance level for most pairs and limits power for regional effects. Motifs and object types come from published catalogues. Texts differing by one sign variant count as different texts except in the merged sensitivity.
