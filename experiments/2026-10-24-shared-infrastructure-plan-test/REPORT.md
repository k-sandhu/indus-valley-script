# Shared infrastructure against excavation plans and dated contexts

We find a townscape in which streets, drains and entrances could follow different histories. Farmana's plans preserve lane sections beside additions to houses and a capped building passage. Harappa's gateway sequence combines restricted access with renewed drainage after the earlier drain clogged. Dholavira's gates combine older masonry with later blocking. These observations support the possibility that connected services persisted while access changed locally. They also challenge any version of the shared-infrastructure idea that requires every route to remain open.

The measured plans and documentary contexts do not yet decide whether common rules, central supervision, engineering constraints or repeated household decisions produced that pattern. Our fixed six-town source audit assembled no complete comparison of street persistence against internal partition persistence, and no matched pair joining documentary use to an independently dated repair. Those quantities remain unknown. We have tested the published evidence's geometry, sequence and ability to support the proposed causal comparison, and we report the failed joins alongside the positive ones.

## What we tested

C-0017 proposes that building groups changed their houses under rules that preserved connected routes and services. Its first prediction compares the fraction of shared-route boundaries retained through rebuilding with the fraction of internal partitions retained in the same excavated area. C-0018 connects sealings with changes in custody between groups. C-0019 extends the proposal of serial tablet administration to obligations for shared work. The last proposal has an existing credence of 0.10 and requires physical evidence that distinguishes work from ordinary goods allocation.

We froze `PROTOCOL.md` before new measurement. Farmana, Kanmer, Banawali, Harappa, Dholavira and Lothal remained in the primary source frame even when their plans proved unsuitable for a persistence comparison. A dated amendment added Mohenjo-daro as a supplementary test of source availability and measurement, without replacing a failed town. Architectural inventories were saved before the new documentary overlays were read. The context module was frozen before its coder received the new architectural outcomes. Earlier published examples were already familiar, so this separation reduces one source of fitting without creating a fresh confirmation sample.

The results register preserves source titles, editions, hashes, page and figure locators, relative phases, scale, north references, reading bounds and reasons for missing fields. Full reports, source images, detailed traces and illustrated products remain local on D:. The new study does not alter the earlier comparison, conjecture credences or the shared benchmark spending record. The versioned synthetic shots result checks workflow guards; it supplies no archaeological score.

## The plans support selective continuity

Farmana gives the clearest bounded illustration of local change. In the eastern Period IIA plan, a single section of Lane 1 measures 1.913 m, with a conditional reading range of 1.783–2.047 m. Lane 2 measures 1.953 m, range 1.822–2.087 m. A section of Main Street measures 3.961 m, range 3.700–4.229 m. These are particular sections of drawn masonry. The report's 1.6 m average for Lane 1 describes a different scope, and that difference remains unresolved rather than being averaged away. The excavators place a bathing platform at Lane 1's exposed end and infer earlier disuse; a later addition projects into Lane 2. Their local sequences include both retained alignment and encroachment.

In the central Period IIB area, Structure 3I gained an eastern buttress. Its drawn projection measures 0.595 m, with a reading range of 0.250–0.949 m, which includes the report's 0.55 m. Two passages give conflicting lengths for that addition, 1.00 m and 2.10 m. They remain one repair case with a source conflict. Passage 4C has a drawn clear width of 1.786 m, range 1.552–2.025 m, below its later northern cap. That cap closes the compound's connection with central Lane 1. The plan does not show closure of Lane 1 itself. These details matter because a town can preserve circulation past a building while withdrawing access into it. The eastern Lane 1 and central Lane 1 are separate named features in separate areas and phases. Source: Shinde, Osada and Kumar 2011, Chapter 5, Figures 5.18 and 5.70, pp. 57,99,103,117,145,152.

Harappa supplies an architectural contrast of the same kind. The Mound ET gateway was partly blocked by the late Period 3C Drain 51, while east–west circulation crossed above its culvert. The earlier drain had clogged; the sequence shows renewed drainage alongside changed access, rather than uninterrupted service. In a separate Area C setting, a local reading of the gateway opening on Kenoyer's composite Figure 4.15 is 2.878 m, range 2.378–3.407 m. That drawing combines gateway stages and cannot assign the width to every stage. We also registered the Area C cart-track and upper street plans, Figures 4.12 and 4.16, using excavation-grid controls independent of the walls. Registration succeeds geometrically, but the upper drawing combines street levels and the lower exposure lacks a comparable inventory of internal partitions. Consequently the intended persistence difference cannot be estimated from this pair.

Dholavira's plans show later blocks in gate passages, with mixed phases preserved in the drawings. Lothal's recovered Figure 17 allows readings of a Phase IV passage and a house entrance, but the held source set does not provide the corresponding complete earlier street network. Banawali's composite town plan and Kanmer's fort plan establish bounded design observations; their publication or excavation updates cannot substitute for occupation phases. Each town remains visible in the coverage table.

Mohenjo-daro provides four readable local chords across First Street in Mackay's successive plans. Their estimates are 7.34,7.89,8.17 and 10.26 m, with broad reading ranges. These sections sit beside different local frontages and are not the same registered ground transect. The fifth frozen section remains unmeasurable because no continuous western bank can be identified at that row. The result establishes the availability of repeated street drawings and measurable local geometry. It supplies neither a street-widening trend nor a count of independent rebuilding episodes. Our independent visual audit confirms the selected 0–50-foot scale marks and retains both plausible edges on Plate XIX.

## The strongest documentary joins have different functions

Harappa's Trench 54 courtyard ties tablet manufacture to a bounded architectural setting. The plan labels workshop 601, walls, stairs and a platform; unfinished and broken tablets occur with production waste. Other Harappa objects have positive room, floor or sump associations. The jar H95-2661/6640-501 was reused in a floor as a sump, giving an operating context for the installed vessel while leaving the earlier inscription events distinct. These observations locate activities more precisely than a site name. Manufacture, later possession and final disposal still require separate histories.

The 22-tablet group near Wall 296 is a counterexample to reading a concentration as an office. Sixteen tablets formed a reconstructed clump, two lay nearby and four were scattered. The source identifies secondary deposition after the wall was built. Its map locates discarded material; it does not locate the original use. Banawali's horned-tiger seal in a paved sitting room offers a more direct household association, although its registration, subphase and dated building event remain unresolved.

A cross-module check after the context freeze found a positive Kanmer floor association in the already held annual-report preview. The report places a terracotta sealing in the southwest corner of grid Z28 on floor levels of layer 9, and separately a button-shaped steatite seal in X30, layer 8. Neither has a registration, exact room or independent date in that passage. `POSTJOIN-LEAD-01.json` preserves the source text format and page locator without adding these mentions to the frozen 32 assessments or identifying them with the three-impression cohort. The lead improves the next field-register request.

Lothal preserves fastening contacts on sealings and a warehouse-associated concentration. Physical traces can distinguish contact with cords, containers or fixed fastenings. They do not independently identify a handover, recipient or labour obligation. Its passage deposit also admits competing formation histories involving discard and fire or collapse. The 93 rows in Frenez and Tosi's Table 1 contain W=35, W1=18, W2=11, O=10 and ?=19, reproduced by a separate visual recount. Their prose gives O=11 and ?=17 and sums to 92. W1 locations depend on stamp similarity, while W2 gives a broad trench. Both cannot be treated as independently observed warehouse findspots in a test using stamp similarity.

Dholavira's fallen ten-sign inlays join writing to the western chamber of the Castle north gate. The original overhead mounting is a reconstruction, and its installation date is unresolved. Three of the 225 context-table rows explicitly name rooms; those leads deserve excavation-record recovery. The table supplies no demonstrated original-use-to-repair chain. The field counts describe what the table records, not how often primary contexts existed in the ancient town.

## Dates change the interpretation of apparent joins

The audit preserves 78 source-specific dating entries, including 76 entries with a reported radiocarbon determination or thermoluminescence result. Repeated publications and paired laboratory methods do not automatically add independent specimens. Two further entries preserve calibrated ages for intrusive Kanmer rice without inventing raw determinations omitted from the paragraph. No pooled calendar model was fitted.

Harappa's hearths below Wall 15 provide actual stratigraphic dating evidence. The excavation report infers construction after about 2470 BC, but the tablet association conflicts: the Trench 22 prose places two faience tablets inside the wall, while their Figure 11 captions say Trench 7/8, wash. Choosing the prose alone would manufacture the desired architectural join. Lothal's dated posthole above Phase IV flood debris and an ashy layer sealing a Phase IIIB wall provide other partial sample-to-feature chains, all outside the warehouse sealing deposit.

Two direct checks defeat tempting transfers of a nearby date. Farmana's PLD-12422 carries a Trench 1E1/Structure 3N label yet dates to AD 616–666 at the source's 95.4% calibrated coverage. Kanmer's PLD-16351 and PLD-16353 rice dates fall in the fourth–fifth centuries AD despite Harappan field associations; the source discusses reworking or percolation. These are positive evidence of intrusion or later activity, and they prevent us from using a structure name or cultural phase as the date of a seal's use.

## What this changes

The study replaces a town-level resemblance argument with named, auditable architectural and depositional cases. It adds measured plan geometry, one limited registration, explicit contradictions and primary-source dates. The central numerical prediction of preferential route persistence remains unestimated because the required comparable excavation windows are absent. The documentary predictions remain below their entry requirements because the object, operating deposit, building episode and independent date do not form a complete matched chain.

The strongest reading supported here is selective continuity: some urban connections were retained or re-established through local rebuilding while thresholds, entrances and access changed. Common rules remain a viable explanation, and their institutional cause is open. The deciding test is a new, sufficiently powered comparison of fully exposed, phase-joined routes and internal partitions with preservation held comparable, followed by documentary contexts dated independently of the connection being tested. If the apparent advantage of shared routes disappears under those controls, or if the seal and tablet relation is generated by manufacture and secondary disposal, the corresponding shared-infrastructure claims fail.
