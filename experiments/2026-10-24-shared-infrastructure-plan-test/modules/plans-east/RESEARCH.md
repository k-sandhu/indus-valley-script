# Local access changes within enduring town outlines

Farmana provides the clearest plan-led result in this module. Its eastern lanes have later encroachments, Main Street has a local wall projection, and the passage labelled Structure 4C loses its northern connection to Lane 1. These events change different parts of an access network. The 4C cap closes a compound connection; it does not close Lane 1. Kanmer and Banawali add source-ordered rebuilding sequences, but their inspected drawings do not resolve a numerical comparison of route and partition survival.

We inspected original excavation-author drawings and photographs for all three towns. The [inventory](inventory-freeze.json) contains five bounded windows, 25 named candidates and four failed pair candidates. It was frozen before the new endpoint readings. Prior town and inscription research is declared. We have not opened or assembled the new documentary overlay. This is an exploratory source study by GPT-6 family / Codex desktop; the exact serving variant is unknown.

## What can be measured

The source plans produce seven retained measurements. Each metre value uses the graphic bar on the same panel. We read endpoints manually against untouched full-page renders, retained the transforms and endpoints on D, and inspected the annotated outputs. The bounds allow each feature endpoint and each scale-bar endpoint to move independently within the declared pixel radius. They describe reading sensitivity. Original survey accuracy and scan distortion are unknown and can exceed them. Doubling the reading radius is included in [measurements.json](measurements.json).

## Farmana: widening walls and closing one connection

[Excavations at Farmana, District Rohtak, Haryana, India, 2006-2008](https://archive.org/details/excavationsatfarmana200608vshindeetal_212_v) explicitly assigns eastern Locality 2 and Figures 5.16–5.51 to Period IIA on printed pp. 52–54. Figure 5.18 is printed p. 57, PDF zero index 70. It maps the platform at the west end of eastern Lane 1 and the adjoining lane boundaries. The later platform is described on p. 70; the excavators infer prior lane disuse. Eastern Lane 2 has a later encroachment by Structure 11A and a separate Early Historic pit, described on p. 78. The western limit of exposure prevents a complete account of either route's access.

The central plans have a different exposure. Printed p. 97 explicitly assigns Figures 5.68–5.137 to IIB. Figures 5.69 and 5.70 are the western and eastern halves of that plan, printed pp. 98–99, PDF indices 111–112. They are not two phases of the same window. Our central board uses Figure 5.70, where the labels 3I, Main Street, 4C and both lanes are visible.

Structure 3I's east reinforcement projects 55 cm into Main Street. Its reported length differs: p. 103 gives 1.00 m, while p. 117 gives 2.10 m for the surviving addition. We preserve both extents within one repair group. The southern addition is separately described as 35 cm wide. The drawing supports a local intrusion; it does not date loss of through-access. A platform attached to Complex 2 also projects into the street, but p. 103 allows that it was contemporary with the complex. Projection alone therefore cannot assign a later episode.

Structure 4C is a passage within Complex 4. Its route and widths are described on p. 145. Printed p. 152 states that a later mud-brick wall capped its northern end, formerly connected to Lane 1. This supplies a local access change with an explicit structural sequence. The event remains undated within or after the passage's IIB use. The same page range describes a 15 cm addition on the inner side of 4D's east wall. That addition reduces room space; it is not described as narrowing 4C. These observations keep street clearance, compound access and internal alteration separate.

## Kanmer: infill with retained alignment

[Kanmer: A Multicultural Site in Kachchh, Gujarat, India](https://nichibun.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/1259/files/symp_016__357__355_376__357_378.pdf), Figure 5 on p. 360, PDF index 5, distinguishes exposed fort segments from gaps and trenches. Its KMR II overview does not separate IIa and IIb. We measured one exposed corner angle; the shorter arm gives a broad bound. The nominal statement “2 CM = 5 M” has no graphic metre bar, so we did not convert printed centimetres into ground lengths. Its N symbol does not specify true, grid or magnetic north.

The original [Annual report of excavation at Kanmer 2007-08 and 2008-09](https://www.scribd.com/document/336418894/OP10-1), OP10, pp. 85–86, describes floors shifting toward a street or open space, subsequent structures within that space, and KMR IIB structures on its earlier extent. Publicly served facsimiles of those original report pages are held locally. OP10 Figure 8 on p. 85, preview page 93, is an oblique photograph. It supplies no phase-keyed partition outlines for registration. No full OP10 PDF was acquired, and preview numbers are not exported as PDF indices.

The two excavation-author accounts have different phase and exposure scopes. OP10 describes three partly exposed rooms in one subphase and more than six rooms in its later phase. The *Multicultural* paper describes eight rooms in each of two superimposed structural phases. We preserve those source sets without summing them into a town total or sixteen independent episodes. Its statement that orientation remained the same accompanies occupation of earlier open space. Alignment retention therefore coexists with spatial infill in the excavators' sequence.

## Banawali: retained enclosure, rebuilt interior

R. S. Bisht's [Dholavira and Banawali: Two different paradigms of the Harappan Urbis forma](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/85200.pdf), *Puratattva* 29 (1998–99), describes domestic rebuilding and a new street system in IC while retaining and widening the antecedent fortification, printed pp. 16–17. Period II then accepts the town outline with marginal changes, redefines streets and replaces earlier houses. These are the excavator's source-level summaries. The paper supplies no enumerated exposed-house denominator from which to measure complete residential replacement.

Figure 1 on p. 33, PDF index 46, overlays Pre-Harappan defences, Harappan defences, structures from several periods, streets, trenches and the moat. Its legend is retained in the board. The site plans in *Indian Archaeology 1983-84 - A Review*, Figure 4, p. 25, PDF index 32, and *Indian Archaeology 1987-88 - A Review*, Figure 1, p. 22, PDF index 29, record different publication and exposure states. Treating them as occupation phases would turn increased documentation into apparent architectural change.

The strongest supported reading is selective continuity: inherited enclosures and street alignments coexist with rebuilt interiors, narrowed clearances and locally closed connections. The deciding test is a registered sequence through the same excavated window, tied to successive surfaces, with a complete predeclared inventory of route edges, open connections and internal partitions. Counting preserved through-access separately from preserved alignment would decide whether shared routes survived rebuilding more often than internal divisions.
