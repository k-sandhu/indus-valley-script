# Retrospective

We began with original sources already held on D and audited the direct plan references for Harappa, Dholavira and Lothal. The main difficulty was that a well-known published town plan often combines several periods, partial trenches and later reconstruction. We retained 23 panels as a bounded source inventory before new pixel endpoints or grid fits. The inventory explicitly lacks a complete population of route boundaries and internal walls, so the persistence contrast stayed ineligible throughout the measurement stage.

The HARP image pages added eight original source images and their caption pages. The Trench54 overview initially available in a small web view led to the directly hosted high-resolution image. The later acquisition preserved both URLs and hashes. Trench54's phase labels and wall voids helped distinguish combined exposures from a contemporaneous plan. The Trench39 pair was inspected because its titles suggested different phases; the actual panels had different extents and later reference features. That route failed to yield a complete comparable pair and remains recorded.

Harappa Area C did permit independent registration. We selected excavation-grid crosses, saved the controls, then fitted each drawing and checked unused crosses. We transferred the cart-track traces only after that fit. The resulting location relation is useful even though the early narrow exposure has no partition-wall denominator. It would have been misleading to count the six traced depressions as separate events or to convert a small registration residual into a survey accuracy claim.

The Dholavira audit required reading the source compass symbols. North points down in the North Gate and well-and-tank figures, while the East Gate original points north right. We recorded these references and the display rotation instead of importing the orientation of another plan. The North Gate crop is coarse, so its width has a broad reading bound. The East Gate has a measurable clear interior and explicitly labelled later blockings. Separating those observations kept local geometry from becoming a claim of uninterrupted access.

The Lothal route remained incomplete. An overly narrow Internet Archive creator query returned no results because the uploaded metadata used different creator fields. The corrected title search found the existing incomplete original report and Volume II, without recovering the missing original plates. The absence of that volume did not justify discarding the usable Phase IV Block B plan in the text scan. We measured its two named openings and preserved the failure of the broader phase comparison. Corrupt OCR was replaced by original page-image inspection.

The first board render had a long source-panel header extending beyond a narrow inset. We shortened only that display header, retained the full citation in the footer and manifest, and rendered the final 12 boards to a new `boards-v2` directory. The earlier render and manifest remain on D. Source pixels, endpoints and measurement results did not change. All 12 initial boards were inspected individually, and the corrected inset was inspected again. The final contact sheet was reviewed for global layout.

The module adds local dimensions and auditable architectural sequences. It supplies zero eligible P1 episodes, rather than an apparent effect assembled from composites. A future run can advance by acquiring the missing phase-separated drawings and construction relationships named in the report. More illustrations of the same composite do not repair that gap.

We used the GPT-6 family through Codex desktop; the exact serving variant was not exposed. The architectural inventory and measurements were saved before receiving a newly assembled documentary overlay. Earlier examples were known, so this was exploratory work with prior exposure.
