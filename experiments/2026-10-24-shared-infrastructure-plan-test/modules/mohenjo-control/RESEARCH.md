# First Street in the original phase plans

The original Mohenjo-daro drawings supply four readable local widths of First Street beside Block 5. The estimates are about 7.3, 7.9, 8.2 and 10.3 m in the source's Intermediate III, Intermediate II, Intermediate I and Late III plans. Their endpoint and bar-reading bounds overlap. The Late III panel has two plausible western-edge readings, both preserved. These are samples at named frontages within each panel, with no claim that the transects have identical ground coordinates.

The printed plans keep First Street beside a changing set of buildings. That supports a recurring corridor in the reported sequence. It does not establish that every passage remained open or that shared routes survived more frequently than internal walls. The exposure, preservation and partition comparison needed for that test is absent. Calendar construction dates are also absent. The source N arrow has no stated true, grid or magnetic reference; the width calculation needs no north correction.

The frozen fifth transect, in the combined Late Ia and II plan, has no uninterrupted phase-attributed western bank. A wider diagnostic search exposed several building and dashed earlier lines. We left the width unknown. Imputing the bank from an older wall would turn missing plan information into apparent street geometry. The original failed reading remains in the record.

We also recovered the official 2026 supplementary archive for Green, Alam and Petrie's *Inequality declined in the Bronze Age city of Mohenjo-daro*. It contains figure images, a summary CSV and a short R script in a nested ZIP. It supplies no street vectors, individual residence polygons or independently dated repair sequences. The 2018 publisher page identifies its supplements as SketchUp models; the paper describes interpretive three-dimensional reconstruction. We do not treat those models as an additional excavation witness.

The original plate scan was already held. Its alternating blank backs caused an initial page-selection error; the rendered blanks remain preserved. Corrected plan indices are 45, 47, 49, 51 and 53 for plates XVI–XX. The title page prints 1937, while the established citation is Mackay 1938. Source and rendition hashes preserve the exact witness. Detailed endpoint inputs and all annotated plan images remain local on D:.

The strongest reading from this control is that the reported First Street corridor accompanied substantial changes in adjacent architecture. A complete common excavation window, with independently joined phases and preservation controls for both street edges and internal walls, could decide whether that is preferential continuity; primary contexts and construction dates must then decide any institutional connection.
