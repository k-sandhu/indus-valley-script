# Sources and coverage represented in the atlas

This module reuses held source material. It acquires no new archaeological publication, catalogue row or adjudicated inscribed object. The 124-file input freeze pins the integrated run, module records, source payloads, selected source boards, modern land outline, inherited town coordinates and Kanmer postjoin addendum. Original sources retain their existing IDs, hashes, rights and exact locators.

The new local products are 30 figure PNGs, nine authored SVGs, selected and all-figure contacts, a figure ZIP and bounded source excerpts rendered from held PDFs. [local-assets-run-v2.json](local-assets-run-v2.json) records their logical and physical paths and SHA256 values. Each plan or context board links back to its source ID and printed page or figure together with a zero-based PDF page index where a PDF is held. The Kanmer OP10 preview has a preview page in place of a PDF index, and its missing page image remains explicit.

The atlas makes several existing coverage limits directly inspectable. Farmana's local measurements have no registered pre-change panel. Kanmer's exposed fort corner does not separate the residential subphases. Banawali's defence drawing combines periods. The Harappa Area C pair registers geometrically but lacks a comparable internal-partition inventory. Dholavira's usable stratum and depth counts remain 211 and 201, using the frozen placeholder rule. Lothal's W1 inferred locality, W2 broad trench and unknown category remain separate from direct warehouse finds. Dates retain their source confidence levels and deposit relations.

The strongest reading is that these source records support a selective urban sequence with unresolved comparative and documentary denominators. The next coverage change should recover paired excavation windows or field registers that join a registered object-use surface to a named, independently dated repair. That evidence could decide whether inherited shared connections had a distinct persistence advantage and whether documentary activity followed their maintenance.
