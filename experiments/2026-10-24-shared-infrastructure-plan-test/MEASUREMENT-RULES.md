# What the plan measurements mean

The excavation drawing is the source observation. A measured line on it inherits the drawing's phase attribution, reconstruction and exposure limits. We retain its scale and north statements and store every endpoint against an untouched parent render. A local metre measurement uses the plan's scale bar; a distance between two buildings on different unregistered panels is unknown.

For a feature length of d pixels, scale bar b pixels representing s metres, the estimate is sd/b. If each endpoint can move within a radius e pixels, a conservative reading bound for d is max(0, d−2e) to d+2e. An independently uncertain bar uses its own endpoint radius. The resulting metre bounds divide the lower feature distance by the upper bar distance and the upper feature distance by the lower bar distance. These are digitization sensitivity bounds, not confidence intervals or the original survey's accuracy. A declared uncertainty in s is carried through separately. Scan distortion or an unknown source error can exceed the reading bound.

We keep geometry and connectivity separate. A street narrowed by a new wall may still connect the same groups. An unchanged bearing may accompany a blocked entrance. Each episode therefore records what remained, what changed and whether the evidence actually establishes continued access. A plan's blank area does not establish that a wall or artifact was absent.

Paired plans need independent controls. The [QGIS 3.44 Georeferencer documentation](https://docs.qgis.org/3.44/en/docs/user_manual/managing_data_source/georeferencer.html) distinguishes transformations and the controls each requires. A local scan can use scale and rotation; a flexible warp can force uncertain features into apparent agreement. We retain control points and residuals and avoid transformations selected to improve the tested match. Ground coordinates are used only when the source supports them. Local excavation coordinates remain local.

Dating must resolve the named event. [Historic England's Bayesian process](https://historicengland.org.uk/images-books/publications/radiocarbon-dating-chronological-modelling/the-bayesian-process/) begins with the question, suitable samples and a design capable of the required precision. We retain the reported sample and its relationship to construction or deposit formation. A charcoal determination from a room does not date every use of that room, and a broad cultural phase cannot establish synchronised repair. We do not fit a chronological model without an adequate sample and stratigraphic specification.

This run's numerical comparisons are exploratory and source-scoped. The C-0017 deciding contrast remains shared-route persistence minus internal-partition persistence within comparable independent episodes. C-0018 and C-0019 require primary activity contexts and recovery controls. Neither missing denominators nor repeated views of one event are replaced by larger nominal sample counts.
