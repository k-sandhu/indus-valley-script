# Retrospective: Harappa legend-periods shot, 20 October 2026

## Approach

The brief came from the session that ran the Mohenjo-daro stratigraphy shot: test the fixed-pattern reading its report ended with, on Harappa seals dated by HARP period, with a positive control. We read the conjecture-shot design and its review response, the bench and loader documentation, the stratigraphy package's protocol, predictions, scorer and statistics module, the chronology package's report and registry entry, and DATA.md, and then searched the other packages of 20 October for any use of Harappa periods with legends. The sign-context package had tabulated the unicorn share and seal-size dispersion by Harappa period label, which ruled out motif and size as the positive control. The card was created with `python tools/kb.py new conjecture`, renamed to a short slug, and its number recorded in the shared session notes before any analysis, since other sessions were writing cards the same evening.

The brief named HARP's Periods 3A to 3C within one mound. The export holds only 36 HARP seals with a period label, 18 of them from Mound E, so a within-mound test of seal legends had to use Vats's Mound F, where 175 seal families have a stratum that the export maps to 3B-1 through 3C-4. Vats's own text (1940, pp. 9–10) and Kenoyer and Meadow's check of his Trench I section supplied the basis for treating his strata as a clock. The partition followed the brief's suggestion of one mound and one label source for confirmation; every other period-labelled Harappa seal became discovery material.

The scorer was written once ([period_score.py](period_score.py)) on top of the stratigraphy package's statistics module, imported unchanged and hashed as an artifact, and used for the discovery rehearsal, the power simulation and the frozen read. Because C-0010 predicted fixity, the drift tests needed three outcomes (drift, no drift, inconclusive) so that the claim could lose. The loader path was dry-run twice in the scratchpad on discovery rows relabelled as Mound F seals: the loader refused the confirmation read before the freeze and accepted it after, and the scorer and credence rule ran on its output.

## Problems and fixes

`import build_inputs` first loaded the stratigraphy package's module of the same name, because the statistics module had put that package's directory at the front of the import path. Appending the path instead of prepending it fixed the shadowing; the failed run had written nothing but an empty output directory, which we removed.

The first equivalence rule failed P1 when a 95% bootstrap interval excluded zero. On populations of Mound F's size that interval excluded zero in 7.7% of populations with no planted change, above its nominal 5%. The frozen rule uses a within-frame permutation test for the fail side and keeps the bootstrap only for the equivalence side; the full power run then gave a false-fail rate of 4.3%. The rule that lets P1 kill the card was also put under the same Holm family as the drift tests.

The quick power pass also set the category minimum for opening and closing signs (five legends) and the inventory minimum (eight legends) by false-positive rate and power on synthetic data. The full run showed the positive control to be weak: a linear rise of rectangular seals from 10% to 25% is detected within trenches in only 38% of Mound F-shaped populations. Zeroing every gain on a control failure would have made the test uninformative in the claim's favour, so before freezing we set failed-control gains to half weight. That decision was made after the census had printed Mound F's object types by stratum, which the protocol discloses; the census showed a rise, but its within-trench significance was unknown until the read.

The census of the confirmation population printed more than the design needed: object types by stratum at face level, overall motif counts and depths by stratum. No legend, sign or length was tabulated against stratum. The protocol lists everything that was looked at, and the positive control is described as confirming a known change rather than as a blind prediction.

The first independent recomputation used 1,999 draws and gave the length p as 0.0005 against the frozen 0.003. A 4,000-draw check showed the difference to be Monte Carlo noise (null standard deviation 0.090 against the package's 0.093), and a 9,999-draw run gave 0.0032. Both files are kept.

## Outcome

The single read happened 21 seconds after the freeze. The closing stock passed equivalence (late minus early −0.04) and the early-third level, opening and closing signs showed no drift, and the positive control passed (p = 0.0017). Variant choice, legend length and sign inventory each showed drift at a Holm-adjusted p below 0.05, surviving single deletions, which kills C-0010 under its frozen rule (0.40 to 0.02; the effects alone would have given 0.32). Every pre-declared sensitivity analysis gave the same outcomes, and the independent script reproduced the population and the length and inventory statistics. After the read, exploratory analyses showed that the drift holds on square seals alone and with format held fixed, that the added length lies between the opening and closing signs, and that the interior signs of later legends are less like Mohenjo-daro's, not more. The report ends with the two-part reading the results support, a fixed closing formula with a changing body, and three predictions frozen for HARP's CISI 3.1 seals.
