# Harappa seal legends through dated periods

Package `2026-10-20-harappa-legend-periods`, 20 October 2026. Conjecture [C-0010](../../knowledge/conjectures/C-0010-seal-legend-fixed-pattern.md). Status: **frozen evaluation**, one confirmation read; the post-outcome sections are exploratory.

Through Vats's Mound F sequence at Harappa, from the 3B/C strata to the end of Period 3C, the seal legend kept its closing formula and changed its body. The fifteen-sign closing stock closes 85% of the legends in the early third of the sequence and 80% in the late third (difference −0.04, 90% interval −0.19 to 0.10), and neither the choice of closing sign (p = 0.70) nor the choice of opening sign (p = 0.39) moves. Between the two ends the legend grew: complete legends average 4.0, 4.8 and 5.3 signs by third (p = 0.003), the sign inventory changes (p = 0.0016), with signs 806, 817 and 233 absent or nearly absent from the early third, and the variant used within Mayig sign classes changes (p = 0.0001). Rectangular seals become commoner through the same strata (p = 0.0017), so the sequence spans the change in seal form that HARP dated to Period 3C. C-0010 claimed that the whole legend stayed fixed. Three drift tests survive the Holm correction and the deletion rule, which is its kill condition, and its credence falls from 0.40 to 0.02.

## Design

C-0010 was the successor reading of the [Mohenjo-daro stratigraphy shot](../2026-10-20-mohenjo-daro-stratigraphy/REPORT.md), where no part of the legend changed with depth but Marshall's sections may have spanned too little time to show any change. The test needed a sequence with its own clock and a positive control ([PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md)). HARP's own period-labelled seals in the export are too few (36 families), so the confirmation population was the longest within-mound sequence: the 175 Mound F seal families with a stratum from M. S. Vats's excavations, which the export assigns to HARP periods 3B-1 (Stratum VII) through 3C-4 (Stratum I). Every other period-labelled Harappa seal, from Vats's other mounds and from HARP's excavations, was discovery material (109 families). A seal's clock is the rank of its stratum over the mound; every null permutes it within Vats's trenches, so each test compares older and later legends of the same trench. Broken legends contribute an opening or closing sign wherever that edge of the legend survives.

C-0010 predicted fixity, so most of its predictions are tests that could find drift. A drift test reads "drift" when p ≤ 0.05 and the result survives deleting any of its ten most influential legends, "no drift" when p > 0.10, and "inconclusive" between. The card dies if the closing-stock share changes or any drift test finds drift, at a Holm-adjusted p ≤ 0.05 over six tests. The positive control, a rise of rectangular seals, gates the gains of the fixity outcomes. The statistic family `harappa_period_sequence` appears nowhere in `bench/spent.json`; the protocol lists what earlier packages computed on these seals and what we therefore do not count.

## Discovery

On 99 discovery seals in seven frames the closing stock held (0.71 of early-third and 0.73 of late-third legends), no drift test reached p = 0.05 (opening signs 0.56, closing signs 0.59, length 0.51, inventory 0.82, variants 0.084), and rectangular seals rose from 0.09 to 0.29 of the seals (p = 0.015). We raised the credence from 0.35 to 0.40 before freezing. The closing stock is the union of Mackay's DK-G stock and the stock of Harappa seals outside the confirmation population, fifteen signs that close 70% of Harappa legends out of sample. Details are in [EXPLORATION.md](EXPLORATION.md).

## Confirmation

The 175 rows give 173 seals in six trench frames: Trench I 37, the Granary area 37, Trench VI 33, Trenches IV and V 24, seals without a trench name 22, Trench III 20. By stratum they number 2 (VII), 19 (VI), 25 (V), 39 (IV), 35 (III), 40 (II) and 13 (I), so the early third (Strata VII to V) holds 46 seals and the late third (II and I) 53. Of these, 130 legends are readable at the closing edge, 130 at the opening edge, 105 are complete, and 172 enter the inventory and variant tests. Leaving out one trench at a time keeps the length drift in every case (p ≤ 0.014) and the inventory drift in five of six (p = 0.055 without the Granary area). The variant drift rests on Trench I: without it p = 0.16, and with any other trench left out p ≤ 0.0013. The pre-declared sensitivity analyses on the whole population repeat every outcome. Permuting over the whole mound gives drift for variants (p = 0.001), length (0.014) and inventory (0.002) and no drift for openings and closings; ranking Vats's printed depth within trenches in place of the stratum gives 0.0075, 0.018 and 0.0015 and the same fixity results; the DK-G stock alone passes P1 (−0.068) and P2; complete legends alone pass P1 (+0.021) and give no drift for openings (p = 0.13) and closings (0.64). Scored separately, the loader's two halves agree in direction and each lacks the size to decide: length correlates with the stratum at r = 0.21 and 0.26 (p = 0.098 and 0.052), variants give p = 0.04 and 0.061, and the positive control passes in both. A separate script written after the read, which imports none of the package's modules ([verify_independent.py](verify_independent.py), [verification-v1/](verification-v1/)), reproduces the 173 seals, the frames and strata, the early and late stock shares (0.8485 and 0.8049), the length correlation (0.2725, p = 0.0032 with 9,999 draws) and the inventory statistic (42.519, p = 0.0012).

## What the power statement allows

Before the read we measured how often each test detects planted effects in populations of Mound F's shape ([discovery-v2/power.json](discovery-v2/power.json)). With no planted effect the drift tests found drift in 3.5% to 5.8% of populations, so the three drifts here are not what a fixed legend produces. The fixity results have the reach the power table gives them. A P1 pass occurs in 56% of populations whose stock share is truly fixed and in 10.5% of populations whose share truly differs by 0.20; the closing stock's share at the end of 3C lies within about 0.2 of its share in the 3B/C strata, and the observed difference is −0.04. P7's "no drift" excludes a shift of half the stock-closing legends to period-specific closers (detected in 99% of populations) and leaves a shift of one in four open (37%). P3's "no drift" excludes an opening drift of one legend in three or more with fair confidence (89% detection) and one in four with less (64%, or 42% after a quarter of the seals are displaced).

The drifts found are as large as the largest effects planted in the simulation. A length increase of 1.2 signs between the thirds was detected in 58% of populations, and the observed increase is 1.27 signs.

## Post-outcome description (exploratory)

Computed after the outcome ([post_outcome.py](post_outcome.py), [post-outcome-v1/summary.json](post-outcome-v1/summary.json); [post_outcome_signs.py](post_outcome_signs.py), [post-outcome-v1/sign-check.json](post-outcome-v1/sign-check.json); [post_outcome_convergence.py](post_outcome_convergence.py), [post-outcome-v1/convergence.json](post-outcome-v1/convergence.json)).

The drift does not ride on the change of seal form. Permuting within trench and format together leaves variants at p = 0.0005, length at 0.0029 and inventory at 0.0052, and on the 147 square seals alone the values are 0.0006, 0.009 and 0.037. Complete legends on square seals average 4.1, 4.7 and 5.2 signs by third. The added length lies inside the legend: complete legends carry 2.0, 2.9 and 3.3 signs between their first and last, while the share closing in the stock stays at 0.79, 0.82 and 0.81 and sign 740 closes 0.42, 0.46 and 0.44 of all legends read at the closing edge.

The inventory drift is carried by a few signs. Of 45, 74 and 53 legends in the three thirds, sign 806 appears in 0, 4 and 8, sign 817 in 0, 7 and 7, and sign 233 in 1, 5 and 8, while 390 appears in 7, 3 and 2 and 741 in 4, 6 and 1. The variant drift is mostly the same change seen through the Mayig classes: in class P086 the early legends use 390 and the later ones 405 and 407 (p = 0.013), in P364 the later legends use 806 where the few early ones use 803 (p = 0.050), and in P385 817 joins 861 after the early third (p = 0.06). P4 and P6 are therefore two views of one shift in the signs used. Sign 817 also opens twelve legends of the middle and late thirds and none of the early third; the omnibus opening test, spread over six categories, did not flag it.

The same signs behave differently elsewhere. In the discovery seals from Harappa's other mounds, 390 also falls (5, 4 and 1 legends by third, p = 0.057) and nothing else moves, and legend length shows no trend (p = 0.53). In Mackay's DK-G sequence at Mohenjo-daro, 817 is commonest at the bottom (20, 14 and 10 legends by third of height, p = 0.099), 806 leans upward (5, 5 and 11, p = 0.19) and length does not change (4.77, 4.76 and 5.06 signs, p = 0.22). The early Mound F legends are shorter than Mohenjo-daro's, and the late ones longer. We asked whether Harappa's legends grew more like Mohenjo-daro's. Scoring each sign by its frequency at Mohenjo-daro against its frequency in the rest of Harappa, the interior signs of Mound F legends become less Mohenjo-daro-like through the sequence (mean score 0.35, 0.08 and 0.04 by third, r = −0.25, p = 0.004), and the whole legend shows no trend (p = 0.69). The body of Harappa's legend moved on a course of its own.

## Earlier work

The stability of the Indus script through the urban period is an old view: Marshall (1931, vol. I, pp. 40–41) saw no perceptible alteration in the sign forms at Mohenjo-daro, Parpola (1994, p. 54) judged that the script changed little during the Mature Harappan period, and Rao and Mahadevan found no change in the frequencies of frequent sign pairs across Mackay's DK-G levels. The opposing view comes from Harappa. Kenoyer and Meadow ("Inscribed Objects from Harappa Excavations 1986–2007", CISI 3.1, 2010, pp. xlix–lvi) traced changes in seal form and boss through Periods 3A to 3C and expected the script to have changed with them, and Ayuha Suga ("Diachronic Changes in the Indus Script Based on the Proposed Arrangement of the Indus Seal", *Journal of General Linguistics* 26–27, 2024, pp. 25–65), dating 1,137 seals by their carving style, found long legends and a larger inventory in the late period. Our result gives Suga's length increase stratigraphic support at one mound, with the period taken from the excavator's strata rather than from seal style, and separates it from the closing formula, which stayed fixed. [CHRONOLOGY-REGION-01](../../knowledge/registry/attempts/CHRONOLOGY-REGION-01.json) measured a length increase of 0.72 signs between Harappa's 3B and 3C faces of all types that shrank inside single excavation frames; within Mound F's trenches the seal legends lengthen by 1.27 signs from 3B/C to late 3C. Fuls (*Corpus of Indus Inscriptions I*, 2022, p. 13, as quoted in the [harappa.com review](https://www.harappa.com/content/corpus-indus-inscriptions-i)) reported the rise of rectangular seals in Mound F that the positive control confirms. Full citations are in the card's `prior_proposals`.

## Boldest reading and the test that would decide it

The results support a reading with two parts. The Harappan seal legend was a fixed closing formula joined to a body that changed with time. The formula was the standard: the same fifteen signs close four legends in five from the 3B/C strata of Mound F to its last Harappan stratum, sign 740 keeps its share, and the twelve of those signs that Mohenjo-daro uses as closers close about three of that city's legends in four at every excavated depth. The body was where the city's practice moved: through Period 3C it grew by more than a sign, took up 806, 817 and 233, dropped 390, 590 and 741, and drifted away from the sign mix of Mohenjo-daro's legends, on the square animal seals as much as on the new rectangular ones. This is C-0006's contrast between a fixed closing and a changing front, returning at Harappa with a clock that demonstrably spans a change in the seals; the change sits in the body of the legend more than in its first sign. The formula named what lasted, and the body named what the city's administration was adding in its last centuries.

The test that would decide it is a frozen run on seals with an independent clock that no analysis has read: HARP's own seals of Periods 3A to 3C in CISI 3.1 ([Q-1008](../../knowledge/questions/Q-1008-acquire-cisi-3-1.md)), within Mound E and Mound AB. We record three predictions for that material now, before any of it is read. The closing-stock share of 3C legends lies within 0.25 of the share of 3A–3B legends. Complete 3C legends carry more signs between their first and last than 3A–3B legends (one-sided p ≤ 0.05 within mounds). Signs 806 and 817 together appear in at most 3% of 3A–3B legends and in at least 8% of 3C legends. If the body grows and those signs arrive while the closing share holds, the two-part legend stands; if HARP's 3C legends are no longer than its 3B legends, the Mound F growth belonged to one excavation and one part of the city.
