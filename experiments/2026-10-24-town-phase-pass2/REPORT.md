# Original sample and restoration records narrow the wall question

We recovered two primary institutional sources and recorded six relations from eleven new pages. The original laboratory entry verifies TF-19's collection locus and its relation to dock fill. The Dholavira management plan identifies modern interventions on the southeast Castle masonry. Neither source completes a surveyed wall-to-construction-sample join. Counts and the frozen protocol hash are in [aggregate.json](aggregate.json); detailed rows and source bytes remain local on D: through the [file manifest](local-file-manifest.json).

## Lothal laboratory context

Kusumgar, Lal and Sarna, *Tata Institute Radiocarbon Date List I*, Radiocarbon 5 (1963), p. 277 (PDF index 4), identifies TF-19 as charcoal from SRG11, locus 5B, depth 2.19–2.25 m, field 6, Phase VA (Period II). Debris containing the sample seals the western dock embankment. The laboratory selected soft charcoal mixed with mud and treated it with HCl and NaOH. The series introduction reports manual removal of rootlets. This is an original laboratory confirmation of the inherited fill relation; the sample's tree species, short-lived character and exact position in a wall section remain unknown. We classify its deposit as later fill using the first-pass excavation account; the laboratory wording itself says debris sealing the wall.

The original determination is 3650±135 years BP, based on 5568-year half-life (p. 273), with 1700 BC printed alongside it. We preserve those conventions without recalibration. TF-23 on the same page is a useful comparator: its sample came from a posthole in a Phase VA mud wall built on Phase IV flood debris. It does not identify a dock foundation. [Publication](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033822200036894).

The missing section is now a precise acquisition target: SRG11 locus 5B, field 6, depth 2.19–2.25 m, with the depth datum, western embankment damage boundary and sample collection position. That section would determine how the debris relates to the damaged face and later basin fill.

## Dholavira conservation context

The ASI *Site Management Plan, Dholavira: A Harappan City*, Volume II, lists dismantling and resetting of uncoursed rubble at the southern reservoir and southeast Castle corner in 2005–06 (p. 16, PDF index 21). It also records documented interventions at the east gate and town walls in 1997–98, east Castle veneer in 1998–2000, and numbered-stone work from the east gate toward the north corner in 2000–01 (p.13). These are modern restoration episodes. [Institutional source](https://whc.unesco.org/document/184543).

The plan says annual programmes and inspection records pass through the Bhuj sub-circle and ASI Vadodara Circle (p.12). Those records provide a concrete route to the numbered-stone photographs needed to identify surviving ancient faces. The inspected pages do not map the southeast intervention onto the southern Stage I–III sections, locate TL samples or supply survey control. The held preliminary January 2015 excavation scan retains its edition status; DHR93/TL/8 and DHR93/TL/4 remain thermoluminescence labels.

## Search and limits

Nine bounded queries located the laboratory PDF and conservation plan. The old Arizona journal URL redirected to a journal landing page; Cambridge supplied the actual ten-page article. The web text service rejected the management plan's size, but direct acquisition returned a 104-page PDF. A failed shell quoting command and a console encoding error are preserved in the retrospective; source bytes and saved text survived.

We found bibliographic and 286-page text candidates for Rao's first volume, but acquired no replacement containing plate XXXVIII. The search record preserves this unresolved route; neither a cover nor a text duplicate has been counted as recovery. Street 1/Street 6 compass wording remains conflicted. A labelled plate still requires surveyed true-north control before yielding a bearing.

The author validation checks source hashes, page bounds, six row bindings and eleven-page accounting. It adds no new determination, bearing, calibration, independent excavation observation or confirmation cohort. Q-1032 remains open.

The strongest supported reading is that visible architectural lines carry both ancient rebuilding and modern resetting. TF-19 anchors the later deposit above a dock wall, and the Castle's southeast face has a documented modern intervention. The deciding test joins the SRG11 sample section and Dholavira's numbered-stone restoration photographs to phase-keyed wall coordinates, then dates short-lived material sealed by the original construction.
