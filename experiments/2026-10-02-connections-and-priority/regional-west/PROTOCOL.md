# Regional numerical connections: exploratory source audit

This run asks which western and Central Asian objects preserve a connection between a graphic mark, an independently measured quantity, and Indus contact. It follows the existing contact and physical-grounding audits. The source selection and diagnostic were designed after seeing the published masses; they are exploratory.

The first endpoint is an object-keyed evidence table. Each row separates observations, author interpretations, and missing records. A common site supports a site join; a shared excavation assemblage supports an assemblage join; matching inventories support an object join. These have separate fields.

The numerical endpoint reconstructs Ascalone's proposed relation for Susa SB 13677, SB 13323 and SB 17774. Decimal masses are exact arithmetic inputs without an assumed measurement precision. Their proposed coefficients are 25, 1 and 2 in an Indus base. The 25 follows a quarter of a 100-unit mina. The historical source supplies these coefficients.

For mass m and coefficient c, the inferred base is m/c. The smallest common symmetric relative tolerance is (largest base − smallest base)/(largest base + smallest base). A grid of 0%, 0.5%, 1%, 2%, 3%, and 5% supplies common-unit intervals. A second calculation permits only mass loss: measured mass lies between (1 − r) times the intended mass and the intended mass. Its minimum allowance is 1 − smallest base/largest base. These chosen radii do not represent a calibrated historical error distribution.

Girsu AO 247 and AO 248 provide a readable control with coefficients 10 and 5 shekels. Apply the same calculation and retain its separation from the Susa hypothesis.

Four additive marks give another hypothesis for SB 13677: a unit of 83.5625 g. The source's quarter-mina proposal and this simple rival remain available for an external unit test. No new authenticated Indus meaning is assigned.

Rank recovery packets for Gonur, Altyn Depe, Tepe Yahya and Susa token envelopes. An unequal mixture requires an exterior count or geometric token/impression correspondence to extend the SB 1940 control.

Raw PDFs, museum exports and images stay local-only. Repository outputs contain factual notes, aggregates, code and source metadata.
