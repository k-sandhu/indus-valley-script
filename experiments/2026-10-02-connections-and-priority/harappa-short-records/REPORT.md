# The repeated three-face record at Harappa

Twenty of the 22 tablets H2218–H2239 have the same three exact inscription modules in the preserved Lipi transcription, once the face rows are treated as an unordered set. H2238 changes the stroke token beside the open form; H2237 changes a variant of the complex form. The source drawings support a repeated count pattern on five fully readable tablets. They leave the proposed count exception on H2238 unresolved. [Computed results](main-v2/results.json), [source readings](observations.json), [independent scalar checks](review-v1/checks.json).

The new dataset keeps all 66 complete two-code faces, their physical object identities, source registrations, excavation units, published engraver groups and displayed face boundaries. All 66 were excluded by the principal length-at-least-three benchmark. This is an exploratory analysis of real records and published object witnesses. The full joined inscriptions and pair tables remain [local-only](../../../data/raw/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/harappa-short-records/main-v2/); tracked files contain our observations, counts, locators, hashes and code.

## What repeats across the faces

C denotes the complex form that the original authors compared to a carrier, A the open curved or angle-like form, and D the closed diamond form. Each occurs with a separate group of upright strokes. These descriptive labels keep the comparison readable without assigning a word or numerical unit.

| Graphic family | Three-stroke rows in the corpus | Four-stroke rows in the corpus | Newly read native-source counts |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| C | 22 | 0 | 9 faces with three |
| A | 1 | 21 | 7 faces with four |
| D | 22 | 0 | 16 faces with three |

The corpus therefore contains one repeated family/count profile, C3–A4–D3, on 21 objects. Its C and D counts have no variation across these 22 records. A supplies the only proposed count change. Ordinary correlation between the three numerical columns would be undefined for C and D because their variances are zero; the informative result is the family-specific repetition shown in the table.

The renewed source inspection assigns 32 counts directly from native pixels, extending the earlier seven-face annotation. Five tablets, H2219, H2224, H2225, H2226 and H2239, have all three counts readable. Each has C3, A4 and D3. Those five occupy three displayed lower-face arrangements, so their count profile persists across the drawing's arrangement variation. All 32 assigned counts agree with their corresponding derivative modules. The observer had already examined the code inventory, so this source check records readable evidence and compatibility rather than blinded transcription accuracy.

The other 34 face counts remain unassigned. Under the authors' stated three-or-four-stroke domain and repeated C/A/D inventory, between 5 and 22 objects can have the C3–A4–D3 profile. Between 0 and 15 can have three strokes on every face. Seven directly observed A4 faces rule out an all-three profile for those objects. Missing counts are allowed to vary freely in these bounds; the corpus pattern is not used to fill them. [Finite bounds and their premises](main-v2/results.json).

## The two exceptions and the object pairs

H2238 is source specimen 18, registration H95-2613/6560-01, in published engraver Group 3 and the scattered-find category. Its imported A module has three long bars where the other 21 records have four. Its middle D face shows three separate strokes, but the bottom A region remains heavily hatched. The native drawing consequently provides no direct confirmation of the changed A count.

H2237 is specimen 17, H96-3125/6937-16, also in Group 3 and the scattered-find category. It changes the internal construction of C while retaining the same stroke count. Rendering the imported observation font distinguishes the straight internal form from the branching variant. Both belong to the same broad C family; the Mayig crosswalk also places the two Wells IDs in one broader class. This supplies a comparison at two graphic resolutions. The fine assignment still needs an individually registered photograph or mould view.

There are 231 unordered pairs of physical tablets. Exact modules agree in 190 pairs. Twenty pairs compare H2238 with a member of the 20-object common set and change only the A stroke token. Another twenty compare H2237 with that common set and change only C's graphic variant. The H2237/H2238 pair changes both fields. Collapsing the C variant produces 21 objects with the same family/count profile, hence 210 identical-profile pairs and 21 pairs with an A-count change.

The source count observations establish ten identical-profile pairs among the five fully counted tablets. Across all 22 objects, 231 pairs could still share that profile, while 221 could differ in exactly one count under a compatible completion. No one-count-difference pair is established directly by the registered drawings. The large possible range identifies the source information still needed; it preserves a readable exception as a test target instead of treating its imported code as an external count.

## Order and orientation

The imported face rows have four exact ordered classes, with sizes 13, 7, 1 and 1. Allowing cyclic row rotations leaves those four classes. Adding row reversal merges the two common classes into the 20-object set. For three faces, rotations and reversals generate every permutation. These calculations concern stored row order, which has no established map to an oriented physical prism.

The actual source drawings have a separate geometry record. Nineteen lower-face pairs are readable and occupy eight displayed arrangements. Exchanging the two lower faces leaves four groups; a common reversal of their displayed ends leaves two, comprising 11 same-side and 8 opposite-side arrangements. These reproduce the earlier representation audit. H2238, H2230 and H2234 retain unresolved bottom families and positions after the new native-raster inspection. The C form's physical orientation and the prism's turning convention remain uncoded. ## Production, deposition and available images

Sixteen tablets came from the unit-8040 clump, two were nearby and four were scattered in the same area. The clump and nearby objects all have the common exact module set in the corpus. Both exceptions occur among the scattered finds and in published Group 3. The authors assigned production groups from microscopic mould examination. Their group sizes are 6, 9 and 7. These linked production and deposition fields prevent treating 66 faces as independent examples of a general accounting practice.

Source readability also differs by group: the renewed count assignments cover 10 faces in Group 1, 19 in Group 2 and only 3 in Group 3. Group 3 contains both corpus exceptions and all three unresolved lower pairs. A comparison which discards unreadable faces would lose much of the evidence most relevant to the exceptions.

None of the 22 tablet keys appears in the preserved Lipi image index or its asset filenames. Their individually registered witnesses are the 22 native drawing crops from the original chapter. Two HARP cohort photographs are also available locally. The three-group photograph has nine displayed panels, eight with assigned counts and one with candidates {3, 4}. In the Group 2 A panel, three prominent grooves are visible, while a narrower left trace could be a fourth. A second informed reader reached the same ambiguity. The photograph gives no registrations for its group representatives. Its panels remain separate from individual tablet rows. [Original HARP group page](https://www.harappa.com/indus2/149.html), [photo provenance](../../../research/corpus/plate_audit/H2238_gallery/gallery_provenance_observations.json).

The same-context comparison seal H1682, registration H96-2796/6876-01, provides a useful repeated module on a different physical medium. The original description identifies the C-plus-three-stroke set among its signs. The imported seal row's exact C variant matches H2237; the other 21 tablets carry the broader family's alternate variant. The source counts three additional signs before the shared pair, while the imported row has four total tokens. Its source and derivative segmentations remain separate. H1710 and H1714 are retained as additional registered comparisons from other contexts, outside the tablet count analysis.

## The next discriminating observation

The smallest informative acquisition is a registered image packet for H2238's three faces, with raking light from several directions, readable groove edges, both ends and adjoining faces. H2230 and H2234 should receive the same treatment because their unresolved lower faces provide controls for the hatching problem. H2237 and a readable common-set tablet from Group 3 should be included to check the C variant under matched production conditions.

A universal C3–A4–D3 template predicts a fourth A groove on H2238. Three securely preserved grooves with no lost or unfinished fourth groove would reject that universal version. A confirmed A3 reading would establish a real count variant while leaving several functions possible. A bounded accounting proposal would then need an independently linked count, measure or operation that changes with A3 versus A4. A name or institutional-label proposal would need an independently identified actor or group connected to the shared C module on seals and tablets. The existing repeated modules and disposal context provide acquisition targets for these proposals; they supply no referent labels to score them against.
