# Lothal: contact geometry and visible cuts on repeated-string objects

L206 preserves a deep curved rear contact, incompatible with the frozen proposal that G01 always records one angular board-edge contact. The available photographs leave the other four pictured G01 objects' contact topology unresolved. They therefore supply one diagnostic object, short of the current test's two-object criterion. The new L190 view adds crossed bands and ridges, while the earlier angular-junction description for L193 remains an interpretation that its dark photograph cannot certify.

The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) was saved before this image coding. The main reader already knew the source authors' interpretations and four earlier development observations. A second model reader inspected the same selected source pixels without receiving the main reader's counts. Both readings are recorded; neither examined original objects. The source is Frenez and Tosi's *The Lothal Sealings: Records from an Indus Civilization Town at the Eastern End of the Maritime Trade Circuits across the Arabian Sea* (2005), Figures 3–7, printed pp.98–102, [author deposit](https://zenodo.org/records/5514283). The [current-test design](../../2026-10-02-decipherment-readiness/corpus/CURRENT-TEST.md) and [earlier trace observations](../../2026-10-02-six-hour/lothal-physical-use/independent-review/selected-trace-observations.json) remain unchanged.

## G01: the narrow contact restriction

The frozen cylindrical-contact proposal predicts a continuous concave or curved contact surface. The frozen board-edge proposal predicts adjoining planes or an angular junction. A broken silhouette by itself cannot establish either shape. Five of the eleven G01 objects have labelled photographs in the selected figures.

| Object and source view | Image observation | Bounded result |
|---|---|---|
| **L206**, Fig.3 top row, left profile and right rear | A deep open recess follows a broad curved arc between projecting margins. The rear shows the corresponding smoother inner recess. | Compatible with a cylindrical-contact geometry; incompatible with the sole angular board-edge prediction. It is one physical object seen twice. |
| **L190**, Fig.4 second row, second, third and fifth views | At least two broad transverse recessed bands cross finer parallel furrows. Side/end views preserve projecting margins and several relief regions. | These surfaces add a useful physical observation. The photographed boundaries do not resolve a dihedral contact angle or a single cylindrical section. |
| **L193**, Fig.5 upper-left group | The profile has projecting regions; the rear is dark, with some parallel marks. The stamp view preserves overlapping impressions. | An angular change in the silhouette can also be a fracture. Both readers leave the contact-plane junction unresolved. |
| **L124**, Fig.7 top-left group, right rear | Crossing short ribbed patches appear on an irregular rear region. | The texture is readable; the contact cross-section and any adjoining planes remain unresolved. |
| **L126**, Fig.7 top-right group, right rear | Uneven broad ridges and folds cross the rear. | The folded relief is readable; the view does not certify one angular junction or cylindrical section. |

L125, L127–L129, L191 and L197 have no labelled photograph in Figures 3–7. They remain in the eleven-object denominator with no image-coded outcome. The test identifies L206 as the present counterexample to the sole angular board-edge restriction. A stronger comparison of different resolved contact topologies needs an additional object with a clearly exposed contact junction; L190 or L193 is a specific imaging target. The textures on L124 and L126 can guide that work without supplying a material identity or a new topological classification.

L164, L166, L167, L169 and L170 are unpictured in the selected figures. The inset captioned “fingernails tallies” has no explicit object identity and contributes zero objects to this analysis. Every pictured counting surface is partly hidden, damaged or incompletely exposed, so the number of complete object totals is zero.

The selected zones expose different upper or side margins and cover no standardized fraction of the marked surface. Their counts remain separate. The arithmetic intersection of the four combined ranges is {3}: even if comparable coverage were added as an assumption, these bounds would still leave equal visible counts possible. A future quantity hypothesis can use the named zones as acquisition targets: obtain complete, calibrated images of the whole marked surface, distinguish deliberate cuts from handling damage, and preserve the same-string join. These photographs supply presence, location and bounded visible multiplicity.

## Record, scope and verification

Same-string membership comes from the frozen [graphic-coverage endpoint](../../2026-10-02-six-hour/lothal-physical-use/graphic-coverage/results.json): G01 has eleven eight-sign records and G02 ten four-sign records. Same-die identification remains unresolved in this audit. L193's overlapping impressions and the incomplete stamp panels require a separate calibrated epigraphic comparison.

The [observation record](observations.json) links all 21 targets and 24 selected views. The main reader's [first pass](first-pass-observations.json) was saved before the [secondary audit](secondary-view-audit.json); its bounds were retained. [Results](results.json) report the two readings and their combined ranges. The [checker](check.py) verifies exact target membership, crop/object joins, interval order, source hashes and pixel identity; its [verification output](verification.json) records every check. Crop coordinates, transformations and local-only status are in the [initial crop manifest](crop-manifest.json) and [additional crop manifest](additional-crop-manifest.json). Source pixels were cropped without scaling, tonal adjustment or annotation, and contact-only rectangles excluded separate stamp panels where the plate layout allowed it.

The result narrows the physical test to one resolved G01 contact and four bounded G02 cut zones. The next useful observations are a readable contact junction on another G01 object and complete marked surfaces for the pictured G02 objects. Those observations would add information that the present halftone plates cannot supply.
