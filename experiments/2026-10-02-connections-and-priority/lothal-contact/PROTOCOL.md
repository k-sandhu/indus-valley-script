# Lothal contact-shape and visible-cut test

Frozen before the new image coding: 2 October 2026, 15:43 UTC. The reviewer has read Frenez and Tosi's interpretations and the earlier selected-trace records. This is a source-informed exploratory test. L124, L126, L193 and L206 are development observations being checked again; L190 adds a pictured G01 object to that audit.

The fixed populations are G01: L124–L129, L190, L191, L193, L197 and L206; G02: L161–L170. The source is Frenez and Tosi (2005), Figures 3–7, PDF pages 36–40, printed pages 98–102. Object IDs join observations to the existing complete-string groups. Same-string membership and same-die identification will be recorded separately.

The G01 test compares two narrow contact proposals. A single smooth cylindrical peg contact predicts one continuous concave surface or curved cross-section, with no resolved angular junction between adjoining contact planes. A single board-edge contact predicts an angular junction or adjoining contact planes, with no resolved deep, smoothly curved arc wrapping around one cylindrical contact. A mixed closure could contain both shapes; this test concerns the restriction to one specified contact geometry. Fine material identification is outside the endpoint.

Each pictured object receives separate view records for visible curvature, angular junctions, channels, crossing or parallel ridges, broken margins and visibility. Stamp panels will be excluded from contact-only crops where the layout permits this. Crop coordinates and image hashes will retain their original source references. A broken outline alone supplies no contact geometry. A feature is diagnostic only when its surface can be distinguished from a fracture or an impression panel. The outcome records which narrow proposal is incompatible with the readable contacts and lists the surfaces that remain unresolved.

The G02 audit inspects object-linked views for L161, L162, L163, L165 and L168. A cut is a separate short arcuate or slit-like depression visible on a continuous outer/contact-margin surface. Long fastening channels, cracks, stamp features and woven ridges are recorded separately. Attribution to fingernail/manipulation remains a morphological interpretation. Counts use one selected view per object, with a lower count of clearly separated cuts and an upper count including specifically identified uncertain cuts. A merged or damaged zone with no finite visible bound has a lower count only. Photographic counts refer to the selected visible zone; a whole-object tally requires a completely exposed, preserved counting surface. Repeated views will not add counts. The unlabelled inset has no object ID and contributes zero objects and no tally.

The checker will validate target membership, unique object/view keys, source coordinates, interval order, image hashes, absent-photo records, and the derived compatibility/count summary. It will not fit a classifier or a synthetic model. Raw source crops remain local-only under the source's unresolved rights. Original source files and previous endpoints remain unchanged.
