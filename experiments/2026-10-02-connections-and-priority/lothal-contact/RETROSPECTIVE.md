# Lothal image test: methods, limits and outcomes

This follow-up used the current-test design and existing selected-trace observations after the independent novelty review had finished. It owned only this directory and new local-only crops under data/raw/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/lothal-contact/. Project instructions, writing and redistribution rules, the relevant earlier retrospective and the PDF inspection skill were read. No frozen endpoint, source witness, manuscript, shared navigation file or registry was edited. No Git operation was performed.

The exploratory protocol was saved before the new coding. It recorded the reader's prior knowledge of the authors' interpretations, the four development observations, the fixed G01/G02 populations and two contact predictions. The reader inspected the largest original PDF-embedded images of Figures 3–7. Twenty-one unchanged pixel rectangles isolated object groups, contact views and cut zones. Three further rectangles exposed marginal marks more clearly. Their parent images, coordinates, hashes and transformation method were saved in separate manifests; all source crops remain local-only.

Direct cropped-view inspection narrowed the old L193 description. Its angular silhouette is visible, but the dark rear does not expose a contact-plane junction independently of fracture. L206's open rounded recess is much clearer. The new L190 photograph adds transverse bands crossed by finer parallel furrows, without settling a single contact angle. This left one diagnostic G01 object and an unmet two-object criterion. The earlier records remain intact.

For G02, the main reader selected one zone per pictured object and saved the first-pass observations before receiving a second model review. The second reader received the protocol and exact crops but not those counts. Both readers found the same clear lower counts on L161, L162, L163 and L168. They differed on faint or marginal candidates at the upper count. Both saw L165's cut boundaries as unresolved and every whole counting surface as incomplete. The saved first pass, secondary observations and range covering both readings remain separate. The zones cover different margins, so no pooled cross-object count variable was defined. Their arithmetic intersection {3} was retained with that scope; it leaves equal counts possible even under an added comparability assumption. The unlabelled inset supplied no object or tally.

The checker rebuilds the bounded summaries from those records and compares every crop with the original source rectangle, including pixel identity and dimensions. It also checks the frozen membership source, all 21 target objects, 24 unique view links, same-string/same-die separation and interval order. Initial checking passed 213 checks before the secondary audit and report existed; final checking passed 234 checks including those later records and local links. Source hashes were retained. No simulation, classifier or broad material inference was run.

The outcome is a source-informed image constraint and an acquisition plan grounded in named objects: obtain a readable contact junction on another G01 object and complete counting surfaces on G02. The second model review adds a photographic visibility check while the remaining archaeological validation requires original-object or laboratory observation.
