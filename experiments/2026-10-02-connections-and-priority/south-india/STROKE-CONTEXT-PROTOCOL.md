# Stroke groups inside compound graffiti

The portal acquisition found a concordance that links base sign 39 and its variants to components of compound signs. Its RMRL component numbers differ from government sign identifiers. This exploratory calculation follows inspection of the concordance schema and the printed stroke descriptions, and precedes calculation of the endpoint below.

Use the frozen `concordance-sign39.json` response and the public deployed client's `va` mapping to recover government identifiers for components. Keep dot-separated identifiers as strings, preserving `39.1` and `39.10`. Compare any repeated whole-form keys before deduplicating; an inconsistent component list remains an unresolved form.

The plain stroke set is sign 39 plus variants 39.1 through 39.15. The printed description gives visible line counts and orientation, including 16 for 39.15. Decorated variants 39.16–39.18 and compound forms have their own shapes. A plain stroke component carries a count description, while the meaning of that component stays unassigned.

For every compound with at least one plain stroke component, record the component length, number of stroke components, number of contiguous stroke blocks, endpoint positions and occurrence count. Test the graphic restriction of exactly one stroke block and exactly one other component. Report form counts separately from the portal's reported occurrences. Compound classifications reflect the authors' normalized drawings; they are not a transcription of free linear inscriptions.

Check several forms against the printed concordance and photographic plates. Preserve any mismatch between routes, form keys or component order. The endpoint can identify the finite comparative population and the cost of imposing a simple count-plus-category grammar. A numerical value would require an independent observation on a named specimen.
