# Source and physical-side extension

We recovered one divided-face crop and provisional side assignments for eight export faces on four objects. The missing-crop queue falls from 43 to 42. The five-object physical review links 13 of its 20 photographs to numbered export faces through primary captions and graphic correspondences. Two export faces associated with a fifth object retain a parts-versus-sides conflict. [Version 2 results](results-v2.json) and [author checks](verification-v2.json) give the counts and hashes.

## What the source review changes

The fixed block comprised the first five image-linked entries in the existing multi-face queue. We inspected seven whole primary plate pages, twenty photograph thumbnails and the ten export code rows rendered in the held source font. The detailed observations, original-pixel bounds, polarity, transforms and residual queue are local in `data/raw/2026-10-24-unit-source-extension/run-v2/`, physically on D:. The [source manifest](SOURCE-MANIFEST-v2.json) records logical and physical paths, source URLs, rights, bytes and hashes. These are author observations submitted for review.

Four reviewed objects support explicit bridges between printed sides and export faces. The bridge uses visible graphic combinations as well as captions. One object's shorter reverse legend is export face .1, while its longer animal-face legend is .2. Another object's second inscription is on printed side C, around a perforation. The photographs and their impressions retain their own orientation and polarity. We applied no mirror or rotation to the recovered crops; annotations record the horizontal reflection needed to compare engraved photographs with normalized source-code order.

The fifth catalogue entry shows two numbered pottery fragments, each captioned side A, and an alternate view of the first fragment. The numbered fragments cannot be called opposite sides. Unknown export signs prevent a secure assignment of those two numbered export faces. The local record preserves both parts and their unresolved physical relation.

## The 43 missing-face routes

We checked every missing-crop entry against the held caption index and export object metadata. One inherited acquire-CISI3 task has a held CISI 2 plate, printed page 140, PDF page 175. Its object and impression panels are now cropped separately. Three visible tiers include a short lower stroke group, and the fracture and unknown-sign qualification remain. We assign no segment-position map from this observation.

The remaining catalogue routes comprise nine Chanhu-daro entries for CISI 3.2 and 22 Mohenjo-daro or Harappa entries for CISI 3.1. The publisher's one-page flyer, *The Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions (CISI)*, fixes the volume scopes and ISBNs. The eleven entries without a CISI key comprise five Harappa records carrying an excavation identifier and figure locator, two Dholavira excavation-number leads and four records needing an object locator. These seven inherited locators support targeted searches, with primary identity verification still due. The local route table retains each identifier and its qualification.

A misleading acquisition filename required correction. The held `cisi3-stream.pdf` is an incomplete download of the URL that the data manifest identifies as CISI 1. PyMuPDF reports zero pages; its 12,058,624 bytes cannot supply missing CISI 3 plates. A bounded request to that legacy route failed TLS negotiation. Bibliographic snippets confirm the CISI 3.1 title through the [University of Helsinki record](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/corpus-of-indus-seals-and-inscriptions-volume-3-new-material-untr/); direct requests to that page and Harappa returned HTTP 403. An attempted Indology URL returned an unrelated fellowship announcement and is excluded from source evidence. These failed and incorrect routes remain in the local access record.

## Reproduction and scope

The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) preceded image viewing. [Amendment 1](AMENDMENT-1.md) added the single held missing-face locator before its image inspection. `prepare.py`, `render_linked.py` and `render_alignment.py` describe rendering; `build.py` consumes the local authored observations and writes the version 2 annotations, crops and aggregate result; `verify.py` independently recounts their coverage and checks hashes and crop sizes. Output files use exclusive creation. A reproduction should choose a new local run directory and aggregate filename. The final code includes the case-safe crop filenames required on Windows.

Version 1 is preserved with a crop-path defect: uppercase object labels and lowercase impression labels collided on Windows. Its aggregate result is superseded for crop use. Version 2 names the polarity in each crop filename. All thirteen author checks pass, including every detailed-output hash and coordinate-derived crop size. This is author validation, with independent review pending.

The completed 29-face adjudication, segmenter, stock and frozen predictions are preserved. We fit no model, claim no complete word boundaries and open no confirmation cohort. All displayed source pages and code sheets are recorded as historical exploratory exposure. The residual queue contains 42 divided faces without recovered crops, two unresolved numbered export faces in the selected block and 373 unreviewed objects from the original 378 image-linked multi-face objects.

The strongest supported reading is that numbered export faces conceal several different physical relations: inscribed fronts and reverses, curved inscribed sides, impressions and numbered fragments. Primary captions joined to visible graphic combinations resolve eight of ten selected face assignments. Expert review of those exact bridges, followed by acquisition of the 31 missing catalogue routes, would decide whether the expanded physical evidence can support a frozen test of reusable inscription units.
