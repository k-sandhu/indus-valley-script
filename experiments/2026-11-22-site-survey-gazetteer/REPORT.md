# Reconciled Indus site and survey-effort gazetteer

22 November 2026. Data reconciliation and metadata audit. Verification tier: submitted (`8/8` independent checks passed in [verification-v1.json](verification-v1.json)).

We reconciled six local site, town, excavation, and prospection packages into a single deterministic register of 144 entities ([gazetteer-v1.json](gazetteer-v1.json), [gazetteer-v1.csv](gazetteer-v1.csv)), backed by 443 alias rows ([aliases-v1.csv](aliases-v1.csv)), 19 documented regional survey campaigns, and effort profiles for all 21 prospection windows ([survey-effort-v1.json](survey-effort-v1.json)). Cross-register reconciliation exposed 24 localities with material coordinate conflicts, including nine sites where held published locators disagree by more than 8 kilometres: Gola Dhoro (178.41 km including an excluded modern town locator, 10.43 km between two archaeological reports), Rojdi (51.06 km), Desalpar (42.17 km), Farmana (25.54 km), Banawali (13.90 km), Kunal (13.74 km), Ganweriwala (13.68 km), Allahdino (11.12 km), and Surkotada (8.10 km). Overlaying the gazetteer against documented survey campaigns shows that two intensively surveyed Haryana and Punjab corridors (`P04` and `P05`) contain zero records in our inscription-centered holdings even though Green et al. (2019) confirmed 199 archaeological map features and 64 off-map locations across that region. Apparent emptiness in a corpus-driven site list measures archive coverage, not settlement absence.

## Scope, units, and shared assumptions

Every record in the gazetteer obeys the protocol frozen in [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) before the merge ran. We preserve four analytical units separately: a `recorded_locality` (a named place in a catalogue, inventory, or continuity review), a `physical_mound_or_scatter` (a discrete mound, fortified enclosure, cemetery, quarry, or unverified remote-sensing landform cluster), a `phase_footprint` (an occupied area or chronological envelope tied to one period or measurement definition), and an `excavated_deposit` (an opened trench, salvage cutting, borehole, or excavation season). Two regional provenance labels (`Dilmun` and `Luristan`) remain tagged as `regional_provenance_label` so they cannot enter settlement counts.

The 144 reconciled entities span four explicit domain roles so downstream analyses can filter cleanly:

| Domain role | Count | Unit class | Composition |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `indus_or_contact_inscribed_locality` | 117 | 115 `recorded_locality`, 2 `regional_provenance_label` | All 115 entities from the Evidence Census and Living Atlas plus 2 newly added post-catalogue inscribed-find localities (`Dhalewan` and `Indiranagar (Kanpur)`) |

## Alias resolution and homonym disambiguation

Spelling variants and cross-register identifier mismatches previously broke automated joins between our own local tables. The [26 October Living Atlas](../2026-10-26-indus-living-atlas/mapping/REPORT.md) joined 32 of the 34 candidate Harappan towns from the [Q-1032 town-axis register](../2026-10-24-q1032-town-axis-register/REPORT.md) because `desalpur` did not match `desalpar` and `gola-dhoro` did not match `gola-dhoro-bagasra`. Likewise, living-town case `LT-11` (`Rupnagar / Ropar`) sat apart from the archaeological site `ropar-rupar`, and 16 site names in the Evidence Census `site-register.json` used spelling variants such as `Gharo Bhiro` for `Nuhato`, `Bala-kot` for `Balakot`, and `Rodji` for `Rojdi`.

Our explicit alias table ([aliases-v1.csv](aliases-v1.csv)) resolves 443 total name rows (144 canonical names and 299 non-canonical aliases) across eight relationship types: 208 spelling variants, 63 catalogue codes, 14 subsite mound names, 7 modern municipality contexts, 4 local mound names, 2 ambiguous toponyms, and 1 rediscovery name. All 34 Q-1032 towns, all 18 Living Towns records (4 merged onto `Harappa`, `Ropar (Rupar)`, `Dholavira`, and `Mohenjo-daro`; 14 added as continuity references), and all 26 Post-Catalogue Finds site rows (24 merged, 2 added) now join cleanly. The table links `Morodharo` to `Kotada (Lodrani)` (Rajesh et al. 2025), `Bagasra` and `Gola Dhoro` to `gola-dhoro-bagasra`, `Rupar` and `Rupnagar` to `ropar-rupar`, `Chanhujo-daro` to `chanhu-daro`, `Lakhian jo Daro` and `Sukkur` to `lakhanjo-daro`, and `Valamio Timbo` to `shikarpur`.

Six alias rows carry `ambiguous_homonym = true` because collapsing them by string alone creates false joins. The Kutchi fortified-mound toponym `Kotada` belongs to both `dholavira` (Khadir Island) and `kotada-lodrani` (Bela Bet), while `Kotda` also appears in Bela Bet and Kachchh Mainland. `Bagasra` and `Desalpur` carry explicit disambiguation warnings because gazetteer lookups confused them with Bagasara town in Amreli district (178 km south) and Desalpur village in Wikidata (40 km southeast). `Sukkur` is flagged because the modern industrial city encloses Lakhanjo-daro without delimiting the ancient settlement.

Separating units prevents false area comparisons. ## Survey-effort layer across P01 to P21

Tagging all 21 editorial prospection windows (`P01` through `P21`) with 19 documented regional campaigns and held spatial concordance files ([survey-effort-v1.json](survey-effort-v1.json)) separates archive omissions from unsurveyed terrain. Forty-six gazetteer records fall inside one or more of the 21 windows (47 records carry at least one regional campaign tag once unlocated `Bahola` is linked to the Cambridge and BHU Land, Water and Settlement campaign).

Four windows fall in `intensive_ground_survey` (`P03`, `P04`, `P05`, `P07`), ten in `targeted_excavation_and_local_survey` (`P02`, `P06`, `P08`, `P10`, `P12`, `P13`, `P16`, `P17`, `P18`, `P20`), three in `historical_map_and_remote_screening` (`P01`, `P09`, `P14`), and four in `reconnaissance_or_geomorphic_only` (`P11`, `P15`, `P19`, `P21`).

The sharpest diagnostic appears in `P04` (Khanauri-Nabha corridor) and `P05` (Maur-Bathinda-Phul corridor). Both lie inside the Green et al. (2019) historical-map field survey footprint, which confirmed 199 archaeological map features and 64 off-map locations across Haryana and Punjab, yet both contain zero entries in our reconciled 144-record gazetteer. Similarly, `P07` (Rakhigarhi-Masudpur) holds only 3 corpus localities (`Rakhigarhi`, `Masudpur`, `Lohari Ragho`) out of 127 all-period locations recorded within 15 km of Rakhigarhi by the Cambridge project, and `P01` (Cholistan) holds 7 records (including 3 remote proposals) out of 337 mound clusters proposed by Orengo et al. (2020). Our held MAHSA API probes illustrate the same boundary: queries over `B02`, `B04`, `Hanumangarh`, `Pilibanga`, and `Juna Khatiya` returned three historic Survey of India map-sheet metadata records (`Cutch No. 6` inside `P12`, plus `Rajputana No. 44 P 01` and `Punjab, Rajputana No. 44 P 05` south of `P06`) and zero bulk site coordinates. Empty cells in `gazetteer-v1.csv` inside `P01`, `P03`, `P04`, `P05`, `P07`, `P08`, `P09`, and `P14` reflect unacquired survey tables, while empty interior cells in `P11`, `P15`, `P19`, and `P21` combine limited published survey with alluvial burial and shoreline shift.

## Boldest supported reading and deciding test

The boldest reading supported by this reconciled gazetteer is that published spatial analyses of Indus settlement networks, site-size hierarchies, and excavation ratios built from unaligned gazetteers suffer from two systematic distortions large enough to invert regional conclusions: multi-kilometre locator errors on major excavated towns (up to 51 km between active archaeological and Wikidata coordinates at Rojdi and 42 km at Desalpar, plus 13.9 km at Banawali and 13.7 km at Ganweriwala) and near-total omission of surveyed small rural settlements from inscription-derived site lists (leaving intensively surveyed corridors like `P04` and `P05` at zero recorded sites and `P07` at 3 of 127 locally surveyed locations).

The concrete deciding test is a two-part concordance audit executed before any new regional density or catchment model is fitted. First, verify the 22 material coordinate conflicts (starting with Rojdi, Desalpar, Gola Dhoro, Farmana, Banawali, Kunal, Ganweriwala, Allahdino, Surkotada, and Mitathal) against primary excavation site plans or surveyed ground control points to establish true site-centre errors below 100 metres. Second, acquire and reconcile at least two full row-level regional survey tables (the Green et al. 2019 Haryana and Punjab check table covering `P03` to `P06`, plus the Cambridge 127-location Rakhigarhi catchment table covering `P07` or the MAHSA regional export) against `aliases-v1.csv` and `gazetteer-v1.json`. If primary ground control reduces median coordinate error across the 22 flagged sites by less than 1 km and adding the row-level regional survey tables changes site density across `P03` through `P07` by less than a factor of two, this reading fails and existing gazetteer coordinates and counts are already adequate for regional spatial modeling.
