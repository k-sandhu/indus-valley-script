# Retrospective: Reconciled Indus site and survey-effort gazetteer

Date: 22 November 2026.

## Approach

We froze the four-unit schema, reconciliation mapping rules, coordinate conflict thresholds, excavation-status vocabulary, and survey-effort rubric in [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) before writing `code/build_gazetteer.py`. Rather than inventing a new site numbering scheme, we anchored canonical identifiers to the existing 115-place Living Atlas slugs and built explicit, auditable mapping dictionaries to pull in the 34-town Q-1032 register, 18 living-town continuity records, 26 post-catalogue inscribed-find rows, 89 Evidence Census site-register rows, 8 non-corpus prospection localities, and 5 unverified remote-sensing proposals. Every generated coordinate assertion kept its exact source register and precision class so an independent script (`code/verify.py`) could check zero entity loss, zero silent averaging, exact Haversine spreads, and exact bounding-box containment without sharing builder code.

## Challenges and fixes

1. **Silent cross-register join failures in earlier packages**: Comparing the 115 Living Atlas sites against the 34 Q-1032 towns and 18 Living Towns revealed three broken joins caused by minor slug differences (`desalpur` versus `desalpar`, `gola-dhoro` versus `gola-dhoro-bagasra`, and `LT-11 Rupnagar / Ropar` versus `ropar-rupar`). Adding an explicit cross-register mapping layer and exporting all 443 name forms into `aliases-v1.csv` repaired all three joins and immediately surfaced the 42.17 km Desalpar and 178.41 km Gola Dhoro coordinate disagreements that had been invisible while the tables stayed separate.
2. **Ambiguous Kutchi fortified-mound toponyms**: Inspecting the local witness `RAJESH2025.pages.json` confirmed that `Kotada in Lodrani` (re-announced in 2024 as `Morodharo`) shares the historical Survey of India toponym `Kotada` with Dholavira on Khadir Island. A naive case-insensitive alias merge would have collapsed Bela Bet onto Khadir Island. We added an explicit `ambiguous_homonym` boolean and automatic cross-entity normalized-collision detection in both the builder and `verify.py` so `Kotada`, `Kotda`, `Bagasra`, `Desalpur`, and `Sukkur` require geographic disambiguation before automated matching.
3. **Mixed precision and unverified Wikipedia coordinates**: Auditing pairwise spreads exposed large divergences where Q-1032 or the inherited atlas drew from unverified gazetteer pins. At Banawali, the Living Atlas carried a Getty TGN coordinate 13.90 km away from the actual protected 6 ha mound that Q-1032 confirmed on satellite imagery; at Rojdi and Desalpar, Q-1032 carried Wikipedia and Wikidata coordinates 51.06 km and 42.17 km away from the Uesugi archaeological coordinates; at Surkotada, the Living Atlas had already copied the Q-1032 imagery coordinate `(23.6113, 70.9174)`, which initially caused our first build pass to tag Surkotada's precision as `catalogued_arcsecond` rather than `image_located_site`. Adding `surkotada` to the Q-1032 primary-precision list alongside `banawali` and `sutkagen-dor` fixed the precision tag while retaining every competing coordinate without averaging.
4. **Prospection-window boundary wording versus exact point-in-box containment**: In our first build draft, four editorial window descriptions (`P06`, `P10`, `P12`, `P13`) casually listed nearby sites (such as Baror, Naru-Waro-dharo, Desalpar, Khirsara, and Pabumath) whose coordinates lie 0.02 to 0.85 degrees outside the rounded 0.1-degree editorial rectangles. Inspecting the computed `matched_gazetteer_ids` caught those edge discrepancies immediately, and we updated the window descriptions to distinguish sites inside each rectangle from adjacent sites outside its border.
5. **Local witness structure variation**: `RAJESH2025.pages.json` stores a top-level JSON array rather than an object with a `pages` key, which raised a `TypeError` on the first run of `build_gazetteer.py`. Adding a schema guard fixed the read cleanly, and a subsequent rebuild confirmed byte-identical SHA-256 hashes across all tracked outputs.

## Outcomes

All 144 entities, 271 coordinate assertions, 443 alias rows, 19 survey campaigns, and 21 prospection-window effort profiles built deterministically and passed all 8 independent checks in `verification-v1.json`. Raw third-party extracts remain confined to `data/raw/2026-11-22-site-survey-gazetteer/raw-concordance-extract-v1.json` on the D: storage volume. The main open task is acquiring primary ground-control coordinates for the 22 material coordinate conflicts and row-level regional survey tables for `P01` through `P09`.
