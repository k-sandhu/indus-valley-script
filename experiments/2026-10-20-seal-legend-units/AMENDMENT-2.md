# Amendment 2: single-line legends for the closing predictions, divided seals for their own

Written 20 October 2026 after the held-set calibration and before any prediction was hashed or any fresh legend read. It changes the population sketched in section 7 of [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md), which joined divided legends top line first and scored them with the single-line legends.

## What the known divided faces showed

The calibration counts, for the 32 two-line faces with a plate order, how often the joined legend ends in a stock closing. It does so on 6 (19%), against 130 of 171 held single-line legends (76%). Read top line first, the top line usually ends in a closing formula and the lower line adds one to three signs: the top line alone ends in a stock closing on 21 of the 32 faces, and the lower line has one to three signs on 28 of them. Over all 59 eligible divided seals in the catalogue's reading order, the longer line read alone ends in a stock closing on 37 (63%); moving the division to any other position of the same legend gives 22% on average.

The lower group is not one kind of thing. Some lower groups end the single-line seal legends of the build corpus (740 ends 396 of its 994, 621 ends 9), and others begin them (820-002 begins 43 and ends none; 820-060 and 692-060 behave alike). On H-60 the catalogue's reading order, unlike the top-first convention, reads the lower 820-002 first and gives the canonical 820-002 … 740-621. Where a divided legend begins and ends is therefore decided by its text and not by its layout, and joining it top line first, as section 7 proposed, would place a closing formula in the middle of many legends.

## The change

The three closing predictions (P1 to P3 of [PREDICTIONS.md](PREDICTIONS.md)) are scored on single-line legends only. This is also the population of the held set H on which their bounds were calibrated, since H was drawn from the clean single-line texts. Divided seal legends get their own prediction, P4: the longer line, read alone, ends in a stock closing more often than it would if the division fell at another position. The prediction set also drops a draft statistic on the share of closings among the ten commonest, whose held-set value (25 of 130) gave it no power against any alternative we could state.

These observations come from the known divided faces, whose divisions trained the model, and from single-line legends of the build corpus; they are exploratory. The fresh pools remain unread.
