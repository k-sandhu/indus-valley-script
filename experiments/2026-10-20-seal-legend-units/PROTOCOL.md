# Protocol: seal-legend units trained on catalogue line divisions

Written 20 October 2026 before any segmenter in this package was scored. Contributor: Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic) in Claude Code, working as a side session of the project's main session. Status class on completion: `frozen-evaluation` for sections 3 and 4, `prospective-prediction` for section 7.

On 20 October 2026 the project set as a target to show, on seals no analysis has read, that a seal legend joins a variable opening unit to a closing formula drawn from a small shared stock ([working model, section 2](../../knowledge/WORKING-MODEL.md)). This package builds the segmentation that defines those units and freezes the predictions before either fresh pool is read. The fresh pools are the 244 absent seal and tablet numbers of the CISI 3.1 range ([CISI 3 ranges](../2026-10-13-cisi3-range-coverage/README.md)), whose catalogue is not held, and inscribed objects published after the catalogues (question [Q-1026](../../knowledge/questions/Q-1026-post-catalogue-finds.md)).

## 1. Why this is new work

The catalogue marks a line division ('/' in the visual code of the Lipi export) on 118 faces. On 69 of them each line recurs elsewhere more often than an equal stretch crossing the division ([F-0018](../../knowledge/findings/F-0018-lines-recur-more-than-crossing-windows.md)), so a division falls between units. Unsupervised segmenters at Indus scale barely beat cutting everywhere on readable scripts ([F-0020](../../knowledge/findings/F-0020-mean-segmentation-accuracy-at-indus-scale.md), [F-0021](../../knowledge/findings/F-0021-seal-legend-segmentation-rehearsal.md)), and the Dirichlet-process segmenter chosen in structure-first step 5 places a boundary at nearly every division but also at 37% of other positions ([step 3](../2026-10-12-structure-first/step3-segmentation/REPORT.md)). No earlier run trained a segmenter on the divisions or scored one on divisions it had not seen. The registry entries `LINE-BREAKS-SEGMENTATION-2026-10-12` and `REHEARSAL-SEGMENTATION-2026-10-12` ask for exactly this retry.

## 2. Inputs

All acquired inputs are local-only and their hashes are written into every output: `data/normalized/lipi_observed_faces.jsonl` (the face records, SHA-256 `1220f559…`), `data/normalized/lipi_clean_rtl_singleline.jsonl` (clean single-line texts in reading order), the Lipi image crops in `data/lipi/public/seal_images/` with the face-level index of [13 October](../2026-10-13-image-index-and-units/README.md), and the normalized Bronze Age records `data/raw/2026-10-12-bronze-age-comparison/normalized-v4/records.jsonl` for the Ur III seal legends. A text is a tuple of Wells sign numbers in reading order; a position i is a cut between the signs at i−1 and i.

Texts and objects are grouped into components before any split. A component joins object families that share a CISI number, an identical clean text, or an identical joined multi-line text under either line order. Every corpus statistic used to score a text is computed with that text's whole component removed, so identical copies, aliases and other faces of the same object never inform its segmentation.

## 3. Plate audit of the line divisions (Q-0027)

The 118 divided faces are listed in `data/raw/2026-10-20-seal-legend-units/plate-audit/worklist.tsv`. The 75 faces with linked plate crops were split into three blocks of 25 and read by three independent agents (Claude Opus 5.5 subagents) under [the audit brief](plate-audit/BRIEF.md), which forbids opening analysis code or results. For each face the reader records legibility, the groups of signs seen and their counts from the top, whether the division is confirmed, where the code's first segment sits (top, bottom, left, right, field), the layout (wrapped text, a separate group, a numeral group) and a confidence. Faces without crops (43, mostly CISI 3 numbers and Dholavira) are accounted for in the audit table with that reason.

The verdicts are used in three fixed ways. A face whose division the reader marks `no` is dropped from every set as a label error. A face whose division is confirmed with high or medium confidence and whose first segment is `top` or `bottom` receives a plate order: the joined text reads the top line first, so the code order (`forward`) when the first segment is on top and the reverse order when it is below. Reading the top line first is the convention of the catalogues and concordances; it is an assumption, and the two all-face sets below score both orders. Layout classes are reported descriptively.

## 4. Training and scoring the segmenter (Q-0028)

**Eligible faces.** A divided face is eligible when its code has no unreadable sign (`000`), no damaged edge (`[`, `]`) and no blank-space marker (`999`), and it is not boustrophedon (direction `BUS`, where the reading direction of each line is unknown). Faces whose component holds a text of the held set H of section 5 are removed so that H stays untouched.

**Model.** Where a scribe breaks a line is modelled as a choice among the positions of the text: the probability of a break at position i is proportional to exp(w·x_i + v·z_i). The unit features x_i are computed from corpus statistics (section 2, own component removed): forward and backward branching entropy rise (the averaged first- and second-order rise of `segmenters.py`), pointwise mutual information of the two adjacent signs, the share of texts that end with the left sign and that start with the right sign, the number of texts that end with the one or two signs before the cut and that start with the one or two signs after it, the count of the adjacent pair, the larger count of the two triples that cross the cut, and the drop in the number of texts containing a one- or two-sign window when it is extended by one sign across the cut, on each side. The layout features z_i are the relative position i/L and its square; they absorb the scribe's preference for where a line ends and are discarded when the model segments. Features are standardized on the training positions, and w and v are fitted by maximum conditional likelihood with an L2 penalty of 1.0 (L-BFGS). Each division is one choice; a face with three lines contributes two choices over the same positions. A face with a plate order contributes its divisions in that order at weight 1; any other face contributes both orders at weight 0.5.

**Cutting rule.** The model cuts wherever the unit score w·x_i exceeds a threshold τ. τ maximizes, on the training faces, the recall of labelled divisions minus the cut rate at the other positions (Youden's J), with ties going to the higher threshold. No other setting is tuned.

**Held-out scoring.** Components are assigned to five folds by SHA-256 of `2026-10-20-seal-legend-units:<smallest family id>`. Each fold's faces are scored by the model trained on the other four folds. The comparison methods are cutting everywhere and nowhere; a layout-only rule that makes one cut where the fold's fitted layout term peaks; and the three unsupervised segmenters of `segmenters.py` (branching entropy, greedy n-gram, Dirichlet process with 2,000 sweeps), each run on the build corpus plus all eligible joined faces in one line order, as in `bench --local-lines`. Every method is scored with `python -m bench segmentation --references … --predictions …` on three reference sets: faces with a plate order in that order (`plate_order`), all eligible faces in code order (`forward`) and in reverse order (`reverse`). A fourth, `conservative`, scores each face in whichever order gives the scored method the lower J; the wrong order joins a text's last sign to its first, which makes a boundary there easier to find, so taking the worse order guards against crediting that artefact.

**Statistic.** A line division marks a position where a boundary must fall, and every other position is unlabelled, so precision cannot be measured on Indus texts. Cutting at random with density r finds a share r of the divisions; cutting everywhere and cutting nowhere are the two ends of that line. The margin over cutting everywhere is therefore J = recall at the divisions − cut rate at the other positions, pooled over faces. A perfect segmenter also cuts at real boundaries that are not line divisions, so J is a lower bound on separation, and the same J can come from different boundary densities. The within-face AUC of the unit score (the share of division–other position pairs in which the division scores higher) is reported without a threshold. Uncertainty comes from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of components with a fixed seed; paired differences use the same resamples.

**Decision rules, fixed now.**

1. Margin over cutting everywhere. The primary set is `plate_order` if it holds at least 25 faces, otherwise `conservative`. The supervised segmenter passes if its pooled held-out J on the primary set is at least 0.25 and the 2.5% bootstrap quantile exceeds 0.10. This margin requires, for example, finding 55% of divisions while cutting 30% of other positions.
2. Improvement over the saved segmenter, the pass rule of Q-0028. On the primary set, J(supervised) − J(Dirichlet process) is positive and its 2.5% bootstrap quantile exceeds zero.
3. Readable-script calibration. In Ur III seal legends, whose editions mark both lines and words, the same model and cutting rule are trained with exactly one labelled line division in each of 75 training legends per fold (chosen at random with seed `2026-10-20-seal-legend-units:sumerian-labels`) and no word divisions. Held-out legends are scored against their complete word divisions with the bench. The calibration passes if held-out boundary F1 exceeds the everywhere baseline's F1 on the same legends by at least 0.05. A second variant trained on every line division of the training legends, and the three unsupervised segmenters, are reported alongside, as is J against the held-out line divisions, so that a J on Indus divisions can be read against a script whose words are known.

Rules 1 and 2 are reported separately; failing rule 2 with a pass on rule 1 means the supervised segmenter finds units but does not beat the Dirichlet process at finding line divisions.

## 5. Unit inventory and segmentation rules

**Held set.** Before the inventory is built, 15% of the components that contain a clean seal legend of three or more signs are set aside by SHA-256 of `2026-10-20-seal-legend-units:H:<component key>` (value mod 100 below 15). Their seal legends of three or more signs form the held set H. The rest of the clean corpus, all object types, forms the build corpus B. H enters no statistic, model or inventory; it serves to calibrate the predictions of section 7 and to measure agreement in section 6.

**Inventory.** The final model is trained on all eligible faces with the rules of section 4, with features from B. It segments every text of B (own component removed). A unit of two or more signs enters the inventory if the model produces it in texts of at least two components of B; every single sign is a unit. The closing stock is the set of final units of B's seal legends of three or more signs that are final in legends of at least three components. The inventory, the closing stock with counts, the model weights and threshold are written to `data/raw/2026-10-20-seal-legend-units/` (they list sign sequences derived from the Lipi export and stay local), and their SHA-256 hashes go into the tracked `FROZEN.json`.

**Rules.** The tracked [RULES.md](RULES.md) states the segmentation in a form a person can apply with the two lists. The closing formula of a legend is its longest final stretch, of at most four signs and shorter than the legend, that is in the closing stock; a legend with no such stretch has no stock closing. The opening, everything before the closing formula, is cut into the fewest inventory units of at most six signs; among equally short covers, the one whose units are longer nearer the end is chosen. The code that implements the rules (`segment_by_rules` in `legend_units.py`) is hashed with them.

## 6. Agreement between two independent segmentations

A second agent, a Claude Opus 5.5 subagent with no access to this package's code, outputs or model, receives RULES.md, the inventory, the closing stock and the H legends, and segments every H legend by applying the rules. Agreement with our implementation is measured as Cohen's kappa over all cut-or-no-cut decisions at the positions of H, the share of legends segmented identically, and the share whose closing formula (or its absence) is identified identically. We regard the rules as operational if kappa is at least 0.80 and closing formulas agree on at least 90% of legends. If either is missed, RULES.md is amended, rehashed and re-tested on a new held set before section 7's predictions are frozen. As a second, internal comparison we report how often the rule-based cuts on H equal the cuts the trained model makes directly.

## 7. Prospective predictions

After the inventory is frozen and H has been segmented, `PREDICTIONS.md` and `predictions.json` state each prediction about the fresh pools with its statistic, null, pass rule, power and effect on credence, together with a joint credence rule and a kill condition. Their hashes, together with the scorer's, go into `PREDICTIONS.sha256` before any fresh legend is read. The population is every fresh seal face (object type `SEAL*`) whose legend is complete, has no unreadable or damaged sign and has at least three signs, with multi-line legends joined top line first; identical legends within a pool count once, and a legend identical to a known text counts and is flagged. Expected values and predictive intervals come from H, which played no part in building the inventory, and power is stated at the expected pool sizes: about 50 such legends among the absent CISI 3.1 seal numbers (84 absent numbers lie between seals, and 60% of the present CISI 3.1 seal faces meet the population rule), an unknown number of post-catalogue finds reported at 20, 30 and 50, and the two pools combined. The statistic families are recorded in `spent-entry.json` for the ledger `bench/spent.json`.

## 8. Run order, budget and what is exploratory

The order is: plate audit; this protocol hashed into `PROTOCOL.sha256`; `sumerian_calibration.py` and `run_cv.py` into `run-2026-10-20/`; the final model, inventory and rules (`build_inventory.py`), hashed; the agreement run; predictions and power, hashed. Each scoring run is done once; a corrected rerun goes to a new dated directory with an amendment, and the first run is kept. Anything examined on the known corpus after the CV outcome, including the H calibration of section 7, is exploratory for that corpus; only the fresh pools can confirm the predictions.
