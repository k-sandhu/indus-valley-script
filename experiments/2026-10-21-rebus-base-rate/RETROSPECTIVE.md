# Rebus base-rate study record

We encoded Parpola's homophone procedure from his 2010 paper, acquired six lexicons, a Tamil compound dictionary, concept norms and a Sangam corpus with a provenance record, froze the protocol and code, and measured how often single homophones and his compound interlocks arise for random words. The frozen rules killed the *veḷ* interlock as evidence, passed his network of interlocks and failed the prediction that single homophones are as easy in Dravidian as elsewhere. An independent reimplementation found one defect in the frozen code; it was repaired under an amendment and every decision held.

## Approaches and repairs

The DSAL dictionaries have no bulk download, so `code/acquire.py` requested them one page at a time with a descriptive user agent, a pause between requests and a JSON log line per page. The Jambu project's parsers gave us the page counts and URL patterns for DEDR (514 pages) and Turner (836); for the *Tamil Lexicon* we probed for the last page and split the download into two page ranges. Three things went wrong. The Concepticon licence path and the NoRaRe dataset name returned HTTP 404; we kept the error bodies in `failed-v1/` and fetched `metadata.json` and the `Brysbaert-2014-Concreteness` dataset instead. All streams stopped for 80 minutes (02:01–03:21 UTC) while the machine slept and resumed on their own. The two *Tamil Lexicon* streams shared one log, and because append mode on Windows is not atomic across processes, 15 log lines were overwritten and one was left as a fragment. The page files are intact; [SOURCES.json](SOURCES.json) recomputes every hash from disk and records the 15 pages without a log line.

The ePSD2 glossary is a 1.9 GB JSON file inside a 213 MB archive, so `code/extract_epsd2.py` decodes its entries one at a time. The parsers needed several repairs before the freeze, all found by reading samples of neutral entries. In DEDR, parentheses opened inside a bold form and closed outside it, optional letters such as *agacāṭ(a)lu* needed both readings, stem notes such as *kala (-pp-, -nt-)* were split at their commas, the appendix on pages 509–514 restarts its numbering, and DSAL prints some entries twice, the second time with supplements; we prefixed the appendix numbers with "a" and kept the fuller record. In the *Tamil Lexicon*, the raw "<" that introduces an etymology was swallowed by the tag stripper. In Turner, one suspicious gloss (*jhaṣa* "sun-heat") turned out to be correct. The Lexibank Burushaski segments pair a source segment with a target segment and carry stress marks, and Rau's Munda forms contain a dash placeholder; both were cleaned.

The synthetic selftest failed twice before the freeze. Stripping Vedic accents also stripped the acute of *ś*, so accents are now removed only from vowels. The calibration check of the shared-member null gave 0.975 against an exact 0.168, because the synthetic anchor words differed only in their final vowel and the stem rule merged them; rebuilt with distinct stems, it gave 0.161. The same property of the stem rule appears in the real data, where *murukkam*, *murukku* and *murukai* are anchored to *muruku*, and it applies to Parpola's words and to the random words alike.

After the freeze the independent check in `code/verify.py` reproduced the real search but drew different replacement pools. The vowel set in `code/rebus.py` contained "y", an IPA vowel, so the stem rule removed the final glide of 1,520 Tamil words such as *nāy* "dog". [Amendment 1](AMENDMENT-1.md) records the repair, a new selftest check and the hashes before and after; `results-v1/` stays beside the repaired `results-v2/`. The same comparison showed that `verify.py` dropped Toda and Kota letters, which we also fixed. The verification then agreed on all seven checks.

Two outcomes needed explanation rather than repair. The frozen restricted *veḷ* search failed for Parpola's own words because the *Tamil Lexicon* glosses *muruka-vēḷ* only by cross-reference; we report the frozen result and, as a labelled post-outcome estimate, the search with the compound added by hand. And the network's low chance rate comes from multiplying four links that are each common; we added a post-outcome calculation of how the rate depends on the number of templates tried.

## What we did not obtain

No machine-readable dictionary of a Munda language or of Burushaski was found, so those languages enter only as comparative wordlists of 113 and 204 concepts. The interlock reruns cover Tamil only; a compound search in another candidate language needs a compound dictionary we do not hold. An earlier package holds a Sangam Tamil table ([published-claim tests](../2026-10-12-published-claim-tests/README.md)); we acquired the Project Madurai texts afresh so that the frequency source has its own provenance record.

## Verification and source handling

[verification-v2.json](verification-v2.json) recomputes the main numbers without the measurement code, and the synthetic and shared bench selftests pass. The bench selftest writes its ledger to a temporary directory, and `bench/spent.json` had the same SHA-256 while it ran and after it finished; no Indus text was read. DEDR and Turner carry all-rights-reserved notices and the *Tamil Lexicon* edition CC BY-NC-ND 2.0, so the raw pages, parsed tables, compound lists and word-level examples are local; tracked results hold aggregates, Parpola's own published words and the replacement headwords. Proposed changes to `knowledge/DATA.md` and `data/MANIFEST.json` are in [DATA-RECORDS.md](DATA-RECORDS.md) and [MANIFEST-PROPOSAL.json](MANIFEST-PROPOSAL.json). During the final writing the C: drive was full apart from a few hundred megabytes; this package's local data take 401 MB, 213 MB of it the ePSD2 archive, and no file of the package was truncated.

## Decision

Rerunning the *veḷ* search on the same dictionary will not move the question. The informative next step is a blind template test with sign identifications frozen before any Tamil compound is consulted, and a second Tamil dictionary with compounds would check that the 30% chance rate is not peculiar to the Madras Lexicon.
