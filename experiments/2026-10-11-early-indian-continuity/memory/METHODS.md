# Documentary and constructive methods

The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) was written before detailed feature coding and model outcomes. Repository memory and initial source snippets had already been inspected, so this is a declared documentary analysis rather than a blinded experiment. Its question concerns what the selected texts actually preserve and which additional observations could distinguish their transmission routes. It has no sample-based estimate of the probability of Harappan inheritance.

The source inventory comprises 18 records: editions/translations of three principal cases, an Iranian control, four river studies and three studies of professional transmission. Several records are presentations of the same ancient passage. The count describes our consulted witnesses and scholarship; it is not the count of independent ancient attestations. The [source ledger](sources.json) gives authors, dates, exact URLs, inspection scope, content hashes, source-access failures and observed rights. Downloaded payloads, extracts and rendered source pages stay in the local raw tree. Tracked observations contain our paraphrases and bounded source checks.

The principal loci are Mādhyandina Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 1.8.1, RV 7.95.1–2 and 10.75.5–6, and DN27's frame and social-origin sequence. The Iranian comparison uses Darmesteter's Vendidad II paragraph numbering. The text audit distinguishes words in the passage from a translator's footnote and distinguishes a different digital edition from an additional ancient witness. Sanskrit and Pali witnesses provide limited controls on specified words and loci; they were not used to produce an independent new translation of the whole text. Thomson's challenge to the conventional RV 7.95.2 rendering is preserved as an unresolved philological alternative.

River-paper observations preserve sampling reach and type of measurement. Burial ages from OSL or shell radiocarbon, provenance signatures and geomorphic inference are separately described. The millions-of-years ages of detrital zircons and micas cannot be substituted for deposition dates. Singh, Giosan and Chatterjee were inspected at the main-paper level; their supplementary numerical tables were not reanalysed. Dave's institutional abstract supplies a reported Chautang finding with no newly extracted numerical age. Giosan's original download filename says `author-summary`, although the payload contains the full main article as well as the later summary.

The initially requested adjacent Śatapatha URL returned chapter 1.7.1 despite its locally chosen filename. Title and body inspection excluded it from the flood case; both the erroneous acquisition and the correct 1.8.1 witness remain preserved. The correct English page has “Alarm's descent” at paragraph 6. The reviewer independently captured Sanskrit 1.8.1.[6], which contains *manoravasarpaṇam*. Our observation uses the intended Manu reference and leaves modern mountain identification unresolved. Allon is cited as a 2022 publication despite the reviewer's local `allon2021` filename, which reflects the acceptance/copyright/DOI year. His Gāndhārī manuscript example concerns that variant of Sāmaññaphala and supplies no witness date for DN27.

The [17 observations](observations.json) classify textual content, a philological dispute, physical process estimates, a reported abstract-level claim, modern ethnographic observations and scholarly transmission comparisons. Each observation gives source IDs, exact loci, our finding and the relevant interpretive limit. Textual absolute composition fields are left unset in this package. Story time, publication, field attestation and estimated physical process time remain differently typed. This avoids imposing a disputed calendar before the actual identifying evidence has been established.

The [claim graph](claim-graph.json) has 16 nodes and 11 edges. `analyze.py` inspects 12 specified routes. Ten are deliberately unsupported dating attempts, such as physical sediment age → assumed river identity → hymn composition. Two are positive controls: a witness may attest the presence of its own text by its witness date. These refer to the Gāndhārī Sāmaññaphala variant and the modern DN27 digital export respectively. The routine tests whether a calendar-bound attachment is permitted by our explicit rule; it does not calculate chronology intervals. The output field reporting zero propagated composition bounds records the rule's result for this graph. An independently justified event/text identification would require new evidence and a new supported relation.

The constructive example is a separate invented dataset. Four operation labels form an acyclic chain. Inputs vary among four named acquisition routes, four invented depths, 24 actor assignments and 27 assignments of three time gaps, giving 10,368 histories. The rewrite preserves the chain and replaces historical attribution with a primordial community. Input labels and time units have no historical calibration or probability. The example enumerates the complete chosen finite space, displays histories from every route, and reports the sizes of the resulting equivalence classes. A changed censure/delegation relation provides a positive control: retained structure can distinguish that change. The premise of a coherent input chain and the erasure rule are stipulated, so this tests information loss rather than narrative sophistication emerging from unconstrained events.

Independent verification avoids importing the enumeration or graph-gating functions. It checks source and input hashes, the existence of referenced observations, specified segment/token controls, the graph's permitted routes, analytic and separately enumerated input size, annotation partition counts and constructive controls. All 96 checks passed. Token presence can catch a wrong locus or corrupted witness; interpretation also required manual reading and the independent source review. Our finite count is not an estimate of how often historical narratives arose by each route.

The interpretive synthesis proposes practice-dependent retention and redaction of origin accounts. The additional hypothesis that narrative resets help explain entitlements was formulated after the source audit and is explicitly marked in the report. It introduces no new fitted endpoint. Its prospective tests, along with controls for textual ancestry, generic agriculture and ecology, are specified in [TESTS.md](TESTS.md).
