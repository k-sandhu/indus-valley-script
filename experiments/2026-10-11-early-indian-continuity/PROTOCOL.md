# Institutions and cultural memory across early Indian history

Protocol, 11 October 2026. This is a new source-based research program, separate from the repository's experiments on script and marked-object continuity. It responds to the user's request for two developed connections between Harappan civilization and early Vedic or non-Vedic literature, with parallel research, imaginative theory and adversarial review.

## Questions and ownership

The institutions package asks which forms of social and technical organization appear in early texts, what mechanisms could have produced their sophistication, and which components can be traced through posturban archaeological communities. It compares local inheritance, Indo-Iranian inheritance, contact, convergence, genre selection and later urban development. Its author owns `institutions/`.

The memory package asks what an early text's account of an older world can preserve. It investigates specified cases of landscape, flood narrative and social origins, using Vedic and non-Vedic witnesses. It compares event memory, inherited motif, repeated experience, diffusion and retrospective social explanation. Its author owns `memory/`.

The independent reviewer owns `review/`, first constructing an independent chronology and source-layer audit, then checking author claims, sources, code and proposed discriminators. The coordinator owns integration, `coordinator/`, navigation and registry additions. Contributors preserve existing work and write only within their assigned new directories.

## Observations and interpretation

Every historical claim needs a source observation and an inspected locator. Source records state whether an entire paper, selected pages, a critical text locus, an abstract or a catalogue entry was read. Source dates, archaeological phase dates, composition estimates, redaction dates, manuscript dates and narrated time are separate fields. Date ranges remain estimates and alternatives remain visible.

The comparison will use concrete practices and sequences, rather than a single label such as complexity. A generic similarity provides a candidate question. A transmission argument additionally needs the proposed carrier, route, intervening observations and features that competing mechanisms would have difficulty producing. Common-source or copied witnesses are not independent archaeological observations.

Literary maturity cannot itself date a text or identify its ancestors. The surviving canon is a selected output of professional composition, performance and collection. The source census will examine those mechanisms alongside potential Harappan and regional contributions. Vedic and non-Vedic sources receive separate genre and chronological assessments.

## Developed theory and calculations

Each package will develop at least one explicit mechanism with rival explanations, consequences and feasible tests. A mechanism may be imaginative; its status must be clear. No hypothetical institution, ethnic identity, language assignment or narrative event will be presented as an archaeological observation.

The coordinator will construct a synthetic model with separate channels for retention of a procedural structure and retention of its historical attribution. Its purpose is to test whether structural sophistication mathematically requires accurate deep-event memory. Invented parameters describe scenarios; they are not estimates of ancient transmission, generations or elapsed time. The primary calculation will enumerate a finite state space, report exact transition results and show parameter sensitivity. Any later historical calibration would require independent data and a new protocol.

No numerical score made from selected source examples will be called a historical probability. Any machine-readable comparison will retain unknowns, source dependence and explicitly assumed links. A text or practice can fit several historical mechanisms; the final account will identify what could distinguish them.

## Endpoints and review

Primary endpoints are the two source-grounded reports, observation/source ledgers, reproducible calculations where appropriate, proposed discriminating tests, independent review and response, and a combined account. The program will preserve its original outputs before any post-result amendment. Reviews will inspect substantive source claims and alternative mechanisms as well as arithmetic.

New acquired third-party witnesses remain under ignored `data/raw/2026-10-11-early-indian-continuity/` unless source-specific redistribution rights are resolved. Own prose, code, bibliographic metadata and paraphrased observations may be tracked. The task performs no publication, commit, push, automation or deployment.

The coordinator will save exact starting bytes of navigation and other directly reused research documents, and hash the broader existing tracked/unignored inventory. These are different preservation scopes. Later shared-workspace revisions will be reported and accommodated without replacing another contributor's work.

## Writing and retrospective

Maximize reader comprehension. Explain each idea directly in connected prose and keep shared assumptions together. Avoid repeated corrective comparisons or warnings that interrupt the argument. Reports will use concrete observations, methods and outcomes. Retrospectives record externally observable approaches, challenges, repairs and outcomes.
