# Tests of selective institutional and technical transmission

These are prospective study designs. Their proposed patterns are hypotheses, not observations added to the current ledger. Exact sample sizes require access to collection inventories and preservation assessments; no arbitrary power claim is supplied here.

## 1. Separate a transmitted bead operation from a surviving bead

Use securely contextualized finished beads, broken production pieces, drills and manufacturing waste from Mature, Late and intermediate early Iron Age deposits in a bounded region. Pair local phases before comparing distant sites. Have analysts blinded to phase and the continuity hypothesis classify drill-hole geometry and microstructure, abrasives, perforation direction, heat-treatment order and material source. Register the operation bundle and exclusions before opening contextual labels. Record each assemblage's context, dating method and possible residuality.

The selective-transmission prediction is persistence of some locally supported operations with loss or modification of procurement-dependent ones. Rare combinations of operations, local waste and matching raw-material supply would strengthen a local lineage of practice. Persistence of finished objects alone can follow inheritance or exchange. Equivalent patterns in ecology/material-matched regions without a documented intermediate trajectory would strengthen convergence. Extensive technical replacement in locally manufactured intermediate assemblages would weaken the proposed carrier.

## 2. Follow a farming system through operations and crop occurrence

Select multiple independently dated contexts from Late Harappan and intermediate settlements in the same catchment, with matched deposit types and flotation/recovery protocols. Directly date short-lived crop remains where feasible. Classify grain and spikelet traits, associated weed ecologies, processing waste and seasonality before identifying a particular sequential or simultaneous crop regime. Retain all low-recovery and uncertain specimens in a declared sensitivity analysis rather than excluding the desired endpoint silently.

Persistent crop-management bundles across settlement change would support transmission through farming communities. Environmental matching is essential: similar seasonal water availability can generate similar choices independently. A specific textual connection requires words describing diagnostic operations in a properly stratified passage; a generic plough or cereal term is insufficient. The current Bahola PGW gap is a recovery target for this design, with local dating needed alongside better spikelet recovery.

## 3. Test inherited role vocabulary against later coordination

Build a bounded philological sample of independently assessed RV family cores, named additions, later RV and selected Brahmana prescriptions. Code role–action pairs, co-occurring responsibilities, patron/recipient relationships and explicit coordination of several specialists. Separate divine predication from human action, and prescriptions from narrative reports. Have a second coder review the Sanskrit/Avestan locus without seeing the proposed archaeological link.

Compare the role words and relevant formulae with Old Avestan cognates. If inherited names are stable but coordination becomes more extensive in later strata, the combination mechanism gains support: older specialist knowledge was reorganized. A demonstrated early diagnostic local operation absent from the inherited controls would provide a better candidate for local input. No difference after stratigraphic uncertainty and genre controls would weaken the claim of later reorganization in this selected sample. Named later staff should not be inferred merely by counting Agni's epithets.

## 4. Separate Buddhist institutional memory from didactic adaptation

For DN 2, DN 5 and DN 16, inspect independent Chinese, Sanskrit, Gāndhārī or other surviving parallels where available. Establish which occupational items, livelihood-support proposals and meeting rules are shared, changed or missing. Record recension relationships and the earliest physical witnesses independently of the narrated kings or Buddha-era frame. Only then compare the shared component with independently dated regional settlement and production evidence.

A stable unusual procedural bundle across independent early recensions could support older institutional memory. A rule expanded differently to suit monastic teaching, or confined to one later recension, would support adaptation. The DN 16 transfer from Vajji meetings to mendicants supplies an observed reason to test that alternative. The design should also compare unrelated group-coordination traditions: frequent harmonious meetings can be generated by ordinary institutional needs.
