# Component histories and early textual institutions: protocol, 11 October 2026

This study asks why the earliest surviving Indian textual traditions already describe skilled work, differentiated ritual roles, patronage and collective deliberation. The unit of inference is a particular practice or institutional component. A proposed connection between a Harappan archaeological observation and a later textual passage must state what could have been transmitted and what evidence would distinguish transmission from other origins.

The protocol was written before the new source observations and analytical outputs were assembled. This is an exploratory synthesis with a reproducible constraint audit, not a preregistered empirical test of cultural transmission. New refinements after source inspection will be recorded separately. Existing script and backward-chronology endpoints remain untouched.

## Source selection and observations

Select primary archaeological studies, philological research and inspectable text editions for five bundles: agriculture, craft skill, patronage, priestly specialization and assemblies. Include Rigvedic family books, later Rigvedic material, at least one Brahmana locus and early Buddhist or Jain material. Prefer approximately 12–18 sources and 20–30 observations with exact locators. Selection is purposive: neither source counts nor surviving mentions estimate the prevalence of an institution.

For each witness record its authors, title, edition or version, URL, access date, inspected sections, local path/hash when acquired and observed rights. Downloads and extracted source text remain in the local-only raw directory. Tracked files contain our paraphrases, bibliographic locators and brief justified excerpts only. An unavailable passage remains a recovery target; a search snippet alone cannot authenticate its contents.

Each observation records evidence type, locus, textual layer or archaeological context, geography, component, and the inference it permits. Composition, collection/redaction, extant manuscript witness and narrated time are separate fields. Dates supplied by an author are retained as that author's assessments. A family-book location does not itself authenticate an early stratum. A ritual or literary passage can attest a representation or named role without establishing the historical occurrence of the described event.

## Competing mechanisms and constraints

Evaluate selective transmission through posturban communities, shared Indo-Iranian inheritance, convergent technical/institutional development, textual genre selection and later institutional growth associated with the second urbanization. These mechanisms may coexist. Do not assign subjective likelihoods or aggregate evidence-strength scores.

The executable audit will check identifier/reference integrity, separate date axes, chronological direction, stratum qualifications, explicit controls and whether a transmission claim has an intermediate practice/context witness. An archaeological sequence can supply continuity of a component without identifying the speakers, ethnicity or institutional meaning of its users. A later textual representation cannot date an earlier archaeological institution by itself.

Report a categorical sensitivity table. Remove late Rigvedic and later textual observations; remove family-book passages with unresolved within-book stratigraphy; require an intermediate archaeological witness; and require a feature capable of separating local inheritance from Indo-Iranian or generic alternatives. These restrictions will expose which claims survive as observations, compatibility statements or candidate transmission tests. They do not manufacture a numerical probability that a civilization survived.

## Development beyond summary

Develop a selective-component hypothesis: portable craft/agricultural knowledge and specialist social roles may persist through dispersed households, apprenticeship and patronage while urban accounting, settlement hierarchy and monumental institutions change. Specify concrete joint observations that this mechanism predicts, what would weaken it, and at least three feasible tests with units, comparisons, outcomes and failure conditions. Coordinate with the separate oral-memory analysis; do not duplicate its synthetic model.
