# Preserved procedures and changing explanations

An older poem can survive within a more elaborate later story. RV 10.95 presents the Purūravas–Urvaśī dialogue. SB 11.5.1 places quoted dialogue within a prose account of a condition, stolen lambs, lightning and separation, then moves toward a prescription for producing sacrificial fire. These are inspectable relationships between texts. [Observations C05–C12](observations.json), [Sanskrit and teaching notes](https://vedisk.sites.ku.dk/rv-10-95-1-pururavas-og-urvasi/), [SB Sanskrit](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/1_veda/2_bra/satapath/sb_11_u.htm), [Eggeling's translation, §§1–17](https://www.sacred-texts.com/hin/sbr/sbe44/sbe44025.htm).

Jamison and Brereton assess the later background as a distortion or misunderstanding of crucial parts of the hymn. That is their philological judgment. The direct observation is narrower: a later explanatory frame coexists with older quoted words. Some background could already have circulated orally before either surviving account. A careful comparison must retain that possibility. [Introduction, printed p. 18](https://vedisk.sites.ku.dk/files/2021/09/13-22-Stephanie_W_Jamison_Joel_P_Brereton_The.pdf), [C04 and C10](observations.json).

The technical ending makes this example useful for a continuity study. SB 11.5.1.13–17 gives alternative configurations of upper and lower *araṇi*, using the names *aśvattha* and *śamī*, before prescribing one. This is a textually observed operation inside an origin story. Its antiquity, botanical identification and relation to archaeological implements each need their own evidence. The story's existence alone does not supply those joins. [C11–C12](observations.json).

## A proposed mechanism

Practices and explanations face different checks. A failed practical operation gives a practitioner feedback. A distant origin claim can persist without such feedback. Teachers may therefore preserve an operation while adapting its explanation to the needs of a new audience or ritual system. This mechanism is a working hypothesis. It also permits faithful historical attribution when there is an effective checking institution; it does not assume that oral knowledge is intrinsically unstable.

The proposed search unit is a linked operation: materials, sequence, division of roles and correction of failure. A common symbol or a single technique has many possible origins. A more specific operation bundle, independently recorded through successive communities, would constrain the transmission route. Reading an origin story could identify candidates for such a search. The archaeological bridge must then be assembled separately. Practical success can validate several workable variants, so feedback alone cannot certify the exact ancestry of a recipe.

For the fire-production example, a future study should freeze the Sanskrit recipe alternatives and competing botanical identifications before examining tool assemblages. Specialists would classify ancient wood and wear with text interpretation concealed, and test replicas under declared conditions. The replica outcome is fire production; the narrative's promise of becoming a Gandharva is a textual belief claim. Similar named ingredients in a later account can guide a test, but only dated intermediate operational observations could support a Harappan-to-later lineage. Later textual borrowing and common solutions remain competing routes.

## What the exact synthetic calculation establishes

The [model protocol](MODEL-PROTOCOL.md) specifies two invented binary channels: preservation of a procedure and preservation of its attribution to one of two invented origins. A procedure can flip to an incorrect form, then be repaired through an assumed check of the reference operation. An attribution can flip without repair. The channels are independent by construction. Here the check returns the reference procedure; a real study would have to test which variants its feedback can distinguish. The model does not generate literary sophistication or estimate real ancient transmission.

In the primary scenario, procedural error is 0.1, repair is 0.9, attribution drift is 0.1, and there are twenty invented transmission steps. After those steps, the procedure is correct with probability 0.9891304 and the attribution with probability 0.5057646. A correct procedure paired with an incorrect attribution has probability 0.4888633. Setting repair to zero reduces procedural correctness to 0.5057646. These are conditional mathematical outcomes, not percentages of historical traditions. [Exact endpoint](run-v1/retention.json), `primary` and `unprotected_reference`; [code](retention_model.py).

The calculation supplies a counterexample to the inference that preserved structural knowledge requires preserved historical attribution. Procedural observations alone have identical distributions under the two initial origins in this model. Attribution labels retain some discrimination: their total variation distance is 0.0115292 in the primary scenario, rather than zero. That distance applies only when the invented law is known. An independent historical anchor would be a different observation.

All 180 predeclared scenarios are retained. They include both stable and drifting attributions and both repaired and unrepaired procedures. Choosing strong procedural repair and attribution drift shows one possible mechanism; it supplies no evidence that those parameter values characterized Vedic, Buddhist, Jain or Harappan communities.
