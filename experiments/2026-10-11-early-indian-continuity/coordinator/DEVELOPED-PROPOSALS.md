# Two mechanisms and the observations that would distinguish them

Exploratory development after initial source inspection, 11 October 2026. These proposals extend familiar questions about selective cultural continuity, oral learning and textual redaction. They are research constructions; they do not establish a new historical lineage.

## 1. Knowledge can move through smaller communities

A complex civilization distributes knowledge across farmers, craftspeople, households, patrons and specialist teachers. The loss of a city network need not remove every one of these carriers. Some skills may remain useful in a village, while institutions that need distant trade partners, large administrations or extensive infrastructure change substantially. A later society can therefore inherit a mixture of old skills and newly organized roles.

The developed hypothesis concerns the conditions of survival. Practices with local feedback and modest coordination requirements should be easier to retain through settlement reorganization. Practices whose usefulness depends on many interchangeable partners should be more sensitive to a change in the network. This gives a prediction about differences between channels, instead of requiring every trait to survive together.

This relationship needs testing. Archaeological visibility is itself uneven: a crop residue, a stone tool and a formal office leave different traces. A disappearance from an inventory may reflect preservation or excavation. The first comparison should therefore use material observations whose recovery processes can be recorded.

For farming, a pilot would extract dated crop-processing contexts and code seasonality, crop association, weeds and processing stage. Site and deposit are separate observation units. Phase totals cannot identify household practice. Before reading the later texts, the material team would identify recurrent operation bundles and their temporal uncertainty. The textual team would separately code the operations described by each locus. The join would then assess whether a proposed bundle has intermediates and whether an Indo-Iranian, eastern regional or newly convergent route can also supply it. [Institutions protocol](../institutions/PROTOCOL.md).

For craft production, a parallel pilot could examine preparation sequence, tool choice, joining technique and repair, using independently dated deposits. The strongest candidates would be unusual linked choices, rather than an isolated tool or visual resemblance. Each proposed lineage would retain its least supported transition. A plausible route without an observed transition is a hypothesis for new collection.

Professional poetic and ritual traditions offer a different carrier. Teaching, performance and patronage can support elaborate knowledge even when architecture and settlement scale vary. Shared Indo-Iranian verbal and ritual traditions are an independent source that must be modeled alongside regional skills. The later combination may be productive in its own right; a component's old age does not imply that its final combination was already present in Harappan society. [Coordinator observations C01–C03](observations.json).

The hypothesis would be weakened if the putatively inherited rare bundles first appear much later, if intermediates use incompatible operation sequences, or if the same bundles are equally well explained by a regional control with no proposed Harappan link. It would gain support from dated intermediates, co-transmission of unusual practical choices and independently established contact routes. A vocabulary match alone cannot supply all three.

## 2. A society can reason about its origins while preserving different kinds of memory

An origin story can organize present institutions into a causal account: abundance, changed behavior, scarcity, rules, punishment and authority. Such an account can be intellectually sophisticated without being an event-by-event archive. It may combine inherited narrative, remembered experience and an author's explanation of social order. Its historical contents have to be assessed detail by detail.

The developed hypothesis is that regularly enacted relationships have a different memory pathway from distant labels and chronology. A practitioner can check an operation; a performer can check a verse against a teaching tradition; a community may reinterpret why the practice exists. These checks can support different levels of fidelity in the same surviving account. The [documentary control](CONTROL-AND-MODEL.md) and [synthetic calculation](run-v1/retention.json) make the distinction explicit.

A comparative pilot would create three independently coded layers for each case: exact verbal or material details, operational relationships, and historical attribution. It would preserve variants and parallel recensions, mark unknowns, and record when later prose explains older verse. Coders would initially receive the loci without a proposed Harappan interpretation. Date assessors would work from linguistic and archaeological arguments without a complexity score.

The discovery set could identify one rare operation bundle. A separate source or assemblage would test it under a frozen prediction. A shared flood, river or origin motif cannot be counted again as an independent discriminator after it selected the case. Multiple editions of the same text also stay in one dependency group. [Memory protocol](../memory/PROTOCOL.md).

The event-memory version predicts that details specific to the proposed old event travel together: the sequence, unusual geography, independently dated physical consequences and intermediate transmission. The retrospective-explanation version predicts that variants track later doctrinal or institutional concerns, even when older wording remains. A practical-memory version predicts stability in enacted steps despite changes in explanatory frame. These predictions can overlap; an informative observation must separate at least two specified routes.

Failure criteria are concrete. A proposed ancient-event join fails if its date comes only from the narrative being tested, if its supposed location is supplied by a later identification, or if all matching details are common to repeated ecological experience. A proposed operation lineage remains unresolved when its intervening evidence is absent. It should be withdrawn if newly recovered intermediates contradict the distinctive sequence. These decisions concern specific proposed links, not the survival of Indian culture as a whole.

## A joint implication

The two problems meet in teaching. Complex knowledge can survive, migrate and be recombined because people train one another in skills and ways of explaining the world. The earliest surviving text may present the result of that process with considerable maturity. The historical task is to recover the individual contributions and their routes. A sophisticated account can draw on several ancestral sources and later recombination, while preserving different amounts of information about each contribution.
