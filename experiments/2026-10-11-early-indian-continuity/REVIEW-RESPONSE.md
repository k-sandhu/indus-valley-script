## What remains to test

The institutional survival mechanism has not been fitted to a population of ancient practices. Recovery processes can imitate disappearance. A finished object can circulate without its manufacturing method; a shared role can arise in several institutional histories. The prospective designs therefore compare linked production operations, dated intermediates and matched regional or Indo-Iranian alternatives.

The procedural-memory mechanism must distinguish repeated performance from copying of a stable textual clause. It also needs comparable operational and attribution units; unequal granularity or a larger number of clauses cannot establish better retention. The [memory test plan](memory/TESTS.md) retains branch dependence, genre, repeated wording and unresolved variants.

The narrative-reset theory is explicitly post-audit and unmeasured. Its discovery examples cannot establish its frequency or function. A future comparison needs controls for genre, topic and length, plus variant evidence before interpreting any association as historical erasure. A remembered catastrophe and a later legitimating use can coexist. The [claim–evidence map](CLAIM-EVIDENCE.json), EI-C11, retains this status.

The frozen constructions establish results under their own laws. No step count, error rate, acquisition route or erasure frequency is calibrated to Vedic or Buddhist history. The known-law primary retention scenario also retains positive attribution discrimination; the reports do not describe its attribution as completely lost.

The joint thesis remains a working historical explanation: practical knowledge can be selectively inherited, and specialist traditions can combine it into mature institutions and accounts of origins. Particular Harappan contributions require new observations that separate those routes. The [review record](review/review-checks.json) pins inspected files and findings; the [final package checks](verification.json) cover file integrity and claim joins.
