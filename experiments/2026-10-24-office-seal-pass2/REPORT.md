## What the figures show

*Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions 1: Collections in India*, printed p. 277 / PDF 313, prints L-164 A and L-167 A, both obverses. Neither has a separately labelled underside or edge panel there. L-163 has A and C, including its narrow upper-side view. Printed p. 273 / PDF 309 adds L-146 A and B. Printed p. 279 / PDF 315 supplies L-174 A. The adjacent p. 278 / PDF 314 was also inspected; it contains other elephant-cohort objects but no additional selected target view. These catalogue pages have native image sizes of roughly 810 by 1,140 pixels. Enlarging them adds no contact detail.

Frenez and Tosi's *The Lothal Sealings: Records from an Indus Civilization Town at the Eastern End of the Maritime Trade Circuits across the Arabian Sea*, Figure 6, printed p. 101 / PDF 39, presents four photographs grouped under L-163, including its opposite face and two narrow profiles. The narrow views show a curved clay strip with projecting ends and transverse curved grooves. One profile exposes a lighter central strip between dark margins. We cannot trace a calibrated corner joining two polished planar contact faces from those pixels. The observations therefore locate the relevant contact surfaces without determining a box or stopper.

Figure 7, printed p. 102 / PDF 40, supplies paired views for L-146 and L-174. L-146's contact view has an elongated channel and fine transverse texture. L-174's contact view has a broad dark band and a lower curved margin. These are useful controls for composite and curved contacts; we did not derive a cylinder diameter or vessel-rim radius from them. Both figures include a common centimetre scale. Their embedded montages are 3,531 by 2,425 pixels and 2,425 by 3,531 pixels. Printed halftone structure, lighting and unknown local perspective limit geometric measurement even at that resolution.

The L-163 and related 2005 photographs belong to a source already used in the earlier physical-use studies. This pass adds a target-specific inventory and interpretation check. It does not claim that those photographs are newly published or globally unexamined. The original Rao drawing and plate inspection below supplies the main added source coverage relative to the first office-seal pass, which inspected prose and a catalogue data page.

## Rao's drawings and plates

Rao's *Lothal: A Harappan Port Town (1955–62)*, Volume II, Figure 38B, printed p. 320 / PDF 71, contains eleven numbered drawings, several paired with the opposite contact face. Elephant examples 6 and 7 depict linear marks and thick crossing bands on their paired backs. Number 6 resembles L-164's obverse in its outline, fractures and nail marks. We retain that as a probable visual candidate, because the inspected figure has no object-number key connecting panel 6 to excavation 1864. Its caption gives an intended scale of 1:1. These are drawings; their strokes cannot supply independent surface measurements.

Plates CLXI–CLXIV are PDF 491–494 in the held scan. Plate CLXI F shows the elephant obverse to which Rao's p. 319 description of excavation 1864 refers. The plate does not separately photograph its underside. Its caption gives scale 1:1. The adjacent cloth-and-reed example in panel E has two views, demonstrating that the plate can show contact faces when provided. The scan reduces many surfaces to dense black and white patches. It does not recover the missing diagnostic contact for L-164. We inspected the remaining three plates and did not find a securely registered additional underside view for either L-164 or L-167.

Plate location required one forty-page thumbnail sheet, PDF 490–529. PDF 529 was inspected at full size to read its plate number, CXCIX; that established the offset to CLXI at PDF 491. These thumbnails expose collateral artifacts and are logged as source exposure. They are excluded from target-view counts. Figure 2 in the 2005 chapter, printed p. 97 / PDF 35, locates the sealing discovery area on a plan, but supplies no individual object positions or revised inventory. Neither the plate audit nor the plan reconciles the earlier 65- and 69-sealing accounts.

## Better-image search and limits

Six web queries led to Frenez's 2007 poster, *Lothal Clay Sealings. Technological and functional studies*, and his 2024 article, *Were the Cretulae (Clay Sealings) from the Indus Port Town of Lothal Part of an Administrative Archive? Contextual, Interpretive, and Comparative Evidence*. The poster's public preview was acquired, but it is only 100 by 115 pixels. Its full PDF route returned HTTP 403. The publisher PDF and author-deposit page for the 2024 article also returned HTTP 403 through ordinary requests. Readable web text confirms that its Figure 9 on p. 225 includes L-163, but those pixels were not acquired. The article reuses the author's documentation of the same excavation assemblage; it supplies no independent excavation event.

The source ledger records three selected objects with registered non-obverse photographs in the audited sources: L-163, L-146 and L-174. L-164 and L-167 remain without such photographs in this block. This is bounded publication coverage, not a claim that no archive photograph exists. Failed responses, source images, drawing candidates and detailed records remain local. No M-895 imagery, confirmation cohort, new functional reading or spent statistic was opened.

The strongest supported reading is that the elephant sealings preserve complex cord contacts whose full geometry is unevenly published. L-164's tentative box and jar-stopper interpretations remain distinguishable hypotheses. The deciding evidence is a registered, calibrated underside and continuous edge series for L-164, compared with the complete contact surfaces of L-163 and L-167. A visible vessel-rim fit or a continuous junction between planar wooden faces would resolve more than another reading of the existing obverse.
