# Shot A exploration: what the discovery half says about rank on seals

12 October 2026. Discovery half only; the confirmation half was not read. Everything here is exploratory, and the conjecture was revised twice after seeing these results. The frozen tests are in [PREDICTIONS.md](PREDICTIONS.md).

The discovery half supports a much narrower claim than the one the shot started with. Closing signs do carry seal size beyond text length, type and site, but the whole effect comes from four rare last signs, 001, 595, 679 and 820. The eighteen seals whose legends end in one of them have faces about twice the area expected for their type, site and length, and sixteen of the eighteen are larger than expected. Remove those four signs and the remaining closing signs, including the jar sign 740 and the X-740 formulas, carry no size information. Seals with identical legends are no closer in size than matched seals. Opening signs are somewhat more local than closing formulae, but the difference is within chance. The strongest size signal in the material is in the image: unicorn seals coded with the shoulder-band variant are much larger, and that ornament appears more often as the face grows, which fits carving room better than rank.

## Population and size model (E1, E2)

The discovery half holds 1,105 seal families. One face per family is used, the face with the most sign positions. The analysis population is every square (`SEAL:S`) or rectangular (`SEAL:R`) seal with both face dimensions, preservation complete, slightly chipped or chipped, and a complete single-line text of at least two signs without unknown signs: 640 seals in 590 components, 483 of them square ([population.json](discovery/population.json)). Most analyses keep one seal per component, so a legend that recurs on several seals counts once. Dimensions came from inch values for 460 of the 590 seals and directly in millimetres for 130; the inch values move in steps of 1.27 mm, which heaps the histogram but is far finer than the size differences studied here.

Size is the log of face area, ln(h) + ln(v). An additive model with seal type, site class (six named sites and "other") and text length class explains 33.5% of its variance, leaving a residual SD of 0.487 in log area, about 24% in side length ([base-model.json](discovery/base-model.json)). Square seals average 23.2 mm on a side with two signs and 36.2 mm with nine. Every permutation test below moves labels only within cells of type × site class × length class, so it compares seals that the base model already treats alike.

## Closing and opening units against residual size (E3, E4)

The test statistic is the between-unit sum of squares of residual size, Σ n·mean², over units with at least a minimum number of seals, divided by its mean under permutation (4,999 draws). A ratio of 1 means the unit carries no size information ([unit-size-tests.json](discovery/unit-size-tests.json)).

| Unit | Units with ≥ 3 seals | Ratio, ≥ 3 | p | Ratio, ≥ 5 | p | Ratio, ≥ 8 | p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Last sign | 28 | 2.14 | 0.001 | 2.31 | 0.005 | 1.13 | 0.34 |
| Closing formula (X-740 or last sign) | 55 | 1.56 | 0.004 | 2.01 | 0.002 | 1.43 | 0.12 |
| Last two signs | 47 | 1.20 | 0.15 | 1.43 | 0.08 | 1.27 | 0.24 |
| First sign | 46 | 1.15 | 0.23 | 1.41 | 0.09 | 1.57 | 0.09 |
| First two signs | 15 | 1.31 | 0.19 | 1.30 | 0.24 | 1.00 | 0.41 |

The last sign is the best closing unit, and its signal disappears once only common signs (eight seals or more) are kept, so it lives in the rare closers. The first version of the conjecture after this step (v1) kept an ordinal grade lexicon: in the score table ([unit-tables.json](discovery/unit-tables.json), `tables.last`), 595, 820, 001 and 679 sit far above the rest (mean residuals +0.53 to +0.82), and 002, 407, 590, 156, 154 and 400 sit below (−0.14 to −0.75), with 740 (227 seals) at +0.01. The score is the sum of residuals divided by n + 3, a shrinkage that pulls units with few seals toward zero; the four high signs score 0.30 to 0.44 and the next sign 0.13.

Matched against the first sign with whole texts permuted together, the last sign's ratio exceeds the first sign's by 0.98 (2.13 against 1.15, p = 0.005). The closing formula against the first sign gives 0.41 (p = 0.07), and the last two signs against the first two give −0.12 (p = 0.63) (`closing_minus_opening`). Five-fold cross-validation of the shrunken scores, repeated 20 times, gives out-of-sample correlations with residual size of 0.114 for the last sign, 0.120 for the closing formula, 0.091 for the last two signs, 0.057 for the first sign and 0.029 for the first two signs ([cross-validation.json](discovery/cross-validation.json), k = 3). Closing units predict size better than opening units, but at about 1% of residual variance.

## Where the closing signal sits (E5, E7, E13)

Scanning every sign on at least five seals by position ([sign-zone-scan.json](discovery/sign-zone-scan.json)), 595 has the highest mean residual of 92 signs (+0.45, z = 3.3), and the effect is positional: +0.70 on the nine seals where it is one of the last two signs, −0.12 on the four where it is medial. 820 is a common opening sign with residual −0.08 on 44 seals, but +0.39 on the eight where it closes. 001 is −0.25 in medial position and +0.37 closing. The high closers occur in recurring pairs: 740-679 five times, 368-001 three times, and 595-820, 820-595 and 420-595 twice each ([high-closers.json](discovery/high-closers.json), `closing_pairs_of_high_seals`). Of the 23 seal faces in the discovery half with a complete legend ending in one of the four signs, none has a legend that, without its last sign, occurs as a complete text anywhere in the half (`legend_minus_last_sign_found_as_complete_text`).

The split-half rehearsal ([power_split.py](power_split.py), [power-split.json](discovery/power-split.json)) divided the discovery seals 300 times at random, built the score table on one half and tested on the other. The low-scoring signs did not hold up: seals ending in signs scored −0.10 or lower on the training half averaged −0.03 on the test half, against 0.00 for the rest. The high-scoring signs did. Whenever a training half produced a sign scoring 0.25 or more, it was one of the same four (595 in 141 of 300 splits, 820 in 108, 679 in 64, 001 in 37; the only other sign, 527, in 4), and the seals ending in the training half's high signs averaged +0.36 on the test half. This was the second revision (v2): the ordinal lexicon collapsed into one marked upper grade and an unmarked remainder. Removing the four signs leaves the last sign with a ratio of 1.09 (p = 0.35), the closing formula with 1.01 (p = 0.46), and the X-740 formulas alone with 0.93 (p = 0.58) (`last_without_high_set`, `formula_without_high_set`, `x740_formulas_only`).

On the full discovery population the eighteen seals ending in the set have mean residual +0.66 against −0.02 for the other 572, a difference of 0.68 in log area (face area × 1.98), p = 0.0002 under the stratified permutation (E13). Sixteen of the eighteen are above zero. Dropping any one of the four signs leaves differences of 0.65 to 0.72 (p ≤ 0.0005); square seals alone give 0.69 and Mohenjo-daro alone 0.81. The largest residual belongs to M-1203 (68.6 mm, four signs); without it the mean is about +0.59. The exceptions are H-80, a seven-sign Gaur seal of 19.1 mm ending 820-595, and a five-sign Dholavira seal of 21.0 mm ending in 820. Three fragments with complete legends ending in the set, H-271, M-1228 and the rectangular M-367, measure at least 45.7, 38.1 and 31.2 mm on their surviving long side. At Mohenjo-daro the high seals come from the DK, HR, SD and VS areas, and their catalogue periods run from 3B-2 to 4, so they do not trace one excavation area or level. On non-seal objects the set closes only four texts (two tablets, two pots).

The rehearsal also shows how little the rest of the score table carries. With half-size halves, the full score table passed its own frozen test (rearrangement calibration, P1b) in 5% of splits, no better than chance, because test halves held a median of two high-set seals. A simulation on resampled discovery seals ([power_sim.py](power_sim.py), [power-sim.json](discovery/power-sim.json)) sets the confirmation expectations: if the high set's true effect is +0.36, P4 passes 82% of the time, P1b 51%, P1a 27% and P1c 25%; with no effect each passes 2–5% of the time.

## Identical legends (E8)

Thirty legends recur on two or more seals of the population, 80 seals and 77 pairs. Their mean absolute size difference is 0.568 in log area against 0.592 for pairs in which one member is replaced by a seal of the same type, length and site class from another component (p = 0.31) ([identical-legends.json](discovery/identical-legends.json)). Same-site and different-site duplicate pairs differ by the same amount (0.561 and 0.562). Some duplicates match closely, such as M-1697 and M-712 (31.8 × 31.8 and 31.8 × 33 mm), and others do not, such as the 003-390 legend on seals of 12.7 to 40.0 mm. The Kalibangan sealing K-82, which carries the M-1697/M-712 legend, is catalogued at 23 × 23 mm (`sealing_K82_and_matching_seals`); if that measures the impression and not the clay lump, it was made by a smaller seal with the same legend. The fourth sentence of the original conjecture, that a shared legend fixes the size, fails on the discovery half and was dropped in v1.

## Site specificity (E9)

Mutual information between a unit and the site, measured as a z score against site labels shuffled within seal type (1,999 shuffles), puts each unit against the chance level set by its own frequency spectrum ([site-specificity.json](discovery/site-specificity.json)). Among 652 seals, first signs are site-specific (z = 2.54, p = 0.011), closing formulae are not (z = 0.88), and last signs are in between (z = 1.43). The difference between first sign and closing formula is 1.66 (p = 0.11); first against last gives 1.09 (p = 0.21), and first two against last two signs 1.38 (p = 0.15). Two other statistics were tried first and disagree with each other (`same_site_pair_shares`). The same-site share of all pairs of seals sharing a unit ranks first signs (ratio to chance 1.13, p = 0.012) and last signs (1.10, p = 0.017) above closing formulae (0.95); averaged per unit instead, closing formulae lead (1.14, p = 0.029) and first signs show nothing (1.02). The pooled share is dominated by the 227 seals ending in 740 and the per-unit average by units with two or three seals, so the frozen test uses mutual information. The direction matches the conjecture and the earlier seal-text identity result; the size does not establish it.

## Image and size (E10)

Iconography carries more size information than the legend ([iconography.json](discovery/iconography.json)). Among unicorn seals, the catalogue variant `Bull1:W` averages +0.26 against −0.14 for the other variants (144 and 238 seals, p = 0.0002), and the cult-object codes ending in F average +0.26 against −0.08 for those ending in N (83 and 273 seals, p = 0.0002). The export has no codebook for these suffixes. In the photographs inspected, the W seals (M-1, M-4, M-12, M-629, M-1203) show a broad double-lined loop hanging over the unicorn's shoulder; the S seals M-42 and M-136 have a narrow band or fold line there, and the J seal M-38 has striations on the neck. The suffixes appear to code how the body is carved. The W share rises steadily with face size, from 3% below 18 mm to 95% above 40 mm. At fixed size (24–32 mm) the W share falls from about 0.5 on two-sign seals to 0.19 on seven-sign seals, but the logistic length coefficient (−0.07 per sign) is not distinguishable from chance (p = 0.17). An intermediate version of the claim, that rank is written in the ornament as well as the size, was considered and set aside: a smooth rise of ornament with face area is what carving room alone would produce. Zebu and rhinoceros seals are larger than their length predicts (+0.29 each, 15 and 11 seals) and Gaur seals smaller (−0.15, 25 seals); the animal factor gives ratio 2.52, p = 0.025. Boss type carries nothing (ratio 1.13, p = 0.34). The unicorn-variant contrast is frozen as auxiliary prediction P5. Adding the image codes to the base model raises its R² from 0.335 to 0.445 and leaves the closing signal in place: the last sign's ratio is 2.04 (p = 0.0008) and the high set's contrast +0.53 (p = 0.0002) (`last_sign_after_image_adjustment`). The legend and the image carry separate size information. This adjustment is not used in the frozen tests, because ornament that follows face size is partly an outcome of size.

## Dead ends (E11, E12)

Discrete size grades do not appear. A Gaussian mixture on residual log side and on raw log size of square seals prefers one component by BIC (21.4 for one component against 44.4 for two; 130.3 against 150.0) ([size-grades.json](discovery/size-grades.json)), and the 2 mm histograms are unimodal apart from heaping at 25.4 mm (one inch) and at whole millimetres. The largest stroke numeral in a legend does not track size (Spearman 0.05, p = 0.33). Neither the final-position propensity of the last sign (−0.05, p = 0.18) nor its initial-position propensity (0.05, p = 0.22) predicts size, although the 35 seals whose last sign usually opens texts average +0.25 ([other-checks.json](discovery/other-checks.json)). At Mohenjo-daro, seals from the VS, SD and HR areas are larger than those from DK (+0.25, +0.13, +0.12 against −0.04); these areas were excavated in different campaigns and published in different reports, so the contrast was not pursued.

## Revisions of the conjecture

The starting claim (v0, [SHOT.md](SHOT.md)) had graded offices, size marking grade, closing signs naming it, opening signs individualising the holder and varying by city, and identical legends cut to the same size. After E3, E4 and E8, v1 kept the closing-sign grade as an ordinal lexicon with high and low closers and dropped the identical-legend sentence. After E7 and E13, v2 reduced the lexicon to a single marked upper grade written with 001, 595, 679 or 820, often in recurring closing pairs, and stated that the other closers mark nothing. The opening-sign sentence stays as a weaker part of the claim. The ornament version was considered between v1 and v2 and set aside.

The main rival to v2 is layout. A large face leaves room at the end of the text band, and a carver or the owner may add a sign there that has nothing to do with grade; the catalogue then records it as the last sign. The discovery data cannot separate the two readings, because both predict the same association. Prospective prediction 3 in [PREDICTIONS.md](PREDICTIONS.md) is a photographic check of where these signs sit on the face. Selection is the other concern: the four signs were picked from 28 after looking. The split-half rehearsal guards against a single lucky seal, not against the possibility that the whole discovery half is unusual, which only the confirmation half can address.

The starting credence was 0.25. The general form of the claim did badly here, the identical-legend sentence failed, and the surviving pattern rests on eighteen seals with a layout explanation available, so the credence entering confirmation is 0.12.
