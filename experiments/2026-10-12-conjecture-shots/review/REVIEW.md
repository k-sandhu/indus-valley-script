# Adversarial review of conjecture shots A and B

13 October 2026. Independent reviewer (Claude Opus 5.5 subagent) who took no part in either shot. The review ran after both halves were opened. Every number below that does not come from a shot's own files was computed for this review and is an exploratory review analysis, not a frozen test. Scripts and outputs are in this directory; output paths below are relative to it. The discovery half was loaded with `load_half("discovery")`, which does not log. The confirmation half was read directly from the pinned faces file and `split.json` after their hashes were checked against `split-summary.json`, so both shots' access logs are unchanged (Shot A 4 lines, Shot B 2). Files that list full legends are in `data/raw/2026-10-12-conjecture-shots/review/`.

The freeze records of both shots check out. All six hashes in Shot A's `PREDICTIONS.sha256` and all three in Shot B's match the current files. Shot B wrote `PREDICTIONS.md` at 03:47:58 UTC and its hash file at 03:48:20, read the confirmation half at 03:48:28 and wrote `results.json` at 03:48:32. The coordinator amendment is dated 03:54:23. Shot A wrote `PREDICTIONS.md` at 04:00:02 and its hash file at 04:00:09, read the half at 04:00:13 and wrote `results.json` at 04:00:41.

The confirmation half could not have reached Shot A's choice of the four signs through the split ([shot-a-leakage.json](shot-a-leakage.json)). None of the 11 confirmation high-set legends occurs in the discovery half, no CISI base number occurs in both halves, and no discovery seal from the same site has the same face dimensions and the same last sign as a confirmation high-set seal.

## High severity

**H1. Shot A's P4 pass depends on three seals ending in 820.** On the discovery half the contrast is stable: every variant tried there gives p ≤ 0.0006, and dropping the three largest high-set residuals still leaves a difference of +0.518. On the confirmation half it does not hold up. The reproduced frozen statistic is 0.353 with p = 0.0196 ([shot-a-robustness.json](shot-a-robustness.json), `confirmation.variants`). Dropping M-6 gives p = 0.138, dropping M-16 p = 0.071 and dropping M-649 p = 0.066, while dropping any of the other eight seals gives p between 0.003 and 0.028. Removing the top one, two or three high-set residuals gives p = 0.143, 0.364 and 0.692. Without 820, the remaining six seals give +0.122, p = 0.40. A rank version of the same permutation gives p = 0.087 to 0.093 ([shot-a-rank-checks.json](shot-a-rank-checks.json)). Excluding dimensions rounded to 0.1 inch gives p = 0.074, excluding seals with h = v (5 high-set seals left) p = 0.051, and excluding non-steatite and blank-material seals p = 0.056.

The pass survives complete seals only (p = 0.011), the longer side (p = 0.023), the shorter side (p = 0.030) or untransformed area (p = 0.018) as the size measure, exact-length factors (p = 0.015), linear length (p = 0.006) or quadratic length (p = 0.008), Mohenjo-daro only (p = 0.029), and all seals instead of one per component (p = 0.018).

The three seals that carry the result are unusual objects. M-649 is catalogued as silver; the only other silver seal in either size population is M-1199 (discovery), which ends in 740 and has residual −0.73. M-6 measures 58.4 mm, the fourth-largest face among 582 confirmation seals, with the third-largest residual. M-16 ranks 13th and M-649 14th by residual; the other two confirmation 820 seals rank 262nd and 563rd. Across both halves, six of the ten 820-closing seals rank in the top 25 residuals of their half and four rank 80th, 262nd, 441st and 563rd. The pattern is a few very large seals, not a uniform shift.

Recommended fix: keep the frozen verdict and report it with these influence results. Before any new test, freeze an influence rule such as a rank-based statistic or a leave-one-out criterion. Check M-6, M-16 and M-649 photographically first.

**H2. The credence update double-counts, and C-0001 still asserts sentences the confirmation half contradicted.** Shot A's report says the passes of P1a and P4 count as one confirmation of one pattern, yet the credence adds +0.10 for P4 and +0.03 for P1a. P1a's signal does not support the claim: it comes mainly from signs outside the high set (220, 400, 368); last signs without the high set still give ratio 1.62, p = 0.027; and first signs carry as much as last signs (ratio 1.67, p = 0.002), while the penultimate, second and a random sign of each text give 0.94, 1.08 and 1.17 (`p1a_specificity`). On this half both ends of the legend carry size information, which contradicts the claim that no other closing sign marks size. A Holm correction across the six scored predictions keeps P1a (0.0074 × 6 = 0.044) and drops P4 (0.0209 × 5 = 0.105).

Recommended fix: record 0.21 as the frozen-rule value and add a dated review credence of 0.13 for the four-sign claim: 0.12, plus 0.05 for P4 at half weight because it depends on three seals, plus nothing for P1a, minus 0.02, 0.01 and 0.01 for P1b, P1c and P3. Rewrite the claim to its surviving form and move the frozen wording into the record.

**H3. Shot B's "dead" is the correct frozen consequence, but the evidence supports only "this map fails this test".** The within-band null is not degenerate. For P1, 85 of 89 eligible signs sit in 11 mixed cells; for P2, 62 of 86 sit in 8 mixed cells, including all 19 official signs. The twelve band cells explain 11.7% of the variance in per-sign site effects and 19.3% in size effects ([shot-b-review.json](shot-b-review.json)).

The weakness is the instrument. Per-sign site concentration correlates at r = −0.092 between halves (Spearman 0.022, 83 signs); size effects correlate at 0.458 ([shot-b-reliability.json](shot-b-reliability.json)), so P1 can detect only very large effects. The frozen code run on the discovery half with real withheld fields, which Shot B never loaded, gives S = 1.256, +0.88 SD, 80th percentile: a fail, but without meeting the kill condition. Planting 1.2× face area on legends with an official sign in real discovery data gives z₂ = 1.43 and S at the 94.75th percentile, a fail; 1.5× is needed to pass, at the 99.05th percentile ([shot-b-discovery-runs/](shot-b-discovery-runs/)). Shot B's own planted run used 1.56× area and added 0.6 site probability to name-bearing families, so its sentence that the test had power to detect the effects it was looking for overstates what that run shows. The kill rule (distance ≤ 0) fires for half of uninformative maps and, by a normal approximation, for about one map in five whose expected z₂ is near +1.

Recommended fix: amend the power sentence; add a review note to C-0002 giving both halves' distances (−0.44 and +0.88 SD) and the P1 reliability; state that the grammar and glosses were not tested.

**H4. Shot B's "boldest reading", a shared name stock with city-specific titles and determinatives, has no support.** Against Shot B's own within-band null, TITLE+DET minus NAME gives z = 1.67, p = 0.057, on the half where it was found. The pattern weakens or reverses everywhere else: confirmation without Mohenjo-daro z = 1.09; discovery z = −1.02; discovery without Mohenjo-daro z = −0.61; discovery, Mohenjo-daro against the rest, z = −1.22. On discovery, NAME has the highest class mean (0.034) and DET the lowest (−0.031). The most concentrated confirmation signs are not concentrated on discovery: 013 goes from 0.474 to −0.102, 621 from 0.248 to −0.072, and 923 from 0.228 to 0.027. This pattern and Shot A's P3 reversal are one observation, closing formulae concentrated at Mohenjo-daro on the same seals of the same half, and must not be counted as two.

Recommended fix: keep the pattern out of the working model. If it is kept as a prospective conjecture, give it a low credence and record the discovery-half reversal.

## Medium severity

**M1. P3 measures sampling noise dominated by Mohenjo-daro.** Splitting the pooled components into random halves 100 times, the P3 z difference has a standard deviation of 1.06 per half, and the two halves disagree in sign in 73% of splits; the pooled value is −0.98 (p = 0.75). Collapsing sites to Mohenjo-daro against the rest gives the most extreme values, +2.03 on discovery and −3.32 on confirmation; without Mohenjo-daro both halves are near zero (−1.32 and +0.16). The observed gap between the halves (1.65 against −2.89) is about three standard deviations of a paired difference, so this split happened to be unusually discordant ([shot-a-p3-p5.json](shot-a-p3-p5.json)). Recommended fix: drop the locality sentences from both shots; future site tests exclude Mohenjo-daro or stratify by site pair, and estimate the variance across re-splits before freezing.

**M2. The export partly informs the layout and direction rivals** ([shot-a-rivals.json](shot-a-rivals.json)). Layout: the penultimate signs of high-set legends are typical closers more often than for other seals (mean closing share 0.545 against 0.350, p = 0.001), and the penultimate sign is 740 on 7 of 29 high-set seals against 5.9% of others, the form of a complete legend plus one sign. All seven are 679 seals, and 679 did not repeat on confirmation; the 820 seals that carry P4 end in 880-820, 130-820 and 571-820, which do not have this form. Direction: the first signs of high-set legends are as opener-like as the rest (0.837 against 0.849, p = 0.31), and the direction field reads R/L on 28 of 29. For M-6, M-16 and M-649 the first signs are too rare to judge (244, 171 and 571 open 1, 3 and 1 seal texts). M-6's last two signs, 880 and 820, both otherwise open legends: 880 opens 5 and closes none, and 820 opens 99 and closes 11 under the review's counting rule. Workshop, excavation area and period do not remove P4: Mohenjo-daro excavation area +0.367, p = 0.016 (p = 0.008 with area in the strata); period p = 0.025; animal and unicorn variant +0.314, p = 0.034; all together p = 0.031. All nine confirmation high-set seals from Mohenjo-daro come from the DK area, against 278 of 359 for all Mohenjo-daro seals. Recommended fix: freeze the photographic protocol for prospective prediction 3 now, and check M-6 first, recording direction, spacing and whether 820 sits in the text band.

**M3. Shot B's leakage record is wrong about `class`, and the CISI identifiers were not stripped.** `class` is a text classification, not an animal code: UC occurs on 279 tablets and 145 pots as well as 189 seals, UC seals have a unicorn share of 0.339 against 0.554 for all seals, and the codes 2L and 3L mark two-line and three-line texts. Stripping it was harmless. `source_cisi` and `metadata.cisi`, which carry site prefixes, stayed in every row; no construction script uses them, and any leak could only have helped a pass. Recommended fix: correct the record in a dated amendment, and strip CISI identifiers and record-order proxies in future shots that withhold site.

**M4. Shot B's size units include damaged seals.** Of 925 sized confirmation seal faces, 114 are fragments and 49 partly damaged ([shot-b-reliability.json](shot-b-reliability.json)), and the size model has no site term. Both weaken P2.

**M5. Process.** The coordinator amendment kept Shot A's P3 unchanged and fair. Shot A's discovery outputs were regenerated between 03:55:54 and 03:57:30 UTC, after Shot B's outcome; nothing in Shot A's files shows that its agent saw that outcome, and P3 was not altered. The confirmation half is now spent for seal size, site and motif statistics. For later shots: freeze all shots before any read; write joint credence rules for predictions that share seals; build an influence or rank criterion into pass rules for small groups; and, before freezing, measure the split-half reliability of every per-sign measure on discovery and plant effects of plausible size.

## Low severity

**L1.** The quoted P4 effect is not centred on its null. The confirmation null mean is +0.056, so the matched excess is 0.297 (×1.35 area), not ×1.42. On discovery it is 0.682 − 0.076.

**L2.** The 820 sentence in Shot A's report overstates the sites: it cites Harappa, Mohenjo-daro and Dholavira, but the Dholavira seal sits below expectation (−0.30, rank 441 of 590).

**L3.** A few identical legends cross the halves outside the high set. Three incomplete discovery seal faces share exact legends with confirmation seals C-2, M-1798 and M-1908, and 14 confirmation legends recur on discovery tablets, pots or tags. This touches P2 marginally.

**L4.** P5's W code does not look like a catalogue artefact in the fields available. W against other coded variants is +0.385 on discovery and +0.379 on confirmation, both p = 0.0002, and holds within Fine or Good condition. The W share does not depend on dimension source (inch 0.373, mm 0.361), preservation (0.366 to 0.436) or Mohenjo-daro CISI range (0.345 to 0.351). A logistic fit gives a coefficient of 4.58 (SE 0.45) on ln size against 0.14 (SE 0.17) on condition, and the W share rises from 0.13 to 0.71 across size quartiles. Carving room and coder visibility cannot be separated without photographs.

## What the results support

**Shot A.** A frozen, uncontaminated test found larger seals among legends ending in the four signs on the confirmation half. That excess comes from three very large 820 seals, one of them silver. The result supports "a legend closing in 820 marks a larger seal" as the claim to test next, and a review credence of 0.13 for the four-sign version. It does not support the four-sign set as a grade marker, the locality sentences, or the claim that no other closing sign carries size.

**Shot B.** The map's class contrasts do not predict site or size beyond frequency and position on either half (−0.44 and +0.88 SD), and the site measure cannot carry a test. The result cannot show that the grammar, the glosses or the class structure are wrong in general. The reading in which names are shared and titles are local has no support.
