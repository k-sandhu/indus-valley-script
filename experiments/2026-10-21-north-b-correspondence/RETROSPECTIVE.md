# Retrospective: northern *b-* against Tamil *m-* and *v-*

## Approach

We ran prediction P5 of C-0011 on 21 October 2026 (UTC) in a side session started from the coordinating session of the Parpola studies. The protocol fixed the population, the unit (one northern language in one entry part), the handling of doubtful and borrowed forms, the vowel and nasal record, the secondary counts and the pass rule, and was hashed at 18:41 before any entry was read for the study. We reused the 514 DSAL pages that REBUS-BASE-RATE-2026-10-21 had fetched that morning, checking each against its logged hash, and wrote our own parser. A label-by-label comparison of the flattened source with the parsed lines found four kinds of parse error and one numbering error in the edition, which we corrected and recorded in [PARSE-CHECK.md](PARSE-CHECK.md) before freezing the code at 18:57. The frozen code computed the counts once. A subagent then wrote a second implementation from the protocol and the parse corrections without seeing our code or results.

## Challenges and repairs

**DSAL's markup is not DEDR's structure.** The protocol defined a language line as starting at an italic label, which is how DSAL usually sets one. The boundary check showed that DSAL sets some genuine labels in plain type (a Malto line in entry 392 was attached to Kurukh), italicises some labels inside cross-references ("Cf. Ta. …"), and uses "R. Br." for the botanist Robert Brown where the parser saw Brahui. We added a mechanical rule for plain labels: at the end of a plain run, followed by a bold form, after the end of a gloss, outside parentheses, after no cue word, and later in DEDR's order of languages than the current line. Its first version lacked the order condition and accepted 23 Tamil citations; adding the condition left six genuine lines, none in the population. A rule excluding italic labels inside parentheses was tried and dropped because unbalanced parentheses would have removed four genuine lines. A protocol that defines lines by typography should also say what to do when the typography fails.

**One number, two entries.** The protocol assumed that a repeated entry number is one entry printed more than once, with the shorter print a prefix of the longer. For four numbers it is not: under 5282 DSAL prints an entry headed by Tamil *vali* and another headed by *viku*, so the longest-print rule would have dropped a Tamil *v-* entry. Clustering prints by the prefix relation and keeping each cluster as its own entry fixed it.

**A borrowing rule that matched a meaning.** The first draft marked as borrowed any line whose text contained "borrow", which caught a Malto verb glossed "to borrow". We narrowed it to the protocol's own words, "borrowed" and "loan"; two false positives remain, both outside the population, and the report names them.

**Our check had its own defect.** In 23 entries the regular-expression tag stripper of the boundary check counted fewer labels than the parser because DEDR's borrowing sign "<" stands unescaped in the HTML. The check, not the parser, was wrong; we recorded it rather than changing the check after seeing it.

**The second implementation found a defect the boundary check could not.** Entry 4886 has an unclosed bold tag before its Kurukh line, so our parser read the preceding gloss and its "?" as a form, and the doubt mark DEDR puts before the Kurukh label was lost. Our boundary check counted labels, not doubt marks, and so missed it. The subagent's parser, written from the protocol without our code, read the mark. The defect moves one Kurukh unit from "other" to "only doubtful forms" and changes no *b-* or *m-* count. We left the frozen output unchanged and reported the corrected cell. A future check should compare doubt flags between parsers as well as labels.

**The recorded time was wrong.** The protocol said it was recorded "at about 18:45"; the hash was taken at 18:40. We corrected the sentence and re-hashed seven seconds later, before reading any entry, and the report says so.

## Outcome

P5 is unresolved under its frozen rule: one entry outside 4876, "crocodile" (4952), has Kurukh and Malto *b-* for Tamil *m-*, and the rule needs two. The correspondence is otherwise regular: Tamil *m-* to northern *m-* in 83 of 88 comparisons and in all six before a long front vowel, Tamil *v-* to northern *b-* in 50 of 55. Malto separates *mínu* "fish" from *bínḍke* "star" by the initial. We proposed lowering C-0011 from 0.20 to 0.15 and adding a prediction, P6, that the northern star words are derivatives of the sky word **wiṇ-*. That prediction can be tested in Malto and Kurukh grammars and dictionaries independent of DEDR.
