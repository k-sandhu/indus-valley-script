# Parse check and parse corrections

Written on 21 October 2026 after step 3 of the [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) and before the code was frozen and any count was computed. The check compares, entry by entry, the Tamil, Kurukh, Malto and Brahui labels found in the flattened source text with the language lines the parser produced; its output is [parse-check/check-v1.json](parse-check/check-v1.json), and the context snippets we read stay in the local raw directory. We looked at mismatches to find parse errors. Five corrections followed. Each changes how the parser recognises DEDR's structure in the DSAL markup; none changes a counting rule, a class definition or the pass rule.

## What the check found

DSAL normally sets a language label in italics. In the 5,623 entry numbers of the edition, 806 Kurukh, 732 Malto, 277 Brahui and 3,626 Tamil labels are italic. Every italic label of the four languages produced a line except one Tamil label written "Ta:" with a colon (entry 3512, headed by Toda, outside the population) and the ten citations described below. The other occurrences of the abbreviations are in plain or bold type, and almost all of them are cross-references ("Cf. 5053 Kur. …"), source notes ("For Kur. and Malt., Pfeiffer") or comparisons after DEDR's " / ". Reading them showed four kinds of parse error and one numbering error in the edition.

## Corrections

1. **Lines set in plain type.** DSAL failed to italicise a few genuine labels, so the parser attached their forms to the preceding language: in entry 392 a Malto form followed the Kurukh line. A label in plain type now starts a line when it stands at the end of a plain run, the next run is bold, not italic, and holds a form, the text before it ends a gloss (with ".", ";" or ")"), it is outside parentheses, it does not follow a cue word (cf., esp., see, s.v., viz., i.e., e.g., also, "="), and its language comes later in DEDR's order of languages than the current line's. Six lines qualify, all outside the Tamil *m-* and *v-* entries: Malto in 392, 395 and 1040, Brahui in 55, 392 and 753. The order condition was added after the first version of the rule accepted 23 plain Tamil labels, all of them citations later in an entry, and two "R. Br." botanical authorities (1487, 4100) as Brahui lines.
2. **Citations in plain type.** When a plain label does not start a line, the bold form after it belongs to another entry, and the parser no longer adds it to the current line. This applied to 1,188 Tamil, 29 Kurukh, 3 Malto and 2 Brahui citations.
3. **Citations in italic type.** An italic label directly after "Cf." or another cue word, or after an entry number, cites another entry and does not start a line. This applies to ten labels (nine Tamil, one Kurukh in entry 969), none in a Tamil *m-* or *v-* entry. A rule excluding italic labels inside parentheses was considered and rejected, because unbalanced parentheses earlier in an entry would have removed four genuine lines (Brahui 764, Malto 2559 and 3605, Brahui 3605).
4. **Different entries under one number.** For 94 repeated numbers every shorter print is a prefix of the longest, as the protocol expects. For four numbers it is not: under 1287, 1288, 2835 and 5282 DSAL prints a second, different entry (5282 is used for an entry headed by Tamil *vali* on page 477 and for one headed by *viku* on page 487). Taking only the longest print would have dropped the first of these entries. Each different entry is now kept under its own key ("5282", "5282/2"), flagged as a misnumbered print, and counted as a separate entry.
5. **Borrowing words.** The first draft marked a line as borrowed when its text contained "borrow" or "loan"; it now uses the protocol's words, "borrowed" and "loan". The narrower rule still marks two Malto lines that are not borrowings, entry 472, whose gloss is "to borrow, take a loan", and entry 3580, whose closing note calls a Mundari word a loanword; neither is in a Tamil *m-* or *v-* entry. Across the dictionary 17 Kurukh or Malto lines carry a borrowed form, 13 of them through the "<" sign; one of these is a Malto *b-* form in entry 5401, where DEDR notes that the word "may be < IA", and the rule removes it.

## Checks that found no parse error

The comparison cut at " / " removed 7 Kurukh, 3 Malto and 2 Brahui labels; in the eight entries concerned the cut text is Indo-Aryan comparison material ("Cf. Turner, CDIAL …", "whence Kur. …"), as intended. In 23 entries the raw count of labels differs from the count in the parser's runs; in each the HTML holds DEDR's borrowing sign as a bare "<", which the regular-expression tag stripper of the check treats as the start of a tag while the HTML parser keeps the text, so the difference lies in the check. Three Kurukh or Malto lines and three Tamil lines have no forms: the Malto lines of 234 and 990 (d) cite only suffixes, the Brahui form of 4428 is not set in bold, and the Tamil lines of 266, 4743 and 812 hold only suffixes or bare cross-references; none of these lines is in the population. Of the 2,800 Kurukh and Malto forms, 138 are marked doubtful, 87 of the 101 doubtful lines through a "?" before the label; samples (entries 698, 1513, 3759) showed the mark placed as DEDR places it. The five entries the root-meanings study coded parse as that study read them: in 4876 the Kurukh and Malto forms begin with *b-* and carry no doubt mark, the Malto form of 5396 (*viṇ* "sky") begins with *b-*, 5496 (a) has *b-* in both northern languages, and 4885 and 5052 have *m-*.

## Exposure

To check the parse we read the context of every flagged label. Most lie outside the Tamil *m-* and *v-* range of the dictionary (entries 4600 to 5560). Inside it we read the plain-type citations in 4788, 4806, 4841, 5088, 5362 and 5401, the Kolami source note "(Br.)" in 5224, the Tamil line of 4743, the two prints numbered 5282, and the five entries already coded. We did not tabulate initials for any of them, and the counting code had not been run on the dictionary when this file was written.
