# Sanskrit comparisons behind the new anchors

The table records the dictionary sense chosen for each anchor. Monier-Williams has several homonyms and separate sense records under the same headword. For example, the barley sense of *yava* is entry 170649, water *jala* is 77764, and the “together” sense of *saha* is 240102. A first search result under those spellings would give a different sense. The catalogue retains all matching entry IDs; this table identifies the ones used in the interpretation.

The preferred probes use *udaka* and *vastra*, for which the dictionary cites Vedic use, while their alternatives include *jala* and *paṭa*. Other comparisons, such as *kṛṣaka*, *sopāna* and the superintendent sense of *adhikārin*, have later attestations in this reference. Those later words help formulate a semantic category; their spelling and sound are not dated to an Indus inscription by this procedure. A person-with-tool can supply a cultivator hypothesis without requiring that the ancient word was exactly *kṛṣaka*.

The search also supplied three specific extensions. *Atibhāra* (2710, p. 12,3) suggests a heavy/excessive load for W233 before the N01 load sign. *Sambhāra* (237019–237020, p. 1179,2) suggests gathering or provisions for W231 in that position. *Sabhāga* (232878, p. 1151,2) suggests having a share for W204 before the N12 share sign. These readings were selected after seeing the output; the earlier broad asset/product meanings and rival sounds remain available.
