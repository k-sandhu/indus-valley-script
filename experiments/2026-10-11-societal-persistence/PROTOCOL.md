# Minimum capacities for long societal persistence

11 October 2026. Exploratory literature survey and theory development. This protocol precedes the new formal examples and source coding; the preceding project already inspected selected Harappan and early Indian sources.

## Question and outcome

The user asks what a society with cities, trade and long habitation must maintain in order to persist, and what the literature may underemphasize. The deliverable is a source-qualified survey, a conditional theory with formal examples, a Harappan application, explicit counterexamples and independent adversarial review.

Persistence receives an explicit unit: residents, a settlement, an urban network, an institution, a practical tradition or a claimed cultural identity. Their survival can differ. Duration, population, density, function and the disturbance being tolerated must be specified before a minimum can be asserted. A long occupational sequence is evidence of repeated occupation at its stated resolution, not a direct observation of every household, administration or practice between phases.

## Initial hypotheses to examine

The provisional theory asks whether necessary services must have feasible ways of renewal as well as current supply. Its candidate elements concern bodily provisioning, productive and infrastructural replacement, reproduction of relevant skills and participants, coordination of obligations, and feasible substitution or relocation. These are functions to examine, not a fixed universal list of institutions.

Cities may obtain services through households, common property, exchange, coercion, public provision or external support. A centralized state, monumental building, writing, a particular religion and uniform material standards are hypotheses about arrangements, rather than assumptions about the minimum. External imports and migration remain explicit; closure will be relative to a chosen boundary and time horizon.

The proposed extension focuses on joint replacement constraints: several apparent alternatives may depend on the same scarce input, instructor, supplier or repair capacity. A service can operate now while its replacement route is infeasible. These ideas have close predecessors in resilience, viability, collective action, maintenance and economic reproduction; the survey must assess those predecessors before describing a contribution.

## Source survey

Use primary research, original theoretical works, excavation syntheses and institutional author copies where accessible. Record queries, dates, title/authors/year, DOI or stable URL, actual reading depth, exact loci, limitations, access failures, dependencies and rights. Distinguish a full article from an abstract, an original theory from a later review, and an archaeological observation from its interpretation.

The survey is purposive and snowball-based. It will cover resilience and transformation, archaeological urbanism, political/collective organization, provisioning and trade, maintenance and learning, formal viability and correlated dependencies. Report that scope; do not claim exhaustive coverage or broad novelty. Candidate omissions must name the comparison they would add to existing work, and remain unresolved when inaccessible sources prevent an assessment.

## Formal work

The coordinator owns an explicit small construction of service provision and renewal, with finite states, declared costs, boundary conditions and disturbance horizons. Freeze each model's definitions before running it. Use exact enumeration where possible. Include positive, negative and boundary controls, imports, common-cause dependence and loss of a replacement route.

These examples establish implications of invented laws. They do not estimate Harappan population, maintenance costs, social legitimacy, collapse probability or a universal minimum. Avoid fitting uncertain archaeological proxies to subjective scores. An archaeological pilot is prospective unless a source-qualified population and measured endpoint can actually support it.
