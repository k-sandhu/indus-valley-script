# Independent review of the persistence proposal

11 October 2026. This review reproduces the coordinator's frozen finite model and tests the scope of its historical interpretation. The resulting theory can support a conditional archaeological test: estimate the usable output of existing assets and other bounded supplies over a defined interval, then ask what additional delivery and joint work schedule continuous service would require. A universal requirement for local renewal, a particular political structure or conscious historical knowledge does not follow.

## What the calculation establishes

An implementation written from the declared transition law, without importing coordinator functions, passes **5,218 checks** against all 32 worlds in [the frozen results](../coordinator/run-v1/results.json). It reproduces the 36 states, 16 safe states, horizons 0–12, descending fixed points, policy witnesses, dependency failure sets and seven accounting controls. The independent [checker](check_model.py), [protocol](CHECKER-PROTOCOL.md) and [complete output](model-checks-v1/checks.json) preserve that comparison. This verifies the declared examples; their quantities have no archaeological calibration.

The dependency calculation also reproduces the distinction between multiple apparent providers and multiple operational routes. If every provider requires the same input, losing that input disables service with one component failure. When the four providers have distinct required inputs, at least two failures are needed. This result describes the two stipulated graphs, with no probabilities assigned to component failures.

The strongest accounting claim needs a precise denominator. For a defined interval, `Dmin` is minimum cumulative demand for a selected usable commodity or service. `Smax` bounds cumulative delivery obtainable from initial stocks and assets without replenishment; `Imax` bounds delivered imports; `Emax` bounds other delivered flow available without the extra mechanisms under examination. These quantities must share units, cover all baseline supply, and avoid double counting. Then `max(0, Dmin − Smax − Imax − Emax)` lower-bounds additional usable delivery outside those bounds. It counts neither repair episodes nor local institutions. Complementary inputs, such as raw water and channel capacity, cannot be added as delivered water. Unbounded imports, unknown asset output or unresolved occupation can leave the informative bound at zero. The seven declared controls produce a positive bound of 5 only in the bounded long-demand case; the other six give zero.

## Assumptions that change the result

The finite model assumes that selected repair restores an asset within the period, that a teacher present at the start can complete training before disappearing, and that a renewed asset retains one unit after a unit shock. It inspects safety at period boundaries and allows no exit and later recovery. One period is an invented interval. A historical interpretation would need to specify within-period outages, repair lead times and which interruptions the definition of persistence permits.

Two reviewer alternatives were fixed in [the checker protocol](CHECKER-PROTOCOL.md) before their calculations. They leave the original endpoint unchanged.

| Changed law | Exact result | Inference affected |
|---|---|---|
| A targeted shock removes two asset units, rather than one | Every robust infinite set is empty when the support includes a nonzero shock, even at budget 3. Ten world/mode infinite sets lose a total of 118 state memberships. Possible infinite sets stay unchanged. | Budget sufficiency depends on the survival margin of a newly renewed asset. More simultaneous work cannot overcome complete immediate destruction under this law. |
| An action requires skill remaining after ordinary aging | Fourteen world/mode infinite sets lose a total of 56 state memberships, with no additions. At budget 1, aging skill and no shocks, the primary state's maximum safe transitions fall from 3 to 2. | Last-period training and action eligibility affect the inferred replacement requirement. |

The counts sum memberships across distinct model worlds; they are not numbers of societies. Both variants shrink the relevant sets. In the severity variant, possible survival can keep selecting the no-shock alternative, while robust survival must tolerate every supported shock. Thus observed longevity can be compatible with a favorable realized history even when indefinite survival under all supported histories is impossible. The model's four supports do not supply matched empirical shock frequencies or correlations.

A later review finding prompted a separately declared shared-operation variant. Repair can also teach a successor, so adding separately estimated repair and training effort may count one actual operation twice. Under the [new invented cost law](CO-PRODUCTION-RESULTS.md), training accompanying repair adds no cost, while training alone still costs one. The calculation preserves all 16 aging-skill worlds, passes 1,133 checks and gains 76 infinite-state memberships across 13 world/mode sets, losing none. Budget 1 without shocks now permits indefinite continuation from the primary state; budget 2 can do so under all four original supports. This removes those separate-cost bottlenecks and requires an archaeological task account to measure shared outputs, actual teaching effort and learning time. The amendment was made after baseline exposure and establishes no historical learning cost.

## Six counterexamples to broader implications

The [counterexample protocol](COUNTEREXAMPLES-PROTOCOL.md), [enumerator](counterexamples.py) and [output](counterexamples-v1/counterexamples.json) retain six invented examples. Each rejects a particular unrestricted implication. All six assertions pass.

| Proposed implication | Counterexample and result |
|---|---|
| Finite survival always requires a renewal route | A stock of 4, losing one per transition, remains safe through three transitions with no renewal action. It fails on transition four. The horizon matters. |
| A feasible plan for each service establishes a joint plan | Two services each need one unit of renewal immediately. Each can be sustained separately with budget 1; neither joint plan fits that shared budget. Budget 2 supplies a positive control. |
| Current-shock observation timing leaves robustness unchanged | With binary actions and shocks, safety requires matching them. An action chosen before observing the shock cannot guarantee safety; an action chosen after observation can. |
| A joint resource-feasible repair plan establishes voluntary implementation | Either of two actors can repair a common service. Each receives benefit 1 from repair but pays own repair cost 2. Two feasible profiles are safe, while the sole pure generalized Nash equilibrium is no repair. |
| Aggregate delivery establishes every resident's minimum | Supply 2 can be allocated as `(0,2)`, `(1,1)` or `(2,0)`. All satisfy aggregate delivery 2; only the middle allocation satisfies both residents' minimum 1. |
| Local supply establishes local self-sufficiency or indefinite upstream support | A local stock of 1 survives three transitions by importing one each time from an external stock of 3. The combined system fails at four transitions unless another source becomes available. |

The voluntary-action example changes the theory's scope most directly. An existence proof assumes that actions can be selected and carried out jointly. The historical mechanism additionally requires actors with relevant knowledge, access, compatible incentives or enforceable arrangements. These conditions may be distributed among households, craftspeople and public bodies. The example demonstrates a failure of automatic implementation; it supplies no archaeological estimate of costs or motives.

## Close predecessors and empirical counterprecedents

The search was purposive, covering five directly acquired works and selected sections of two witnesses acquired by the literature contributor. [The source ledger](sources.json) records eight successful witnesses for seven works, plus one unavailable lead. [Ninety source checks](source-checks-v3/checks.json) verify pins, selected locators and claim links; [the retrospective](RETROSPECTIVE.md) retains two unsuccessful PDF locator attempts. Witnesses and full extractions remain local. Multiple versions of one work count once. The earlier protocol's provisional reference to “Deffuant 2004” was mistaken; the coordinator identified the intended viability source as Sophie Martin's 2004 paper. This correction does not alter a frozen input.

[Graham and Thrift's *Out of Order: Understanding Repair and Maintenance*](https://raley.english.ucsb.edu/wp-content/Engl800/Graham-Thrift-repair.pdf), printed pages 4–8, already connects continuing urban provision with maintenance, apprenticeship and improvisation. It treats repair as a source of change, including modifications and recombination. That precedent requires a historical model to consider changing operations and service substitutes rather than assuming every repair restores the same object and function.

[Cavdaroglu and colleagues' *Integrating Restoration and Scheduling Decisions for Disrupted Interdependent Infrastructure Systems*](https://mitchjrpi.github.io/papers/integrated.pdf), manuscript pages 1–4 and 7–11, already jointly chooses repairs, assigns work groups and schedules completion under dependencies. Its objective concerns service during a restoration horizon. [The author's page](https://mitchjrpi.github.io/papers/integrated.html) dates online publication to 5 September 2011 and the journal issue to 2013. The inspected manuscript keeps completed components available for the rest of its horizon; the coordinator's examples instead allow repeated aging. That difference defines the example's law rather than establishing a new general scheduling principle.

[Zhang, Kong and Simonovic's 2018 joint-restoration model](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0195727), sections “Joint restoration strategy model” and “Model of restoration process,” supplies another explicit predecessor. It assumes recovery of a repaired physical node within one step and models recovery sequences and losses through time. Action duration is therefore a substantive assumption in this literature.

[Ostrom's official 2009 Nobel lecture](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/ostrom_lecture.pdf), printed pages 411–412, retrospectively reports productive metropolitan water arrangements and efficient autonomous direct-service producers, alongside some concentrated indirect services. These are empirical counterprecedents to a necessary single centralized provider. The original study data were not reanalyzed here. Printed pages 414 and 420 distinguish a model's precise assumptions from broader institutions and identify action rights, information, decision rules and payoffs. The witness is the Nobel lecture with pagination beginning at 408, not the differently paginated journal version.

[Marx's *Capital*, Volume II, chapter 20, section II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1885-c2/ch20_01.htm) already separates consumption products from means of production and discusses fixed capital surviving annual wear. Its capitalist reproduction scheme is a theoretical counterprecedent to treating the output/reproduction distinction as newly discovered. Only that section was used, and the online translation's edition details remain unresolved.

The archaeological measurement problem is likewise explicit in primary scholarship. [Smith and colleagues](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8157985/), section “What Is Settlement Persistence?”, distinguish occupation, demographic scale, institutions and cultural practices, and question nominal continuity produced by modern labeling. [Lawrence and colleagues](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12036995/), methods section “Settlement Persistence,” measure substantial occupation episodes, separate abandonment and reoccupation, exclude ephemeral phases and allow changes in size within an episode. Their results also discuss dating resolution that can conceal short abandonments. A long site name or broad pottery phase therefore cannot substitute for continuous delivery to an unchanged population.

## Conditions for an archaeological test

A test should choose the persistence outcome, interval, service minima and boundary before examining the favored long-lived cases. It should estimate initial assets' cumulative usable output from independent material and environmental evidence, bound delivered outside support, and record plausible substitutes. Maintenance need, maintenance visibility and actual successful maintenance are different observations. A positive delivery deficit then defines a task for historical explanation without choosing its institution in advance.

Candidate replacement tasks also need dates, time windows, skill requirements and overlapping resource demands. A feasible joint schedule is stronger than separate task feasibility, but it still establishes an opportunity. Evidence of knowledge, rights and coordination is needed to explain actual action. Conversely, a missing written instruction does not establish missing procedural knowledge. Innovation, migration or lower population can change demand or the available action set and must be represented when independently attested.

A work-window bound requires additive resource use after shared operations have been represented. For consumable resources it must include usable inventory carried into the interval. These conditions prevent repair/teaching co-production or earlier material deliveries from creating a false deficit.

Sampling only survivors would make the proposed mechanism hard to distinguish from other persistence requirements. A prospective comparison should include short-lived settlements and observed service failures, report unresolved and censored durations, and repeat the analysis under consistent occupation and demographic definitions. The [falsifier ledger](falsifiers.json) specifies what can reject each operational version, what must be bounded first, and which broader interpretation would remain unresolved.

The present package supports exact verification of a small conditional model and exposes failure points for its social interpretation. An archaeological claim about Harappan persistence requires the missing dated deliveries, replacement operations and joint constraints to be measured.
