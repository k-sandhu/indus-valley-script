# Prospective tests and rejection criteria

These are proposed tests of particular historical explanations. They were written after the finite examples and counterexamples; none has been performed with archaeological data. The machine-readable [ledger](falsifiers.json) preserves the claim, required observations and rejection rule for each.

The first measurement should be a commodity batch or other independently defined delivered output. Counting structures gives no direct upper bound on service delivered over centuries. Experimental wear estimates and dated replacement traces can help estimate output from initial assets, but surviving use alone cannot be used to infer the very lifetime the model needs to test.

| Test | Required design | Result that changes the explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Bound the delivery deficit | Fix an occupation interval and minimum demand. Independently bound usable initial output, imports and other baseline supply in common units. Represent known substitutes and population changes. | A proposed local-renewal requirement disappears if baseline sources can cover demand. If documented delivery exceeds the entire proposed supply envelope, the particular bounded model is rejected and an omitted source, measurement or law must be investigated. |
| Compare shared work schedules | Identify at least two replacement operations with independent duration, skill and resource bounds and overlapping dated deadlines. Test all schedules allowed by the stated uncertainty intervals. | Continuous relevant service despite no feasible schedule under all admissible measurements rejects that task/resource reconstruction. Separate feasibility cannot rescue it. |
| Audit action timing | Determine whether repair can finish before a relevant service interruption and whether knowledge transfer can finish before loss of instructors. Keep intraperiod outages visible. | Replacement completing after the permitted interruption, or a training duration longer than available overlap, rejects the stated instantaneous-repair or last-period-training mechanism. |
| Trace outside support | Test independently defined provenance, delivery and redistribution evidence across the chosen boundary. Distinguish finished usable supply from complementary inputs. | Sufficient outside delivery rejects a specifically local self-sufficiency interpretation while remaining compatible with regional interdependence. Unknown imports leave local necessity unresolved. |
| Examine implementation | Identify relevant actors' rights, communication, task assignment, compensation or enforceable coordination. Permit polycentric arrangements. | Resource-feasible but persistently unperformed work under conflict rejects sufficiency of physical feasibility alone. Successful distributed provision rejects a requirement for a single centralized controller. |
| Compare resident access | Define which residents the service minimum covers and seek delivery/distribution evidence at that scale. | Maintained aggregate service with excluded residents rejects the aggregate-to-universal-access inference. It may still satisfy a narrower persistence outcome. |
| Test explanatory use beyond survivors | Include short-lived and long-lived settlements from the same sampling frame, with consistent outcome definitions and dating resolution. Retain censoring and uncertainty. Freeze capacity observations without using later duration. | Failure to distinguish subsequent documented service continuity from failures weakens the proposed predictor. It does not refute the accounting identity or establish that capacity caused longevity. |
| Test destructive-shock scope | Estimate destructive events' effects on newly renewed assets and available recovery time. Report the supported event set without inventing rates. | Events capable of removing all restored service reject an unconditional robustness claim for the baseline law. Favorable realized survival remains a separate possibility claim. |

The delivery test is partly a test of the proposed measurement envelope. Conservation itself cannot tell an archaeologist which source was omitted. An absent repair trace should therefore be evaluated against excavation coverage, preservation and the expected visibility of the particular task. The joint-schedule test is more discriminating when operations have independently constrained durations and resources: it can expose an impossible proposed mechanism even when each operation appears feasible alone.

The post-baseline [shared-operation variant](CO-PRODUCTION-RESULTS.md) adds a task-definition test. Establish whether the same operation actually repairs an asset and transfers competent practice, what additional learner or teacher effort it consumes, and whether their overlap fits the required service window. A measured shared operation can reject the additive task account that double-counts its work. Failed competence transfer or substantial extra effort can reject the particular zero-increment teaching law. Neither result authenticates an ancient teaching institution from finished artifacts alone.
