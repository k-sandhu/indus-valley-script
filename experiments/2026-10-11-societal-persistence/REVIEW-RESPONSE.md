# Response to independent review

11 October 2026. The review reconstructed the frozen examples independently and tested broader interpretations with six exact counterexamples. Its [report](review/REVIEW.md) and [source audit](review/source-checks-v3/checks.json) remain separate from this response.

| Review finding | Accepted change or retained scope |
|---|---|
| Initial stock can cover a finite horizon without renewal | Minimum functions are conditional on the required outcome and exhaustion of bounded baseline delivery. The accounting controls retain short-horizon and large-stock cases. |
| Separate service plans can compete for shared inputs | The theory requires a joint feasible course of action and adds an ordinary work-window necessary constraint. Passing a mean-work check does not establish a complete schedule. |
| A model's controller can choose work that actors will not perform | Technical feasibility and historical enactment are distinct conditions. The empirical design seeks knowledge, access, incentives and participation; it permits distributed arrangements. |
| Imports can relocate the renewal burden outside a city | Boundary and external delivery are explicit. Regional dependencies require their own observation; local occupation does not imply self-sufficiency. |
| Aggregate delivery can exclude residents | Define whose service minima the outcome covers and code distribution separately. The reviewer retains an exact unequal-allocation counterexample. |
| Summed task costs can double-count one operation | Resource use is additive after accounting for co-production; carried material inventory counts toward interval capacity. A new post-baseline variant explicitly changes training's incremental cost during repair, preserving the separate-cost baseline. |
| A repair can be destroyed by a more severe shock | The baseline result is tied to single-unit shocks. Severity-two review results remain a separate law and are reported beside the baseline. No disturbance probabilities are fitted. |
| Last-period action eligibility changes replacement opportunities | Baseline eligibility is tested at the start; the reviewer separately requires skill after aging. Report both timing laws and collect historical lead times before interpretation. |
| The no-op remains available with exhausted skill | MODEL-RESULTS specifies that only nonempty actions require positive skill. |
| A robust guarantee applies to a policy, with stated observations | THEORY uses policy wording and states full observation in the finite checker. Historical reconstructions can restrict available information. |
| Delivered-output units differ from asset counts and complementary inputs | The bound uses commensurable cumulative usable output over the full horizon. It identifies additional delivery, leaving mechanisms and institutions to discriminate. |
| A future test cannot erase exposure to known outcomes | Capacity extraction should be masked from later duration; an already exposed comparison remains exploratory. Stop rules require a positive supported demand lower bound. |
| Reviewer protocols freeze laws but do not give CLI commands | The reproduction guide now points to the review README's actual commands. |
| Maintenance, joint scheduling and viability have close precedents | The manuscript and gap assessment state those precedents directly. The contribution is a proposed archaeological inference procedure; empirical priority remains limited to the inspected scope. |
