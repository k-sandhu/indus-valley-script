# Renewing complex societies: functional requirements and archaeological inference

11 October 2026. Exploratory manuscript, developed from a primary-source survey, exact invented examples and a source-qualified Harappan application.

## Abstract

Long occupation, large cities and trade imply recurring demands on people, material supplies and coordination. They leave open how those demands were met. We develop a conditional procedure for identifying renewal requirements: specify the persistence outcome and horizon, bound delivery available from initial assets and other baseline sources, then test additional demands against shared resources and completion windows. Existing work on economic reproduction, maintenance, viability and institutional adaptation supplies the main theoretical precedents. A finite construction shows how working services can coexist with an exhausted replacement route, and how average affordable work can conceal a deadline conflict. Independent reconstruction passes 5,218 checks and preserves two sensitivity analyses. Harappa's replacement drainage and Dholavira's selective maintenance provide observations for the proposed procedure. The reviewed sources do not supply the quantities needed to estimate a Harappan renewal budget. The result is a testable framework and measurement design, with its historical explanation still requiring an empirical comparison.

## The question posed by longevity

A long-lived settlement presents evidence of repeated habitation. A large city presents a further problem: many people have to obtain services through interacting production, distribution and infrastructure. Extensive trade can increase available inputs and expertise while making continued access dependent on other places. These observations justify investigating the arrangements that reproduce material and practical capacity over time.

The outcome must be specified. Residents, a built settlement, an urban network, an institution and a craft tradition can persist separately. People may remain after infrastructure contraction, or continue a practice elsewhere after a city's abandonment. Some observed occupation sequences include gaps and reoccupation. Smith and colleagues' [settlement-persistence study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8157985/) already distinguishes these outcomes, while Lawrence and colleagues' [2025 comparison](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12036995/) gives an operational occupation endpoint and dating limitations. A period of occupation is therefore used at its measured resolution rather than assumed to represent an unchanged society through every intervening year.

Our working thesis is that required services must remain obtainable through the defined horizon. When initial supplies, unmaintained assets and outside delivery cannot cover that demand, further provision becomes necessary. The available activities must be compatible in their shared inputs and timing, and people must have a way to perform them. The minimum concerns these functions and conditions. A particular institutional form requires more specific evidence.

## Related work and the proposed contribution

The survey brings together literatures that already study societal reproduction from different directions. Economic reproduction examines the replacement of production inputs through exchange. The selected first chapter of Sraffa's *Production of Commodities by Means of Commodities* provides a direct predecessor. Resilience research asks how functions continue or change under disturbances; collective-action research studies resource users, providers and institutions. Maintenance research examines the work that sustains infrastructure, including practical learning and improvisation. Viability theory identifies states from which admissible future trajectories remain available.

These precedents constrain the contribution. [Graham and Thrift 2007](https://raley.english.ucsb.edu/wp-content/Engl800/Graham-Thrift-repair.pdf) already connects repair, apprenticeship and urban continuity. [Cavdaroglu and colleagues](https://mitchjrpi.github.io/papers/integrated.html) jointly select restoration operations, assign workers and schedule recovery. [Pichancourt, Brias and Bonis 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11867035/) joins adaptation pathways, institutional arrangements and viability. Jervis's [2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/00438243.2024.2387660) and [2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10816-026-09803-2) work treats repetitive material practices and heterogeneous relations in urban endurance. [Priß and colleagues' 2026 network study](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2025.10272) is another close comparative approach. Actual reading depth, acquisition limits and dependence among witnesses are recorded in the [literature ledger](literature/SOURCES.json), [coordinator ledger](coordinator/SOURCES.json) and [review ledger](review/sources.json).

The proposed contribution is an archaeological inference procedure joining demand and output bounds, observed renewal, shared work windows and social enactment. In the inspected settlement-persistence and network studies, the reported metrics do not directly measure asset-output ceilings, imported delivery bounds or the combined replacement schedule. That is a defined measurement opportunity. A purposive survey cannot establish that the entire field has omitted it. The [gap assessment](literature/GAP-ASSESSMENT.md) distinguishes close precedents, selected empirical gaps and unresolved priority.

## Functional requirements

The necessary functions depend on population, environment, technology and horizon. Continued habitation requires usable bodily support at the defined level. A productive or infrastructural arrangement whose existing capacity will expire requires continued provision through repair, replacement, acquisition or substitution. Tasks that outlast a practitioner cohort require access to competent successors or a different means of accomplishing them. Shared demands require allocation and participation compatible with their timing. Alternatives become necessary when the specified disturbance would otherwise interrupt the chosen outcome.

The provider can lie outside the settlement. People, finished goods, specialist services and usable supplies may cross its boundary. Moving the boundary changes where the renewal burden appears. A city may persist through immigration while its original families do not, or import a product while its own production tradition disappears. A regional explanation must then examine the suppliers whose capacity sustains the urban outcome.

The burden also has a distribution. A maintained aggregate water supply need not reach every resident. A political organization can sustain facilities while imposing costly work on particular groups. The model's acceptable-state definition should therefore state whose service minimum it represents. The reviewer supplies a counterexample in which aggregate delivery is sufficient and one resident receives none.

Complexity has several dimensions here. Specialized tasks, interdependent routes and time-sensitive obligations can increase coordination demands. Shared work and specialized competence can also reduce them. The evidence should describe those quantities separately rather than convert monument size or the number of roles into an assumed rank of institutional maturity. [THEORY.md](THEORY.md) sets out the functional table and its inference conditions.

## A conditional lower bound

For a commensurable delivered commodity or service, let minimum demand over a stated horizon be `Dmin`, maximum output obtainable from initial stocks and unmaintained assets be `Smax`, and upper bounds on imported and other baseline delivery be `Imax` and `Emax`. Additional delivery must be at least `max(0, Dmin−Smax−Imax−Emax)`. This follows from ordinary accounting and requires the supply bounds to cover the allowed baseline routes.

The residual can be useful without identifying an institution. It requests some additional usable delivery beyond the bounded sources. It leaves production, prolonged asset use, repair, replacement and reacquisition as mechanisms to discriminate. Unknown import ceilings, uncertain initial output, substitute services or a lower demand after contraction can make the bound uninformative. A count of wells or walls supplies no direct estimate of usable output over centuries.

A second necessary constraint applies to work windows. Operations whose entire permissible execution windows lie inside an interval cannot demand more of a resource than its usable capacity in that interval, including carried inventory for materials. Shared operations such as repair which also teaches an apprentice must be counted once before treating resource use as additive. This is an established scheduling condition. It can reject a narrowly specified renewal mechanism even when its long-run mean cost looks affordable. Passing the condition does not establish a complete schedule, and a feasible schedule does not demonstrate that people could implement it with their actual knowledge, rights and incentives.

## Exact constructions and independent review

The finite example represents two essential assets with life 0–2 and an instructor or skill resource with life 0–3. Asset repair and training each cost one unit from a shared period budget. Assets age each period; training and repair restore capacity; a supported shock can subtract another asset unit. Actions are chosen from full state information before the current shock, with period-boundary safety. The [model protocol](coordinator/MODEL-PROTOCOL.md) fixes those invented laws before calculation.

At the primary state `(2,2,3)`, budget 1 can maintain both assets indefinitely when skill does not age. With aging skill and no shocks, the maximum is three safe transitions. Both assets can still operate at the endpoint while the capacity to renew them has expired. Budget 2 permits indefinite continuation from the primary state without shocks, but cannot do so from every currently safe state. The state `(1,1,1)` requires two repairs and training together, exceeding that period's budget even though the mean renewal demand is only `4/3` actions per period.

Under an optional joint shock to both assets, budget 2 with aging skill permits only three robust safe transitions from the primary state. Budget 3 permits indefinite continuation under the baseline law. The independent reviewer shows why the shock law matters: if a supported shock removes both units from a newly renewed asset, every robust infinite set is empty, including budget 3. Requiring skill to survive ordinary aging also changes the replacement opportunities.

The calculations cover 32 worlds and all 36 states, finite horizons and fixed points. The independent implementation imports no coordinator functions and passes 5,218 checks. Separate dependency graphs show that multiple providers sharing one required input can have a single failure point. Seven accounting controls show where a positive bound exists and where it becomes uninformative. These results and the review variants are recorded in [MODEL-RESULTS.md](MODEL-RESULTS.md) and [the review](review/REVIEW.md). The quantities establish implications of their declared laws, with no fitted ancient population, labor cost or collapse probability.

A further review finding prompted an explicitly post-baseline variant: repair and learning can be outputs of one operation. Under an invented law assigning zero incremental training cost when it accompanies repair, budget 1 permits indefinite primary-state continuation without shocks, and budget 2 permits it in all four original supports. The new 16-world calculation receives 1,133 reviewer checks and a separate 1,968-check reconstruction by the coordinator. It demonstrates that the baseline replacement deficit depends on separate action costs. A historical account must therefore examine the actual relation between work and learning; apprenticeship is already a mechanism discussed in the maintenance literature.

## Harappan observations

The Harappa Mound E gateway provides a direct sequence of renewal. [Meadow and Kenoyer's 1997 report](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer1997_Excavations%20at%20Harappa%201994-1995%20New%20Perspective.pdf) describes successive drains; the final Period 3C drain clogged with silty refuse, and a replacement culvert was cut farther east through the former gateway. The observation records an attempt to continue the service through a changed route. The required work, responsible participants and total usable delivery remain unmeasured. The inspected field locus is printed page 146, PDF page 8, with its source lineage in the [evidence study](evidence/APPLICATION.md).

Dholavira gives a different outcome. The official [*Indian Archaeology 1991–92—A Review*](https://www.asiaurangabadcircle.com/assets/files/publications/1991-1992.pdf), published 1996, reports Stage VI after a brief desertion. Occupants retained outer-wall portions needed by some reservoirs, salvaged stone and changed streets and housing. This records selective renewal and reuse in a changed settlement configuration. [Bisht's original preliminary report](https://archive.org/details/dholavirareportpreliminaryjan2015_860_K) separately describes an eastern reservoir losing its water function and accommodating later activities. Its failure does not determine the condition of every reservoir. The interruption and the remaining services both belong in the outcome definition.

Other selected evidence concerns maintained access to drains, diverse crop strategies and specialized craft inputs. These observations help identify operations or dependencies. Repeated brick forms or finished beads alone do not identify a teaching institution or local production. The [application](evidence/APPLICATION.md), source ledger and tests keep actual observations, the original authors' interpretations and the proposed extensions distinct.

The available sources do not provide independently bounded demand, initial usable output, imported delivery and annual shared work capacity for these cases. Consequently, this study does not calculate a Harappan renewal deficit or identify a necessary form of government. It identifies observed renewals and the measurements that could make a more discriminating test possible.

## A research program and its implications

The first pilot should reconstruct a documented service sequence, retaining occupation gaps and changed routes. It should source every observation, distinguish wear from repair and actual operation, and identify substitutes and external providers. A work-window test becomes useful when operations and resources can be dated at the required resolution. A later comparison should include both service continuity and failure in a common sampling frame, with capacity extraction masked from later outcomes where possible. The [empirical protocol](EMPIRICAL-PROTOCOL.md) states those decisions and stop rules, and the [reviewer's criteria](review/FALSIFIERS.md) specify which reconstruction each result could reject.

The theory also clarifies the relationship between long societal development and mature concepts. Practical knowledge can accumulate in recipes, measures, access rules and expectations about work. Its carriers can change while useful procedures continue. A sophisticated textual tradition can therefore be investigated as part of a wider history of knowledge reproduction. Attributing a particular text or account of the remote past to the Harappan civilization requires the separate chronological and transmission evidence examined in the [preceding continuity studies](../2026-10-11-early-indian-continuity/REPORT.md).

Long duration motivates questions about repeated provision and inherited capacity. Its most informative consequences emerge when demands, supply routes and renewal opportunities can be bounded independently. The present package supplies those inference rules, exact examples and source-qualified pilots for the next empirical study.
