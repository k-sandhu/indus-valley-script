# Harappan renewal and persistence: documentary protocol

11 October 2026. This protocol freezes the bounded archaeological application before new source coding. Search results and the earlier settlement and continuity packages supplied candidate works; no new quantitative endpoint has been run.

The question is whether archaeological observations distinguish a settlement's repeated occupation from the renewal of particular services, materials and skills. The parent [protocol](../PROTOCOL.md) defines the provisional theory. This application examines Harappan provisioning, water and drainage, rebuilding, craft production, trade dependencies, household organization and reorganization after urban contraction. It does not estimate a universal minimum or fit archaeological proxies to a complexity score.

Each observation receives a source, exact locus, geographic and chronological unit, reading depth, observed material or analytical result, and the author's interpretation where relevant. Reconstructed production, maintenance and transmission processes remain identifiable in the ledger. A multi-century phase range describes a dated archaeological sequence at its available resolution; it does not establish uninterrupted service, household membership or political administration. Repeated studies or author syntheses drawing on the same excavation have one evidence lineage.

The purposive survey aims for roughly ten to fifteen primary field or research works, with inspection depth taking priority over count. Crop and food evidence, physically repaired or repurposed structures, production traces and changing material access will be read for their actual measurement and context. Earlier rice and literary chronology results serve only as scoped background. No earlier analysis is rerun. Access failures and incomplete inspection remain in the source ledger.

The strongest within-site comparison will contrast occupation or reuse with the continued operation of a particular service. A reservoir converted to another activity, or a craft technique retained while a specialized raw material disappears, can make that distinction observable. A demographic or cultural-identity claim requires its own evidence rather than the continued presence of some material practice.

The initial hypotheses are that repair frequency and replacement routes constrain persistence; apparent alternatives can share scarce water, labour, tools, instructors or suppliers; and loss of a service can precede either settlement abandonment or transformation to another viable arrangement. Proposed tests must name measurable outcomes, chronological resolution, a sampling unit, plausible alternative causes and a rejection condition. Any theory developed after the audit is marked as such and remains prospective.

The final package contains authored prose, a source ledger, machine-readable observations, source checks, operational tests, a retrospective and a changed-path inventory. Raw downloads, extracts and rendered source pages remain local under `data/raw/2026-10-11-societal-persistence/evidence/`. The protocol and all dated outputs are preserved. Only this `evidence/` directory and its matching raw directory are owned by this contributor.
