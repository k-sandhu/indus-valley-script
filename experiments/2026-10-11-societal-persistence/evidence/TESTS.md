# Measurements that can decide narrower renewal explanations

11 October 2026. Prospective tests following the documentary audit. No archaeological parameters were fitted and none of the experiments or field acquisitions below has been performed.

The first target is an operating service within a stated area and interval. Record the allowed interruption, the people or activities it served and the observations that show operation independently of the proposed repair. An occupied site, a filled reservoir and an ornament of a familiar appearance are different outcomes. Keep their dates, sampling coverage and evidence lineages separate. The [parent procedure](../EMPIRICAL-PROTOCOL.md) supplies the accounting and joint-schedule gates; these tests identify what the Harappan cases would need to pass them.

## First pilot: Harappa Mound E

Start with the replacement drain described in [S03/E07–E08](observations.json), printed pp.143–146/PDF pp.5–8. Reconstruct the earlier drain [50], the replacement culvert, associated entrances, connected house outlets and any alternative disposal routes from the available field plans and sections. Use local stratigraphic relationships. If the record cannot distinguish these installations' co-use, preserve that uncertainty rather than merging the whole gateway into one contemporaneous network.

The first decision is whether the refuse accumulated during use, after cessation or as intentional closure. Compare sediment structure, access to the deposit, inlet/outlet relationships and associated surfaces. A primary-use deposit before replacement would support clogging as an operational problem. Deposits inserted after closure would weaken that causal interpretation even though the replacement sequence remained valid. Changes in connected households or circulation could explain a larger or relocated culvert without an unchanged demand.

Only after this decision should reconstruction experiments estimate task durations. In a declared replica, measure sediment volume removed, tool and worker requirements, material preparation, construction and time to restored flow. Vary drain dimensions, sediment properties and access within source-supported ranges; record the experiment's modern equipment and safety constraints. Output measures should concern usable flow or removal under specified conditions. Performance under those conditions is an input to a bounded reconstruction, not a universal ancient labor coefficient.

The joint test needs at least two operations with independently justified windows and a demonstrated shared resource. Removing deposits and building a replacement might be successive tasks with ample time; they might instead compete with other work before a seasonal disturbance. The present phase dates cannot choose. If date uncertainty exceeds the proposed deadline window, stop the historical schedule test and retain the measured operations as a prospective resource study.

## Dholavira: selection among inherited structures

Use the reported Stage VI interruption and reoccupation explicitly. Map maintained reservoir-support wall segments from [S04/E10–E11](observations.json) and establish which reservoirs they supported. Keep the East reservoir sequence [S05/E12–E15](observations.json) separate until an actual connection is documented. The later fireplaces and kilns there do not establish a continuously occupied workshop or continued water storage.

This selective-retention hypothesis was developed after the documentary audit. Compare retained segments, salvaged structures and abandoned components with their reconstructed contribution to later water access and shelter. Construction type, exposure, surviving height, proximity, salvage convenience and later disturbance form controls. Two coders should assign the physical observations before they receive the proposed functional explanation; outcome exposure and disagreements remain in the log. A map should show retained segments with unidentified functions as well as those compatible with the hypothesis.

The prediction is that retained work supports services used in the changed settlement, while salvage can redirect inherited material to new shelter. If preservation and convenience explain retention without a remaining functional association, the functional-selection explanation loses support. Demonstrated maintenance of components with no compatible later service would require another explanation, such as status, defense or inherited constraints. An independent water-demand range is needed to evaluate whether fewer installations could still serve the later population; a smaller settlement description alone is not that range.

## Production, input access and technical transmission

This support-chain hypothesis was developed after the documentary audit. Select a bounded production sequence before inspecting its later product range. Include contexts with drill manufacture, reshaping, reuse, finished products and unsuccessful pieces, together with crafts that continued. [S08/E20–E23](observations.json) identifies material stages; [S10/E25–E26](observations.json) supplies competing accounts of specialized-tool loss and substitution. Use equivalent recovery and identification methods, since small manufacture debris may be missed by older collection practice.

| Proposed mechanism | Discriminating observation | Rejection or rival interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Loss of raw access | Independently characterized tool material becomes unavailable before dependent products contract; retained stock is exhausted or substituted | Fresh access and continued replacement-tool production during the alleged shortage reject a general access failure |
| Loss of working knowledge | Suitable inputs remain accessible while repeated failed manufacture appears and successful complex output contracts | Successful local replacement-tool manufacture weakens the claim; imported finished products must be separated |
| Reduced demand | Production and imports of the difficult product contract while usable inputs and working technique remain available | An earlier independently evidenced input interruption favors another ordering |
| Deliberate substitution | Alternative products or techniques preserve a defined ornament function with demonstrable changes in input and work requirements | Similar finished appearance without manufacturing evidence does not establish a local substitute production route |

Develop a modern blind reference collection before assigning ancient error signatures to learners. Include beginners, experienced makers, difficult materials, faulty tools and interrupted work. Record which signatures distinguish learning from material failure or expedient production. The archaeological transmission question additionally needs dated overlap or succession of operations and practitioners. Replicated styles or tool presence alone cannot identify a teaching institution, a training duration or an unbroken community.

For supply organization at Gola Dhoro, test [S11/E27–E29](observations.json) against deposition and coverage: were missing waste categories genuinely absent, moved elsewhere or not recovered? Map raw-material provenance and transport paths before counting providers. Separate collectors and several workshops could still depend on a shared source or route. The inspection should retain observed production, author-inferred logistics and later occupation as distinct records.

## Provisioning and correlated alternatives

Use the site-phase sample as the initial unit. Crop and weed evidence in [S06–S07/E16–E19](observations.json) distinguishes possible management regimes more closely than species counts. Add appropriately dated crop isotopes and processing contexts to test water conditions and crop mixing. Require evidence for growing regimes before treating winter and summer remains as two independent productive routes. Reuse the earlier rice findings only within their original scope; this design does not rerun that audit.

Then identify shared prerequisites: land, water access, storage, preparation, transport, animals and skilled work. A provider map should preserve unknown edges, with source evidence for every known dependency. A common-input hypothesis predicts simultaneous constraints in routes that share that input; routes without it provide controls. Joint stress cannot be inferred from the presence of several crops, nor can archaeological century-scale changes estimate annual failure covariance. If temporal resolution prevents the relevant comparison, report it as unresolved.

Food-practice continuity can be tested separately with a matched residue design. Define an isotope or molecular contrast capable of detecting the proposed ingredient change; fix an equivalence margin from analytical and local reference information before using it. Match phase, site, vessel form and depositional setting, and retain mixture uncertainty. The existing non-detection in [S12/E30–E32](observations.json) does not complete that equivalence test. A food-delivery account needs independently supported quantities and an appropriate boundary in addition to ingredient proxies.

## Outcome controls and stop conditions

Within Dholavira, the East reservoir's continued activity with lost water function is a control against equating use of place with continued service. Gola Dhoro's reported later occupation with changed craft/network indicators supplies another local distinction. Matched rural food-practice samples can compare daily practice with city form at a different scale. None identifies the same residents merely from a surviving practice.

The skeletal comparison [S13/E33](observations.json) makes distribution and access explicit. A future analysis must preserve funeral selection, demographic composition, preservation and burial-area chronology before interpreting differences in health. The regional survey [S14/E34](observations.json) needs systematic revisitation and coverage checks before population relocation becomes an outcome. Do not derive population totals from settlement area or from counts of reported locations.

No positive minimum-delivery bound can be evaluated until a demand lower bound and upper bounds for initial delivery, imports and other allowed baseline routes share one usable unit over the same horizon. No historical scheduling rejection can be evaluated until work requirements, completion windows and shared capacity have independent support. These are currently missing in the documentary ledger. Complete the source-linked sequence when the gates fail; leave the corresponding bound or scheduling result unresolved.

If independent service continuity is established but every admissible task/resource account is infeasible, reject that account and examine omitted inputs, alternative routes, incorrect timing or service interpretation. A feasible account remains a possible mechanism until participation and actual intervention are independently evidenced. These decision rules preserve what a successful measurement could establish and what a failed reconstruction would require revising.
