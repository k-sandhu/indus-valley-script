# Documentary application retrospective

11 October 2026. Observable research actions and outcomes for the Harappan renewal package.

The assignment asked for a bounded archaeological application of a provisional service-renewal theory. The earlier settlement and cultural-continuity retrospectives supplied constraints: broad site phases do not count residents or continuous occupation, preserved practices do not identify an unchanged community, and later or alternate editions do not add independent evidence. The documentary protocol was frozen before new coding. The existing rice audit and chronology models were not rerun.

The most productive search path was to follow actual changes of function and construction. The Harappa gateway paper records successive drains, clogging, a replacement culvert and altered access. The Dholavira field review records renewed occupation after a desertion, selective reservoir-support wall retention and salvage for a different layout. The preliminary full report supplies a useful within-site comparison: the East reservoir lost its water function while later activities used its fill. These became separate observations rather than a single site-longevity claim.

Acquisition required several routes. Publisher and PMC web pages sometimes returned challenge HTML, including files with a PDF extension. Those responses were preserved but excluded as primary texts. Publisher XML, institutional repositories and author-hosted papers supplied usable alternatives. A file advertised as the full Dholavira report proved to be a modern interpretive wrapper with selected report images and rebus claims, so it was excluded. Official full-report URLs returned 404. The archive original eventually arrived intact; its printed pages were read and rendered to check the earlier mirror transcription. Failed archive image routes were retained, and TLS verification was not disabled.

The full Dholavira witness resolved an access limitation without expanding the claim. It confirmed the repair and cessation sequence, and its next page expressly limits the later activity to something short of regular continuous occupation. It also preserved the distinction between an observed named reservoir and the excavator's extrapolation to others. The 938-page file was not represented as fully read: inspected chapters, sections and pages are listed in the source ledger.

Bibliographic corrections prevented false chronology. The Jansen composite includes a 2011 magazine item and a 2014 chapter, the ASI 1991–92 field review was printed in 1996, and a craft filename saying 1998 identifies a nominal 1996 issue. The publisher's 2026 migration date for the Gola Dhoro note is separate from its 2005 issue. Technological loss claims were attributed to the scoped 2007 synthesis, rather than the earlier craft article. Its obsolete river/migration account was excluded from the reused scope.

The tool-production study supported an extension from inventories to replacement operations: roughouts, waste, wear and reshaping show that drills themselves belonged to a reproduced craft chain. Its modern rates and proposed apprenticeship were kept separate from ancient measured work and observed instruction. Crop and residue studies supplied possible provisioning and practice-continuity comparisons, with overlaps, mixtures and sampling limitations retained. The residue article's text count of 172 differs from the Table 4 total of 171. The discrepancy remains unresolved; no new significance fit was made from those figures.

The first integrity audit passed 273 of 275 checks. Two short source anchors failed because scanned-PDF extraction rendered the expected words as `farther ~ast` and `making _debitage`. The source pages had been visually inspected. Shorter stable fragments fixed the location checks in a fresh second run, which passed all 275. The initial audit, its exact original code, frozen observations and raw witnesses were preserved. These checks establish bookkeeping and source-location consistency; they do not authenticate the hypothesized mechanisms.

The package verifier rebuilt the three documentary ledgers byte-for-byte and tested the overwrite guards. Its first run recorded a missing link to the verification report that the run had not yet written. A fresh run after that report existed passed all 16 checks. The first outcome was retained. Subsequent document changes receive a final reference check in a separate run.

Three positive hypotheses were refined after the audit and explicitly labeled prospective. Selective retention relates later repairs to still-needed services. Support-chain failure predicts that loss of replacement inputs or learning can precede product contraction. Concentrated renewal work asks whether individually feasible operations conflict over a shared input and deadline. The reviewer emphasized independent work durations, actual service/demand bounds and joint windows as the next discriminating measurements. The test programme incorporates that requirement and gives alternatives and rejection conditions for each case.

No inspected sequence supplies the complete set of quantities required for a historical minimum or joint schedule. All six service/schedule measurement fields remain null in all 34 records. This is a documented limit of the application, rather than a fitted zero capacity or a universal conclusion about institutions. The next study must improve the specific measurements and chronology; another generic inference that old infrastructure needed upkeep would not resolve the present questions.

Only the owned evidence and matching raw directories were written. Code, authored ledgers, prose and aggregate integrity outputs are prepared for repository use; third-party source witnesses and derivatives remain local. No global index, registry, earlier frozen package, CI/CD configuration or external publication was changed.
