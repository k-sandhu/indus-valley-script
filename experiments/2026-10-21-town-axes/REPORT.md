# Were the Harappan towns laid out on the stars? Report of 21 October 2026

We built the first table of axis bearings, horizons and layout dates for planned Harappan towns and tested on it the claim of study 4 in the [direction note of 20 October](../../knowledge/directions/parpola-forward-2026-10-20.md): that builders aligned the main axes of new towns on the setting point of a bright star, so that a town's deviation from the cardinal directions records when it was laid out. The frozen test is undecided, because satellite imagery referenced to true north could be measured at only four dated towns and the kill condition needs eight. Its Pleiades version fails outright: two of the four towns, Dholavira and Lothal, are turned anticlockwise, where the Pleiades predict clockwise. A secondary analysis declared before any value was read, which adds seven towns measured on published plans whose north is not stated, finds no significant correlation between deviation and layout date (r = 0.46, one-sided p = 0.082, n = 11). Under that amendment's rule the claim is weakened to a credence of 0.04.

## What we built

The [settlement-axis table](table-v1/settlement-axes.json) ([CSV](table-v1/settlement-axes.csv)) has 29 towns. For each it records the site coordinates, the measured lines and systems from four measurers with their sources and errors, the plan measurements with the label of each plan's north arrow, the apparent horizon east and west, and the layout phase with its dating evidence. Four agents measured blind to each other and to this test, under [MEASUREMENT-BRIEF.md](MEASUREMENT-BRIEF.md): img-A picked wall and street endpoints on Esri World Imagery at zoom 18 (about 0.5 m per pixel), img-B took gradient-orientation peaks inside polygons drawn on the same imagery, and plan-A and plan-B measured published plans against their own north arrows. A fifth agent compiled layout phases and 382 radiocarbon entries from 199 sources under [DATING-BRIEF.md](DATING-BRIEF.md). Horizons come from the Copernicus GLO-30 and GLO-90 elevation models out to 250 km ([horizons](horizon-v1/horizons.json)).

Deviations are in degrees, positive clockwise. Rakhigarhi, Shikarpur, Bhirrana, Kot Diji, Nausharo, Juni Kuran and Kotada Bhadli have coordinates and horizons but no measurable Harappan line in the imagery and no usable plan; the dating researcher recorded eight further towns that no measurer could locate or measure. Evidence tier 2 is a calibrated start stated on radiocarbon from the site, tier 3 an excavator's cross-dated start; no Bayesian model of a layout boundary has been published for any town ([dates](dates-v1/dates.json)).

The imagery measurers agree closely where the architecture is exposed. At Harappa they differ by 0.02°, at Lothal by 0.08°, at Dholavira by 3.0° and at Mohenjo-daro by 3.7°; neither difference exceeds three combined standard errors. Mohenjo-daro's difference has a cause in the city itself. Its house blocks and lanes run within a degree or two of north (img-A +0.5 ± 0.4), while the edges of its main north–south streets run +6.0 ± 1.0, and img-B's combined lower-town value (+4.2 ± 1.4) mixes the two. The plans of Marshall and Mackay show the same split: the HR, VS and DK street lines lie at +5.7 to +9.9 against the plans' arrows, and many block walls at +0.5 to +3.

## Wanzke's two figures

Wanzke's tables in *Interim Reports* vol. 2 are in gon, 400 to the circle (footnote 2, printed p. 38). His "system 1", "ca. 8 gon clockwise" (about 7.2°), is the main streets of the SD, L, HR, VS, Dales and DK areas, and his Fig. 11 draws them visibly turned. Only his p. 37 gives "1° to 2° clockwise", and the Aldebaran match rests on that sentence; the direction note and the secondary literature took the figure from there. Our measurements confirm both readings of the city: the main streets run near 6–7°, as his tables say, and the house blocks near 0.5–2°, as his p. 37 says. The frozen check D1 compares his figures with the main system the imagery measurers declared, which is the house blocks for img-A; 1–2° lies within two standard errors of it and 7.2° does not ([results](confirmation-v1/results.json), `D1_mohenjo_daro`). His Pleiades setting point for 1500 BCE (8.0 gon) is about 1.8° lower than the geometric value our Vondrák precession gives for Alcyone at the latitude of Mohenjo-daro ([validation](validation-v1/validation.json)).

## The frozen test

The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) was frozen at 01:49 UTC on 21 October, before any measurement was opened or any star position computed (SHA-256 in `PROTOCOL.sha256`). The validation gate passed: mean obliquity 23.973° in 2500 BCE and Thuban nearest the pole in 2796 BCE. Synthetic controls behaved as designed: a planted Pleiades model passed P0, P1 and P3, and deviations without a date trend failed P0 ([positive](synthetic-v1/positive/results.json), [negative](synthetic-v1/negative/results.json)).

Under the frozen rules a town's deviation comes from imagery, or from plans whose arrow is stated to point to true north. Only Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Lothal, Dholavira and Sutkagen-dor have imagery values, and Sutkagen-dor has no dated layout. Kalibangan, a discovery town, is backfilled and has no primary value. The [stage-1 choice](discovery-v1/choice.json) on Mohenjo-daro and Harappa picked the equinoctial sunset (χ² = 0.38), with Alcyone second (0.97), Aldebaran 12.0 and Procyon 14.7. The [stage-2 results](confirmation-v1/results.json) follow.

## The secondary analysis on plans

[Amendment 1](AMENDMENT-1.md) was written and hashed at 05:01 UTC, when we knew which towns had measurements and how their arrows were labelled but had read no value. It lets towns without imagery take the main line of the plan measurers, with an added error for the plan's north estimated on the four towns that have both. That calibration gives a mean plan-minus-imagery difference of +1.2° and a root mean square of 3.1° ([table](exploratory-v1/table.json), `plan_north_calibration`). Plan-A did not declare main systems before it stalled, so plan-B supplies the main lines; where plan-A measured the same towns, its values agree within a few degrees (Surkotada citadel −15.5 against −15.1; Chanhu-daro Mound II −22.1 against −20.7 before correction; Kalibangan KLB-2 −12.9 against −15.5).

With eleven dated towns the [exploratory results](exploratory-v1/results.json) are: P0 fails (r = 0.46, one-sided permutation p = 0.082, Spearman ρ = 0.33, leave-one-out minimum r = 0.07), and the confirmation towns alone give r = 0.52, p = 0.085, n = 8. The stage-1 choice with Kalibangan included is Alcyone, which then fits the confirmation towns badly (χ² = 243 on 8 towns, against 281 for cardinal intent and 268 for a constant offset). The power of P0 under the Pleiades model at these dates and errors was 0.62. The weak positive correlation depends on Rangpur, whose single long wall lies at +40.5° and whose layout Rao dated latest; without it r = 0.07 (p = 0.42, n = 10). Under the amendment's rule the credence falls to 0.04 and the status becomes weakened. The `credence_rule` field in the exploratory results applies the protocol's rule mechanically and reads 0.02; Amendment 1 replaces it for this analysis.

Two limits bound this result. The plans' arrows are unreliable at the level of several degrees: plan-B found the Dholavira castle at +6 to +8° on two plans, −7° on a third and −0.9° on the graticuled ASI survey, against −5.5° on imagery. And five of the eleven dates are excavators' cross-dated starts, among them Mackay's 3000 BCE for Chanhu-daro, which rests on cross-dating of the 1930s. Neither limit can rescue the claim in its stated form, because the plan-only towns deviate by 8 to 30°, beyond the reach of any star or equinox in this period, and the true-north towns already fail the Pleiades sign.

## Sensitivity

The [primary checks](sensitivity-v1/primary.json) show that P0 does not depend on the astronomy: extinction coefficients of 0.20 and 0.45, no extinction and a flat horizon leave r unchanged, and the stage-1 choice stays with the equinoctial sunset. Dates of first occupation reverse the sign (r = −0.46). Every imagery system taken as its own point, 37 in all, gives r = −0.45. In the [exploratory checks](sensitivity-v1/exploratory.json), first-occupation dates give r = −0.11, and IntCal20 dates of the oldest layout-phase determination exist for only five towns (r = 0.56, p = 0.18). The horizon matters for individual predictions only where hills are near: Mohenjo-daro's western horizon reaches 0.86° on the Kirthar range, Kanmer's 1.95°, Nausharo's 4.86°.

## Comparison with earlier values

Wanzke quotes Jansen (1979) for six sites, in gon from an unstated north. Where we measured the same walls the plan values agree: Surkotada town wall −16 gon (−14.4°) against our −15.1; Kalibangan main street −13 gon (−11.7°) against −15.5 and −12.9; Kalibangan exterior west wall −2 gon (−1.8°) against −3.6 for the KLB-2 west city wall; Sutkagen-dor −2 gon (−1.8°) against −0.4 on imagery. They disagree at Lothal, where Jansen's dock wall is +5.5 gon (+5.0°) and both imagery measurers find −1.9°. Vahia and Menon (2013, *Journal of Astronomical History and Heritage* 16: 261) write that "the whole city was inclined 6° to the west of north" (arXiv version, p. 6), which matches our −5.5° in size and direction.

## Decision and next evidence

We do not rerun this test on plans of unstated north. The decisive measurement is a true-north bearing at each town whose architecture is now covered: a GNSS or drone survey of exposed walls, or a published plan registered to ground control points. Kalibangan, Surkotada, Kanmer, Chanhu-daro, Farmana, Banawali, Rakhigarhi and Shikarpur are the towns that would raise the true-north count from four to twelve, and a Bayesian start boundary from new samples at Dholavira, Lothal, Surkotada and Mohenjo-daro would replace the cross-dated tier-3 starts. With the eleven dated towns of the secondary analysis measured to true north at an error of 1°, and their present date errors, the frozen P0 would detect the Pleiades model with power 0.97 ([planning simulation](planning-v1/power-true-north.json), post-outcome).

The boldest reading these measurements support is this. The builders of the Indus-plain cities fixed true north astronomically and laid out Harappa's Mound F, the house blocks of Mohenjo-daro and the Lothal dock within two degrees of it; the walled towns of Kutch, Saurashtra and the Ghaggar plain were set on local axes turned anticlockwise by 8 to 30°, and a town's deviation records its setting, not its date. The test that decides it is the true-north survey above, followed by a comparison of each town's axis with the azimuth of its local landform (the river channel, ridge or shoreline beside it, measured on the elevation model): the reading predicts that the regional towns deviate anticlockwise by more than 5° on true-north measurements and that most of them match their landform within 5°. It is recorded as [C-0016](../../knowledge/conjectures/C-0016-plains-cities-on-true-north.md); the tested star claim is [C-0015](../../knowledge/conjectures/C-0015-towns-laid-out-on-star-settings.md).
