# img-A: manual measurement of town axes on Esri World Imagery

This directory holds the img-A measurements of the town-axes package of 21 October 2026. We measured the directions of straight Harappan walls, street edges, drains and fortification faces on Esri World Imagery tiles by picking the two ends of each line in global Web Mercator pixel coordinates and refining them against the edge itself. One JSON file per town is in `towns/`, the picked endpoints and offsets that produce each file are in `picks/`, the code is in `code/`, and every imagery file, metadata query and gazetteer lookup is listed in `sources.json`. The imagery itself is third-party material; it stays under `data/raw/2026-10-21-town-axes/img-A/`, which Git ignores, with the attribution "Source: Esri, Vantor, Earthstar Geographics, and the GIS User Community".

## Shared assumptions

The tile service delivers 256-pixel tiles in Web Mercator (EPSG:3857) with north up. Web Mercator is conformal, so angles measured on the tile grid are true angles on the ground, and a line from pixel (x1, y1) to (x2, y2), with x growing east and y growing south, has the bearing atan2(x2 − x1, −(y2 − y1)) from true north. We fold the bearing into the range 0 to 180 degrees and the deviation into −45 to +45 degrees, positive clockwise, so that a line turned a few degrees clockwise from north and a line turned the same amount clockwise from east have the same deviation. Lengths use the Mercator scale at the latitude of the line's midpoint: one pixel at zoom z covers 2πR·cos(latitude)/(256·2^z) metres, with R = 6,378,137 m. The imagery is an orthorectified mosaic; we assume its residual distortion rotates a line of a few tens of metres by no more than about 0.2 degrees and add that as a floor to every line's error.

## Imagery and site location

For each town we first located the site from published positions and then on the imagery. We queried Wikipedia and Wikidata coordinates and OpenStreetMap's Nominatim gazetteer, read a few Wikipedia pages and one journal article for positions, and saved the lookups in `data/raw/.../img-A/meta/`. A published point was accepted only after we recognised the mound, its excavations or its conserved architecture around it on the imagery; the `location` field of each town file gives the coordinates we used and how we confirmed them.

We stitched the tiles covering each site into one PNG mosaic with `code/tiles.py mosaic`, which records the zoom, tile range, pixel origin, retrieval time and SHA-256 of the mosaic. At every site the service has real detail only to zoom 18. Requests at zoom 19 return a uniform grey "map data not yet available" tile, which the script now recognises by its MD5 hash and counts in the mosaic record; Kot Diji is the one place where the service also serves zoom 19, from a GeoEye-1 scene of 0.46 m, and there the extra zoom only enlarges the same pixels. We therefore measured at zoom 18, where one pixel covers 0.51 to 0.55 m on the ground at these latitudes. For each measured part of a town we asked the service's `World_Imagery/MapServer/identify` endpoint for the imagery under it, which returns the provider, product, sensor, acquisition date and source resolution; these records give the `imagery_date` of each source. We did not use Sentinel-2 as a second witness, because at 10 m it does not resolve the walls of any site we could measure.

## Picking a line

We looked at each site through crops of the mosaic drawn with a labelled grid in global pixel coordinates (`code/tiles.py crop`), first at reduced scale to find the exposed architecture and then at two to six times magnification to choose lines. A candidate line was a wall face, street edge, drain or fortification face that stayed straight and continuous for at least 20 m where the architecture allowed. On these images the sharpest edges are usually shadows: a standing wall casts a dark band on the side away from the sun, a street between rows of houses shows as a pale strip bounded by wall shadows, and an excavated wall face shows as the boundary between a bright wall top and a dark trench. We read provisional endpoints for the line off the grid.

Reading endpoints off a downscaled picture is good to a few pixels at best, so each provisional line was then checked on a rectified strip made by `code/strip.py`. The strip resamples the mosaic along the provisional line, one native pixel per column along the line and a fraction of a pixel per row across it, with a tick scale of the perpendicular offset in pixels on both sides. Two side panels plot the mean brightness across the line over its first and last quarter, which shows where a dark band or a step in brightness lies at each end. An edge that runs exactly along the provisional line appears as a horizontal feature on the zero row; an edge that is rotated against it slopes across the rows. We read the edge's offset at each end from the strip and its profiles and moved the endpoints by those offsets along the line's normal. Because each offset is read over a quarter of the line's length, this amounts to fitting the line to many points along the same edge rather than to two single pixels. We then drew the strip again along the corrected endpoints and kept the line only if the edge lay flat on the zero row from end to end. Lines whose edge broke into offset segments, bent, or faded over much of their length were rejected at this stage; at Mohenjo-daro, where the house blocks are dense with short walls, we rejected 21 of 39 candidate lines this way. The `picks/` files keep both the provisional endpoints and the offsets, so every measured endpoint can be redrawn.

As a check on whether the strips pulled our reading towards the line we had drawn, we re-ran three Harappa strips with one provisional end moved by 3 px; we read the same edge positions within 0.7 px.

## Errors, systems and the main layout

Each endpoint received a one-sigma perpendicular uncertainty between 0.8 and 2.5 px, chosen from the sharpness of the edge on the strip: about 1 px for a crisp shadow line, 1.5 to 2 px for a faint or soft edge, and up to 2.5 px for a broad, diffuse band. A line of length L pixels then has an angular error of √2·σ/L radians from its endpoints, combined in quadrature with the 0.2 degree floor. Each structure also carries a confidence label, which records how sure we are that the line is a Harappan structure rather than how precisely it is measured: high for unmistakable conserved walls, medium for clear walls whose identification depends on our reading of the site, and low for faint, short or possibly modern lines.

We grouped lines into systems by part of the town and by direction, putting a line and the lines at right angles to it into the same system when their deviations agreed within a few degrees. Where a set of lines in the same part of a town shared a distinct direction, such as the street edges at Mohenjo-daro or the L area of its citadel, we kept them as a separate system. For a system, `code/calc.py` computes the length-weighted mean deviation, the unweighted standard deviation of its lines, and a standard error that is the larger of two quantities: the scatter-based error of the weighted mean, using the effective number of lines implied by the weights, and the measurement error propagated from the individual line errors. The main system of a town is the one we judge to represent its overall layout, normally the largest coherent set of house walls or fortification faces; each town file explains the choice.

## What we excluded

We measured only structures that we could attribute to the Harappan period from their position in the excavated areas and their construction. We excluded roads, field boundaries, canals, fences, visitor paths, protective roofs, museum and site buildings, excavation trench edges and spoil heaps. At Mohenjo-daro we also excluded the Kushan-period stupa and monastery on the citadel, and at Kot Diji the 18th-century fort on the hill. Some lines remain uncertain: a sharp double line south-east of the Mound F "granary" at Harappa could be a modern boundary wall, and the walls of the Sutkagen-dor enclosure are identified from their straightness and position rather than from visible masonry. These carry low confidence and are reported in their own systems or with an explanation in the town's notes.

## Coverage

We measured lines at Mohenjo-daro (lower town and citadel), Harappa (Mound F), Lothal (dockyard and town), Dholavira (castle and bailey) and, among the optional towns, Sutkagen-dor (walled enclosure). Kalibangan, Surkotada, Banawali, Rakhigarhi, Chanhu-daro, Kanmer and Shikarpur are written as not measurable: their excavations are backfilled, grassed or hidden under scrub on the current imagery, and at Surkotada we could not identify the site at either published position. Among the optional towns, Kot Diji, Nausharo and Bhirrana are also written as not measurable for the same reasons. We looked at the published positions of Juni Kuran, Gola Dhoro, Desalpur, Khirsara, Kotada Bhadli, Kuntasi and Rojdi but could not identify the sites there (for Gola Dhoro both published points fall in salt pans and mangroves), so we wrote no files for them. The remaining optional towns were not attempted.

## Problems and repairs

Our first Mohenjo-daro mosaic was fetched at zoom 19 and consisted entirely of the grey placeholder tile; we discarded it, added the placeholder check to the mosaic script and refetched at zoom 18. Two crops came out black because we had typed coordinates outside the mosaic; the crop command now refuses a box that leaves the mosaic. Two mosaics, at Kot Diji and Nausharo, were first centred on mistyped coordinates that missed the site; we deleted them and fetched new ones centred on the mound. The first version of the strip tool marked the zero row with a dotted line that hid the very edge we were reading; we moved the offset ticks to the margins and added the brightness profiles. During the work the shared disk ran out of space; our own raw directory held about 200 MB, so we deleted only our viewing crops, which the scripts regenerate, and kept every mosaic whose SHA-256 is recorded.

## Reproducing

From the repository root with Python 3.13 and Pillow, NumPy, SciPy and Requests:

```text
python experiments/2026-10-21-town-axes/measure-img-A/code/tiles.py mosaic <slug> <tag> <lat> <lon> <half-width-m> --z 18
python experiments/2026-10-21-town-axes/measure-img-A/code/tiles.py crop <mosaic.png> <gx0> <gy0> <w> <h> --scale 3 --grid 10
python experiments/2026-10-21-town-axes/measure-img-A/code/strip.py picks experiments/2026-10-21-town-axes/measure-img-A/picks/<slug>.json --corrected
python experiments/2026-10-21-town-axes/measure-img-A/code/calc.py experiments/2026-10-21-town-axes/measure-img-A/picks/<slug>.json --write
python experiments/2026-10-21-town-axes/measure-img-A/code/sources.py
```

The tile service may serve newer imagery in future, which would change the mosaics and their hashes; the identify records in `sources.json` name the scene each measurement used.
