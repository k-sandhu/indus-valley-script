# Dating the town layouts: method

This directory records, for each Harappan town in `../towns-v1.json`, when its exposed layout was built and on what evidence. By layout we mean the fortification walls, platforms and street grid visible today; by layout phase, the excavator's phase or period in which they were first built. Towns were often occupied long before their layout, and different parts of one town were often walled at different times, so each town file in `towns/` describes every part separately: the excavator's label for its layout phase, the excavator's words on what was built and the page that says so, the excavator's date range and its basis, every published radiocarbon determination from the layout phase and from the phase before it, any published Bayesian model of the phase boundary, and later syntheses that re-date it. Each file also gives the phase of first occupation. `sources.json` lists all 199 sources searched or cited, and `code/` holds the scripts. The directions of walls and streets are left out throughout: they belong to the measuring agents, and the few orientation phrases that reached the files were removed (see Checks).

## How we searched

We started from the primary publication of the radiocarbon ages. The Tata Institute (TF), Physical Research Laboratory (PRL), Birbal Sahni Institute (BS) and University of Pennsylvania (P) laboratories published their South Asian determinations as date lists in *Radiocarbon*; we found these lists, with two sections of the journal's 1950-1965 comprehensive index, through Crossref (`code/crossref_date_lists.py`), downloaded them from Cambridge Core with their text layers (`code/fetch_sources.py`) and registered them as sources `rc-tf-i` to `rc-penn-xxii` (`code/radiocarbon_lists.py`). Two Indian compilations from the Internet Archive serve as a second witness for the TF and PRL dates of the 1960s and 1970s: Ramachandran 1975 (*Radiocarbon Dates of Archaeological Sites in India*, ASI), which prints each age on both half-lives, and Mandal 1972 (*Radiocarbon Dates and Indian Archaeology*). Every later determination came from the excavation report or journal paper that prints it, except the 2026 Mohenjo-daro results, which we know only from a newspaper's quotation of an unpublished report.

For the layout phases we read the excavators' own reports. Several were already held in the repository (Marshall 1931, Mackay 1938 and 1943, Vats 1940, Jansen and Urban 1987, Kenoyer and Meadow 2010, the Kalibangan Part II report, Rao 1963, the Shikarpur 2008-09 report, the Dholavira preliminary report of 2015). The Internet Archive supplied most of the rest: the Lothal, Surkotada and Kalibangan memoirs of the Archaeological Survey (MASI 78, 87 and 98), Nath's draft final report on Rakhigarhi, the Farmana report, Possehl and Raval's *Harappan Civilization and Rojdi*, *Pakistan Archaeology* 2 with Khan's Kot Diji report, and many numbers of *Puratattva* and *Ancient India*. The ASI's re-typeset volumes of *Indian Archaeology: A Review* gave the season-by-season accounts and their radiocarbon notes. Harappa.com supplied the Harappa Archaeological Research Project's papers, and HAL, Current Science, PLOS ONE, Frontiers and the journal sites supplied recent work. Scanned books without a usable text layer were read from rendered page images. Every file we relied on was saved under `data/raw/2026-10-21-town-axes/dating/` and logged with its URL, retrieval time and SHA-256 in the `acquisitions.jsonl` of its subdirectory; files named `*.bin` there are JPEG page images from the Internet Archive. We also queried the XRONOS chronometric database for India and Pakistan (`shared-xronos/`); it holds only four direct grain dates from these towns, none from a layout context, and we did not use it further.

The work was divided by region among eight research agents, all given the same instructions, conventions and independence rules: Sindh (Mohenjo-daro, Chanhu-daro, Lakhanjo-daro); Harappa and Ganweriwala; Kalibangan, Banawali and Karanpura; Rakhigarhi, Farmana, Bhirrana and Mitathal; Lothal and Saurashtra (Rangpur, Rojdi, Kuntasi); Dholavira and Kutch (Surkotada, Kotada Bhadli, Juni Kuran, Desalpur); Kanmer, Shikarpur, Gola Dhoro and Khirsara; and the optional Pakistani sites. The coordinating pass then validated every file, checked recorded values against their sources, harmonised the half-life fields and built `sources.json`. The scripts the agents used to assemble their files are kept in `code/agent_scripts/` as a record; some files were edited by hand after those scripts ran, so the scripts document the work rather than rebuild the files.

## Conventions

A radiocarbon age is recorded exactly as the source prints it, with the half-life the source states. The *Radiocarbon* lists state the 5568-year half-life (the Birbal Sahni lists say 5570), and their "B.C." figures are simply 1950 minus the BP age, not calibrated dates. Indian excavation reports often reprinted the same ages on the 5730-year half-life; for those we give the printed value in `age_bp_printed` and the 5568-year conventional age, the printed value divided by 1.03 and rounded, in `age_bp_5568`. When a source does not state the half-life, `half_life_printed` is null and the 5568-year fields are filled only where the laboratory reports conventional ages by convention (Beta Analytic, Groningen, Gliwice, Paleo Labo and other AMS laboratories, or a Pennsylvania reprint identical to the Pennsylvania list). Birbal Sahni ages without a stated half-life keep empty 5568-year fields, because the laboratory's practice varied: its lists of 1978-1982 use 5570 years, while Sarkar et al. 2016 state 5730 years for the Bhirrana series. A source that prints only a B.C. figure gets a BP value only if it states both the half-life and the reference year.

The same laboratory number appears once per source that prints it, and again under another source only when that source prints a different value, half-life, context, calibration or phase. This keeps disagreements visible, and it is why the 196 determinations we found (183 with laboratory numbers and 13 published without one) fill 382 entries. Phases are given in each source's own words, since several excavators relabelled their periods. A value taken from a later work that quotes an unavailable original names the later work as its source and the original in a note. Years BCE are positive and older first; uncalibrated and calibrated ranges are labelled as such in the text fields, since the excavators' ranges mix both. Page numbers are printed pages unless written "pdf p.". Null fields mean that no source we could read gives a value, and the notes say what was sought.

The optional towns, in brief: Kot Diji (Kot Dijian wall on bedrock and a later Harappan lane plan; 4 Pennsylvania dates), Nausharo (Period II enclosure and platforms, Period III blocks; 3 Beta dates from earlier periods), Allahdino (one planned building complex; 3 P dates), Sutkagen-dor (walled citadel of Dales's Phase 2; no dates), Ganweriwala and Lakhanjo-daro (no excavated layout published; 1 date at Lakhanjo-daro), Karanpura (Period II wall and Area E street; no dates), Farmana (Period IIA plan; 21 AMS dates by stratigraphic phase), Bhirrana (Period IIA wall; 7 BSIP dates), Mitathal (Period IIA; 3 PRL dates), Rojdi (Rojdi C wall; 15 dates), Rangpur (IIA platforms; no dates), Kuntasi (Period I wall; 2 dates), Kotada Bhadli (single-period wall; 4 AMS dates), Juni Kuran and Desalpur (Mature Harappan walls by thin reports; no dates), Gola Dhoro (Phase II wall; 2 results without laboratory numbers) and Khirsara (outer wall in the first and citadel in the second structural phase; 6 dates). We wrote no file for Kunal, Pabumath, Navinal or Balakot: the reports we could read describe no Harappan wall, platform or street plan at those sites.

No published Bayesian model of a layout boundary exists for any of these towns, and no radiocarbon sample comes from a foundation deposit of a wall except at Mohenjo-daro (2026, unpublished), Kotada Bhadli (ash beneath the wall) and Harappa's Mound ET (a hearth beneath the wall extension, giving a date after which it was built).

## What we could not obtain

The largest gaps are the compilations and recent chronological papers behind paywalls or lending restrictions. Possehl's *Radiocarbon Dates for South Asian Archaeology* (1989), *Indus Age: The Beginnings* (1999) and *The Indus Civilization* (2002) were not available; the last is lending-only on the Internet Archive, as are Agrawal and Kusumgar 1974 and Dales and Kenoyer 1986 on Mohenjo-daro. Sengupta et al. 2020 (*Journal of Quaternary Science* 35), the only radiocarbon series for Dholavira's seven stages, could be read only as an abstract; Goyal et al. 2013 (*Radiocarbon* 55) on Kanmer's chronology is closed on Cambridge Core and its open archive copy sits behind a bot check. The French mission's chronological papers on Chanhu-daro and the Nausharo reports in *Pakistan Archaeology* were likewise out of reach, and HAL, OpenEdition, Persée and the Arizona archive served bot-check pages that we did not try to pass. Part I of the Kalibangan Harappan volume (MASI 110), which holds the excavators' final chronology, has a dead ASI link and no other copy we could find. Each town file lists the items sought for that town.

## Conflicts

Four kinds of disagreement recur. The first is calibration: excavators of the 1960s and 1970s gave uncalibrated dates (Kalibangan Period II c. 2300-1750 B.C., Lothal Phase IIa c. 2350 B.C., Surkotada IA 2055-1970 B.C.), and the same determinations later calibrate some three to four centuries older (Kalibangan Period II from c. 2600 BC in Lal 1997 and Lal et al. 2003; Surkotada c. 2500-2300 BC in Possehl and Raval 1989). The second is half-life: Possehl and Raval's Table 1 prints the Lothal ages under a "5568 half-life" heading although they are the 5730-year values, so its calibrated ranges are about a century too old, and Kanungo et al. 2025 reprint them unchanged. The third is relabelling: HARP renumbered Harappa's early periods after the Ravi phase was found in 1996, Bisht moved Banawali's citadel and lower town from Period II to a new Period IC in 1986-87, and the Bhirrana excavators placed the first wall in three different sub-periods over three seasons. The fourth is that the radiocarbon often does not reach the excavator's start date: most of Harappa's Period 2 determinations and its only Period 3A determination calibrate after 2600 BC, Lothal's only Phase I and II dates are no older than its Phase IIIB anchor and were rejected by Rao, and Kanmer's single KMR IIa date is younger than the overlying KMR IIb date and every AMS date. Bhirrana is a special case: the Period IIA range of 4500-3000 BCE and first occupation of 7500-6000 BCE come from Sarkar et al. 2016, a synthesis co-authored by the excavator, and sit far outside the ranges of every other town. Smaller conflicts (misprinted laboratory numbers, sigmas that differ between reprints, a contradiction on Rao's p. 44, transcription slips in the re-typeset IAR volumes) are recorded in the `conflicts` field of the part concerned.

## Checks

`code/validate_towns.py` checks every file's keys, the order of BCE ranges, the 5730-to-5568 conversion, that every cited source is listed, and that each SHA-256 belongs to a local file or a logged download; all 29 files pass. `code/check_rc_against_text.py` looks for each laboratory number and printed age together in the text layer of its source: 213 of the 382 entries match automatically, and most of the rest come from scanned pages without a usable text layer or print a B.C. figure instead of a BP age. We checked a sample of those by eye against the page images: the 13 Rojdi ages in the PRL-VI table, five Beta ages in Meadow and Kenoyer's 2005 Table 2 for Harappa, the first rows of the Period I table on p. 25 of Lal et al. 2003, the seven IUAC ages in Vahia et al. 2016 for Rakhigarhi, TF-160 in TF-III, three Banawali ages in PRL-II, four Surkotada ages in TF-X and two Kanmer AMS ages; all agree with the files. `code/harmonize.py` applied the half-life rule above (filling the 5568-year fields for eight conventional ages and emptying them for five Birbal Sahni ages at Kanmer) and normalised five SHA-256 fields; `code/strip_orientation.py` removed the direction words from nine phrases in five files. `code/build_sources.py` writes `sources.json`, keeping variant citations where two towns cite different pages of the same work.

## What the dates support

Read together, the excavators' sequences point to two building horizons. Walled layouts already stood before the Mature Harappan at Harappa (Mounds AB and E in Period 2), Kalibangan (Period I), Kot Diji, Dholavira (Stage I), Banawali (Period IB) and, by the 2026 report, at Mohenjo-daro's first perimeter wall, so planned enclosure in the Indus plains and the Ghaggar basin began around or before 2800-2600 BCE. The walled towns founded on fresh ground in Kutch and Saurashtra have radiocarbon that calibrates mostly after about 2550 BCE: Surkotada IA (2555-2285 B.C. and later in Possehl and Raval's calibration), Kanmer's lower Harappan levels (about 2570-2150 cal BC), Kotada Bhadli (the ash under the settlement 2464-2206 BCE), with Lothal's few dates later still and Shikarpur undated. Dholavira's divided city of Stage III is dated by Bisht to c. 2800-2500 BCE on stratigraphy and pottery alone. Our working reading is that the Gujarat towns were laid out a century or more after Harappa's Period 3A walls. It fails if Bayesian start boundaries built from the determinations compiled here, together with new AMS dates from the foundation deposits of the walls at Dholavira, Surkotada, Lothal and Shikarpur, put the Gujarat foundations within a century of the start of Harappa Period 3A.
