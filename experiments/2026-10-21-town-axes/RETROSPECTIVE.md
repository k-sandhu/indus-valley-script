# Retrospective: the town-axes package, 21 October 2026

Contributors: Claude Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`) in the Claude Code desktop app as coordinator, with five general-purpose subagents of the same model as measurers img-A, img-B, plan-A and plan-B and as the dating researcher. The package answers study 4 of the [direction note of 20 October](../../knowledge/directions/parpola-forward-2026-10-20.md) and the data card [Q-1032](../../knowledge/questions/Q-1032-town-axis-table.md).

## Approach

We read Wanzke's paper on the scan before writing anything, then wrote the measurement and dating briefs, hashed them with the town list and launched the five agents in parallel. None of them was told about stars or calendars, each wrote only in its own directory, and the measurers were asked to keep values out of their replies. While they worked we wrote the protocol and froze it with its SHA-256, so the candidate targets, the precession and extinction models, the rules for a town's deviation and date, the stage-1 choice and the predictions were all fixed before any measurement was opened. Only then did we write and validate the astronomy code, the DEM horizon code and the scorer, and run synthetic positive and negative controls. The dates were applied and hashed before any bearing was read, the table was combined mechanically, stage 1 was hashed before stage 2 ran, and stage 2 ran once.

## What happened and what we changed

The scan's page offset in the brief we were given was reversed: printed p. 33 is PDF page index 35, not 31. Reading the right pages showed that Wanzke's architectural tables are in gon, so his main-street system is about 7.2°, while the "1° to 2°" that the direction note quotes comes from a single sentence on p. 37. We recorded this in the protocol before freezing it and left a note for the other sessions.

The first test of the astronomy module returned rising rather than setting azimuths for the three stars, because the side of the event was read from the target's name. The validation run against Wanzke's table exposed it before any real data were touched, and the fix takes the side from the fixed target list.

Imagery showed far less than we planned for. Of the eleven required towns only Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Lothal and Dholavira have exposed walls that both imagery measurers could measure; Kalibangan, a discovery town, is backfilled, and Surkotada's mound could not be found at its published coordinates. Nearly every published plan has an arrow lettered only "N". The frozen rules therefore left four dated towns with true-north values, against the eight the kill condition needs. Knowing only which towns had measurements and how their arrows were labelled, we wrote and hashed Amendment 1 before reading any value: a secondary analysis on plan-based deviations, with the plans' north error calibrated against imagery. The primary analysis ran exactly as frozen.

Plan measurer B stalled for ten minutes on a download and resumed when asked. Plan measurer A wrote eight town files without declaring main systems and fell silent for more than an hour; its last write, at 04:53 UTC, was an empty Kanmer specification file, made when the disk had no free space, which probably stalled it. After two requests we stopped it. Its files are kept as they are, enter the table as plan measurements without a declared main system, and are used in the report only to check plan-B's values. It wrote no METHOD.md or sources.json.

The C: drive filled to zero during the run from causes outside the package, and at one point command output could not be written. We switched the horizon code to read DEM tiles into memory, recording each tile's URL and SHA-256 instead of keeping it, deleted our own cached tiles and scratch environment, rebuilt the environment with hard links from the uv cache, and asked the session that started us before touching anything outside the package. Space recovered later.

Three small repairs were made before their outputs were used. The IntCal20 check in `dates.py` matched "Phase II" inside "Phase IIIB" at Lothal; we narrowed the keys and rebuilt the date table, which nothing had yet read, before hashing it. The sensitivity script failed on towns with a first-occupation date and no layout date; it now gives them the tier-3 date error. The figure's second panel included a plan-based town and was redrawn with imagery towns only.

All five subagents wrote temporary files to one shared scratch directory, which the briefs did not mention. The dating agent later copied that directory's scripts into `dating-v1/code/agent_scripts/` as its record, sweeping in working files of plan-B, img-A and img-B; we moved them to local-only storage and listed them in `MOVED.md` there. The shared directory weakens the independence guarantee at the level of files. We checked what we could: plan-B's transcript shows no read of another measurer's directory, the two imagery measurers' code and pick files never reference each other, and their methods differ (endpoints against gradient histograms). The transcripts of the other four agents were not retained, so their file access cannot be audited.

## Outcome

The settlement-axis table covers 29 towns, 21 with coordinates and horizons, 14 with measured lines, 4 with two independent true-north values and 11 with layout dates. The frozen test is undecided; its Pleiades version fails on Dholavira and Lothal; the declared secondary analysis on eleven towns finds no significant correlation of deviation with layout date, and C-0015 is weakened to 0.04. The [report](REPORT.md) gives the numbers and the boldest reading they support, recorded as C-0016.

## What we would do differently

A protocol for archaeological measurement should plan for sites that cannot be seen from above. We would ask from the start for true-north registration of the excavators' plans against ground control points visible in imagery (corners of standing walls, survey pillars, modern structures shown on the plan), which gives a measured north for backfilled towns, and we would name a substitute discovery town for one that might be invisible. We would also ask each measurer to write its town files and main-system declarations in a fixed order, so that a stalled agent leaves complete records behind, and give each agent its own scratch directory.
