# Plan-B measurement of town axes from published plans

This directory holds the plan-B measurement of the directions of Harappan walls, streets, drains and fortifications. We worked only from published plans: excavation reports, annual reviews, journal articles and a World Heritage nomination. For each town we found the plans that show Harappan architecture together with a north arrow, measured the arrow and each straight line on the rendered page, and expressed every line's direction relative to that plan's own north. We did not use satellite imagery and did not correct any plan for magnetic declination. Each plan is measured and reported separately, so a town with three plans has three independent sets of lines and systems.

## What a measurement means

A line's bearing is its direction clockwise from the plan's north, between 0 and 180 degrees. Its deviation is the bearing folded to the nearest cardinal direction, between -45 and +45 degrees, positive when the line is turned clockwise. Because every bearing is relative to the arrow drawn on the plan, the reliability of a town's value rests as much on the arrow as on the walls. Most arrows in these reports are lettered only "N" and say nothing about whether they show true, magnetic or grid north; we classified those as `unspecified`. Mackay's Chanhu-daro plan is the one labelled magnetic ("MAG. NORTH NOV 2ND 1935"). The ASI's 2020 contour survey of Dholavira is the one plan whose north comes from a printed latitude-longitude graticule rather than a drawn symbol, and we classified it as `true`. Kanmer's arrow is drawn along the graph paper on which the plan was made.

Several towns show that drawn arrows cannot be taken on trust. At Dholavira the castle walls are square to each other on four plans, yet relative to the plans' own arrows they lie six to eight degrees clockwise on two plans, seven degrees anticlockwise on a third and within one degree of the graticule on the fourth. At Surkotada the same ramparts on two figures of one report differ by about five degrees relative to their arrows. We report all of these as separate sources and say in each town's notes which we prefer and why.

## Finding the plans

We began with the reports already held in the repository: Marshall 1931 and Mackay 1938 on Mohenjo-daro (we downloaded their plate volumes), Vats 1940 on Harappa, Mackay 1943 on Chanhu-daro, Bisht's 2015 preliminary report on Dholavira, the Kalibangan report Part II, the Shikarpur 2009 report and the Rakhigarhi inventories. We then searched the Internet Archive's catalogue by title through its advanced-search and metadata interfaces, which gave Joshi's Surkotada memoir (MASI 87), Rao's Lothal memoir (MASI 78, part 1, a scan whose plates are missing), Wheeler's Harappa 1946 report, Nath's Rakhigarhi report, Possehl and Raval on Rojdi, three issues of *Puratattva* and thirty-one volumes of *Indian Archaeology - A Review* between 1955 and 2001. The UNESCO World Heritage Centre supplied the Dholavira nomination dossier and its volume of drawings. Kanmer's fort plan came from Kharakwal and colleagues' paper in a Nichibunken symposium volume, found through a web search. Every download was saved under `data/raw/2026-10-21-town-axes/plan-B/downloads/` by `code/fetch.py`, which records the URL, final URL, retrieval time, HTTP status, size and SHA-256 in `downloads.json`; `sources.json` in this directory compiles those records with the repository files we used.

The Kalibangan report Part II held locally covers finds and has no plans of structures; Part I, which has them, is not online. We used instead the two settlement plans printed in *Indian Archaeology - A Review* for 1967-68 and 1968-69. For Banawali we used Bisht's site plans in the same series for 1983-84 and 1987-88.

## Rendering and picking

Scanned pages were rendered with PyMuPDF (`code/render.py`) at the scan's native resolution, which was 100 dpi for the archive.org plate volumes and 300 dpi for the other scans. Where a figure is embedded as a single raster image, as in the Dholavira dossier, the *Indian Archaeology* volumes and Rao's Lothal memoir, we extracted the image itself at native resolution (`code/extract_image.py`) and measured on it; the figure's placement on the page is a rigid transform and does not change angles. The Kalibangan 1967-68 plan is printed white on black, so we measured on an inverted copy.

The Read tool shows images downscaled, so we cropped each plan to the arrow, the scale bar or a group of structures and drew a labelled pixel grid over the crop (`code/crop.py`), which lets coordinates be read directly in page pixels. For each line we picked two endpoints by eye on a wall face, street edge, drain or fortification face, choosing the longest continuous straight stretch and avoiding corners. `code/refine.py` then fits the line to the drawn ink: it takes the dark pixels (or, for solid masses on a stippled ground, the Canny edge pixels) within a few pixels of the hand-picked segment, drops the ends, and fits a total-least-squares line in three passes. Where a line is interrupted by lettering, as the Chanhu-daro meridian is, we fitted the clean pieces together. `code/check.py` draws each pick and its fit on the crop so that every line was checked by eye against the drawing before it was accepted; `code/lsd.py` runs OpenCV's line segment detector to suggest candidate edges, which we then chose among and re-fitted. Arrows were fitted the same way along their shafts, or, for compass roses and double-lined arrows, taken from the midline of the shaft to the tip. Scale bars were picked at their end ticks and converted to metres per pixel; lengths in feet were converted at 0.3048 m.

All picks, fits, sources and groupings for a town are written in `code/specs/<town>.json`, and `code/build_town.py` turns each spec into `towns/<town>.json`, so every number can be regenerated from the spec and the rendered image. Rendered images and crops stay in the Git-ignored raw directory, and their SHA-256 values are recorded in the town files.

## Errors

Each line's one-sigma error combines three independent parts in quadrature. The picking term is the angle subtended by the endpoint uncertainty over the line's length, atan(sqrt(2) e / L), where e is the larger of one pixel and the RMS scatter of the ink about the fitted line, and L is the line's length in pixels. The arrow term applies the same formula to the arrow shaft and adds a drafting term of 0.5 degrees for a ruled arrow, 0.75 to 1 degree for a small or decorative rose and 1.5 degrees for a short freehand arrow, chosen from how the arrow is drawn. The rendering term covers distortion of the reproduction. A rigid rotation of the scan turns the arrow and the walls together and cancels, so what matters is shear and curvature; where a plan has a printed frame, grid or graticule we measured its lines (`code/gridtrace.py` for long grid lines) and took the root sum square of half the difference between parallel lines and the frame's departure from a right angle. Where there was no frame we assumed a value between 0.1 and 0.7 degrees according to the kind of reproduction, from a native digital drawing to a creased fold-out photographed at 100 dpi, and said so in the source's `rendering_check` note.

A system is a group of lines in one part of a town that share a direction within a few degrees, together with lines square to them. Its deviation is the length-weighted mean of its lines' deviations; its standard deviation is the weighted scatter of the lines; and its standard error is the root sum square of the scatter divided by the square root of the effective number of lines and of the arrow and rendering terms, which are common to all lines of one plan and do not average down. Where the parts of a town differ, as the citadel and lower town do at Kalibangan, or where one plan disagrees with another, we report each as its own system and name the main one with a reason.

## Problems and how we resolved them

The disk filled during the work. We deleted our own scratch crops and two large downloads that we did not use (the HARP 1986-1990 volume and the Dholavira dossier's annexes); their acquisition records remain in `downloads.json` and `sources.json`. The first download of the Kanmer paper returned an HTTP 406 page because the server rejected the request headers; we changed the client's headers and fetched the PDF again. Several plans are printed sideways or upside down on the page, and many arrows point to the left or right; because every bearing is taken from the arrow's measured page direction, orientation on the page does not affect the result. Some plans could not be found or used. Rakhigarhi's published site plans show mounds only and are marked not to scale, and Nath's report reproduces sections; Shikarpur's one structural plan is replaced by an image placeholder in the open-access article we found. Both towns are reported as not measurable, with the sources we examined listed in their files.

## Files

- `towns/<town>.json`: one file per town in the format of the brief, with extra fields recording the arrow measurement, scale bar, rendering check, render file and its SHA-256 for each source.
- `sources.json`: every source used or examined, with acquisition records.
- `code/`: the scripts named above and the per-town specs in `code/specs/`.
