# Full Tell Brak catalogue recovered

We recovered Donald M. Matthews’s *The Early Glyptic of Tell Brak: Cylinder Seals of Third Millennium Syria* (1997), OBO Series Archaeologica 15, as a 387-page institutional scan. The complete catalogue resolves the earlier acquisition target Reg.257 to Matthews catalogue 308 and TB1058. We admit no legend. The frozen totals remain 11 Akkadian legends, 23 Tamil-Brahmi legends including coins, and 16 Tamil seals and rings.

The DOI redirected to ZORA’s new handle 20.500.14742/147809. Ordinary pages returned bot checks. The public PID API returned the item metadata and links to its public original-file bundle. We followed those links to the PDF. The title page confirms author, title, series and publishers. Repository metadata gives 1997 and describes the scan as the published version. The delivered file contains 9,033,197 bytes. Its checksum differs from the repository’s stored bitstream metadata; the delivered file includes a current repository cover. We pin both observations in source-manifest.json without claiming a checksum match.

Matthews printed p.265, PDF280, describes catalogue 308 as an impression on an irregular bulla fragment, with crushed grooves on its back. Its aliases are REG257 and TB1058, its location is W ER, and its extant height is 20 mm. The two-line cased inscription accompanies a lion fighting a bullman. Eidem’s Appendix B, printed p.308, PDF323, identifies the cup-bearer Išar-mūpī and publishes the legend with a partly restored final sign in the name. His introduction on printed p.307 places this name among parallels in the southern Old Akkadian milieu. We defer admission because completeness, the strict language rule and baseline deduplication still need adjudication. The local ledger preserves the exact edition reading. This is a source-dependent reading of an excavated carrier, not another excavation observation.

Catalogue image number 257 and excavation registration REG257 belong to different identifier systems. Otto’s accessible article, *Much more than just a Decorative Element: The Guilloche as Symbol of Fertility*, fig.6 on printed p.382, labels Matthews 257 as a guilloche-only design. This does not identify REG257. Its PDF and extracted text remain local, with the publisher’s private-use notice. No new inscription was admitted from it.

The revised Tamil catalogue remains unavailable within eight search strings. The library book-detail route returned HTTP404. The strongest supported reading is that Reg.257 records a named cup-bearer on a Tell Brak bulla fragment. A specialist check of the restored name sign, language classification and aliases against the frozen corpus would decide whether this legend can enlarge the comparison population.
