# A town has several axes and several building dates

We built a source-backed register of 34 candidate Harappan towns, including all eleven required by Q-1032. It preserves 1,380 line and system records, 118 assertions about dated town parts and 620 radiocarbon assertions. The nonempty laboratory labels form 298 town-and-label keys; seven assertions have no label. Repeated uses of a sample retain their separate sources, and physical sample identities have not been adjudicated. The [coverage](run-v5/coverage.json), [record audit](audit-v5.json) and [town table](TABLE.md) give the exact counts and routes into the observations.

The measurements support a register of architectural parts. They do not support assigning every town one bearing and one founding year. Mohenjo-daro has both nearly cardinal axes and turned street axes in its published survey. Surkotada's exposed stone walls belong to a rebuilding of an earlier enclosure. At Lothal two imagery products give different bearings for the dock. The table keeps these distinctions in the fields used for subsequent analysis.

## How to read the bearings

A line bearing is clockwise from north, modulo 180 degrees. A system's deviation folds its axes to the nearest cardinal direction, between -45 and +45 degrees; positive means clockwise. Every source keeps its north classification and arrow label. A plan lettered only N remains unspecified. A bearing transformed by registration is stored separately, with the original plan reference and the failed package validation. It is exploratory evidence.

Two observers of one image estimate reading consistency. They do not supply two independent georeferenced images. The inherited Sentinel-2 measurements also retain the verification study's warning that errors depend strongly on line length and were underestimated by its fitting procedure. No new pooled town bearing is calculated here. The site coordinates in the index are labelled by their evidence quality; exact line endpoints remain in the measurement records or their hash-preserved source records.

At Lothal, the earlier comparison gives -1.88 +/- 0.30 degrees on Esri imagery and +0.19 +/- 0.30 degrees on Sentinel-2 for the dock. The reported difference is 2.07 degrees, about 4.9 combined reported standard errors. The Sentinel-2 empirical error at these short lengths is larger than that nominal error. We retain the discordance and both error records, without treating either product as an independent validation of the other. See [comparison-v3](../2026-10-21-town-orientations-verification/comparison-v3/comparison.json) and the source-linked rows in [measurements](run-v5/measurements.jsonl).

## Primary-source checks

We visually read Wanzke's complete article, *Axis Systems and Orientation at Mohenjo-Daro*, printed pp. 33-44, on the held Jansen and Urban volume. These are zero-based PDF indices 35-46. The scan has no usable text layer. We entered 35 published rows separately from our image measurements in [published-wanzke.json](run-v5/published-wanzke.json). The original gon values, intervals, lengths, page locators and precision statements remain available; one gon equals 0.9 degrees.

Wanzke's p. 33 describes an astronomically oriented survey grid and lists different excavated axes. Moneer N-S 5 and N-S 6 have deviations -0.4 and -0.1 gon, while the HR First Street entry is 8.3 gon. These become -0.36, -0.09 and +7.47 degrees. On p. 34 he distinguishes the two systems and warns that the definition of north and precision at other towns are unknown. His other-town entries are attributed to Jansen 1979; they enter our register as published reports with unspecified north. The topographical axes have a stated possible error of +/-1 gon, which we retain as a bound, without calling it a one-sigma error. Source identity, hash and reading locations are in [source-checks.json](source-checks.json).

We also inspected Joshi's Surkotada Fig. 8, zero-based PDF index 61, an unpaginated fold-out. Its arrow reads N and does not state true or magnetic north. Its western and southern ramparts and gateway match the exposed architecture at the corrected site. The plan identifies the stone rebuilding as IC. The inherited dating records distinguish that building episode from IA, when the enclosure line was established.

## Surkotada at the corrected location

The earlier imagery search used a point roughly 8 km west of the exposed citadel. We used the corrected location, about 23.6113 N, 70.9174 E, and inspected the current and archived Esri mosaics. Vegetation obscures the faces in the current image; archived release 8432 shows them more clearly. That source choice and the picks are exploratory because earlier bearings were already known. The underlying image date is not supplied; the release number is preserved without turning it into an exposure date.

The western face is 26.7 m long and has a WGS84 geodesic deviation of -11.83 degrees. The southern face is 25.7 m long and has a deviation of -11.60 degrees. Three-pixel endpoint uncertainty gives approximate digitizing errors of 4.96 and 5.18 degrees. Total error remains unknown because the provider supplies no local orientation accuracy and we have no restoration survey. The two adjacent walls share one image and one observer. [Picks](new-observations.json), [geodesic check](geodesic-check.json), and measurement IDs `new-surkotada:SK-W` and `new-surkotada:SK-S` preserve the calculation and its limits.

For the phase evidence we checked Agrawal and Kusumgar's *Tata Institute Radiocarbon Date List X* (1973), printed pp. 574 and 579-580, zero-based PDF indices 0, 5 and 6. Its individual BP ages use a 5568-year half-life. The IC-associated charcoal determinations in the inherited excavation join are TF-1297, 3635 +/- 95 BP; TF-1311, 3625 +/- 90; TF-1294, 3620 +/- 95; and TF-1307, 3510 +/- 105. The date list gives sample contexts, while the excavation join supplies their IC attribution. Its accompanying BC figures are arithmetic ages, not calibrated calendar dates; its general comments use a different half-life, stated in the p. 574 footnote. These four determinations were already present in the independent verification. Our contribution checks their primary printing and connects the later wall episode explicitly to the table. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033822200009024), [radiocarbon assertions](run-v5/radiocarbon.jsonl).

## Horizons and dates

The inner 2 km is excluded. We retained 0.1-degree rays, eye height 1.6 m and terrestrial-refraction coefficient 0.13. Forty-six tiles, 349,539,705 bytes, were acquired to D: with URLs, retrieval times and hashes. All returned HTTP 200. The chronology table retains the two compilations' period labels, calendar claims, radiocarbon contexts, laboratory codes, half-life conventions, errors and published calibrations. ## Checks and remaining evidence

The register builder passes its structural checks, and [audit-v5.json](audit-v5.json) verifies 207 input hashes and 3,009 references, including their JSON Pointers. No radiocarbon source key is unresolved. The [bench selftest](bench-selftest.json) passes on synthetic controls; it is a harness check. We have read the earlier settlement-axis use records and spend no new confirmation population. No scientific orientation statistic or credence is updated.

The [asset-hash audit](source-integrity-v4.json) matches 444 source-use records to referenced assets. Ninety-five records lack a path-and-hash pair at that metadata level; their original source records remain linked. Earlier asset audits are preserved and superseded because they treated annotated file lists as literal paths. The knowledge-base and prose checks pass. The broad repository verifier retains the same 58 pre-existing link errors, with no new errors; [CHECKS.json](CHECKS.json) records the comparison.

Q-1032 remains open. Most towns need a surveyed true-north bearing or a plan tied to surviving control points. Every town needs a defensible association between the measured wall episode and its dated deposits before an orientation-date test can use it. The [search scope](SEARCH-SCOPE.md) records the 34-town frame and the added publication route; it does not assert that the frame exhausts every measurable Harappan settlement.

The strongest reading supported by this register is that the relevant unit is a particular architectural system in a particular building episode. Harappan towns preserve both cardinal and turned systems, and a single town-wide bearing can hide that structure. The deciding test is a true-north survey of named, phase-linked walls: measure Mohenjo-daro's Moneer axes and HR First Street, Surkotada's western and southern IC ramparts, and the Lothal dock with positional control sufficient for an angular error below 0.5 degrees. Before reading the survey, freeze those wall identities, document restorations and register the dated stratigraphic joins. The reading predicts that Mohenjo-daro retains both a near-cardinal system and a street system turned more than 5 degrees, and Surkotada's IC ramparts remain anticlockwise by more than 5 degrees. If the surveyed phase-linked walls collapse onto one common axis within their errors, this reading fails; the dock survey separately decides the imagery discrepancy.
