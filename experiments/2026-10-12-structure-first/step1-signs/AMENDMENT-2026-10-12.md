# Amendment after review: adoption rule, run order and post-outcome comparisons

Recorded 12 October 2026 after the adversarial review (`../review/REVIEW.md`, findings 4, 7, 10 and 14). The frozen run `run-2026-10-12/` is unchanged.

**Adoption rule.** The protocol adopts "the coarsest grouping that passes" as the feature layer. The code ranked coder 1's families first, although coder 2's families are coarser (63 groups over the vocabulary, 59 attested, against 85 and 78) and also pass. Under the protocol's wording the adopted layer is coder 2's family partition. The `decision` field in the frozen summary still names coder 1 and is superseded by this note. With a token-weighted adjusted Rand index of 0.975 between the two partitions, the choice changes little.

**Run order.** The plan ran steps 2–5 after step 1 had fixed the representation. In practice steps 2, 4, 3 and 5 ran while the second coder was working, and their summaries were written before step 1's endpoint. They use Wells codes alone, with numerals identified by coder 1's stroke families and counts. No step used a family layer, and no family-level version of test 2d or of the segmentation was run. The step reports therefore describe Wells-code results.

**Post-outcome comparisons.** The review ran five comparisons that the protocol did not specify. They are reported in the step 1 report under that label: random contiguous cuts of the Wells number line (100 partitions with the coder-1 group count), the identity partition that isolates the effect of model mixing, numeral families split into single codes, folds rebuilt so that one-substitution near-copies cannot cross them, and the baseline with an end symbol. None changes the frozen pass decision. The first two change how the gain should be described.
