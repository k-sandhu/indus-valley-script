# Second-coder notes

I coded the 722 codes shown on the sixteen contact sheets (001 to 958, excluding 000 and 995 to 999) from the sheet images alone, using cell crops enlarged twice to check fine detail and a scanline count of strokes for 039, 049, 050, 051 and 056. A code joined a family when its largest graphic element matched the family's element and everything else in the glyph could be described as an addition from the fixed vocabulary; a code whose main element matched no other code stayed alone. Several conventions follow from that rule. One or two lines inside a shape count as `inner` and three or more as `hatch`. Teeth or ticks on the inner edge of a rectangle or oval count as `side`. A stem under a U counts as `pole`. Multiple parallel bent strokes in the knee-shaped family B294 count as `double`. For numerals the stroke count goes in `count` and `mods` stays empty unless something is added, so slanted strokes (025, 029, 059) are `variant`, bracketed numerals are `encl`, the bar over 051 is `cap`, and 931 (five strokes under an arch) and 041 (one short stroke between two tall ones) are short-stroke numerals with `encl`. The result has 63 families, 10 of them single codes, and 100 of the 722 rows are marked low confidence.

The hardest boundaries were between related outline shapes. I separated the fish (B220) from the plain lens (B790) by the crossed tail, which left 250, 251 and 827 with the fish but sent lenses with crossing lines at both ends (252, 253, 255, 256, 287, 794) to B790 with `X`. The plain X (B645) and the X closed into one or two triangles (B530) are separate families, with the bowtie 540 coded as B530 plus a closing stroke. Among U-shapes, only out-turned ear hooks put a code in the jar family B740; forked, looped or in-turned rims (718, 720, 759 to 786) went to B700 with `top`. The comb on a stem (B415) and the prongs over an oval (B350) meet at 353 and 354, and I split them by whether the stems below the bar close into a loop. Hatched-body birds (B268), hatched teardrops (B275) and the squatting figure (B175) share hatching and leg ticks, so 264, 277, 278, 953, 954 and 955 are uncertain, as is placing the bent bands 280 and 281 in B294 and the outline chevrons 923 to 925 with the brackets in B900. I could not assign the following with any confidence: the single codes 058, 092, 180, 258, 310, 930, 932, 952 and 957; the composite signs 940 to 947 and 951, which juxtapose two or more signs and which I attached to the family of their largest element; and 682, where a large caret contains a small asterisk. The codes shown with 0/0 counts (114, 258, 275, 289, 399, 726, 867, 873, 954, 955) were coded like the rest, from the rendered glyph only.
