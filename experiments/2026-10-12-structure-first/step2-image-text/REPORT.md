# Step 2: image–text pairing on tablets and no detectable transfer to seals

On bas-relief and incised tablets from Harappa, texts that recur stay with one image class, and a text's image can be predicted from other text types better than chance. Under the frozen rule this means that on those tablets texts pair with their images within the genre. On copper tablets, recurring texts also stay with one image class, but held-out prediction fails, so the frozen conclusion does not apply to copper. Copper sign groups specific to an image class do not occur more often on seals showing the same animal; the test could detect an enrichment of fivefold or more, not a twofold one. The endpoint is [run-2026-10-12-v3](run-2026-10-12-v3/summary.json); earlier runs and the corrections are described in the [first](AMENDMENT-2026-10-12.md) and [second](AMENDMENT-v3-2026-10-12.md) amendments, and the near-duplicate, power and object-level checks come from the [review](../review/REVIEW.md).

## Prior work

Parpola (1994; 2008, pp. 134–136) classified the Mohenjo-daro copper tablets and observed that tablets of one image type share an obverse text; the copper result of test 2a restates that observation on the Lipi export. The Harappa moulded and incised tablets were published with their image–text sets in the HARP and CISI volumes.

## Population

Objects come from the full face export. An object's image class is the union of the image labels on its faces, with unicorn variants, goat variants and short-horned bulls merged (rules in the second amendment); objects labelled none, other or unknown are unlabelled. Texts are complete faces with no unknown sign, in reading order. There are 190 copper objects (162 with a text, 76 with one image class), 709 bas-relief objects and 527 incised objects. The largest copper classes are hare (12 objects), anthropomorph (11), elephant (11), composite animal (8) and gaur (8).

## Results

| Test | Copper | Bas-relief | Incised |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2a: repeated texts on one image class | 10 of 12 (null mean 0.56, p = 0.0001) | 31 of 42 (null 2.18, p = 0.0001) | 10 of 14 (null 2.00, p = 0.0001) |
| 2b: sign-pair Jaccard, same class minus different class | 0.040 (p = 0.026) | 0.0035 (p = 0.054) | 0.0019 (p = 0.31) |
| 2c: image predicted from other text types | 19.2%; majority 26.9%; p = 0.26 | 24.8%; majority 21.5%; null 16.0%; p = 0.003 | 57.4%; majority 48.1%; null 42.3%; p = 0.002 |

Test 2a is what copies of one model produce: the 12 hare tablets carry one text and the 11 anthropomorph tablets carry one text. The copper 2b difference rests on few pairs: 4 of the 33 same-class text pairs share any sign pair, one of them a near-duplicate, and without near-duplicates the difference is 0.024 (p = 0.025). Without near-duplicates the bas-relief 2b difference falls to 0.0017 (p = 0.15), while 2c survives on both Harappa genres (bas-relief 25.5% against a 21.5% majority, p = 0.0025; incised 55.6% against 48.1%, p = 0.004). Treating the copper "other" bin as a class raises copper 2c to 44.7% against a majority of 42.1%.

**Test 2d, transfer to seals.** Eight labelled copper classes also occur as seal field animals. For each, the sign pairs and triples found in that copper class and in no other labelled copper class were counted in 1,209 distinct seal text–animal pairs. Seals whose animal matches the copper class contain those groups 3 times; permuting animal labels gives a mean of 3.01 (p = 0.62). Half of the expected count comes from the unicorn class, where 2 of 921 unicorn seals and none of the 288 other seals contain unicorn-specific copper groups. Elephant-specific groups occur on 18 seals with other animals and none of the 29 with elephants (0.43 expected); rhinoceros-specific groups on 14 seals with other animals and none of 20 rhinoceros seals; anthropomorph-specific groups on 12 others and the single anthropomorph seal does not contain them. Counting seal objects instead of distinct text–animal pairs gives the same result. In simulation, raising each class's rate on matching seals to *k* times its rate elsewhere gives power 0.22 at *k* = 2, 0.91 at *k* = 5 and 1.0 at *k* = 10.

## What the result decides

The frozen rule's conclusion, that texts pair with their images inside their genre and are not reused as labels across genres, applies to the bas-relief and incised tablets, where 2a and 2c pass and 2d fails. For copper, 2c fails, so the rule's condition is not met; copper 2a restates Parpola's observation and copper 2b is descriptive. Test 2d gives no support for the strongest label reading, in which a text names the depicted animal and recurs where that animal is shown, at the fivefold level the test could detect. Three readings remain open: the copper text names something associated with the image other than the animal, seal texts never name their emblem animal, or the pairing is a convention of producing these objects. A comparison between seals and sealings from one excavated deposit, where the same issuer is expected on both, would separate them better than a cross-genre count.
