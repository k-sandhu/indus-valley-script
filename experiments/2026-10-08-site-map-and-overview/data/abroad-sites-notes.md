# Locations and dates for the finds-abroad rows

Status: exploratory, 8 October 2026. [abroad-sites.json](abroad-sites.json) lists all 36 rows of the finds-abroad census ([site-rows.json](../../2026-10-06-evidence-census/sites-deep/d-finds-abroad/site-rows.json)) under their census names. Object counts, classes and source ids are copied from the census `published_counts` without reconciliation, together with the census minimum and local face count. The five Pakistani rows and Shortughai are kept as the census assigned them; the Pakistani rows carry `south_asia_cross_reference: true`.

Coordinates come first from Pleiades representative points (23 rows), then from published gazetteers: UNESCO World Heritage component tables for Hili, Umm an-Nar and Janabiyah, the Bahrain Ministry of Culture page for Al-Hajjar, and the Pakistan Department of Archaeology and Museums site register for Nindowari, Miri Qalat, Sibri and Kinneru Damb. Three rows fall back to a labelled last resort: Wikidata settlement points for Ras al-Jinz and Kalba, and German Wikipedia for Murda Sang. Where the census had already recovered a UNESCO or Getty coordinate, the Pleiades point agrees with it to within about 0.3 km, except Tepe Hissar, where the minute-rounded Getty point lies about 3 km west. Several points mark a site complex rather than the findspot. Kish is a centroid of two mounds, the Konar Sandal point covers the North and South mounds, Failaka uses Tell F6 for seals from F3 and F6, the Saar point marks the settlement beside the cemetery that yielded the seal, and the Salut point marks the Iron Age settlement near tower ST1. The Kalba and Ras al-Jinz points are town or village locations, because sites K4 and RJ-2 were not found in a gazetteer. The DOAM point for Nindowari differs by roughly 80 km from the Wikipedia point; the register value is used and the conflict is recorded. Each row's `precision_note` states these limits.

Two rows are left unplaced. Luristan is a provenance region for an unexcavated seal, and Dilmun is the British Museum's production-place label for a seal excavated at Ur (Gadd no. 16). The Ornach area row is placed at Kinneru Damb as a candidate locality, because the census has not verified that the Kinneru caption refers to the local fragment.

Dates are BCE. Each entry's `basis` records what the range describes. "Object context" means the dated layer or feature of a counted object: Gadd's 1932 context list for Ur, Kish, Tell Asmar and Tello, Nissen's grave datings as reported by Laursen (2010, pp. 120–121), Zettler's Kassite Level III at Nippur, Laursen's period IIa at Qala'at al-Bahrain, the Ras al-Jinz levels in Frenez et al. (2016, p. 117), Kaniuth's late Namazga V for the Altyn Depe seal, and Laursen's reported early-second-millennium context at Kalba. Gadd and the excavators named periods, so their absolute ranges come from the CDLI periodization or, for the Isin-Larsa period and Samsu-iluna's reign, from Wikipedia's Middle Chronology dates; the source field names both. "Site period" means the phase range of the site, used when no layer date was recovered. "Conventional" covers the Gulf Type seal horizon of c. 2100–2000 BC (Laursen 2010; Parpola 2018), the Louvre's date for Susa SB 2425, the Mature Harappan range of 2600–1900 BCE from the CISI 3.3 preface, and the Umm an-Nar period range from the UNESCO tentative list. One Kish entry is a terminus ante quem only and has a null start. The Gonur end year of 1900 BCE is where the CISI 3.3 table ends Late Namazga V; Kaniuth's estimate extends into early Namazga VI, for which no end date was sourced.

Ten rows are context-dated, 25 rely on site or conventional periods, and Umma has no dating because its historical sealing was bought and the survey impressions carry no published context. For Tepe Yahya, TY-106's phase was not established, so both the Period IVB and the tentative Period IVA ranges from Encyclopaedia Iranica are given. Find events record the excavation seasons or first publications that the census and the sources above support. Where campaign years were not recovered, only the publication year appears, and Al-Hajjar's 1970–71 exploration is not tied to the prism seal.

Three sources could not be opened live on 8 October 2026: Encyclopaedia Iranica, harappa.com pages and the CDLI wiki returned 403 or access-denied responses. Iranica and Murda Sang text was taken from the census web witness, and the CDLI period pages were read at cdli.earth. One census locator may need checking: the Tell Abraq row cites Laursen's Table 1 no. 121, which the table lists as Tarut Island, while Laursen discusses the Tell Abraq ivory seal in note 5 and the Fig. 16 caption.
