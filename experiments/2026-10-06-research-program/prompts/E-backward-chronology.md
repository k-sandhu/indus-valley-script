# Package E: working backwards from later South Asian scripts and mark systems

Owned directory: `experiments/2026-10-06-backward-chronology/`. Local raw directory: `data/raw/2026-10-06-backward-chronology/`. Use live web search.

## Question

The user proposes working backwards in time: start from the periods for which South Asian writing and marks are well understood, establish what is fully or partly known in each earlier period, and see how far the chain reaches toward the late phases of the Indus civilization. Build the dated evidence record that such an argument needs, measure where the chain is continuous and where it breaks, and design tests that could distinguish transmission from independent invention.

## Evidence table

Assemble `evidence-table.json` and `.csv` for South Asia and adjacent regions from the earliest Indus-related marks to the early historic scripts, roughly 3500 BCE to 200 BCE. Rows are dated corpora or assemblages, for example: Ravi and Kot Diji phase potters' marks and graffiti at Harappa and elsewhere; Mature Harappan inscriptions; Late Harappan and post-urban material (Cemetery H, Jhukar, Pirak, Rojdi, Daimabad, the Bet Dwarka seal, late Gujarat sites); Chalcolithic graffiti of the Deccan, Malwa and Ahar–Banas cultures (Inamgaon, Daimabad, Navdatoli, Prakash); Ochre Coloured Pottery and Copper Hoard contexts; Painted Grey Ware and Northern Black Polished Ware contexts; Iron Age and megalithic graffiti of South India and Sri Lanka; and the earliest dated Brahmi, Tamil-Brahmi and Kharosthi inscriptions, including the radiocarbon-dated contexts at Anuradhapura, Porunthal, Kodumanal and Keeladi.

For each row record: region and sites; date range with the dating basis (radiocarbon with lab numbers where published, stratigraphy, typology or palaeography) and the strength of that basis; object classes; number of marked or inscribed objects and the count basis; the size of the mark repertoire where reported; published corpora and reports with exact citations; any published claim of a relation to the Indus script or to Brahmi, with who made it and on what evidence; and the accessibility of the data. Include B. B. Lal's 1960 study tracing megalithic graffiti back to Harappan signs, Mahadevan's work on graffiti, K. Rajan and R. Sivanantham's 2025 morphological study and 2026 *Inscribed Potsherds of Tamil Nadu* volumes (local copies under `data/raw/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/south-india/`), and the major positions on the origin of Brahmi.

## Analysis

Summarize the chronology as a century-by-century coverage table: for each interval, how many dated assemblages and marked objects exist, how secure their dates are, and whether a published corpus with images exists. Identify the longest intervals without securely dated marked material and the regions where the record is densest. For each published continuity claim, state what intermediate evidence it relies on and what is missing.

Then write `TEST-DESIGNS.md` with concrete, falsifiable designs for distinguishing transmission from convergence: dated intermediate examples that preserve a rare compound sign or positional rule; feature-level shape comparison against complexity-matched null inventories (see Changizi, Zhang, Ye and Shimojo 2006, *American Naturalist*, on shape configurations shared across writing systems); seriation of dated graffiti repertoires; and positional or combinatorial statistics shared beyond chance. For each design give the required data, the comparison, what result would support or reject transmission, and whether the repository already holds the data.

Keep the dating basis visible for every row, and do not merge dates from different methods. Record failed searches.
