# Package F: what leading researchers recommend for moving the field forward

Owned directory: `experiments/2026-10-06-field-recommendations/`. Local raw directory: `data/raw/2026-10-06-field-recommendations/`. Use live web search.

## Question

The user watched a lecture by Dennys Frenez (https://youtu.be/1i9q17ld_jU) and summarized his recommendations: team-based interdisciplinary work; chronological and regional corpora rather than one monolithic corpus; contextualizing sign frequency (for example sign 740, about 10% of occurrences) against site size and type; and studying non-canonical inscriptions found outside the Indus Valley. The user's summary also records his views that the script was an indigenous invention of the early third millennium BCE, served identification, property control and administration, that the seals form an "unbalanced oligopoly", and that Indus people deliberately rejected the conventions of neighbouring writing systems. Collect what other leading researchers recommend, so the project can see which recommendations it can act on.

## Tasks

1. Try to obtain the lecture's title, date, venue and transcript (for example through the YouTube page or a transcript service). If you obtain a transcript, store it in the local raw directory and check the user's summary against it, with timestamps. If you cannot, say so and treat the user's summary as the record of the talk. Find Frenez's written publications that state the same positions and cite them.
2. Survey published recommendations, research agendas and stated priorities from researchers in Indus archaeology, epigraphy and computation, 1990–2026. Include at least Jonathan Mark Kenoyer, Asko Parpola, Bryan Wells, Andreas Fuls, Massimo Vidale, Gregory Possehl, Iravatham Mahadevan, Rajesh Rao, Nisha Yadav and Mayank Vahia, Richard Sproat, Steve Farmer and Michael Witzel, Bahata Ansumali Mukhopadhyay, Marta Ameri, Adam Green, Cameron Petrie, Rita Wright, Shereen Ratnagar, Randall Law, Heather Miller, Vasant Shinde, R. S. Bisht, K. Rajan, Dennys Frenez and anyone else you find with a substantive published agenda. Use their own publications, interviews and recorded talks.
3. Build `recommendations.json` and `.csv`: one row per recommendation with researcher, exact source and page or timestamp, the recommendation in a sentence, the data or collaboration it requires, whether it is testable with existing data, whether other researchers agree or dispute it (with sources), and whether this repository already addresses it (link the relevant experiment or knowledge file, using `knowledge/INDEX.md` and `knowledge/experiments/registry.json`).
4. In `REPORT.md`, group the recommendations by theme (data and corpora, context and chronology, linguistic approaches, computational method, collaboration and access, new excavation and science), state which are widely shared and which are contested, and list the recommendations this project could act on with its current data, in priority order with reasons.
