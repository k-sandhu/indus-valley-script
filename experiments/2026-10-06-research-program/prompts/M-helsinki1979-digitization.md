## Why this package

Koskenniemi and Parpola's *Corpus of Texts in the Indus Script* (1979) is published under CC BY 4.0 by the University of Helsinki (`research/corpus/primary/koskenniemi_parpola1979.pdf`, 180 PDF pages, handle 10138/342677). The census found no openly licensed, complete, machine-readable Indus corpus with object identifiers. A reliable transcription of this edition would be the first such resource the project knows of, and it would supply fresh text for testing the frozen restoration model in `experiments/2026-10-06-modern-restoration/frozen-model/`.

## What earlier work established

Read `research/corpus/primary/1979_extraction_assessment.md`, `knowledge/retrospectives/corpus-and-transcription.md` (sections on the chart reference and the font-transcription pilots), `research/corpus/primary/font_fresh20/` including its `geometry_audit/`, `research/methods/FONT_PILOT_AUDIT.md`, `src/font_pilot.py`, `src/font_fresh20.py`, `src/evaluate_font_pilot.py`, `src/evaluate_font_fresh20.py`, the chart reference `research/chart_reference/manual_numeric_reference.json` (687 cells, 16 lower-code ambiguities) with the atlas in `output/chart_atlas/`, and `data/normalized/koskenniemi1979_definitions.json`. The corpus prints drawn glyphs plotted from the same font as the sign chart, with an inscription number and classification codes beside each text; the OCR layer is unusable for glyphs. Dashed underlines mark uncertainty, blanks can mark line or side boundaries, and some boundaries are given only by classification codes. The frozen fresh-20 evaluation failed mainly through crop geometry: a fixed row pitch drifted against the true spacing and cut glyphs. After clean vertical bounds, a post-reference development rerun matched 18 of 20 row counts, with 6 rows fully correct at top one and 16 at top five. The retrospective's retry rule applies: a new method must freeze its crop procedure and matcher before any new reference is made.

## Steps

1. **Layout.** Detect pages, columns and text rows without assuming a fixed pitch (for example per-page projection profiles or connected components), separate the number and code fields from the glyph field, and segment glyph units, editorial auxiliaries (uncertainty underlines, loss and inaccessibility markers) and boundary blanks.
2. **Recognition.** Build a classifier from the chart's glyph crops, which share the corpus font: template matching combined with a small learned embedding or CNN trained with augmentation (scale, stroke width, noise, small rotations). Output top-k candidate codes with calibrated confidence, keep the chart's fine-code ambiguities as candidate sets, and read inscription numbers and classification codes with a digit and letter classifier checked against the numbering order and the OCR layer.
3. **Development and freezing.** Develop on pages already used in earlier pilots. Then freeze the full pipeline (code hashes, parameters, chart snapshot) in `PROTOCOL.md` and a manifest before any evaluation reference exists.
4. **Fresh evaluation.** Draw a seeded random sample of at least 60 rows from pages not used in development. Create the reference by independent visual inspection of high-resolution crops, made blind to the pipeline's outputs (for example by a separate annotating agent with no access to predictions), recording uncertain units and disagreements. Report row segmentation, unit counts, exact-sequence accuracy, top-one and top-five candidate accuracy, inscription-number accuracy and calibration, with intervals.
5. **Full run.** Transcribe the whole corpus into JSONL: inscription number, page, column and row coordinates, the unit sequence with candidate codes and confidence, auxiliaries, classification codes and flags. Build a review queue of low-confidence rows.
6. **Cross-check where possible.** If a reliable concordance to CISI or other catalogue numbers exists (for example in the 1980 *Documentation and Duplicates* volume), compare a sample against the local corpus at sign-class level and report agreement without treating either as ground truth. Coordinate the join with package L, which is building an object ledger with an import interface for this output.

## Outputs, rights and disk

The edition is CC BY 4.0, so the derived transcription, code and small evaluation crops can sit in the owned directory with attribution to Koskenniemi and Parpola and the University of Helsinki; record the licence and attribution in the README. Disk space is very low (about 2 GB free): render pages on demand, process them and delete the renders, keep only the evaluation crops and a compressed set of row crops needed for review, and keep total new storage under 500 MB.
