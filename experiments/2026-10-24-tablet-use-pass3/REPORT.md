# Workshop manufacture and a broader Trench 11 dump context

We inspected the complete held 19-page scan of Meadow and Kenoyer (2005), *Excavations at Harappa 2000-2001: New Insights on Chronology and City Organization*, pp. 207–225. The scan has no text layer. We rendered all pages and read Tesseract OCR, then checked nine full-page images against the source. This closes the access gap in pass2, where the remote PDF yielded metadata alone. The source adds workshop manufacture details and a broader Trench 11 deposition account. It supplies no new operational-use relation for H-2218–H-2239 or the carrier controls.

Pages 217–219 describe mapped workshop debris with canisters, setters, faience wasters, beads and tablet blanks. Figure 9 locates lots 2913 and 2934 in room [601]. Figure 10 identifies a manufacturing dump in the Period 3B Trench 54 South workshop. The three registrations already recovered in pass2 remain prior records. Figure 10.2 describes H2001-5087/2934-01 as eroded and fired steatite. The pass2 web caption calls it unfinished and broken. We retain both descriptions as a source difference; neither establishes deliberate cancellation. Figure 10.1 and 10.4 identify H2001-5084/2913-07 and H2001-5068/2913-01 as unfired steatite. Pages 218–219 distinguish uninscribed blanks, partly inscribed tablets and a possible mould for faience. Their manufacture cannot identify the use of the fired 1997 series.

Pages 219–220 report more than 150 inscribed pieces outside Trench 11 perimeter wall [250] during 1993–1997, apparently deposited with rubbish from inside the city. This extends the published context of the area. Figure 12 on p.221 provides a plan of Trenches 9, 10 and 11 and wall [250], without an exact unit-8040 clump locator. Figures 13 and 14 describe later registered finds from street, fill and foundation contexts. These are separate populations. The exact 1997 field photograph and unit treatment record remain missing. The bibliography on p.225 identifies Meadow, Kenoyer and Wright, *Harappa Archaeological Research Project: Harappa Excavations 1997*, submitted 25 March 1998 to the Director-General, Department of Archaeology and Museums, Government of Pakistan. This is a precise archival acquisition target.

The four-query institutional search reached Kenoyer's 2017 Wisconsin CV and a blocked Harvard page. The CV confirms the 2001 report's title and INDO-KOKO-KENKYU 22:19–36 but supplies no full report. A changed-host route to [the chronology section](https://www.harappa.com/indus4/e3.html) succeeded, resolving to the current Harappa page. Its lines 32–33 describe red pigment on some steatite tablets, blue-green glaze on faience and Period 3C stamped goblets. These are class-level claims. They give no registration connecting pigment to our 22 tablets and no contents, attachment or residue identification. We recovered one previously inaccessible section, not the full 18-page 2001 report. An institutional 2012 SEM-report search hit mentioning unit8040 remained a lead because the source open failed with 502; no content claim is admitted from its snippet.

The target register keeps four populations separate: 22 incised steatite tablets, forty vessel stamps H-1727–H-1766, six clay tags H-1719–H-1724 and stopper H-1725. New ancient impression, attachment, residue, wear, cancellation and contents relations remain unknown for each population within the inspected sources. The red-pigment statement is a decoration report with unspecified registrations. The mini-kiln experiment is a modern manufacturing demonstration. The suggested mint and credit roles on p.219 are the authors' functional interpretations. The publications share HARP excavation lineage, so reading them adds source coverage without independent excavation replication.

The protocol, aggregate, source manifest and exposure record bind this scope to exact local files. Detailed observations, OCR and complete web responses stay on D:. Author validation checks all source hashes, page counts, population accounting and the protocol hash. No confirmation material or bench statistic was opened. There has been no separate specialist review.

The boldest supported reading is that Harappa manufactured tablets in localized workshops and later accumulated inscribed objects in secondary deposits outside its city wall. The 22-tablet series still needs a registered physical link to its operation: an ancient impression matching a tablet would establish stamping; an enclosing residue or excavation photograph would establish packaging; a registered container and independently identified contents would decide a contents or allotment claim.
