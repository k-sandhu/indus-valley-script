# Indus weight metrology across sites and regions

Dholavira's intact certain weights support different scales across mass bands. The **90 weights of 5–100 g** give **13.716 g with signal CV 1.591%**, close to Hemmy's approximately 13.70 g level. The **147 weights of 1.3–5 g** give **14.280 g with CV 5.777%**. The pooled 14.003 g fit combines these groups and should not be presented as a single Dholavira or Harappan unit. Western compatibility counts are below or ordinary under controls that remove fine mass structure. **These counts give no evidence of Indus-standard weights in the western assemblages.** [Mass bands](runs/2026-10-07-review-response-v4/mass-band-estimates.json), `fits`; [chance controls](runs/2026-10-07-review-response-v4/western-chance-baselines.json), `nulls`.

## Evidence and selection

The input is Enrico Ascalone's *Bronze Age Weights from Mesopotamia, Iran & Greater Indus Valley* (2022, Weight & Value 3, Wachholtz, with a contribution from Jan Tavernier). It provides 2,058 catalogue entries, including 938 Greater Indus records. Dholavira supplies 870, Bagasra 19, Nagwada 16, Rakhigarhi 15, Farmana 8, Shikarpur 7, Kuntasi 2 and Nageshwar 1. The book's Dholavira ranges identify 435 certain weights, 11 possible objects and 36 excluded related finds. The remaining 388 conflict with its prose count of 393 potential objects; its 505 contextual objects also require reconciliation with field coverage. These source discrepancies remain recorded. [Counts](runs/2026-10-07-review-response-v4/counts.json), `sites`; [Ascalone](https://airus.unisalento.it/retrieve/c39309c8-8dae-4dba-b990-86171612edf6/E.%20Ascalone%2C%20Bronze%20Age%20Weights%20from%20Mesopotamia%2C%20Iran%20and%20Greater%20Indus%20Valley_compressed.pdf), printed pp. 135–136; [context coverage](summary-tables-review-response/context-coverage.json).

Primary selection requires a positive complete mass, an intact condition label and no wear, damage or +x. Dholavira's primary frame contains 298 certain weights; western selection contains 701 certain/class-unresolved records after the earlier Susa accession-collision exclusions. Secondary wear and all-complete frames retain their declared conditions. Catalogue records remain the sampling units; unresolved repeated identifiers and selection by excavation or publication limit population interpretation. The mixture assigns equal prior density to each allowed denomination, fits multiplicative dispersion and includes a broad background. Its CV describes the fitted signal, rather than manufacturing accuracy for every selected object. Intervals are conditional on the recorded masses, selection and model. ## Parser repair and validation

The original 60-entry rendered-page sample found five period errors, repaired before the frozen fit, and zero mass/+x errors in that sample. The independent review checked 36 additional random entries and targeted unusual records. The full scan demonstrates why the sample's zero observed mass errors cannot imply a zero catalogue-wide rate: cats. **1201 and 1730** use decimal commas and were read as **444.0 and 184.0 g**, rather than **4.44+x and 1.84 g**. Both are corrected; cat. 1201 remains excluded by +x. Cat. 1730 remains eligible and moves into the small-mass band. Its hundredfold error barely changes the pooled Indus likelihood because 1/8 and 12.5 are both allowed levels, an alias that does not protect the binary competitor. The corrected binary-then-decimal AIC advantage over pure binary is **11.609**, compared with **15.262** in the frozen run. [Parser audit](runs/2026-10-07-review-response-v4/parser-audit.json), `decimal_comma_corrections`; [models](runs/2026-10-07-review-response-v4/model-comparisons.json), Dholavira rows; frozen [models](runs/2026-10-06-main/model-comparisons.json).

The parser clears **111 false grid fields** among 1,188 non-Dholavira records: 90 Tepe Yahya dates and 21 accession/excavation patterns. Three occur in the original 60-entry sample, cats. 956, 964 and 1055, bringing the retrospectively known initial errors to **8/60 (13.333%; Wilson 6.914–24.165%)**, and known v6 errors to **3/60 (5.000%)**. The original zero-remaining verdict is superseded. These post-review tallies reuse source witnesses and do not estimate a fresh independent validation rate. [Validation amendment](parser-validation-review-response.json), `known_original_v3_error_entries_n`, `known_v6_errors_in_original_sample_n`.

Caption leakage affects **seven entries**, cats. 887, 1069, 1083, 1121, 1137, 1148 and 1173. Cat. 887 contains the next chapter's map caption; the other six are the field leaks enumerated by the review. Caption blocks and their italic continuations are removed before field parsing. Six condition fields lose a stray “copper/”; five material labels change to the joint copper/bronze label because one was already joint. Cat. 593's post-mass annotation is now captured. All 2,058 corrected rows reproduce byte for byte on re-extraction, and the frozen primary selection is unchanged. The two central bands account for the readable contrast; the lowest band fails the concentrated-unit rule, and the highest has a wide interval on eight observations. Only **52/90** middle-band weights and **46/147** small-band weights lie within 3% of their fitted denomination, so the signal CV cannot describe all rows as equally precise. Fixing the pooled scale at 13.65 g loses **17.223 log-likelihood units**, with **16.194** from the corrected 1.3–5 g band. Cat. 1730's reclassification explains the difference from the review's uncorrected band decomposition. [Band estimates](runs/2026-10-07-review-response-v4/mass-band-estimates.json), `fits[].bootstrap`, `within_3_percent_n`; [model tests](runs/2026-10-07-review-response-v4/dholavira-model-tests.json), `log_likelihood_loss_by_band`.

Hemmy's 1931 0.857 g level and 1938 0.8565/0.857 g calculations correspond to **13.712 and 13.704 g** at ratio 16, commonly described as 13.70 g. His 1938 appendix instead estimates 13.625 g from a frequency curve, and the 1943 modal analysis gives 13.63 g after rejecting doubtful ratios and weights below 6 g. Kenoyer (2010, p. 116) calls ratio 16 approximately **13.7 g**, gives an exceptional 8/3 ratio near **2.28 g**, and discusses newly recovered **0.3 and 0.6 g** categories. His preceding seed discussion uses 0.871 g; multiplying it by 16 gives 13.936 g, which is a different rounding/definition route from his stated approximate 13.7 g level. Vahia and Yadav (2007) use a **0.89 g** base, equivalent arithmetically to **14.24 g** at ratio 16. These figures and their selections should accompany any comparison of nominal units. [Hemmy 1938](https://rws.xoba.com/indus_weights/Hemmy-1938-weights-Mohenjodaro-Mackay.pdf), pp. 601–602, 674; [Hemmy/Hall 1943](https://rws.xoba.com/indus_weights/Hemmy_Hall-1943-weights-Chanhudaro-Mackay.pdf), pp. 236–237; [Kenoyer](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/Kenoyer%202010%20Measuring%20the%20Harappan%20World.pdf), pp. 116–117; [Vahia/Yadav](https://www.harappa.com/content/harappa-weights); exact metadata in [sources.json](sources.json).

Ascalone already separates a conventional Harappan group from a **95-object** heavier Dholavira group, reporting mean **14.44 g** and range **14–15.24 g** (pp. 169–171). The corrected extraction of that analytical list contains 95 unique IDs and a mean of the printed normalized values of **14.4457 g**. Its 82 eligible members fit **14.454 g, CV 2.480%**; the other 216 primary objects fit **13.681 g, CV 1.287%**. Of the 147 small-band rows, 55 belong to the book's heavier list. The list was chosen by metrological fit, so the partition documents the earlier analysis rather than independently authenticating a Ghaggar regional origin. [Model tests](runs/2026-10-07-review-response-v4/dholavira-model-tests.json), `book_heavier_list`; [strata](runs/2026-10-07-review-response-v4/stratified-estimates.json), `book-heavier-list` and `not-in-book-heavier-list`.

The two-unit gain supports heterogeneity; the diffuse second component and boundary fits leave its historical interpretation unstable. The absolute-error model shows that allowing low-mass broadening also helps, while the offset model moves the scale close to Hemmy's level. The latter describes a systematic excess: at a fixed 13.65 g reference, the nearest 1/8 and 1/4 classes have median signed offsets **+0.09375 and +0.08750 g**, compared with **+0.030 and +0.040 g** at 1/2 and 1. The variance model is a normalized log-mass mixture whose component variance depends on its target mass; its absolute term is a fitted statistical scale, not a measured balance resolution. Adding **1/6, 1/12 and 1/24** levels, documented in the earlier fractional discussions and Ascalone's heavier group, raises log likelihood by **61.945**, gives a pooled scale of **14.103 g**, CV **8.586%** and signal probability **0.921**. In 120 controls with three randomly added levels, mean gain is **17.179**, the 95th percentile is **53.467**, and **1/120** gains at least as much. Thus missing fractional support is a consequential candidate explanation. This sensitivity was selected after review; denomination assignment and frequency assumptions still affect the likelihood. [Model tests](runs/2026-10-07-review-response-v4/dholavira-model-tests.json), `extra_fractions`, `random_extra_levels`.

Admitting worn weights retains the contrast: small-band fit **14.249 g, CV 13.661%** on 158 rows; 5–100 g fit **13.731 g, CV 1.683%** on 103. Mass loss from an initially exact 13.65 g series alone cannot produce the positive small-class medians. Wear combined with different starting masses or measurement bias remains possible. Calibrated reweighing, balance resolution, edge-loss documentation and unfinished objects are needed to distinguish manufacturing allowance, systematic measurement offset, wear, fractional denominations and mixed standards. ## Quantograms and stratified precision

The corrected 290-object Dholavira quantogram, capped at 100 g, has its highest peak at **1.71 g, Q = 8.0635**. Eight times 1.71 is **13.68 g**. Its second peak is **14.67 g, Q = 7.6210**; this is below the **7.7566** global 95% threshold. The maximum has conditional Monte Carlo **p = 0.006**. The small band peaks at **1.79 g, Q = 10.3885** (eightfold 14.32 g), while the 5–100 g band peaks at **6.90 g, Q = 8.7992**, with subsidiary **3.41 and 1.71 g** peaks. Both band maxima have **p = 0.001** under the same declared jitter null. These are quanta and harmonics shaped by fractional denominations; the second pooled peak does not establish an additional unit. The all-class maximum remains at the **15 g search boundary**, p = **0.323**, failing the quantogram rule. [Peaks](runs/2026-10-07-review-response-v4/quantogram-peaks.json), Dholavira and band `samples`; the referenced CSV grids include pointwise and global envelopes. Each new group uses 999 surrogates and the protocol's common seed.

Mass bands also clarify material and shape comparisons. Chert fits **14.252 g, CV 4.530%** in the small band (n = 51), and **13.691 g, CV 0.924%** in the middle band (n = 34). Other stones give **14.201 g / CV 15.486%** and **13.873 g / CV 3.375%** (n = 83 and 50). Cuboids give **14.331 / 13.747 g** with CV **5.947 / 1.653%** (n = 113 and 73). This package adds a complete reusable parser with source audit, specified eligibility/background models, bootstrap intervals, mass-band and context comparisons, and chance controls for compatibility counts. Its heavier group and Susa objects are established research topics. The catalogue does not record the repeated calibrated measurements, rounding resolution or loss of mass needed to assign a physical cause to dispersion. The [final verification](review-response-verification.json) checks 129 likelihoods, 203 scalar cosine points, 6,309 western scores and all source/output hashes; the frozen endpoint's [separate verification](review-response-frozen-verification.json) still passes.

Further work should reweigh identified objects with repeat measurements and scale calibration, document wear and unfinished manufacture, reconcile DHR/context/class counts and recover the restricted precedent chapters. Western object review should pair chronology, identity, stone sourcing and morphology with local-standard alternatives and complete Gulf assemblages. These measurements address the specific ambiguities exposed by the fitted bands and the chance-negative screen.
