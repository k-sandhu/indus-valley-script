# Bronze Age records as comparisons for the Indus inscriptions

Started 12 October 2026 UTC (11 October in Toronto), before new numerical analysis. The user authorized pursuing Sumerian, Akkadian, Egyptian and Mycenaean Greek. This is an exploratory comparison of source-observed records and a test of methods on known systems.

## Questions and observation units

The study asks which properties of short Indus inscriptions also occur in records whose language or function is understood. It separately asks whether a procedure using anonymous reading units can recover source-supplied categories. Writing maturity is operationalized through record conventions, specialist administration, object use and dated textual traditions. No numerical ranking of the maturity of spoken languages is assigned.

Acquire primary institutional editions and their documentation for all four traditions. Prefer complete seals, labels, receipts and short accounts. Retain complete object texts, natural lines, sentences and modern lexical entries as distinct units. Never cut an arbitrary window from a long text and present it as a complete short inscription. A line/sentence extension is a separate comparison population. Record date, place, genre, physical object or text identity, source edition, acquisition depth and whether semantic annotation is supplied by the source or introduced here. Do not merge languages on the basis of a period name: an Old Akkadian-period object can contain Sumerian.

The Indus source is the pinned local Lipi observed-face corpus. Reuse its source and recording caveats. Primary structural comparison retains clean, complete, single-line, right-to-left faces of length 3–12; report the extension to 13 and 1–2 separately. Close physical-family and identical-text links before selecting one representative per complete text. Preserve the original corpus and all earlier fitted outputs.

## Fixed analysis recipe

Before fitting, freeze normalized input hashes, eligibility rules and group identifiers in a manifest. Source-specific parsers retain damage, restoration, number, punctuation, word-boundary and sign-versus-word distinctions. An unresolvable sign segmentation excludes that record from sign-level comparison while retaining it in the documentary census.

1. Describe whole-record and natural-unit cohorts separately: length, inventory, repeated texts, position concentration, numeral-bearing units, source annotations and date/genre coverage. Cross-script sign inventory and bits-per-unit measures are not directly commensurate when encodings differ.
2. Compare order with within-unit permutations that preserve length and the unit multiset. Use the same fixed frequency threshold and formula across corpora; give effect sizes and 1,999 seeded permutation references. Descriptive positional concentration uses normalized position rather than presumed phonetic values. Treat duplicates and correlated source families as groups.
3. Known-record control: conceal meanings from the feature/model stage. Where source-supplied token categories exist, use document/text-group held-out prediction from anonymous-unit identity, position, length and neighbors. Compare with a position/length baseline and a frequency baseline. Group exact repeated records and physical/text identity before splitting. Report class support and unavailable annotation; do not convert our pictorial conjectures into an answer key. The blind aspect concerns model inputs, not the researcher's ignorance of already published readings.
4. Apply the same structural recipe to IVS. Matching profiles can constrain record organization. An inferred functional category remains a hypothesis requiring independent object-context observations.
5. Lexical feasibility audit: document the available dated lexica, mapping premises, ambiguity and segmentation for the four languages and the existing Sanskrit proposal. Run only comparisons whose observation units and allowable mappings can be specified symmetrically. A phonetic language ranking is not produced from shape similarity, Latin transliteration letters or incomparable dictionary sizes. Preserve any demonstrated ambiguity or failed recovery.

Use seed 20261012 and fresh dated output directories that refuse overwrite. Primary comparisons are exploratory, including corpus-route and parser choices. Any outcome-dependent adjustment receives an amendment and a separate endpoint.
