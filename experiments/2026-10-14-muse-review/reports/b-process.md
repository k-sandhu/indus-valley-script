# Review: knowledge layer, process and rules

## 1. Verdict

The knowledge layer does the job it was built for in the 12 October restructure: an agent can arrive cold, follow `AGENTS.md` to a card and its experiment package, and produce work that preserves frozen evidence, provenance and falsifiable conjectures. The cost is reading load: the mandatory path already exceeds what a new contributor will actually read, and the same rules are restated in five or six places so no single file can be trusted as the whole contract. The card system has seven kinds where four would carry the same information, and the generated 603-line map has become an inventory dump rather than a navigation aid. The confirmation protocol is well designed for the two shots it was built around, but it is written as if it governed all work, while the current first priority — object metadata — needs almost none of it and is governed by a different, simpler set of rules buried in `knowledge/DATA.md`. Nothing here needs another restructure; it needs pruning, one short contract, and enforcement moved from prose into `tools/kb.py check` where the check is cheap.

## 2. Observations

**1. The mandatory reading path is longer than any contributor will follow, and it is defined in three places that do not quite agree.** `AGENTS.md:10` requires `README.md`, `knowledge/INDEX.md`, `knowledge/experiments/registry.json` and the relevant retrospective before research; `AGENTS.md:5` adds `knowledge/DATA.md` before any analysis; `AGENTS.md:16` adds `knowledge/editorial/WRITING.md` before writing; `AGENTS.md:12` adds `bench/README.md` when choosing a protocol; `AGENTS.md:14` adds `knowledge/provenance/REDISTRIBUTION.md`, `REUSE.md` and `LICENSE` before tracking third-party material. `CONTRIBUTING.md:3` gives a different starting triple (`AGENTS.md`, `knowledge/DATA.md`, `knowledge/MAP.md`) and `CONTRIBUTING.md:7` adds linked paper methods. Each skill in `.claude/skills/` then adds its own chain: for example `.claude/skills/add-data/SKILL.md:8` requires `DATA.md`, `data/MANIFEST.json`, `knowledge/directions/context-data-plan.md` and `REDISTRIBUTION.md`. Estimated lengths from line counts and table density: `AGENTS.md` about 950 words, `README.md` about 1,300, `knowledge/INDEX.md` about 1,800, `knowledge/DATA.md` (265 lines, mostly tables) about 6,000, `knowledge/MAP.md` (603 lines) about 7,000, `CONTRIBUTING.md` about 900, `WRITING.md` about 1,400, `knowledge/registry/VOCABULARY.md` about 600, plus a card, a retrospective and an experiment package. A data contributor who reads only what the data route strictly needs faces roughly 12,000–15,000 words (about 16,000–20,000 tokens); an agent that takes "read `registry.json`" and "read the map" literally faces well over 40,000 words, because the generated registry serialises all 185 attempts. The layering in `AGENTS.md:24` (Layer 0–3) is the right idea, but the hard rules above it do not follow it: they pull Layer 3 material (full registry, paper methods) into Layer 0.

**2. Core rules are duplicated across files, so edits must be made in several places and drift is already visible.** The no-CI rule appears in `AGENTS.md:9`, `CONTRIBUTING.md:3`, `REPRODUCING.md:3` and `knowledge/provenance/REDISTRIBUTION.md:41`. The bench-attachment rule appears in `AGENTS.md:12`, `CONTRIBUTING.md:13` and `bench/README.md:19`. The seven-day claim lapse appears in `AGENTS.md:57` and `CONTRIBUTING.md:21`. The comprehension sentence ("maximize reader comprehension…") appears in `AGENTS.md:20`, `CONTRIBUTING.md:25` (with an instruction at `CONTRIBUTING.md:27` to carry the `AGENTS.md` version verbatim) and `experiments/2026-10-12-conjecture-shots/DESIGN.md:25`. The status vocabulary is defined in `knowledge/INDEX.md:70-81` (seven classes), copied into `knowledge/registry/VOCABULARY.md:22` with the words "copied exactly", then extended at `VOCABULARY.md:34` with two more classes (`source-review`, `record`); `tools/kb.py:20-21` enforces nine. A reader of `INDEX.md` alone learns a stale vocabulary. Licence terms are stated in `AGENTS.md:14`, `CONTRIBUTING.md:54`, `REUSE.md:3`, `bench/README.md:27` and `knowledge/DATA.md:253`, each slightly differently scoped.

**3. Two direct contradictions put a new contributor in an impossible position.** First, `AGENTS.md:5` says to "record every new source, field and change in coverage in `knowledge/DATA.md` and `data/MANIFEST.json`", while `CONTRIBUTING.md:17` says contributors never edit shared documents and must propose such changes inside their own package for a maintainer to integrate; `.claude/skills/add-data/SKILL.md:8` follows the second rule. Both cannot be followed at once. Second, `CONTRIBUTING.md:44` tells contributors to run `python tools/kb.py check` before a pull request and paste the summary, while also telling them that new cards leave the generated files out of date; `tools/kb.py:450-452` makes generated-file freshness part of `check`, so every legitimate contribution is told to paste a failing check. The restructure retrospective already noticed this pattern (`experiments/2026-10-12-repo-restructure/RETROSPECTIVE.md:27`).

**4. Several stated rules are enforced by nothing.** The verification tiers (`submitted`, `reproduced`, `reviewed`, `accepted`) in `CONTRIBUTING.md:42` and `VOCABULARY.md:44` have no field in any card schema: `tools/kb.py:369-374` lists required fields for findings, questions, conjectures and attempts, and no tier appears. Disclosure of model and harness in `authors` (`AGENTS.md:57`, `CONTRIBUTING.md:19`) is required for conjectures (`tools/kb.py:372-373`) but `authors` is not in the required attempt fields (`tools/kb.py:26-27`) or finding fields (`tools/kb.py:369`), so the two commonest records can omit it and still pass. Claim expiry is recorded but never acted on: `kb.py next` (`tools/kb.py:533-535`) filters only on `status == open` and empty `claimed_by`, and nothing compares `claim_expires` with today. "About a third" long shots is stated for runs in `AGENTS.md:18` and for open conjectures in `knowledge/conjectures/README.md:21` — different denominators — and neither is measured anywhere. Link checking in `kb.py check` covers only the file set in `new_markdown()` (`tools/kb.py:311-319`): entry documents, registry notes, findings, questions, skills and the restructure package. Most experiment prose, directions, conjectures' bodies and papers are not link-checked, so "claims must link to exact outputs" (`AGENTS.md:11`) is enforced only where frontmatter `evidence` exists.

**5. There are seven card kinds, but only four carry distinct information.** Findings (35) are citable measured results with `population` and JSON-Pointer evidence, for example `knowledge/findings/F-0001-sequential-prediction-on-unique-texts.md:7-9`; they aggregate across attempts through `registry_ids`, which justifies keeping them separate from attempts. Questions (69) are the work queue with priority, access, compute and claim fields, for example `knowledge/questions/Q-0001-review-a-30-object-identity-block.md:2-16`; they earn their keep, and `kb.py next` depends on them. Conjectures (5 cards plus `knowledge/conjectures/TEMPLATE.md`) are the project's distinctive contribution — plain claim, credence history, prediction table, kill condition, for example `knowledge/conjectures/C-0001-rank-written-twice.md:39-60` — and should stay. Attempts (185 JSON files in `knowledge/registry/attempts/`) are the provenance log; their `challenge` and `remedy` fields (for example `knowledge/registry/attempts/ROOT-02.json:7-8`) restate in two lines what the experiment's retrospective says at length, and their free-text `status` duplicates `status_class` (in `ROOT-02.json:4` and `:14` the two are identical), a duplication `VOCABULARY.md:3` explicitly preserves. Directions (14 files) have no frontmatter schema at all — `kb.py` indexes them with status `document` (`tools/kb.py:181-184`) and validates nothing — and in content they overlap all neighbours: `knowledge/directions/what-12-october-established.md` is a synthesis of findings, `knowledge/directions/context-data-plan.md` is a schema that `DATA.md:167` now owns, and several others are conjecture or question material written before those kinds existed. Retrospectives exist twice: 18 curated essays in `knowledge/retrospectives/` and roughly 150 `RETROSPECTIVE.md` files inside `experiments/`, both swept into one kind by `tools/kb.py:171-173`. Papers as a kind are harmless because they are manuscripts, not cards.

**6. `knowledge/MAP.md` has stopped being a map.** Of its 603 lines, about 250 list registry attempts and about 160 list retrospectives (`knowledge/MAP.md:444-603`), with attempt titles truncated at 95 characters by `short()` in `tools/kb.py:201-203`, so many entries end mid-thought ("…what indepen…", `MAP.md:203`). The generator also lists whatever directories happen to exist on the generating machine (`tools/kb.py:218-222`), which is how `.playwright-cli/` and `tmp/` entered a committed navigation file (`MAP.md:13`, `MAP.md:27`). Meanwhile three other files do overlapping navigation work: `knowledge/INDEX.md` (a 125-line narrative that opens by pointing at the map, `INDEX.md:3`, then retells the research history), `knowledge/TIMELINE.md` (the preserved pre-restructure README), and the findings list in `README.md:11-20`. The curated knowledge — 35 findings, 69 questions, 5 conjectures — fits in about 120 lines; everything else in the map is better reached by `kb.py list` and `kb.py search`, which already filter by kind, status and area (`tools/kb.py:507-511`).

**7. The confirmation protocol is proportionate for conjecture shots and disproportionate as a general gate.** For its designed purpose it is genuinely good: closure over aliases and identical texts, freeze-before-read enforced by the loader, per-prediction pass rules with credence effects, and the seven corrective rules folded back into `knowledge/conjectures/README.md:11-17` after Shot A's review showed a pass resting on three seals. `bench/shots/README.md:14-16` describes a loader that rechecks every participant's hashes on each confirmation read and logs denied reads. But `AGENTS.md:12` and `CONTRIBUTING.md:13` extend bench attachment to "every submitted reading, sign key, meaning map or conjecture", and the five bench tasks (`bench/README.md:7-11`) cover only evaluation of proposals against held-out data. The current Priority 1 queue is 21 questions, nearly all metadata joins, transcriptions and source audits (`knowledge/MAP.md:109-129`); none of them has a bench task, so the rule as written either does not apply to the project's first priority or forces contributors to hunt for an irrelevant task to attach. The bench also needs NumPy and SciPy (`bench/README.md:3`) where `tools/kb.py` is stdlib-only, raising the setup cost for exactly the small contributions the queue is designed to attract.

**8. The confirmation half is protected procedurally, not actually, and the documents half-say so.** `experiments/2026-10-12-conjecture-shots/DESIGN.md:17` lists seven corpus-wide aggregates computed before the split that already include the confirmation half, so "fresh" means fresh for a statistic family, not unexamined. `bench/spent.json` records the October 12 split as spent for `seal_size`, `site` and `motif` (`bench/spent.json:8-12`) with an empty `reads` list marked `historical` (`bench/spent.json:15-16`), and the new bench round inherits the same three families (`bench/spent.json:22-26`), which is honest bookkeeping. But protection depends on every agent choosing to read through `bench.shots.load_half`; `bench/shots/README.md:18` concedes "The ledger protects recorded workflow, and contributors retain responsibility for reads performed outside it." With many agents holding local copies of the same normalised export, nothing prevents direct reads, and the two-sign tablet exception (`DESIGN.md:15`) means identical short tablet texts legitimately appear in both halves. The protocol's real strength is the frozen `PREDICTIONS.md` hash and the kill condition, not the split's purity, and the write-up should claim exactly that.

**9. `tools/kb.py` is the strongest part of the process layer, with two gaps that will bite.** It is a single stdlib file (569 lines) that parses a documented YAML subset without a YAML dependency (`tools/kb.py:122-154`), validates IDs, areas, statuses, credence ranges and JSON-Pointer evidence (`tools/kb.py:364-446`), and generates both `registry.json` and `MAP.md` deterministically. The gaps: `kb.py new` supports only `question`, `finding` and `attempt` (`tools/kb.py:512`), so conjectures — the kind with the most required fields — must be copied by hand from the template; and question creation caps at Q-0999 (`tools/kb.py:467-468`), so the CLI cannot create the Q-1001+ data-acquisition cards that `AGENTS.md:51` defines and that make up 30 of the 69 questions. The hand-written frontmatter parser keeps unsupported structures as raw strings (`tools/kb.py:123`), which means a contributor who writes ordinary YAML slightly outside the subset gets a card that parses without error but validates against string values — failures will look mysterious. Search is substring counting with body hits capped at 10 (`tools/kb.py:552-555`), adequate at 438 items.

**10. `knowledge/DATA.md` is excellent and is quietly becoming a second home for rules.** It is the best single document in the repository for its purpose: every coverage number links to the run that produced it (for example `DATA.md:3`, `DATA.md:87`), the target record schema is explicit (`DATA.md:165-192`), and the ranked acquisition table (`DATA.md:198-227`) duplicates the question cards' `priority` field with a second, independent ranking that can drift from it — Q-1001 is rank 1 in the table and also Priority 1 in its card, but nothing checks that the two orderings agree. `DATA.md:247` also restates the acquisition-record rule from `REDISTRIBUTION.md:27`. As data work accelerates, contributors will treat `DATA.md` as their contract and never reach `CONTRIBUTING.md`; the process should acknowledge that rather than fight it.

**11. The writing rules are doing real work but cost more reading than they save in their current form.** `knowledge/editorial/WRITING.md` mixes enforceable style rules (`WRITING.md:17-39`) with a literature review of AI-writing research (`WRITING.md:41-71`) that is interesting provenance but not something a contributor needs before editing. The bold-claims rule (`WRITING.md:35`) is load-bearing for conjectures and is correctly restated in `knowledge/conjectures/README.md:5`; the rest could be a one-page checklist with the research kept as an appendix. The requirement to paste the comprehension sentence into every subagent assignment (`AGENTS.md:20`) is self-propagating boilerplate: it will be copied into prompts long after anyone checks whether it changes output.

**12. The 12 October restructure was additive by design, and its leftovers are now the clutter.** `experiments/2026-10-12-repo-restructure/PLAN.md:12` committed to moving and deleting nothing, which was right for a two-week-old evidence base. The consequence is visible in navigation: the old README survives as `knowledge/TIMELINE.md`, the old registry survives as 185 files plus a generated aggregate at a different path (`knowledge/experiments/registry.json`, generated from `knowledge/registry/attempts/` per `knowledge/registry/README.md:5` — two "registry" locations), and pre-card memos survive as directions. The retrospective's own closing advice (`RETROSPECTIVE.md:47`) was to test the route with an independent contributor on one metadata task and one reproduction task; nothing in the tree records that this test happened.

## 3. Recommendations

**R1. Replace the scattered entry rules with a one-page contributor contract, and demote everything else to reference.** Create `CONTRACT.md` at the root (about 600 words), make `AGENTS.md` point to it as the whole mandatory read, and rewrite `AGENTS.md` as a router (keep the intent table at `AGENTS.md:26-35`, delete restated rules). Outline of the contract, keeping every guarantee the project makes:

- *What this project is*: a checkable record on an undeciphered script; data completeness first; no decipherment or sound-value claims, ever.
- *The five hard rules*: (1) never overwrite a dated output; new work gets a new `experiments/YYYY-MM-DD-slug/`; (2) every claim links to exact outputs; (3) acquired corpora stay local, check rights before tracking anything third-party; (4) state conjectures boldly with credence, predictions and a kill condition, and test them frozen; (5) no CI or automation; maintainers build generated files and the site by hand.
- *Your first hour*: read this page, run `python tools/kb.py next`, open one card and its linked package, claim the question's issue.
- *What you own*: your dated directory and your new card files; never generated files or others' files; propose shared-document changes inside your package.
- *Before you submit*: protocol recorded before outcomes; retrospective in your package; `kb.py check` clean on your files; disclose model and harness in `authors`.
- *Where detail lives*: one line each to `DATA.md`, `bench/README.md`, `WRITING.md`, `REDISTRIBUTION.md`, `VOCABULARY.md`.

Cost: one maintainer pass over `AGENTS.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `llms.txt` and the seven skills to replace duplicated rule text with links; no evidence links break because no evidence file moves. It works if a fresh agent, given only the contract, completes a Priority 1 metadata question without a rule violation — testable in one session, which also finally runs the test proposed in `experiments/2026-10-12-repo-restructure/RETROSPECTIVE.md:47`.

**R2. Shrink the generated map to the curated layer and fix its generator.** Change `map_document()` in `tools/kb.py:206-256` to emit entry documents, findings, open questions and conjectures only (about 120 lines), with one line telling readers to run `kb.py list --kind attempt` or `--kind retrospective` for the rest; replace the live directory scan (`tools/kb.py:218`) with the fixed `descriptions` dict so local directories can never enter the committed file again. Cost: `tools/build_site.py` builds its own pages from cards (its navigation at `tools/build_site.py:34-38` does not depend on the map), so the site is unaffected; anyone deep-linking to a map section for attempts loses that anchor, which `kb.py show <ID>` replaces. It works if `knowledge/MAP.md` drops below about 200 lines and `kb.py check` passes after one maintainer build.

**R3. Retire directions and the retrospective kind without deleting anything.** Freeze `knowledge/directions/` to new files; for each of the 14 memos, record in `knowledge/INDEX.md` whether its live content is now a conjecture, a question, part of `DATA.md`, or historical. Remove `direction` and `retrospective` from `KINDS` (`tools/kb.py:22`) and from the map, keeping the files exactly where they are so existing links survive; keep indexing experiment retrospectives for `kb.py search` only. Fold the attempt fields `challenge`/`remedy` guidance into one sentence in `knowledge/registry/README.md` pointing at the package retrospective as the full account. Cost: `AGENTS.md:42` loses two `--kind` values; `knowledge/retrospectives/README.md` becomes a hand-kept essay index, which it already mostly is. It works if contributors stop asking which kind a memo is, because there are only findings, questions, conjectures and attempts to choose among.

**R4. Scope the bench requirement to the work it actually governs.** Amend `AGENTS.md:12`, `CONTRIBUTING.md:13` and `bench/README.md:19` so bench output is required for readings, keys, meaning maps, restorations, segmentations and conjecture shots — and explicitly not for data acquisition, metadata joins, transcription or source audits, which instead require the acquisition record in `REDISTRIBUTION.md:27` plus a coverage delta against `DATA.md` section 2. Add one paragraph to `bench/shots/README.md` stating plainly that the split guards statistic families, that corpus-wide exposure predates it (`DESIGN.md:17`), and that the freeze hash is the guarantee. Cost: none to evidence; the two completed shots are untouched. It works if the next five Priority 1 data contributions submit without anyone asking which bench task applies.

**R5. Move three cheap rules from prose into `kb.py check`, and fix the two CLI gaps.** In `tools/kb.py`: add `authors` to required fields for findings and attempts (non-empty list); warn or fail when a `claimed` question's `claim_expires` is in the past; add `conjecture` to `kb.py new` using `knowledge/conjectures/TEMPLATE.md`; and let `new question` accept a `--data` flag that allocates from Q-1001. Split `check` output so generated-file drift is reported as a maintainer note, not a contributor error, resolving the contradiction in observation 3. Cost: existing cards missing `authors` will fail until backfilled — a one-time maintainer edit across card files only, no evidence touched. It works if `kb.py check` passes on the merged tree and a contributor can create every card kind from the CLI.

**R6. Make `DATA.md` the single ranking for data work and generate question priorities from it, or drop one of the two.** Either delete the ranked table at `DATA.md:198-227` in favour of card `priority` plus a generated list, or keep the table and have `kb.py check` verify that table order and card priorities agree for Q-1001+. Also resolve the edit-ownership contradiction (observation 3) by changing `AGENTS.md:5` to match `CONTRIBUTING.md:17`: data contributors record coverage changes in their package, maintainers fold them into `DATA.md`. Cost: one edit each to `AGENTS.md`, `DATA.md` and possibly `kb.py`. It works if there is exactly one place a contributor looks to learn what data task matters most.

**R7. Split `WRITING.md` into a one-page checklist and an appendix, and retire the verbatim-paste rule.** Move `knowledge/editorial/WRITING.md:41-71` (the research review) into the same file below a clear appendix heading or into `knowledge/editorial/WRITING-RESEARCH.md`; keep lines 15–39 as the checklist agents must read. Delete the self-propagating sentence requirement (`AGENTS.md:20`, `CONTRIBUTING.md:23-27`) and replace it with "follow the writing checklist". Cost: none; frozen evidence wording is explicitly preserved by `WRITING.md:3` and stays so. It works if pre-writing reading drops from about 1,400 words to about 500 with no change in `tools/review_prose.py` flag rates on new packages.

## 4. Keep as is

The frozen-output discipline (`AGENTS.md:11`, `CONTRIBUTING.md:34`) and the refusal to move or delete evidence during the restructure (`experiments/2026-10-12-repo-restructure/PLAN.md:12`) are the foundation everything else stands on; do not trade them for tidiness. The conjecture card format — plain claim, single credence with dated history, prediction table with pass rules and credence effects, kill condition (`knowledge/conjectures/TEMPLATE.md`, `knowledge/conjectures/C-0001-rank-written-twice.md:6-12,39-60`) — is the best-designed artefact in the repository and should not be merged into anything. The two-stage shot design with hashed frozen predictions (`experiments/2026-10-12-conjecture-shots/DESIGN.md:9`) and the practice of folding review lessons back as numbered rules (`knowledge/conjectures/README.md:11-17`) should stay, as should `bench/spent.json` as an honest exposure ledger. Findings with JSON-Pointer evidence into exact output fields (`knowledge/findings/F-0001-sequential-prediction-on-unique-texts.md:8`) give claims an audit trail most projects lack. `tools/kb.py` being stdlib-only and deterministic, per-file registry attempts that let parallel contributors avoid merge conflicts (`knowledge/registry/README.md:3-5`), the local-only corpus policy with tracked aggregates (`knowledge/provenance/REDISTRIBUTION.md:23`), the no-CI rule, and `knowledge/DATA.md` itself are all working and should be left alone except where R4 and R6 touch their edges.
