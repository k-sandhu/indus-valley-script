# Calibrating the project's semantic sign assignments

12 October 2026. The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) was frozen before any permutation, bound or transfer outcome was computed ([hash and time](PROTOCOL-FREEZE.json)). The tested assignments belong to other packages and are unchanged: the [anchor-expansion family map](../2026-10-12-anchor-expansion/visual/families.json) of 12 October, the leading positional codes of the [11 October adversarial review](../2026-10-11-bonta-adversarial/REPORT.md), and the lexical seed map of the [9 October Bonta extension search](../2026-10-08-bonta-extension/run-2026-10-09-v5/REPORT.md). Every number below comes from the linked outputs, and [`verification-v2.json`](verification-v2.json) recomputes the headline values by separate code paths. An independent read-only review of the first draft found an annealer error, an unmet adjusted threshold, a verification check of the wrong file and values without saved outputs; [Amendment 1](AMENDMENT-1.md) records the corrections. Post-outcome decompositions and the amendment's resolution extension are marked where they are used.

## Question

Study A of the [published-claim tests](../2026-10-12-published-claim-tests/sanskrit-key/REPORT.md) placed Yajnadevam's Sanskrit key among frequency-stratified rearrangements of its own values and found it 1.2 to 2.4 standard deviations clear of them, against 27 to 72 for true keys on synthetic Indus-shaped corpora. The project's own semantic work counts three kinds of support for its sign meanings: dictionary windows and intersections around Sanskrit anchor stems, role coherence of sign families in complete inscriptions, and the share of inscription slots its map covers. This package asks of each frozen assignment whether those statistics depend on which sign received which assignment, how far the same freedom can push them, and whether fitted role constraints carry from one half of the corpus to the other.

## Findings

**The family-level role result is what rearrangements of the same map produce.** The anchor-expansion map assigns 673 Wells codes to 70 visual families and declares a positional role for 53 of them. Twenty-one of those 53 families meet its rule: at least five containing strings, and more matching tokens than the bag-shuffle expectation in both the discovery and transfer frames. When the family labels are permuted among the assigned codes within token-frequency strata, so that every family keeps its role and its number of members of each frequency, 19.9 families meet the rule on average (sd 2.9, range 11 to 29), and 42% of 1,000 rearrangements reach 21 or more. Within-stratum swaps optimized for this count reach 47 families in each of four restarts, both with the first annealer and with the corrected one of Amendment 1. [Role summary](roles/summary.json), [corrected bound](roles/amendment-r1-bound.json), [figure](figures/role-calibration.svg).

**Code-level and whole-string counts are modestly above rearrangements, and much of the excess comes from one sign.** Sixty-two of the 539 constrained member codes meet the rule individually under their family's role, against 49.1 ± 4.4 for rearrangements (p = 0.003); exact assignment within the same strata reaches 115, so the published map reaches 20% of the gain over rearrangements that its freedom allows. The total excess of matching over expected tokens is 827, against 194 ± 295 (p = 0.026), with an exact maximum of 1,824 (39% of the achievable gain). In 185 strings every constrained token matches its role, against 80 ± 37 (p = 0.012; 0.007 in the 10,000-draw extension of Amendment 1) and a bound of 598. The rearrangement distribution of total excess has two modes, and the jar sign W740 produces them. W740 has 902 of the 8,535 tokens, ends 655 of the 891 strings that contain it ([supplement](diagnostics-supplement.json)), and contributes 519 of the published 827 excess tokens through its closing label. Three of the fourteen assigned codes in the top stratum carry closing labels, so 206 of the 1,000 rearrangements also give W740 one, and 12.1% of those reach the published total. With W740 unassigned in every map, the published total is 308 against 147 ± 127 (p = 0.115). The code-level count stays above rearrangements under both adjustments (p = 0.005 and 0.007). Split by stratum, only the 32–63-token stratum has an excess beyond its rearrangements, 159 tokens against 40 ± 45 (p = 0.006, the smallest of eight unadjusted stratum p values; the others are 0.09 or more). That stratum holds the carrier, panel and staff codes whose meanings the 11 October review chose after their positions were known. This decomposition is a post-outcome diagnostic. [Diagnostic](roles/diagnostics-post-outcome.json).

**Two families meet the adjusted threshold only at finer resolution.** Each constrained family's excess under its declared role was compared with the same family's excess in the 1,000 rearrangements. Carrier/yoke, bound stalks and fish shapes have unadjusted p ≤ 0.005. The protocol combines the 0.01 threshold with Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment across the 53 families, and no family meets it: the smallest q is 0.026, for carrier/yoke and bound stalks. With 1,000 draws the smallest p is 1/1,001, so q ≤ 0.01 needs at least six families at that floor. In a post-outcome extension to 10,000 draws, bound stalks has no draw at or above its excess and carrier/yoke one, both at q = 0.005; fish shapes has q = 0.065 and flag panel 0.126 ([resolution extension](roles/amendment-resolution.json)). Eighteen of the 21 families that meet the two-split rule have p > 0.01 under the frozen draws.

**Position fits made on half the corpus transfer to the other half further than the published roles.** Closure components were split by hash into halves H1 (1,096 strings, 816 components) and H2 (763 strings, 728 components); one component of 237 linked strings falls in H1 and makes the halves uneven. A map fitted only to positions in H1 by exact within-stratum assignment, keeping the published roles and each family's member counts per stratum, scores 663 excess tokens on H2, against 81 ± 126 for rearrangements of the published map scored on H2 (z = 4.6; the largest rearrangement reached 457), and gives 42 families positive excess on H2 against 27.1 ± 3.2 (z = 4.7). The published map scores 330 on H2 (z = 2.0, p = 0.039) with 29 positive families (z = 0.6). Fitting on H2 and scoring H1 gives z = 4.5 for the fit and 2.3 for the published map. Removing evaluation strings within one edit of, or contained in, a string of the fitting half (278 of 763 in H2 and 388 of 1,096 in H1) gives z = 4.9 and 4.8 for the fits and 1.9 and 2.1 for the published map. Letting each family also choose its role on the fitting half changes 26 and 27 of the 53 roles and transfers at z = 4.2 and 4.4, measured against rearrangements of the refitted map. The positional regularities the roles describe are stable properties of signs, and a fit to positions on half the texts recovers about twice as much of them as the published map, which was built with the whole corpus in view. **Lexical windows and intersections do not depend on which sign carries which value.** The October 9 search seeds 134 Wells codes with Sanskrit value lists, 117 inherited from Bonta's Table 18 and 17 new anchors, and looks for Monier-Williams headwords that two- and three-sign windows with one unseeded code can complete. Permuting the value pairs among the seeded codes within strata keeps the window frame, and the 362 searched codes and 274 codes with several contexts recur in every rearrangement, so those counts record which codes were seeded. Nonempty full intersections number 164 against 158.0 ± 10.5 (p = 0.28), and 158 against 152.9 ± 10.6 in the alternative-probe scenario (p = 0.32). Codes whose top five residuals include one with two exact contexts in both scenarios number 201 against 208.0 ± 13.0 (p = 0.74), and the mean support fraction of each code's best residual is 0.757 against 0.756 ± 0.021. Moving the 17 new anchors to random frequency-matched codes without inherited seeds gives 395 ± 175 new-anchor windows against the published 256 (p = 0.91) and 160 ± 9 nonempty intersections against 164 (p = 0.47); in a post-outcome breakdown, the upper mode of the window count comes from the 208 draws that place the *gaṇa* value on W002, the two-stroke sign with 478 tokens, which average 728 windows against 308 for the other draws ([supplement](diagnostics-supplement.json)). Within-stratum swaps optimized for intersections reach 251 and 255 in two restarts, with 114 and 115 of the 134 seeded codes holding a different value pair from the published one, so the published values reach 6% of the gain over rearrangements that the same freedom allows. Swaps optimized for the repeated-residual count reach 264 in both restarts, and the published 201 lies below the rearrangement mean. [Lexical summary](lexical/summary.json), [figure](figures/lexical-calibration.svg).

**The three post-outcome residuals fit the values they were chosen under.** Literal support for W231 → sam is 7 of 22 windows under the published values against 2.5 ± 1.2 under rearrangements (p = 0.001), for W204 → sa 5 of 8 against 2.3 ± 1.1 (p = 0.017), and for W233 → ati 6 of 46 against 4.6 ± 1.7 (p = 0.30). These residuals were selected because they completed *bhāra* and *bhāga* at the published anchor codes, so their support falls when the anchor values move. The best residual for each code, chosen afresh in every rearrangement, has the same support as under the published values: 20 of 46 windows for W233 against 19.0 ± 2.1, 10 of 22 for W231 against 10.5 ± 1.5, and 7 of 8 for W204 against 5.1 ± 1.0 (p = 0.40, 0.74 and 0.059).

**Coverage is fixed by how many codes received labels.** The family map assigns 7,897 of the 8,535 tokens (92.5%), a share that every rearrangement repeats exactly. Tokens held by constrained-family codes number 5,946 against 6,032 ± 356 (p = 0.68), within an achievable range of 4,927 to 6,666 for the same stratum counts. Tokens held by codes that meet the rule individually number 3,453 against 2,301 ± 466 (p = 0.014), which restates the code-level result. The Bonta extension gives each of its 713 codes a meaning, so its coverage is complete by construction.

## Decision rules

| Rule (frozen before outcomes) | Outcome |
|---|---|
| A statistic supplies no assignment-specific support when p > 0.01 | Met for families meeting the two-split rule (p = 0.42), total excess (0.026), whole-string compatibility (0.012; 0.007 with 10,000 draws), constrained coverage (0.68) and every lexical count over codes (0.28 to 1.0). The code-level count (p = 0.003) is the only role statistic below the threshold; among the tracked post-outcome residuals, W231 → sam is below it (p = 0.001) because it was selected under the published values |
| When p ≤ 0.01, report the share of the achievable gain | The code-level count reaches 20% of the gain over rearrangements that within-stratum assignment allows (62 against a mean of 49.1 and a maximum of 115) |
| Per-family p with Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment across 53 families | Not met by any family under the frozen 1,000 draws (smallest q = 0.026 for carrier/yoke and bound stalks; fish shapes 0.088). The post-outcome 10,000-draw extension gives q = 0.005 for those two and 0.065 for fish shapes |
| Fitted constraints transfer if the half-H1 fit exceeds the 99th percentile of the null on H2; compare with the published map | Fits exceed every rearrangement in both directions, with and without near-duplicates (z = 4.5 to 4.9); the published map stands at z = 1.9 to 2.3 |

## What the results establish

The key calibration memo asks whether the corpus prefers a proposed assignment over assignments with the same freedom. For the lexical windows of the October 8–9 work it does not. Rearranging the same values reproduces the intersection and residual counts, swaps of those values reach 255 intersections against the published 164, the window counts depend only on which codes were seeded, and the new anchors open fewer windows than the average frequency-matched placement. For role coherence, the code-level count exceeds rearrangements, and the carrier/yoke and bound-stalk families do so at the adjusted threshold once the null has 10,000 draws. Total excess is inside its rearrangements once W740 is set aside, and the one stratum with excess beyond rearrangements holds the carrier, panel and staff codes whose meanings were chosen after their positions were known. The family-level count reported by the anchor expansion, 21 of 53, lies 0.4 standard deviations above the rearrangement mean of 19.9, and 42% of rearrangements reach it. In every comparison a map fitted to positions alone, on half the texts, transfers further than the published map.

Role statistics depend only on which role each code receives; the anchor-expansion builder notes that item and issuer readings of a family score identically. These results therefore measure how much of the corpus's positional structure a map captures, and they register the pictures and Sanskrit senses only through the roles those imply. A semantic map would need support from observations outside sign order, such as the image–text pairing and object-function evidence of the [structure-first program](../2026-10-12-structure-first/README.md), before its meanings could be distinguished from rearrangements of themselves.

## Methods

The population is the 1,859 distinct reading-order strings of the anchor-expansion complete frame (2,930 complete, clean, single-line Lipi faces of length at least two), with closure components and the published 70/30 discovery/transfer labels read from that package's local companion. Strata are the token-count bins of the published-claim tests (1, 2–3, 4–7, 8–15, 16–31, 32–63, 64–127, ≥128) over those strings. Every null permutes assignments only among codes that already hold one, within a stratum. Seeds come from `SeedSequence(20261012)` with a fixed spawn key per study.

Study R uses the anchor-expansion role definitions and analytic expectations unchanged and reproduces the published 21 families, 62 codes and every family's token counts and expectations exactly. The code-level count and total excess are sums over codes of quantities that depend only on the role each code receives, so their maxima come from linear assignment within each stratum (SciPy `linear_sum_assignment`). The family count and whole-string compatibility are maximized by simulated annealing over within-stratum label swaps, four restarts of 1,000,000 proposals from permuted starts; the family count reached 47 in all four restarts and compatibility 595 to 598. Amendment 1 repeats the family-count search with the corrected annealer. The half split assigns each closure component by the first byte of SHA-256(`indus-anchor-calibration-2026-10-12-half-v1|<component_id>`). The near-duplicate rule is the one used in Study A's Amendment 4.

Study P computes final-position z with the fixed analytic moments of the anchor-expansion `aggregate()` function: for a code occurring n times in a string of length L, the expected final count is p = n/L with variance p(1 − p), summed over strings. The closing excess uses the anchor-expansion closing role.

Study L reimplements the October 8 `lexical_search` with cached dictionary queries and cached per-window residual sets. On the published assignment and on three rearranged assignments it agrees with the frozen function in every code's pattern count, candidate-class count, full intersection and top-five ranking, and in every summary statistic ([engine check](lexical/engine-verification-v2.json)). The frozen normalization maps ṭ to t and t to d in one pass, so it is not idempotent, and the original normalizes residuals cut from already normalized headwords; the reimplementation does the same. Null L1 permutes each seeded code's pair of preferred and alternative value lists among the seeded codes within strata. Null L2 keeps the 117 inherited seeds fixed and moves each new-anchor value pair to a random code of the same stratum that has no inherited seed. The bound anneals within-stratum swaps of value pairs, two restarts of 10,000 proposals per objective from rearranged starts, updating each affected code's counters incrementally; a separate check confirms that the incremental state equals a fresh search after random swaps.

## Problems and amendments

The tested packages and local corpora are uncommitted files in the main checkout, while this session had to write in a git worktree. The scripts locate the inputs through the worktree's common git directory, and the local outputs sit in the worktree's ignored `data/raw/2026-10-12-anchor-calibration/`.

The first cached lexical search disagreed with the frozen function for 336 codes because it treated the normalization as idempotent. The comparison failed before any null was run, and the fix made all four test assignments agree. The first role run stopped with an error in its transfer step after computing its nulls and bounds and before writing any output; it was rerun from the same seeds. The first lexical run was stopped after a timing test showed about three seconds per annealing proposal; the engine was then given per-window caches and incremental counters and split into separate null, anneal and summary stages. The first engine and its check are kept in [`history/`](history/); the kept copy of the first engine differs from the checked version only in the guard against overwriting its output.

An independent review of the first draft found that the first R1 annealer never filled its containing-string counts, so it searched with a wrong five-string condition; its best map still evaluates to 47 families, and the corrected annealer also reaches 47. The review also found that `verify.py` read the first lexical engine check, that the per-family adjusted rule was reported without saying it was unmet, and that several cited values had no saved output. [Amendment 1](AMENDMENT-1.md) records each correction; the run versions of `roles.py` and `verify.py` are kept in `history/` with their outputs.

Timing tests before the bound runs showed annealing values: 5,000 role proposals from one rearranged start reached 43 families, and 200 lexical proposals from the published values reached 185 intersections and 237 repeated-residual codes. The protocol did not fix proposal counts; they were then set to 1,000,000 for roles (from a planned 300,000) and 10,000 for the lexical search (from 6,000). The stratum and W740 decomposition of total excess was written after the role summary was seen and changes no frozen output.
