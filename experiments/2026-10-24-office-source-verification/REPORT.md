# Source review of L-164

The five inspected primary page images support an object-linked difference between published container reconstructions. CISI1 printed p. 373 / PDF 409 identifies L-164 as excavation object 1864 and museum registration LTH SRG 1232. Rao 1985 printed p. 319 / PDF 70 describes overlapping cords on 1864's flat underside and reconstructs a jar closed by a flat stopper. Frenez and Tosi 2005 printed pp. 78–79 / PDF 16–17 includes L-164 within L-161–172 and reconstructs polished wooden containers for that group.

The later reconstruction needs its published qualification. Its Type 5 heading is “Wooden Surfaces (Boxes?)”; the text treats closure of boxes as an inference and calls their shape and size conjectural. It leaves packaging strings versus a fastening device unresolved. Edge-passing strings are identified for L-163 and L-167, so that detail cannot be assigned specifically to L-164 from these pages. The author's main result is supported when described as a disagreement between container interpretations attached through the catalogue identity chain.

This is a separate source review, with four matching PDF hashes and five newly rendered and inspected page images. The exact witnesses and original acquisition URLs are in [source-manifest-v3.json](source-manifest-v3.json), and the inspected pages and participant are in [exposure-manifest-v1.json](exposure-manifest-v1.json). Detailed notes and images remain physically on D:. The review used the author's report to select pages; it did not reuse the author's annotations as its primary observations. [verification-v1.json](verification-v1.json) gives the reviewed scope and decisions. The full assertion ledger, physical specimens, die attribution and office conjecture were outside this review.

The later discussion explicitly engages Rao's nail-mark interpretation and cites Rao 1985 p. 321. The shared excavation assemblage and catalogue identifiers make the publications dependent sources for archaeological identity and provenance. Our separate inspection verifies what those publications say; it adds no independent excavation event.

The bounded count check also agrees with the author's account. Rao 1979 printed p. 113 / PDF 124 reports 65 sealings in successive layers near blocks 41i and 41j, with ash, burnt reeds and a charred log. Rao 1985 p. 319 reports 69 of 90 sealings from a small area at the southern end of the easternmost passage. The inspected pages do not reconcile their inventories, so both counts retain their own source scope.

The strongest supported result is a traceable published disagreement over the support beneath L-164's cord fastening. Calibrated underside and edge views of the identified object would decide whether the contact geometry fits a vessel stopper or a wooden container; this review does not supply those views.
