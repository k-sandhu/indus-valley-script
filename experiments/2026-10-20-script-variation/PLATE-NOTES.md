# Four objects in shared cross-city frames

We inspected these local CISI photograph crops after the frozen scores and the complete source worklist had been read. This is a catalogue-informed visual check by the study author. The source photographs and complete texts remain local. The [image index](../2026-10-13-image-index-and-units/README.md) supplies plate locations and file hashes. Its source page rule is PDF page minus printed page = 35 for CISI 2.

| Object | CISI 2 printed / PDF page | What the photograph shows | Context still needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| H-407 | 266 / 301 | A square seal, a one-horned bovine and apparatus, a rounded leaf or lozenge above the animal, two groups of vertical strokes, and a forked sign. The leaf outline is rounded. | A checked excavation registration and dated context. The export's chronology is a source label, with no calendar conversion in this study. |
| M-712 | 45 / 80 | A square seal with the same broad animal, apparatus and inscription arrangement. The leaf or lozenge outline is angular, and the strokes occupy a different vertical arrangement. | A dated deposition and an image-based carving-style comparison with H-407. |
| H-775 | 324 / 359 | An elongated tablet. Side A carries an A-like shape, an oval enclosure, a sinuous figure and horizontal strokes. Side B has no visible inscription. The top of the A-like shape is rounded; fine features at its foot are unresolved at this crop resolution. | An archaeological context for registration H87-214 and confirmation of the manufacturing comparison. |
| M-1419 | 191 / 226 | An elongated tablet. Side A carries the same broad four-part arrangement as H-775. Its A-like shape has an angular apex. The opposite side has a worn central feature, whose fine identification remains open. | A checked depositional association and secure date. |

The export assigns the changed seal target to Wells 817 at H-407 and 861 at M-712. Both belong to Mayig compatibility class P385, whose definition groups rounded leaf and angular diamond forms. The photographs support that broad shape distinction. They do not independently authenticate the complete fine-code transcriptions. The tablets' changed target is assigned Wells 472 at H-775 and 467 at M-1419, both in P186. The photographs support the common four-part arrangement and the different apex, while the target's foot remains too small for a fine-code audit.

M-1419 has two exported faces. Its photographed A side visibly matches the four-part inscription on record `3706.2`, rather than the one-code record `3706.1`. We propose a source-qualified A-side link for `3706.2`. A B-side link for `3706.1` is an inference by elimination and should retain that status. H-775's B photograph records a plain reverse; it does not add another inscribed face.

These comparisons provide a bounded source check of shared written slots across Harappa and Mohenjo-daro. The same class assignments and related substitution patterns already appear in the project's earlier work. The added evidence here is the source-qualified cross-city worklist, its restriction counts and these identified sides. No novelty claim is made for the graphic classes.
