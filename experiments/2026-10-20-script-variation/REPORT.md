# Cities share graphic conventions in the tested families

Knowing a city did not improve the frozen prediction of which fine sign variant was written. On 306 confirmation components in nine graphic families, adding site reduced the mean score by **0.01584 bits per target**, with a 95% bootstrap interval from **−0.04107 to +0.00610**. The declared useful improvement was **+0.03 bits**, with the interval above zero. That prediction failed. Every leave-one-family-out score remained negative. The result favours the pooled predictor for these observations; it leaves the spoken language or languages unidentified.

## What was compared

We asked whether local drawings of related signs could distinguish regional writing traditions after recorded object differences were taken into account. A graphic family is a set of Wells codes grouped by the Mayig annotator as compatible shapes. It is an input to the test, rather than a newly established sound or meaning. The target-code cutoff at 200 did not exclude fish, despite the frozen protocol's mistaken rationale. It admitted 49 discovery and 47 confirmation fish targets, all code 240. Their class failed the required discovery support for two different variants, so none entered the 306 primary scores. The [scope correction](SCOPE-CORRECTION.md) preserves that error and its exact effect; the frozen code and outcomes are unchanged.

The baseline predicts a target's fine code from its graphic family, carrier, material, animal or other motif, preservation, CISI volume, position, text length and neighbouring graphic classes. The alternative adds site at each level of the same count model. Discovery supplies the counts, using a fixed shrinkage weight of 20 and one pseudocount per candidate code. Confirmation supplies the scored observations. Physical aliases and repeated complete texts were grouped before selection. A deterministic hash selects one target per component, so the primary score gives one vote to each independent group of records.

Discovery supplied 618 eligible components. Nine families contained at least two fine codes attested three times each, leaving 316 discovery and 306 confirmation observations for the primary model. Mohenjo-daro contributes 181 scored components, Harappa 76 and Dholavira 18; fourteen other site labels contribute the remaining 31, including two unknown sites. The numerical support threshold, 100 components and three sites with 15 each, was met. Calendar dates and independent carving-style or workshop labels are not available for this sample.

Each restricted model is refitted on the corresponding discovery objects, and its eligible family set can change. These comparisons share observations and provide one sensitivity record. The object-weighted result has a wider interval than the component-weighted primary result because repeated texts receive additional weight. Adding source volume after site also reduced prediction, by 0.01907 bits. All eleven prespecified comparisons are retained in the [summary](run-v1/summary.json).

Generated controls detected a planted city spelling effect of +0.40540 bits. They returned −0.00421 under no city effect and −0.00429 when city changed the motif mixture and motif determined spelling. The same observed city-conditioned probabilities can be generated by one language with local orthography or several languages using common written conventions. The scorer has no observation that distinguishes those two mechanisms. See [controls](synthetic-v1/controls.json).

## Shared frames and their archaeological support

The frozen exact-frame test required the other fine signs, carrier, motif, preservation and CISI volume to match. It had **zero informative cross-city groups**, both with fine-code frames and with broader graphic-class frames. Its test is infeasible: the stored p-value of 1 accompanies an empty comparison, not an estimated absence of regional association.

After reading that result, we counted matching support across all 2,774 eligible target events with a known site. This is a source worklist, and observations can overlap within inscriptions. It supplies no relaxed significance test.

The [support audit](audit-v1/support.json) preserves the counts. Two strictly matched pairs have held CISI 2 photographs: H-407 with M-712, and H-775 with M-1419. Their source plates and the broad common arrangements were checked in the [plate notes](PLATE-NOTES.md). The seal pair substitutes a rounded leaf form assigned Wells 817 for an angular form assigned 861. The tablet pair substitutes two A-like forms assigned 472 and 467; their fine feet remain unresolved in the small images. The third pair, C-66 with M-1697, lacks a source-volume locator in the held basic data. A `not-held` value matches missing source coverage, so that pair needs its plates before source-qualified use.

These examples support common written slots across cities. They also show where contextual testing stops: of the six strictly matched objects, only one has the study's unbracketed excavation-source qualification. A date and a workshop cannot be inferred from a shared text frame.

## Working model and decisive next observation

**The tested graphic families belong to a cross-city written standard.** We assign this working model a credence of **0.55**. The pooled convention predicts the fine variants at least as well as this frozen city-conditioned model, and source photographs preserve shared written slots in two pairs of objects from Harappa and Mohenjo-daro. A common language, several languages sharing written formulae, and institutions using standardized categories remain observationally equivalent here. The result supplies a constraint for each account: regional differences must explain observations beyond the city label's tested increment.

The decisive next observation is a source-qualified set of new cross-city objects whose matching frames, dates and carving styles are coded independently. The kill condition is a regional fine-variant gain of at least 0.03 bits, with its 95% interval above zero, surviving the matched source, manufacture and workshop comparisons and every leave-one-family-out check. That would require a model of local written conventions within the shared system.
