# Seven missing-face source routes

We completed the seven inherited locator routes and recovered one primary catalogue photograph as a candidate for a missing divided face. No new face bridge meets the frozen admission rule, so the missing-crop count remains 42. Five Harappa locators can now be read more precisely as year, excavation-unit and item references, with their primary figures still missing. [Results](results-v2.json), [source records](SOURCE-MANIFEST-v2.json) and the local seven-row disposition file preserve these outcomes.

The strongest new lead concerns the Dholavira record carrying the unresolved number 348. Its Bailey location, grid square, depth and dimensions agree with accession 20781 in *Dholavira Report (preliminary, Jan 2015)*. The context table places this accession at serial 44, printed page 249, PDF page 266. The illustrated catalogue gives it serial 92, printed page 280, PDF page 297. The exact entry crop is local at `data/raw/2026-10-24-unit-source-pass2/20781-entry-candidate.png`. It shows an incised seal with writing in an upper row and a shorter group below on the right, and a missing lower left corner. We rendered the published orientation without mirroring or rotation. Its original PDF rectangle is [45,425,560,562] points, rendered at three pixels per point.

The source caption counts eight signs, whereas the inherited export counts nine. The report describes six signs in the upper row and two below on the right. The export's number 348 has no verified relation to accession 20781. These records therefore support a specific source candidate, with both the count disagreement and the identifier gap retained. The other Dholavira number, 341, still lacks a unique accession bridge. Direction 1 independently found broad candidate families for these numbers and supplied its page mappings; its ten-case block contains no verified concordance for either number. We reused the held report and acquired no second copy.

The Harappa references contain four digits between the year and the item. Footnote 4 on printed page 3 of Kenoyer and Meadow's *Inscribed Objects from Harappa Excavations 1986–2007* explains that this format omits the registration number and gives the excavation unit and item. This makes the inherited strings usable as lot-and-item lookup targets. It supplies no photograph for those particular pieces. Their attached figure numbers also need a publication title before they become reliable page locators. The [public chapter](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/KenoyerMeadow%202010%20Inscribed%20Objects%20from%20Harappa.pdf) is a 15-page excerpt already held by the project.

Targeted identifier searches returned no matching primary figure. The Harappa slideshow pages supplied excavation descriptions, but the inspected pottery-bat page identifies a different object and cannot resolve the target in the same trench. Direct requests to both slideshow indexes and two pottery pages returned HTTP 403. We also searched the held 2005 report, the 2010 chapter and the 1999 report's existing twelve-page OCR for the five numerical stems and figure strings. None returned a match. The 1999 PDF itself has no extractable text, so its empty text search is recorded separately from the OCR search. These checks do not rule out figures elsewhere in unpublished excavation reports.

The lawful CISI access check reached the [publisher announcement](https://acadsci.fi/en/publications/the-corpus-of-indus-seals-and-inscriptions-3-2-humaniora-383-has-been-published/), the [University of Helsinki bibliographic record](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/corpus-of-indus-seals-and-inscriptions-volume-3-new-material-untr/) and the publisher-linked bookstore. The announcement gives a print purchase route. The institutional record supplied bibliography, and the store returned a bot challenge despite HTTP 200. No catalogue plates or full CISI 3.1/3.2 volume were obtained. The misleading truncated CISI 1 download was excluded. We made no purchase or external contact, and found no new source that warranted reopening H1001.

The protocol preceded acquisition and image viewing. The search ran one extra object query for each of the 9406 and 2229 stems beyond the intended three-query cap, and an additional figure query for 9406; the local search log records this deviation. It produced no admitted match. One preliminary table crop captured the wrong row, and a second was too narrow. Both remain local; version 3 shows the intended context row. A helper initially named inspect.py shadowed Python's inspect module and failed on import; renaming it repaired the run, and the generated cache was moved to D:. One shell regular-expression command failed parsing before execution.

All new source payloads, imagery, extracted records and detailed annotations remain physically on D:. Four primary photographs were displayed together on PDF page 297; the candidate crop repeats one of them. The exposure record includes all text and graphic inspection as historical exploration. We fit no model, change no stock or segmenter, merge no physical identities and open no confirmation cohort. Author validation checks the seven-case accounting, hashes and image coordinates; independent review remains due.

The boldest supported reading is that the Dholavira record carrying 348 points to accession 20781, whose published photograph shows the divided inscription needed by this queue. A publication or register concordance linking those identifiers, together with review of the eight-versus-nine sign count, would decide whether this candidate can remove one face from the missing-source queue.
