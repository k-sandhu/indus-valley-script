# Retrospective: Mackay and Marshall seal tables, 13 October 2026

## Approach

The protocol was written after reading the question cards, the data inventory and a profile of the local Lipi registrations, and before the repository was cloned. That profile had already shown the decisive fact for the join: the export writes Mohenjo-daro registrations with the excavator's catalogue number appended, so `DK343942` is DK 3439, Mackay No. 42. The protocol therefore made field number plus catalogue number the primary key and used type, material, size, findspot and level only to confirm or reject.

The repository was cloned with `--no-checkout` and checked out at the pin; none of its code was run. Hashes were taken from the committed blobs, because Git converted line endings in the working tree for some files on this Windows checkout, and the four notice files matched the witnesses the inventory had saved on 12 October. Mackay vol. I was not held, so its 51 MB Internet Archive scan was downloaded once; the table starts on PDF page 392 (printed page + 23), found by reading two page headers.

## Problems and fixes

The first development join treated Lipi `SEAL:C` as a cylinder seal. It is a circular seal (`SEAL:CY` is the cylinder), and the code also extended type contradictions beyond the square seals the protocol named. Together these made 18 rows ambiguous: twelve ivory rods, sealings and incised tablets that the excavators list in their seal tables, and six circular seals. The comparator was corrected to the protocol's wording. The block-and-house parser read `8III` as block 8I and house II, which turned 386 findspot comparisons into disagreements until it was fixed.

Marshall's area letters appear under other prefixes in the export, and the export lists several catalogue numbers in one registration (`HR 2240471/472/473`) for objects the excavator listed more than once. Both forms were added as Amendment 1, with the aliases read from development matches in which serial and plate number both agreed. A variant key that accepted any field-number-only registration one edit away brought in 40 candidates for 30 rows, mostly copper tablets contradicted on type, material or size; it was dropped and replaced by the narrower forms in Amendment 2. Eleven exact-key candidates were contradicted on material or size. Reading the print showed that ten were real conflicts between the excavators' tables and the Lipi record (Mackay prints silver for two seals the export calls steatite; one Lipi size belongs to the row above), so Amendment 3 added an adjudication file rather than a looser automatic rule.

Locating sampled rows on the page took longer than planned. Tesseract misread the hand-set row numbers often enough that a nearest-neighbour interpolation landed several rows off; a Theil-Sen line through the numbers it did read placed the Marshall crops correctly. The census's Marshall scan is binarized and drops the arms of E and F, so rows 429 and 482 first looked like transcription errors; a second scan through the Internet Archive IIIF service showed E in both. Four of the eight IIIF page requests went to the wrong pages before the canvas numbering was understood. The CISI 2 scan stores a faint page image under a masked full-resolution one, so the page had to be rendered, not extracted, and its basic-data lines were found by cropping estimated strips and reading them by eye.

Several regenerations of the final run directory followed small corrections (an unspecified `SEAL` type treated as a disagreement, object values taken from the first face only, two added coverage fields). Each time the row dispositions were compared by hash with the run from which the join sample had been drawn, and they were identical.

## Outcome

The tables are CC BY 4.0 and now held. The Mackay transcription matched the print in all 30 sampled rows; the Marshall transcription had errors in 3 of 30, and its serial column has more, which the variant-key joins expose. 1,146 of 1,269 rows joined, with 29 of 30 sampled joins confirmed and none wrong. The joins add little to the export's size, material, level and findspot coverage, because the export already holds values derived from these tables, but they give 1,134 objects a separated field number, a publication reference, a size with its printed source and a level with its datum, and they record 72 object-field disagreements between the tables and the export and 14 places where the transcription differs from the print. Mackay's plate volume and CISI 3.1 would close the remaining checks.
