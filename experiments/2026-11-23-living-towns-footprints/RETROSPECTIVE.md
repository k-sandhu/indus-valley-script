# Retrospective: georeferencing buried Indus urban footprints

Date: 2026-11-23.
Package: `experiments/2026-11-23-living-towns-footprints/`.

## Approach

We began by inventorying all primary texts, site plans, imagery mosaics and elevation rasters already held under `data/raw/` before downloading any external files. All three Copernicus GLO-30 30 m DEM tiles, Harappa's ESA WorldCover 2021 clip and zoom 16–18 mosaics, Kenoyer's 1991 and 2008 Harappa site plans, Biagi and Vidale's 2022 Lakhanjo-Daro paper and *Indian Archaeology 2011–2012 – A Review* (containing Prabhakar's 21-trench Rupnagar datum grid) were already present locally. We froze [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) before acquiring open ESA WorldCover 2021 windows, Esri World Imagery zoom 17–18 mosaics and OpenStreetMap extracts for Lakhanjo-Daro and Rupnagar, then built deterministic registration, rasterization and scoring code ([code/build_footprints.py](code/build_footprints.py)) paired with an independent verification suite ([code/verify.py](code/verify.py)).

## Challenges encountered and fixes applied

1. **Reading ESA WorldCover 2021 Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs without `rasterio`**: `rasterio`, `pyproj` and `shapely` are absent from the local Python 3.13 environment. Because ESA WorldCover v200 files are classic DEFLATE-compressed tiled GeoTIFFs on AWS S3 supporting HTTP range requests, we implemented a 60-line pure-Python TIFF IFD and `zlib` range reader in [acquire_inputs.py](code/acquire_inputs.py) that fetched only the intersecting 1024×1024 tiles (< 50 KB per site) and wrote clean WGS 84 GeoTIFF clips, while using exact Snyder USGS Transverse Mercator formulas for all UTM metric calculations (< 0.05 mm roundtrip error).
2. **Transient OpenStreetMap Overpass failures**: The initial Overpass query returned HTTP 406 until an explicit `User-Agent` and `Accept: application/json` header were supplied, and the first Lakhanjo-Daro query hit a transient HTTP 502 gateway error. Retrying the Lakhanjo-Daro bounding box succeeded and returned 1,368 elements.
3. **Published coordinate offset at Rupnagar**: Fetching our first zoom-18 mosaic around the printed *IAR 2011–12* coordinate (`30.9664°N, 76.5331°E`) caught only the extreme eastern edge of the archaeological mound. Inspecting the zoom-17 overview, Copernicus GLO-30 DEM and OpenStreetMap `Ropar Harappan Site` polygon located the physical mound 1.06 km northwest around `30.9716°N, 76.5244°E`. Re-centring a zoom-18 mosaic directly over the mound resolved the exact north-east corner of Nalagarh Kothi's pavement (`30.971621°N, 76.523804°E`), and projecting all 21 trenches from that corner aligned every trench group onto its described terrain feature.
4. **Control-point diagnostics and cartographic errors on Biagi and Vidale (2022, Fig. 3)**: Early wide-frame picks on Lakhanjo-Daro showed 15–35 m residuals caused by mismatched eastern lane picks. Overlaying Figure 3 directly onto zoom-18 imagery locked all road and wall lines together, reducing isotropic similarity RMSE across six sharp Road 3, 7 and 8 intersections to 2.00 m while revealing that the printed 200 m scale bar spans ~510 m on the ground and the caption latitude sits 455 m north of the drawn Mound D rectangle.
5. **ESA WorldCover 2021 false positives on unbuilt Harappan mounds**: Visual inspection of `harappa_4layer_map.png` showed WorldCover class 50 (Built-up) pixels over conserved ancient brickwork and dry clay slopes on Mounds F, AB and ET. Rather than altering the deterministic WorldCover layer, we added per-zone `wc_built_overlap_ha` accounting to every Layer 1 polygon so both the raw WorldCover overlap (39.3%) and the true modern Harappa town overlap (18.34 ha, 25.2%) are reported transparently.
6. **Raster-to-polygon self-intersection caught by `verify.py`**: The first independent verification pass failed on a single vectorized Layer 3 polygon (`HARAPPA-L3-OVERLAP-07`) where `cv2.approxPolyDP` crossed a 1-pixel diagonal pinch. Applying a 2×2 morphological opening and checking ring simplicity across decreasing approximation tolerances in `vectorize_mask_utm()` removed the self-intersection, and the subsequent verification pass succeeded across all 91 polygons and 28 windows.

## Outcomes

All deliverables are complete, reproducible from local inputs and independently verified (`overall_passed: true`): the frozen protocol, three four-layer GeoJSON files, `summary-v1.json`, `windows-v1.csv`, `verification-v1.json`, three diagnostic figures, `REPORT.md`, `DATA-UPDATE.md` and `RETROSPECTIVE.md`.
