# Georeferenced buried Indus urban footprints beneath Harappa, Lakhanjo-Daro and Rupnagar

Modern buildings, factories and streets conceal substantial portions of all three living Indus-period towns we mapped, yet clear open parcels and public street corridors still survive immediately beside every buried urban sector. Across the three sites, our registered archaeological footprints cover 139.09 hectares in total, and 73.05 hectares intersect modern built-up land in ESA WorldCover 2021. Separating genuine living-settlement cover from remote-sensing artifacts clarifies where buried strata sit and where non-invasive surveys can still reach them: modern Harappa town covers 18.34 hectares (25.2% of our 72.79-hectare mapped ancient footprint), matching Kenoyer's 1991 estimate of approximately one quarter; Sukkur's industrial estate covers 36.92 hectares (70.5%) of Lakhanjo-Daro's 52.41-hectare terrace envelope while leaving Mound D itself unbuilt; and old Rupnagar town covers 7.03 hectares (93.7%) of the 7.37-hectare southern mound, or 54.1% of the 13.89-hectare four-mound complex.

All spatial outputs follow the frozen [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) and pass independent verification in [verification-v1.json](verification-v1.json). The four-layer GeoJSON datasets live in [harappa-windows-v1.geojson](harappa-windows-v1.geojson), [lakhanjo-daro-windows-v1.geojson](lakhanjo-daro-windows-v1.geojson) and [rupnagar-windows-v1.geojson](rupnagar-windows-v1.geojson). Aggregate metrics are recorded in [summary-v1.json](summary-v1.json), and all 28 prioritized geophysical parcels and municipal rescue corridors are ranked in [windows-v1.csv](windows-v1.csv). The three PNG map overlays under `figures/` composite local Esri World Imagery reference mosaics, so those raster figures remain local-only under `.gitignore` and `knowledge/provenance/REDISTRIBUTION.md` and can be regenerated locally with `code/build_footprints.py`.

## Registration checks and audited cartographic errors

Every published map or metric datum passed the pre-specified registration bounds in [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md), and direct georeferencing exposed three concrete coordinate or scale errors in published sources that would otherwise misplace fieldwork.

At Harappa, registering Jonathan Mark Kenoyer's 1991 site plan (*Urban Process in the Indus Tradition: A Preliminary Model from Harappa*, Figure 4.2, printed p. 31) onto Esri World Imagery zoom 18 using six permanent features spanning Mound F, Mound AB, Harappa Museum and the Rest House yields a planar root-mean-square residual of 5.12 m, a maximum leave-one-out error of 11.09 m and a fitted scale of 1.0406 m per pixel (within 1.15% of the printed 200 m HARP grid). Independent cross-checks of R. E. M. Wheeler's 1947 Plate XV (*Ancient India* 3: 58–130) using the repository's previously audited control-point files produce 5.32 m RMSE (12 points, registrar R1) and 3.14 m RMSE (9 points, registrar R2), confirming the Mound F and Mound AB geometry.

At Lakhanjo-Daro, registering Paolo Biagi and Massimo Vidale's 2022 annotated satellite figure (*Asian Perspectives* 61(1): 6, Figure 3) onto Esri World Imagery zoom 18 using six intersections along Roads 3, 7 and 8 yields 2.00 m RMSE, 5.84 m maximum leave-one-out error and an isotropic scale of 1.2194 m per pixel that matches the independent OpenStreetMap Road 8 to Road 3 separation (285.3 m) to 0.02%. That ground check uncovers two errors in the published figure: first, the black bar printed as 0 to 200 m spans 418 pixels on the rendered page, corresponding to 509.7 m on the ground (a factor of 2.55); second, the printed caption coordinate (`27°43'44"N, 68°50'55"E`) falls 455 m north of the actual Mound D rectangle drawn south of Road 8 (`27.72479°N, 68.84827°E`, or `27°43'29"N, 68°50'54"E`). Once placed from the registered roads, Mound D sits only 103 m northeast of Manik Mustafa Shar and Saeeda Shah's 2022 coordinate (`27.72417°N, 68.84750°E`) and 207 m northeast of the Department of Archaeology and Museums inventory point (`27.72389°N, 68.84643°E`).

At Rupnagar, V. N. Prabhakar's excavation report in *Indian Archaeology 2011–2012 – A Review* (pp. 72–76) fixes a metric grid datum `(0, 0)` at the north-east corner of the pavement of Nalagarh Kothi and lists exact metre coordinates for 21 trenches of 5 m by 5 m. Locating that pavement corner on zoom-18 imagery (`30.971621°N, 76.523804°E`; UTM 43N `645522.77 E, 3427452.78 N`) places all four trench groups onto their exact described terrain features: trenches `N5E20` to `N5E30` fall directly inside the tree-filled north-south rain gully east of Nalagarh Kothi, `N45E120` to `N50E145` sit on the northeastern mound slope, `S65W20` to `S70W5` sit on the southern slope above modern houses and `S20W175` to `N20W185` sit on the western cemetery mound east of College Road. By contrast, the report's printed site coordinate (`30.9664°N, 76.5331°E`, p. 72) sits 1.06 km southeast near Bela-Ropar Road, and the ASI Chandigarh Circle site card (LT-S13) prints `30°28'N`, roughly half a degree south of Rupnagar.

## Harappa: modern town overlap and bare-tell misclassification

Mapping Harappa's eight published archaeological zones from Kenoyer (1991, Figure 4.2; 2008, Figure 3) demonstrates both the ~25% living-town overlap and an important limitation of global land-cover masks on arid brick mounds. Inside our 72.79-hectare union of Mound F, Mound AB, Mound E, Mound ET, the northeastern walled sector under modern Harappa town, the Low Western Mound, Cemeteries H and R37, and Area G, raw ESA WorldCover 2021 class 50 (Built-up) marks 28.62 hectares (39.3%). Per-zone auditing explains the gap above Kenoyer's 1991 estimate: inside the northeastern walled sector beneath modern Harappa town (`HRP-L1-NE-TOWN-SECTOR`, 21.76 ha), WorldCover records 18.34 hectares of modern structures (83.4% of that sector, or 25.2% of the total 72.79-hectare site), whereas the remaining 10.28 hectares of class 50 pixels fall on unbuilt conserved ancient brick masonry and high-albedo clay crests inside the protected reserve on Mound AB (4.75 ha), Mound F (2.57 ha), Mound ET (1.89 ha), Area G (0.77 ha), Mound E (0.28 ha) and Cemetery H (0.02 ha). Global built-up rasters therefore overstate urban encroachment on exposed tell sites unless audited against high-resolution imagery.

![Harappa four-layer map](figures/harappa_4layer_map.png)

Comparing the 1990 town outline on Kenoyer's Figure 4.2 against 2025 imagery shows that modern housing expanded south and east toward the northern edges of Mounds E and ET while leaving two critical transitional clearings open. Our scoring prioritizes four Tier 1 targets and four Tier 2 open parcels:

- **Tier 1 open geophysical grids**: `HRP-W04` (Cemetery H to Mound AB south open ground, 2.12 ha, score 86.27, +5.8 m relative elevation, 68.1% clean open surface) and `HRP-W03` (Low Western Mound open terrace, 1.77 ha, score 84.02, +4.5 m, 78.5% clean open surface) offer immediate, unobstructed ground-penetrating radar (GPR) and fluxgate gradiometry windows over Harappan and Late Harappan deposits.
- **Tier 1 rescue-section corridors**: `HRP-W09` (Chichawatni–Sahiwal road frontage, 1,078.5 m, score 89.04) and `HRP-W08` (Harappa town western and southern perimeter street corridor, 720.4 m, score 85.30) trace public rights-of-way directly across the concealed northeastern sector margin and southern gateways.
- **Tier 2 town-contact open parcels**: `HRP-W01` (central inter-mound clearing along the unexcavated western wall line of the town sector, 1.80 ha, score 60.39) and `HRP-W02` (open transition strip between southern Harappa town and Mounds E and ET, 2.09 ha, score 57.09) are the highest-value open parcels for testing whether Early Historic and medieval occupation spans the post-1300 BCE gap beneath the living town.
- **Demotions and remote-sensing audit**: `HRP-W07` (Mound AB Muslim tomb and citadel core) is hard-demoted to Tier 3 under rules D1 and D5 (`heritage_or_sacred_restricted`), and `HRP-W10` (eastern Harappa town margin, 76.8% built-up in WorldCover) is hard-demoted under rule D1. `HRP-W06` (the eutrophic green pond at the northwest town edge) highlights a second WorldCover bias: algae cover causes WorldCover 2021 to label standing water as vegetation (`wc_water_frac = 0.0`), though Copernicus GLO-30 detects its -0.56 m local depression.

## Lakhanjo-Daro (Sukkur SITE): industrial burial and the surviving Mound D window

Lakhanjo-Daro is the most heavily urbanized of the three sites, yet our registration proves that the exact vacant parcel containing Mound D and Trench C remained unbuilt in the latest imagery. Following ul-Haq and Ali (2001: 29, quoted in Biagi and Vidale 2022: 5) and the Directorate of Antiquities, Government of Sindh (2022), we bound Layer 1 to the 52.41-hectare elevated limestone terrace across the Sukkur Sindh Industrial and Trading Estate (SITE) rather than adopting the unauthenticated 300-hectare claim. We do not invent unmapped trench shapes for Mounds A, B or C. Within that 52.41-hectare terrace, modern industrial sheds, paved yards and streets cover 36.92 hectares (70.5%, uncertainty bounds 27.52 to 47.36 hectares).

![Lakhanjo-Daro four-layer map](figures/lakhanjo_daro_4layer_map.png)

Inside this industrialized landscape, open survey capacity concentrates tightly around the Road 7, Road 8 and Road 3 block where all three independent site coordinates converge:

- **Tier 1 urgent open parcel**: `LKD-W01` (Mound D vacant industrial plot, plots D9–D11 south of Road 8, 1.17 ha, score 75.45, 90.7% clean bare ground, 9.3% edge built-up) sits directly over the 1996 Trench C copper-workshop and `GrN-23123` radiocarbon context. Because industrial infill surrounds it on the east and south, `LKD-W01` is the single most time-sensitive open geophysical target across all three cities.
- **Tier 1 arterial utility corridors**: `LKD-W05` (Road 3 southern spine, 933.6 m, score 76.87), `LKD-W06` (Road 7 western craft-sector spine, 729.7 m, score 76.32) and `LKD-W04` (Road 8 northern spine directly fronting Mound D, 748.4 m, score 74.01) follow the exact metalled rights-of-way where ul-Haq and Ali (2001: 29) recorded water, sewerage and gas trenches exposing Harappan architecture.
- **Tier 2 contiguous open plots**: `LKD-W02` (central-west unbuilt plots directly containing the DOAM and Shar and Shah anchors, 4.88 ha, score 61.67) and `LKD-W03` (walled vacant plots across Road 8 north of Mound D, 5.86 ha, score 58.41) provide over 10 hectares of accessible unbuilt ground for electrical resistivity tomography (ERT) and GPR mapping of lower surviving craft and residential strata.
- **Demotions**: `LKD-W07` (active factory sheds east of Mound D, 79.9% built-up) is hard-demoted to Tier 3 under D1 and D4; `LKD-W08` (disturbed eastern refuse-and-pond zone documented by the Directorate of Antiquities 2022) is soft-demoted to Tier 3 under D4 and redirected to deep ERT and targeted coring; and `LKD-W09` (northwest off-terrace flood basin, 38.9% water, -1.54 m relative elevation) is hard-demoted under D3.

## Rupnagar (Ropar): the elevated southern mound beneath old town streets

Combining Prabhakar's 2011–2012 datum grid with the Copernicus GLO-30 digital elevation model resolves Rupnagar's living-town geometry cleanly. The four-mound archaeological complex spans 13.89 hectares in our Layer 1 mapping, matching Prabhakar's published estimate of roughly 12 hectares (*IAR 2011–12*, p. 72). Elevation data separate the protected northern, central and western mound reserve (`RPR-L1-PROTECTED-MOUND`, 6.33 ha, mean 284.3 m MSL, peaking at 293.1 m near Nalagarh Kothi) from a second, even higher mound mass immediately to the southeast (`RPR-L1-SOUTHERN-MOUND-TOWN`, 7.37 ha, mean 287.1 m MSL, peaking at 293.83 m at `30.9697°N, 76.5264°E`, +20.9 m above the Sutlej terrace plain). Dense old Rupnagar houses cover 7.03 hectares (93.7%) of that southern mound, producing a total concealed overlap of 7.51 hectares (54.1% of the 13.89-hectare site, uncertainty bounds 5.02 to 10.10 hectares). All four 2011–2012 excavation clusters lie inside the unbuilt protected reserve (0.0% built-up).

![Rupnagar four-layer map](figures/rupnagar_4layer_map.png)

Because the southern mound is densely built and the northern mound slopes carry heavy tree cover, actionable windows split cleanly between the municipal sports fields immediately north of the Harappan trenches and three public streets crossing the built-up mound flanks:

- **Tier 1 open geophysical grids**: `RPR-W01` (Nehru Stadium bare southern playfield, 0.95 ha, score 79.93, 63.5% clean bare ground) abuts the northern foot of the protected mound only tens of metres from the 2011–2012 Harappan and Bara trenches (`N45E120`–`N50E145`), where earliest occupation lay just 50 cm below the modern surface; `RPR-W02` (Nehru Stadium athletics track and cricket turf, 2.59 ha, score 79.69, 73.9% clean open turf) extends this unobstructed testing ground northward.
- **Tier 1 rescue-section corridors**: `RPR-W06` (southern mound old-town contact street, 590.4 m, score 95.00), `RPR-W07` (College Road flanking the western Harappan Cemetery mound, 492.8 m, score 95.00) and `RPR-W08` (old Rupnagar bazaar arterial crossing the 293.8 m southern mound crest, 476.2 m, score 95.00) provide the only direct non-demolition access to the concealed southern mound deposits.
- **Demotions**: `RPR-W09` (dense old bazaar housing block, 96.7% built-up) and `RPR-W04` (Government College quadrangle, 94.2% built-up in WorldCover) are hard-demoted under D1; `RPR-W05` (wooded northern mound flank, 92.6% tree cover) and `RPR-W03` (Nalagarh Kothi plateau lawns, 68.2% tree cover at 10 m WorldCover resolution due to surrounding canopy blur) are soft-demoted under D2 to fluxgate gradiometry or canopy-gap ERT rather than continuous wheeled GPR.

## Methods and shared assumptions

All spatial measurements were computed in WGS 84 UTM projections (EPSG:32643 for Harappa and Rupnagar; EPSG:32642 for Lakhanjo-Daro) on a deterministic 2 m metric rasterization grid and exported to WGS 84 GeoJSON (EPSG:4326) via [code/build_footprints.py](code/build_footprints.py). Layer 2 modern built-up footprints come from ESA WorldCover 2021 v200 (10 m, class 50) cross-checked against Esri World Imagery zoom 16–18 mosaics and OpenStreetMap extracts. Relative topographic prominence ($\Delta z$) is measured in Copernicus GLO-30 (30 m) above the 15th-percentile elevation of a 300 m to 1,500 m annulus around each site (160.87 m at Harappa, 59.61 m at Lakhanjo-Daro and 268.23 m at Rupnagar). Layer 3 overlap uncertainty bounds propagate each Layer 1 feature's stated positional uncertainty buffer ($\pm 6\text{ m}$ to $\pm 50\text{ m}$) by morphological erosion and dilation before intersecting Layer 2. Every GeoJSON file, area calculation, projection roundtrip (< 0.05 mm closure), registration residual, Layer 3 overlap and Layer 4 score was recomputed independently by [code/verify.py](code/verify.py) with zero failures.

WorldCover built-up polygons measure surface land cover in 2021 rather than subsurface preservation depth: a modern paved street or single-storey house can seal intact Harappan walls below shallow footings, whereas bulldozing or deep refuse pits can remove upper levels under an unbuilt lot, as documented at Lakhanjo-Daro. The priority scores therefore rank physical survey accessibility and topographic promise; they do not predict artifact density or substitute for field permits and stratigraphic verification.

## Boldest supported reading and deciding test

The boldest reading our georeferenced footprints support is that all three settlements preserve substantial, still-testable Indus-to-later stratigraphic transitions directly beside or beneath living urban quarters. Under this reading, the apparent chronological break at Harappa after c. 1300 BCE and the documented Period I-to-PGW hiatus at Rupnagar partly reflect where excavators could dig (the protected mounds) rather than town-wide abandonment, because 25.2% of Harappa's walled core and 93.7% of Rupnagar's tallest southern mound remain sealed beneath occupied streets. At Lakhanjo-Daro, the convergence of all three independent coordinates onto a still-unbuilt 6-hectare cluster of vacant SITE plots (`LKD-W01` and `LKD-W02`) shows that a major urban industrial centre of the Upper Sindh Indus core remains accessible to rapid non-invasive mapping before factory construction closes the window.

The deciding field test combines two coordinated, permitted campaigns across these Tier 1 and Tier 2 windows:

1. **Co-registered GPR and electrical resistivity tomography (ERT) grids** on `HRP-W01` and `HRP-W02` (Harappa town contact), `LKD-W01` and `LKD-W02` (Lakhanjo-Daro Mound D and central-west core) and `RPR-W01` (Rupnagar Nehru Stadium bare playfield) to trace buried mud-brick perimeter walls, street grids and intact cultural deposit depths (> 2 m) without large-scale excavation.
2. **Systematic municipal trench-watching and small-diameter geoarchaeological coring with AMS radiocarbon dating** along the three urban-crest rescue corridors (`HRP-W08`, `LKD-W04` and `RPR-W08`) whenever water, sewerage or foundation cuts open beneath living streets. Recovering dated Late Harappan, Painted Grey Ware, Early Historic or medieval occupation surfaces in `HRP-W01`/`HRP-W08` or `RPR-W08` would close the living-town continuity gap directly; finding sterile flood silt or clean cultural breaks in those same corridors would establish genuine multi-century abandonment across the full urban footprint rather than local mound shifting.
