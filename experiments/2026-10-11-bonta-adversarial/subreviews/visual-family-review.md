# Visual review of carriers, toothed implements and divided panels

The carrier family supplies the closest picture-to-meaning comparison in this selection: a central person-like figure sits beneath a crossbar with two suspended loops. The other selected graphics support toothed implements, divided panels and adjacent grids more directly than they support a particular occupation, account category or building. The next catalogue should preserve those graphic descriptions beside its imaginative semantic choices.

This review inspected actual pixels with `view_image` after reading the proposed meanings. It is therefore an informed, unblinded comparison. Font glyphs, chart drawings and photographs have different evidential roles: the first two regularize a sign, while the photographic crops preserve the visible source objects. The photographs remain local. The review did not independently identify every Wells variant on an artifact or establish what any inscription says.

## Carrier components and variants

The local W151 font glyph has a central vertical stroke, two spreading lower strokes, a horizontal crossbar, diagonal strokes toward the bar ends and a loop suspended from each end. The lower strokes agree with the person-like shape used by W130 and W142 in the same font sheet. A person carrying a shoulder pole with two loads is the strongest immediate scenario. A balance with two pans or another suspended-vessel arrangement is a shape rival; component topology alone does not assign an occupation. The meanings worker, consignment and issuer emblem remain separate interpretations of the same carrier picture.

W154 and W156 retain the two suspended loops and add paired short hook-like marks above the crossbar. W156 retains diagonal internal strokes that W154's regularized glyph omits. Those marks make a qualified carrier family a useful proposal. They do not draw a complete vessel: “jar-like head handles” already includes the container interpretation. Describe the visible addition first, then keep the jar or container qualification as a scenario supported by the cited prior work.

The public W154 and W156 pictures are identical licensed P004/V145 class crops. Their SHA256 values both equal `30b648254a6b7493fc59e7b77d61c2b16f435525c87a9976c4a61c79d9bb0d33`. They preserve the broad family while showing none of the font's internal-stroke distinction. The existing class-image caption is accurate; a family-specific note should also explain why these two public pictures look identical. Separate occurrence counts cannot be interpreted as evidence about a visible distinction in those displayed crops.

W161 has the suspended-loop design plus a small triangular feature above the crossbar. The local font joins the upper side contours more continuously than the public class drawing. “Triangular-head carrier variant” is the clearest visible label across the two representations. “Enclosed carrier” can suggest a whole surrounding boundary that the public drawing does not clearly supply. The image provides no rank, protection or crew-size distinction.

## The comb and the photographed implement

W400's regularized drawing contains one long spine and several parallel strokes projecting from one side. The public P098/V051 crop preserves that topology but has a different number and spacing of projections. Its immediate object comparisons are a comb, rake or harrow-like toothed implement. A category or counted group comes from the proposed functional interpretation and its closing positions, rather than from a literal depiction of people or a completed sum.

In the local M-16A crop, the person-like sign holds a short horizontal object with multiple upright projections. Its similarity to the standalone comb motif is stronger than its similarity to a plough with a long beam and a working share. The public W130 chart class preserves the person, bent arm and toothed object; the font simplifies the object into a compact branching outline. Keep “person holding a toothed implement” as the observation. A tool-using worker is a useful immediate interpretation; cultivation, another craft, a comb or a carried emblem require additional context. The photograph does not settle those alternatives.

The M-16A panel has a separate gridded object in front of the animal. That element belongs to the larger pictorial scene. It should not be joined to the text-band tool sign as though the photo directly illustrated a cultivation transaction. The current report avoids that step.

## Divided panel and paired grids

The local W527 glyph is a narrow panel with horizontal divisions between two side strokes; the left side extends above the panel and the right side below it. The public P301/V041 crop has fewer internal divisions while retaining that asymmetric frame. “Barred or divided panel” states the shared graphic more clearly than “hatched bounded panel.” Neither representation has a roof, a person passing through an opening or grain contents. Door panel, ladder segment, rack, container partition and abstract class mark remain reasonable shape/function alternatives. The public crop cannot support an exact compartment count for W527.

W527 also resembles a W400 comb whose free projection ends have been joined by a second upright. This supports a related-design or modified-comb rival. It does not establish that one sign was historically derived from the other or that they are interchangeable allographs. That stronger claim needs artifact variants and matched context substitutions; similar closing roles alone do not supply the join.

The W617 font shows two upright rectangular grids separated by white space. In the upper M-81A panels, two distinct grid outlines are also visible with a gap between them. I see no joining stroke that requires treating them as one compound sign. The crop includes both the incised seal view and the raised impression view, with their mirrored layouts; these are views of the same catalogued object. The shape supports two adjacent grid marks. A single paired category and two repeated independent tokens are both available segmentations, and the source-coder disagreement makes that choice material to subsequent lexical windows.

The public W617 SVG is an authored two-grid schematic with its own row and column counts. The template explicitly labels it a schematic. It illustrates adjacency but should not be used to judge the number of cells, exact spacing, engraving differences or whether a token boundary exists. “Two grids; token boundary unresolved” would make the headline observation easier to distinguish from the proposed paired-compartment meaning. A store, field, container or measure is a functional hypothesis rather than an object identification supplied by these pixels.

## Recommended changes to the next catalogue

- Give W154/W156 the component description “carrier with paired hook-like marks above the crossbar,” and say that both public cards use one shared class drawing. Retain container-associated carrier as a semantic scenario.
- Use “triangular-head carrier variant” for W161's direct graphic description; retain other qualifications as alternatives.
- Describe W400 and W130 through their toothed components. Keep group/category and cultivation choices beside their statistical/context evidence without treating the tool shape as independent evidence for those specific functions.
- Replace W527's hatch wording with “barred/divided panel,” and avoid transferring cell counts from either representative to an artifact.
- Make W617's two-grid observation and unresolved token boundary prominent. Preserve both the one-composite and two-token interpretations before running word-window tests.
