The unjoined queries samvāha and saaṃśa remain absent. A statement about absence therefore needs to name the literal query model; it cannot cover these attested joined forms.

## New semantic anchors and lexical flexibility

The five comparison words have different dictionary productivity under a fixed unknown-component bound of one to six SLP1 letters containing a vowel. These are generic two-component queries, with no new phonetic inscription windows asserted. The following table gives the number of candidate components before each anchor and the fraction of 1,000 same-length random dictionary stems with at least that many components. Both orders and all fixed alternative stems appear in [RESULTS.json](RESULTS.json).

| Anchor | Literal candidates before anchor | Finite-join candidates before anchor | Literal control fraction | Finite-join control fraction |

| bhārin | 4 | 4 | 0.250 | 0.293 |

| dvāra | 69 | 69 | 0.013 | 0.026 |
| kṛṣi | 0 | 0 | 1.000 | 1.000 |

Varga and dvāra readily produce numerous short components in the dictionary. Their many completions make isolated compound matches a weak way to select a sign meaning. Bhārin produces four preceding components, compared with 41 for bhāra and 15 for bhārika. Kṛṣi has no preceding component within this model, but has fourteen literal and twenty-four finite-join components after it. Lāṅgala has seven preceding components, compared with twelve for the harrow comparison matya. The counts depend on exact dictionary forms and the stated join rules; this model has no stem morphology or unrestricted Sanskrit parser.

The control fractions compare lexical productivity. They do not measure the probability that an Indus sign bears the comparison word's meaning. The useful semantic work remains the comparison of distinct predictions for the carrier, counted container, collective, compartment and toothed-implement candidates. This repair supplies a reproducible account of which dictionary matches and rival rankings can support that discussion.
