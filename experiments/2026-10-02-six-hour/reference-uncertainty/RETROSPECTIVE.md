# Reference-uncertainty investigation

CRYPTO-02 produced wrong maps with high mass under a fitted stationary reference. I developed two follow-ups: integrate finite-reference parameters with a Dirichlet predictive likelihood, and introduce one plausible but erroneous supplied identity. The protocol fixed new systems, sample sizes, identity conditions and held-out comparisons before the pilot and main outcomes.

The integrated score initially needed a careful accounting choice. The data are ordered strings, so the calculation uses gamma ratios for start and transition counts without multinomial coefficients. Direct sequential prediction checked that formula. A separate compound-score check confirmed that held-out prediction used the reference updated with the mapped training records.

The pilot completed 192 trials. The main run completed 2,592 trials across 24 systems. The results reduced confident errors in the unchanged administrative process and exposed remaining failures after a process change. Wrong supplied identities often carried a held-out penalty, with saved exceptions showing when this diagnostic missed them.

The first review attempt tried to import the frozen program from its archive location. Its root-directory calculation then sought the generator under the wrong path and stopped before producing a review endpoint. The repair executes the frozen bytes with their original source-path context, while checking the archived program and helper hashes. No benchmark result was rerun or replaced. The corrected review independently checked held-out scores in all 216 conditions.

The next question concerns observation choice. When several independent identity measurements are available, can a mapping posterior choose a useful next measurement under process mismatch? The current results provide a reason to retain both its conditional confidence and the measured recovery curve. A separate protocol will fix that comparison before new outcomes.
