# Source and object methods

The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) was written and hashed before new source acquisition. It selected the three reported Kanmer pendants and allowed additions explicitly connected by an original source. Initial search snippets had already exposed the three-object group, a common stamp, different reverses, the reported perforation and a page 832 lead. The selection was therefore a bounded evidence audit. The 2023 fourth pendant and the excavators' associated objects entered through that stated rule.

## Publications and source coordinates

The acquired 2010 Antiquity PDF is the original five-page report. The publisher's current website gives an April 2026 online date while retaining its March 2010 volume and issue. Both dates are recorded. Figure assets labelled `fig3` and `fig4` by the website correspond to printed Figures 3a and 3b. The [first image observations](antiquity-first-observations.json) preserve six source-relative views before registration joins.

The annual report in Occasional Paper 10 supplies the reported dimensions of the presumed stamp and a clay impression on printed page 90, public preview page 98. Its public text uses private-use characters for decimal digits. The extraction helper preserves the original characters and creates a separate view mapping U+F0030 through U+F0039 to 0 through 9. The strings 26.7, 25, 34.52 and 4.15 were checked against the web-readable report text. The original figure atlas shows three fronts and a 5 cm scale.

The public Chapter 8 draft has 13 pages with its own page headers. Pages 4–6 contain individual registrations and physical entries. The later 2023 chapter cites final-volume pages 749–759, while its bibliography dates the chapter to 2011. The acquired Chapter 5 author copy and institutional catalogue establish the volume's March 2012 publication. Draft page numbers remain the coordinates for our inspected Chapter 8 text; the final Chapter 8 pages have not been acquired.

The Chapter 5 file was reached through NDL's explicit source-library link, then the PDF icon on the Science Tokyo repository record. It contains 55 pages: a metadata page, an author-copy cover, and printed pages 165–217. The GIS section ends at 196; the PDF also includes two later survey sections. PDF page 25, printed page 187, contains the labelled scanner images used here. The [reviewed manifest](gis-figure-manifest-reviewed.json) preserves original embedded raster bytes, page bounds, hashes and image sizes.

The 2023 author-hosted chapter text and native figure assets establish four displayed pendants. Figures 3 and 4 show the fronts and numbered reverses; Figures 5–8 show comparative objects. The public figure page's automatically selected caption misidentified the following asset, whose pixels show Figures 9 and 10. We use the printed captions and original pixels. The chapter's front matter states CC BY 4.0 unless otherwise specified; individual figure exceptions remain unchecked, and all third-party witnesses stay local-only.

## Identities, faces and context

The scanner captions identify `899c`, `900c` and `1286b`. Their numerical stems are unique among the Chapter 8 entries, allowing joins to 09-899, 09-900 and 08-1286. The source suffixes remain scan labels. Each caption contains front and reverse views of one displayed object, along with one or two profiles. This provides stronger face correspondence than pairing separately photographed panels by their arrangement.

The 2023 reverses are numbered 1–4. The proposed old-three joins use matching broken areas and the authors' corresponding alternative sign identifications: No. 1 to 08-1286, No. 2 to 09-899, and No. 3 to 09-900. Those registrations are absent from the 2023 caption. The parallel panel arrangement suggests the front/reverse pairing, which remains separate from the caption-stem joins. [First 2023 annotations](primary2023-first-observations.json) preserve pixel bounds and visible features; [scanner observations](gis-first-observations.json) preserve each rendered view.

The 2023 group paragraph gives three finds in the settlement's central part and one outside the fortification. It leaves that outside find unassigned to a displayed number. Its likely relation to No. 4 depends on the old-three joins. The original Chapter 8 entries assign all three registered pendants to layer 8; the group paragraph describes Z30 at the top of layer 9 and the two open-space specimens in layer 8c. Both levels of description remain in the ledger. No acquired context register resolves them.

The excavators assign these objects to KMR IIB from the archaeological sequence. A radiocarbon table in the multicultural-site report provides dates for sampled contexts, but the acquired specimen entries lack a direct join to an individual laboratory determination. Registration, phase assignment and laboratory dating therefore have separate fields.

## Physical observations and use

Author-reported material, measurements and operations are kept beside visible observations. A dark opening in a photograph establishes a visible hole mouth. Its full axis, edge wear and residue require another view or direct measurement. The source scanner report gives the instrument's minimum sampling pitch of 0.1 mm and describes mesh wrapping. It does not state the actual pitch for each pendant. The acquired PDF has no embedded mesh file; its rendered views provide profiles and face correspondence, with limited access to bore geometry and fine wear.

The production sequence reported in the GIS section is stamping and perforating clay before baking. The Chapter 8 and 2023 reports describe reverse engraving without establishing its precise place in that sequence. The authors' country, community, individual name and passport interpretations are stored as published proposals. Our operational comparison asks which handling actions can produce the observed form and future measured wear.

The [audit helper](../../../src/six_hour/kanmer_audit.py) computes field coverage and descriptive measurements from the eight-object ledger, verifies source references and witness hashes, and saves a protected endpoint. Seven registered objects have 25 displayed scanner views. Repeated photographs of the same specimens add source views rather than new archaeological objects. Width, mass and thickness variation describe three reported measurements and are presented with that denominator.
