# Kanmer clay pendants and their proposed use

The acquired sources now support a four-object photographic frame for the Kanmer inscribed clay pendants. The original three have registrations, dimensions, masses and trench/layer entries. A 2023 chapter adds a fourth photographed specimen, whose registration and measurements remain unavailable. The 2012 scanner report supplies caption-linked front, reverse and side views for the original three. These records make a physical handling experiment possible and identify the source joins that still need excavation-register confirmation.

The frame contains eight objects: four clay pendants, a related broken clay impression, two associated steatite seals and a two-hole shell object discussed by the excavators as a possible suspended object. Each field in the local [object ledger](object-ledger.json) identifies its source or observation basis. The [source register](source-register.json) gives publication and page coordinates. The detailed ledger, first face annotations, original PDFs, photographs and extracted source text remain local-only. A fresh repository checkout contains their hashes, source pointers, aggregates and [schema](OBJECT-LEDGER-SCHEMA.md); the local project contains the complete records.

The original three measure 32.5–34.1 mm in reported length, 34.5–35.4 mm in width and 15.7–16.6 mm in thickness; they weigh 15.0–15.7 g. Their width coefficient of variation is 1.15%, calculated descriptively across these three surviving specimens. The 4.15 mm perforation diameter is a shared source datum repeated in several publications. The [computed endpoint](main/results.json) preserves the values and denominators.

All four photographed fronts have the same broad animal/object arrangement and two upper graphic units. Their four numbered reverses have different marked layouts, with two partly broken or weak examples. The common stamp is an excavator assertion supported by broad visible correspondence. The acquired figures lack the raw surface coordinates needed to compare a matrix geometrically. The authors also interpret fading animal details as seal wear; clay transfer, firing and preservation need to be measured together before that mechanism can be isolated.

The earliest acquired explicit passport proposal is Andrew Lawler's May 28, 2010 Science report, which attributes the suggestion to Toshiki Osada. The March 2010 Antiquity report already shows the three objects and suggests different uses or users from their reverse markings. The original 2009 excavation article remains uninspected, so the proposal's earlier publication history is open. Later reports develop a shared authority symbol plus individual reverse-mark interpretation and suggest thread suspension. The acquired sources supply no measured suspension wear or documented cord residue.

The next useful experiment is [a controlled suspension and handling study](PHYSICAL-TEST-PROTOCOL.md). Its target is the excavator's specific claim that the off-centre perforation helps display the motif. Original geometry, resting orientation and hole-edge wear can distinguish freely hanging display from attachment to a parcel and repeated hand inspection. Matching those traces would help specify the object's physical task; sign meanings would still require a separate linguistic argument.
