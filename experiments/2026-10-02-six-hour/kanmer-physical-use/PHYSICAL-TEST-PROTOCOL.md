# Test the display and wear produced by suspension

The excavators propose that a thread passed through the slightly off-centre hole and that its position helped display the stamped motif. The acquired photographs show the opening, and the scanner figures show convex reverse profiles for three registered pendants. The immediate physical question is whether those geometries produce a stable, legible display when suspended, and which hole-edge traces each handling action produces.

This is a proposed experiment written after the source audit. No replica was manufactured or physically tested in this run. The design uses the original three shapes and keeps the fourth specimen as a separate later addition once its registration and geometry are recovered.

## Measurements to acquire first

Acquire the original point clouds and meshes for scan labels 899c, 900c and 1286b, or obtain equivalent complete object scans with scale and accuracy checks. Record the actual instrument settings, sampling pitch, registration residuals, smoothing and hole-filling operations. The source reports a minimum pitch of 0.1 mm; its wrapped renderings leave the bore interior unresolved.

For each object, record the complete hole centreline, mouth diameters, internal taper, edge-rounding profile, body volume, surface normals and the relationship of both faces to a fixed physical coordinate frame. Mark the stamped motif's upper direction independently of camera labels. Record repairs, loss and density variation. Photograph and measure the bore before cleaning, including adhered fibres, mineral deposits, directional polish and striations. The object register should reconcile the layer 8/top-of-layer-9 descriptions and identify the photographed fourth specimen.

Use three-dimensional surface correspondence to compare the stamped fronts and the surviving portion of seal 09-1996. Fit only intact regions, report alignment residuals and include deliberately mismatched seals as controls. This step can establish whether a specific die produced the impressions and distinguish seal wear from transfer and clay damage. The later site's use interpretation does not enter the fitting criterion.

## Replica and handling block

Make 42 replicas: 36 used specimens and six unused controls. For each of the three object shapes, make two replicas for every combination of two declared cord materials and three handling actions, plus two unused controls. Use the measured bore geometry and the reported mass range of 15.0–15.7 g. Record clay, firing, shrinkage and replica deviations. Prefer actual measured density and shape over resizing a convenient uniform disc.

The actions are freely hanging display, attachment to a fixed parcel surface, and repeated hand inspection while attached to a cord. Use one plant-fibre cord and one animal-fibre cord, both 1.5 mm in diameter, as declared laboratory choices. The acquired sources identify no surviving ancient cord. Pass a single loop through the bore, hold its ends 30 mm apart and 100 mm above the suspended body, and record the knot and measured contact position. In the parcel action, hold the convex reverse against a flat backing and attach the same loop along a recorded axis. Record cord tension around the 0.15 N gravitational load expected from a 15 g object, with the same environmental conditions across the comparison.

Before wear conditioning, release each replica from eight equally spaced starting rotations, ten times each. Measure the resting orientation of the motif, which face points outward, settling time and rotation variability. Record an unloaded static condition and a standard small motion condition. Measure cord tension and contact position. Calculate the centre of mass from the measured geometry and density, then independently compare its predicted equilibrium with filmed resting orientation.

Condition used replicas for 1,000 declared handling cycles, with scans and microscopy before use, after 100 cycles and after 1,000 cycles. Keep unused controls in the same environment. The parcel action pulls along a recorded attachment axis; the hanging action permits free rotation; the inspection action alternates controlled turns to the two faces. Preserve force and motion logs so that equal cycle counts remain interpretable.

## Outcomes and decision rule

The primary display outcome is the distribution of resting motif angles and outward-facing surfaces for each shape/cord/action combination. A prespecified display score records whether the stamped face lies within 45 degrees of the front camera and whether the motif's upper direction lies within 30 degrees of vertical. Report the continuous angles beside this score. The wear outcomes are edge-radius change, polished area, groove depth, striation orientation and abrasion on the convex reverse. Annotators should score these without seeing the handling label, using source-independent specimen codes.

A hanging-display account predicts repeatable resting orientation or an easily maintained face-forward orientation, together with concentrated wear where the cord bears on the hole. Fixed parcel attachment predicts a different contact-axis and body-contact pattern. Hand inspection predicts repeated changes in cord contact and potentially broader handling wear. These patterns can overlap; report the measured separation and overlap across replicas.

Use one replica from each shape/cord/action pair to develop trace criteria and the second as a held-out observation, giving 18 used specimens in each set. Compare archaeological surfaces only after measuring that separation, and include excavation/preservation damage in the reference set. If the intact geometries hang with inconsistent or obstructed faces, the specific claim about automatic motif display loses support. If a shape requires an additional loop, knot or backing to display consistently, that addition becomes an explicit archaeological prediction. A matching trace would specify a handling action; administrative checking, travel and ownership remain historical functions that need context and distribution evidence.
