# Work record for the known-channel follow-up

The parent task proposed a bounded observation-error follow-up after the noisy-face endpoint was frozen. The protocol was written while that calibration run was still active. Recovery fitting began only after its main endpoint completed and its independent audit passed. The follow-up reused all 1,000 stored populations, preserved their hashes and left their calibration scores and references unchanged.

The first calculation derived agreement and marginal inversion for a shared symmetric eight-category channel. A small test checked rational identities, zero/random-channel limits, probability normalization, simplex projection and expectation/maximization arithmetic. A deterministic rounded-expected-count table recovered ε near .1 and matched a separate constrained optimizer. These test inputs, sources and outputs are retained in [selftest](selftest/) and [large-expected-counts](large-expected-counts/).

Before fitting the stored populations, the comparison amendment added a simple distribution estimate with the joint fit's .5-per-category shrinkage. This control was needed to read the frequency result alongside its prior. It proved useful: its mean total variation was .227690, compared with .245280 for the joint fit, in the ten-object cells. The joint fit's advantage over projected inversion alone therefore does not identify the channel correction as the best small-cell estimate in this study.

Two finite calculations were prepared independently of the main recovery outputs. Exact absence probabilities and a category-set recurrence predicted negative known-rate inversions in almost every population. The binomial agreement-count enumeration supplied the direct estimator's expected rate error and bias. Their observed recovery summaries agree with the stated sampling scale. The additional unequal-rate limit shows how nonuniform marginals supply a rate ratio; a uniform latent distribution retains an exact rate ambiguity. Every calculation has its design note, source snapshot and endpoint.

The main fit and bootstrap took 11.57 seconds. All starts converged within 34 iterations, their objectives were monotone, and the three unknown-rate starts agreed in final objective to 1.42e−13. The independent audit checked every population, count table, objective, trace, frequency error and projection; selected replays and constrained-optimizer comparisons passed. The raw inversion produced 10,335 negative components, closely matching the exact expected count of 10,331.87 across 1,000 populations. Pooling sixty thousand pairs reduced the correction's sampling error enough for its frequency estimate to improve on the observed marginal.

The source review found one protocol detail to make explicit in a future run: at an undefined direct inverse, the code returns a uniform frequency estimate. The original protocol named the boundary rate but did not specify that distribution fallback. No main population reached it. [IMPLEMENTATION_DOMAIN_NOTE.md](IMPLEMENTATION_DOMAIN_NOTE.md) preserves the discrepancy without changing the completed source or results.

The source check used Dawid and Skene's primary journal abstract and bibliographic record, Dempster, Laird and Rubin's full author-hosted article, and Allman, Matias and Rhodes's author paper. [RELATED-WORK.md](RELATED-WORK.md) states the relationship of those models to the restricted channel used here. A future misspecification study should vary one source mechanism at a time and keep its latent truth outside estimation.
