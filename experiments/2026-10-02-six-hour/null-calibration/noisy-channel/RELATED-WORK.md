# Paired measurements and latent observation errors

Dawid and Skene estimated observer error rates for categorical patient responses with unobserved true categories. Their model treats true responses as latent variables and uses expectation/maximization to estimate observer behavior. The primary journal abstract and bibliographic record support that connection: [*Maximum Likelihood Estimation of Observer Error-Rates Using the EM Algorithm*, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series C 28 (1979), 20–28](https://doi.org/10.2307/2346806). The present simulation replaces general observer-specific confusion probabilities with one shared symmetric eight-category replacement rate.

Dempster, Laird and Rubin give the expectation/maximization construction and its extension to posterior modes. The extension adds the log prior to the maximization step and increases the penalized objective. [*Maximum Likelihood from Incomplete Data Via the EM Algorithm*, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series B 39 (1977), 1–22; discussion 22–38](https://www.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/jchan/GLM/Dempster1977EM.pdf). This follow-up explicitly states its Dirichlet and Beta penalties and checks the full objective trace.

Identification concerns whether different latent parameters produce the same observable distribution. Allman, Matias and Rhodes derive conditions for latent mixture identification using three-way arrays and conditional independence. Their source also discusses latent label permutations and exceptional parameter choices. [*Identifiability of parameters in latent structure models with many observed variables*, Annals of Statistics 37 (2009), 3099–3132](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.5032). For the restricted two-face channel here, agreement directly determines ε on the declared branch and the marginal determines p. Additional independent views are a useful next measurement when a future observation model allows separate confusion probabilities or latent categories.

The algebra, exact absence counts and recovery outputs test that restricted channel. A possible archaeological application would first need aligned observations of the same inscribed category and a measured account of how their errors co-vary. Independent photography, physical impressions and separately produced transcriptions offer candidate observations with different dependence structures. Their joint source history belongs in the observation model.
