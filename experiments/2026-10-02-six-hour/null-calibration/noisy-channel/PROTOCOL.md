# Recovery of a known symmetric observation channel

Written October 2, 2026, before fitting this follow-up. This is a post-outcome research extension of the noisy-face calibration design. The two timing-pilot outcomes and the original independent and unique-record calibration summaries have been seen. The noisy-face main endpoint is still running. This follow-up will begin its recovery calculations after that endpoint is complete and its 1,000 saved populations are frozen. It reuses those populations and creates no new archaeological observations or simulation populations.

Each population contains 20 objects, three slots and two observed faces per object. The eight latent categories and six site/slot cells are known. The generating replacement rate is .1. A face retains its latent category with probability 1−ε and otherwise chooses uniformly from the other seven categories. Face errors are independent given that category; ε is shared across the six cells, while each cell has its own latent distribution p. Corresponding faces represent the same latent category, and objects and slots follow the independence rules of the saved generator. These assumptions identify the restricted channel tested here. We select the branch `0 ≤ ε < 7/8`, where a face retains positive association with its latent code. All recovered rates refer to these synthetic populations.

Let H be the probability that the faces agree. The channel gives `H=(1−ε)²+ε²/7`. Invert it on the declared branch with `ε=(7/8)[1−sqrt((8H−1)/7)]`. Use the empirical agreement fraction from all 60 object/slot pairs as the direct estimator. Report an agreement fraction below 1/8 as outside the inversion domain and place its estimate at the branch boundary for the constrained comparison. Let m be the mean of the two faces' empirical marginal distributions in a cell. With `λ=1−8ε/7` and `t=ε/7`, the direct latent estimate is `p=(m−t)/λ`. Save that raw vector, including negative components. Apply Euclidean projection onto the probability simplex for the direct estimator's frequency comparison. Also compute raw inversions at the known ε=.1, which isolates the sampling problem from channel-rate estimation.

Fit a second estimator to the ordered pairs. Their probability is `r(a,b)=Σx p(x)Kε(a|x)Kε(b|x)`. Maximize the pair log likelihood plus `.5 Σcell,x log pcell(x) + .5 log ε + .5 log(1−ε)`. This fixed penalty corresponds to Dirichlet(1.5) cell distributions and a Beta(1.5,1.5) channel prior restricted to the declared branch. The calculation is a penalized joint fit. The expectation step computes each latent category's posterior probability. The maximization step adds .5 to its expected count and normalizes by `ncell+4`; it updates ε to `(expected replacements+.5)/(2N+1)`, clipped to `[1e−10,7/8−1e−10]`. A known-channel companion keeps ε=.1 and uses the same distribution penalty.

Use three starting rates, .02, .1 and .3, with the observed cell marginals as the initial distributions after adding .5 per category. For each start, retain every penalized-objective value and stop when both the maximum parameter change is at most 1e−8 and the objective increment is at most `1e−9 max(1,N)`, or after 500 iterations. Select the start with the largest final objective; report every failure to meet the stopping rule and any objective decrease larger than 1e−8. Fix these settings before the main fits. The known-channel fit uses one start because its distribution objective is concave. No tuning uses latent truth.

For each saved population, report ε error, absolute error and squared error for the agreement and unknown-channel joint estimators. Compare four cell-frequency estimates with the generating p: the observed marginals, projected agreement inversion, unknown-channel joint fit and known-channel joint fit. Report mean total variation and mean squared component error across the six cells. Count negative raw components and affected cells/populations for the estimated and known-channel inversions. Preserve all fitted distributions, raw inversions, traces and input hashes. Pool pair counts within each of the six corresponding cells across all 1,000 populations and repeat the same fits as a larger-sample check.

Estimate uncertainty across the 1,000 independent populations with 20,000 bootstrap resamples using seed 6,026,100,207. Report mean rate bias, mean absolute error and frequency error, and the paired difference in total variation between the joint and projected agreement estimates. These intervals describe repeated sampling under this generator. The fit outputs also report likelihood and penalty separately so a frequency comparison can be read alongside its optimization objective.

Before using the stored main populations, verify the agreement and marginal identities with rational arithmetic, joint-probability normalization, ε=0 and ε=7/8 limits, and a small latent-assignment likelihood enumeration. Independently check the simplex projection and expectation/maximization arithmetic. After the run, replay selected fits, check every saved estimate's domain and trace, and compare selected joint solutions with a separate constrained numerical optimizer. Save all mismatches and any repair in a new verification record. Calibration audits and their reports retain priority if this bounded branch takes longer than expected.

The latent observer-error model follows [Dawid and Skene (1979), *Maximum Likelihood Estimation of Observer Error-Rates Using the EM Algorithm*](https://doi.org/10.2307/2346806), with a shared symmetric kernel replacing their general observer error rates. The expectation/maximization steps and posterior-mode extension follow [Dempster, Laird and Rubin (1977), *Maximum Likelihood from Incomplete Data Via the EM Algorithm*](https://www.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/jchan/GLM/Dempster1977EM.pdf). Their framework supplies the fitting method; the agreement inversion and restricted identification calculation are derived explicitly above.
