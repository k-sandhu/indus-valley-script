# Prospective test of the contact boundary

The first physical experiment should measure which parts of one carved matrix transfer onto clay at known curvature and pressing angle. This addresses the largest missing premise in the forward model: whether retained groove relief follows the local penetration rule. Archaeological acquisition can then supply comparable measurements from an original vessel fragment and its candidate matrix.

This protocol was written after the synthetic model. No physical specimens have been produced and no outcome has been selected. Freeze a dated copy and measured matrix geometry before taking impressions.

## Materials and measurements

Use a rigid 24×8 mm matrix carrying the four invented graphic classes in eight slots. Prepare four cyclic shifts of a repeating four-class row, so each class occupies each position across the set. Measure the face, grooves and any edge bevel with calibrated microscopy or profilometry. Preserve the digital matrix design, fabrication method and measured surface mesh. A groove depth near 0.4 mm and width near 0.16 mm connects the test to the primary run, but predictions must use the measured dimensions.

Prepare a single documented clay batch in coupons supported on flat, 25 mm and 100 mm radius forms. Record coupon thickness, forming method, elapsed drying time, gravimetric water content from companion samples and temperature. Make sacrificial flat impressions to measure retained relief at displacements of 0.1, 0.4 and 0.8 mm. Record load and displacement throughout pressing, dwell and withdrawal. The load curve will reveal whether displacement control is reproducible and whether the coupon support deforms.

Use a jig to set row rotation at zero and 90 degrees, row tilt at minus two, zero and plus two degrees, and displacement at 0.1, 0.4 and 0.8 mm. Three radii, two rotations, three tilts, three displacements, four layouts and three repeats give 648 impressions. Randomize their order within clay-state blocks; retain every failed or incomplete impression with its failure code. If this is too large for an initial batch, first use the flat and 25 mm supports at rotation zero, keeping all layouts, tilts, displacements and repeats. That pilot has 216 impressions and must be labelled as the reduced design before fabrication.

## Observation and analysis

Measure the surface before and after pressing with calibrated three-dimensional imaging. Map each observed impression to the measured matrix in its physical coordinate system, including positive-impression polarity. Keep both the raw surface and registration transform. Calculate transferred stroke length and relief height continuously before imposing the simulation's 0.08 mm and 80% thresholds. Photograph each coupon with diffuse and raking light, a scale and a documented pixel resolution.

Estimate retention from the sacrificial flat calibration impressions. Use those parameters to predict the curved impressions without fitting them again. Report absolute stroke-fraction error, boundary-location error and class visibility disagreements, grouped by radius, tilt and displacement. A displacement model with no curvature term is the prespecified comparison. Numerical checks should include measured matrix irregularities as well as the ideal straight-stroke design.

Paired opposite tilts provide the bounded symmetry test. After restoring all images to the same physical coordinate frame, exchange row ends in the negative-tilt observations and compare them with positive-tilt observations. Pool the paired conditions with equal weight, then measure the first and last class distributions. Analyze intact surfaces first. Create left-quarter and right-quarter masks digitally on the registered surface maps for a separately labelled preservation test; real fractures would add a new physical process.

A repeated failure of the relief law on held-out curved impressions should lead to a new material-flow model with independently measured clay parameters. Preserve the geometric model and its errors. A successful contact test would support acquiring comparable radius, wall thickness, relief, matrix grooves and break coordinates for an archaeological specimen.
