# Reserved identity verification

Written after CRYPTO-04 and before this experiment's pilot. The earlier entropy policy reduced mapping cost while increasing wrong confidence-based stopping decisions under a changed administrative process. This run adds a separately selected observation after the candidate map has been frozen.

Use 24 new systems from each of the two frozen eight-symbol generators, seed 510261002 with the existing family and repetition offsets. Reference sizes are 40 and 400 records; training sizes are 40 and 160; process-mixture changes are 0, 0.35 and 0.70. Samples within a system use nested prefixes. Preserve the integrated first-order score over all 40,320 bijections.

Acquire reliable identities by maximum marginal entropy until the selected map first has conditional mass at least 0.95. Freeze that candidate, the revealed pairs, remaining codes and probability vector. A verification policy then chooses up to one, two or three previously unqueried codes. Compare uniform sampling without replacement with the highest marginal-entropy codes under the frozen posterior. The latter does not update its ranking during the audit. Query selection receives no answer key or oracle state. A separate oracle returns each selected identity. Accept a candidate only if every verification response agrees with its frozen prediction. A failed audit records a rejected proposal; this experiment does not search for a replacement afterward.

Report initial proposal accuracy, wrong acceptances, correct acceptances, rejections and additional observations. A correct proposal always passes under the reliable-oracle assumption. For a wrong bijection with U unqueried identities and m disagreements, the exact probability that a uniform k-code audit passes is choose(U−m,k)/choose(U,k), using zero when the numerator has insufficient codes. Enumerate every verification subset to check this formula. Since revealed pairs are correct, a wrong bijection has at least two disagreements among the remaining codes. This provides a conditional worst-case bound with m=2. Include a fourth uniform rule selecting the smallest k with that bound at most 0.05, or observing all remaining identities when needed.

The seven policies share each frozen proposal and observation history. Evaluate generated systems as the independent comparison unit, averaging four reference/training conditions within each process change. Preserve actual sampled audits and their exact conditional error probabilities. Review score calculations on an independently regenerated condition from every system and compare audit probabilities with exhaustive subsets. Keep failed pilots and fresh completed endpoints separately.

The identities are supplied synthetic measurements. The experiment studies verification cost and model mismatch; an archaeological use needs an independently justified measurement likelihood and an explicit account of measurement error.
