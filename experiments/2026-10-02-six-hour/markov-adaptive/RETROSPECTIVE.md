# Work record for additional history shrinkage

The earlier second-order endpoint improved top-one restoration by 2.6845 percentage points over first order and increased bucket loss by 0.127736 bits. Selecting its global strength by inner loss returned first-order backoff in every fold. This follow-up asked whether a training-count-dependent weight could retain useful two-state histories while reducing their probability error. Both earlier outcomes were known when the new protocol was written.

The preserved second-order factor already shrinks its empirical history counts toward the first-order factor with weight N/(N+α). Adding a second weight N/(N+κ) produces effective strength α+κ+ακ/N. Writing this equivalence before fitting clarified the intervention: an extra penalty on rare histories. It also exposed equivalent parameter pairs. Their removal reduced twenty finite grid entries to seventeen distinct factors, with the pure first-order option retained. First-order strength twenty, the parameter grid, inner loss criterion and tie ordering were fixed before outcomes.

The implementation imports immutable snapshots of the original grouping, Markov baseline, second-order model and flat-count reviewer. Those files compute workspace paths from their original location, so the loader supplies the original import names and restores the baseline workspace root after loading its copy. The source hash checks confirm which functions are in use. This avoided edits to older code or a PM/PMB refit. Masked restoration uses the candidate's own factor plus its effects on the next two factors, including the end state. Toy complete-chain likelihoods verified the beginning/end cases before the corpus fit.

Every outer fold chose pure backoff. The closest finite candidate, α=2000 and κ=500, had inner loss about .009 bits above it in all five training populations. The full candidate table and the exact equality to M1 remain saved. The objective and grid were kept unchanged after this outcome. The 195-second fit and 12-second independent review completed without a failed or restarted endpoint.

The review derives finite factors directly from effective strength rather than repeating the production mixture. It independently counts 13,045 contexts, checks 3,859 rational coefficient identities, and reconstructs all masked and complete-chain probabilities. It also checks selection against the saved inner allocations, query identity, training-only alphabets, unknown handling, hidden-target invariance and all reported resampling summaries. Numerical discrepancies stay within 2.22×10⁻¹⁶ for factors and 1.55×10⁻¹⁵ for complete-chain normalization. All saved top-k predictions agree, so the exact-rational tie mechanism found no disputed ranking.

The result narrows the current model-development path. This extra count-based shrinkage family still favors first order when selected for probability loss. The earlier accuracy-selected second-order endpoint remains useful for ranking, with its recorded loss cost. A future probability-focused test would need a different premise, such as training-only calibration of the complete conditional distribution or a separately measured class of contexts. That design should use a fresh protocol and preserve this candidate table as the comparison it must improve upon.

Rao et al.'s [2009 Markov paper](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~rao/PNASIndus.pdf) supplies the published restoration context. Its smoothing and corpus differ from this benchmark. The algebraic mixture here reuses the repository's preserved models; the claim is the measured outcome of the stated grid and selection rule.
