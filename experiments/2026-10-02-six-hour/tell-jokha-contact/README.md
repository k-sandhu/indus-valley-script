# A community lead traced to a scholarly figure

The Tell Jokha lead now has a visible source chain. A June 2026 community post named TJ17-PRO-S56 and the SAHI season-two report's Figure 6.61. Frenez's 2023 chapter reproduces a Tell Jokha square seal as Figure 3D, citing the same report figure and page 59. We acquired its public composite photograph and inspected panel D. The [source manifest](source-manifest.json) records the local witness and hash; the [observation table](observations.json) gives panel coordinates and the basis for each field.

The displayed object has approximately square, damaged margins. Large graphic forms occupy its upper region, above an incomplete figurative region. An enclosed pointed oval-like form and a tall open cup-like form are visible toward the upper centre/right. The panel supplies neither a numeric scale nor a rear view. Its small resolution leaves the fine sign inventory and carving polarity unassigned.

This later reproduction already places the graphics above the figurative field. Comparing it with the original SAHI illustration would establish the transformation between publications. The original report's object caption and body remain the acquisition target for registration, material, surface-find coordinates and dating basis. Those fields currently retain their community attribution or an empty value.

The chapter also points to a separate Umma clay sealing and to inscribed tablet impressions on local vessels at Tepe Yahya and Salut. These are useful comparison targets because attachment traces and pottery production can reveal the act performed with the inscribed object. [Author chapter](https://www.academia.edu/108938835/Indus_Valley_Early_Commercial_Connections_with_Central_and_Western_Asia), [public figure and citation](https://www.harappa.com/content/indus-valley-early-commercial-connections-central-and-western-asia).

## Access and next work

The public figure and Harappa source page were acquired by ordinary HTTPS requests. The author's Academia page was readable through the web tool, including Figure 3's caption and note 74, while a direct local request returned 403. The original report's ResearchGate page supplied bibliographic metadata, but its observed PDF link returned 404 through the web tool and 403 through an ordinary request. The Academia profile supplied a report preview and a download link whose retrieval was restricted. The [access record](access-log.json) preserves these routes. No report PDF was acquired.

A repeat search should start with the specific missing report pages and object registration. High-resolution face and rear photographs, material identification and the project's prospection coordinates would allow a better comparison. The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) fixes these targets and keeps acquired third-party witnesses local.
