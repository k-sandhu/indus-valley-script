# Work record: choosing independent validation observations

KHI-04 retained a wrong decoder that passed eight fresh account equations. Those equations repeated one carry relation and had rank one. This follow-up asked whether an equal number of validation checks could be allocated using coefficient independence. The prospective policy choices for this comparison were frozen while the underlying worlds and candidate decoders remained the previously scored KHI-04 population.

The design gave the selector eleven coefficient rows and their observation types, keeping measured targets and decoder candidates in separate files. A known unit anchor was supplied as a coefficient row only in the counted view. Account-first, count-first and greedy rank choices were sealed before scoring. Uniform random was evaluated over every subset, avoiding run-to-run sampling variation.

## Routes that leave a wrong decoder

Account-first repeated the rank-one failure through all three tested budgets. It also left one of the four-swap baseline's nine wrong rays compatible after three checks. Greedy rank made the rows independent and removed every wrong ray after two checks with a unit anchor or three checks without it.

Count-first used fewer checks on the same population: one count removed all nine wrong rays with known scale, and two counts removed them without scale. A single count without scale allowed all nine wrong rays to fit different positive scales. Greedy rank also produced a wrong absolute point at its second check on the seen rank-one witness; its third check excluded that point. These intermediate false solutions remain saved in the endpoint.

The count-first comparison shows why the generic rank rule should be kept beside a simple physical-observation policy. Rank measures independence of the selected coefficient rows. Validation also intersects the original candidate set, which may already restrict several degrees of freedom. A selected count can therefore distinguish a candidate with less than full rank in the validation rows alone.

## Family and scale handling

The implementation represented normalized points as positive rays and line families as positive homogeneous planes. It retained the original collective equality or halfspace and added selected observations by rational reduction. A separate inequality-elimination implementation classified every deterministic family and checked its positive witness. Exact counts fixed some rays' scale; homogeneous relations retained arbitrary positive scale.

The first independent review covered deterministic families and random-ray expectations. The random-family expectations initially remained a narrower coverage item: their aggregates had been rebuilt from saved per-world results, while independent family arithmetic covered deterministic selections. An additional review then classified the random subsets of every gate-passing ambiguous optimum and preserved compatible wrong random-choice witnesses. It extended coverage without changing fitted candidates, selected IDs or scores. The original review's scope remains recorded.

The constructed geometry checks and both numerical reviews passed. No retry or numerical repair was needed. The failed routes in this branch are selection outcomes, including repeated dependent account checks, a wrong scale fitted to one count and the generic rank policy's extra check relative to count-first on this population.

## A prospective physical test

A physical collection study can give each possible account or count observation a measured acquisition cost and an uncertainty model. Its selection pool must be available independently of the proposed decoder, with coefficient transcription recorded before measuring numerical targets. A fresh source-defined collection would then test whether rank-guided selection, count-first or a candidate-set-based policy yields more discriminating observations per unit of actual effort. Missing membership and noisy counts require compatible interval constraints rather than the exact equations used here.

The present endpoint supplies a fixed-population comparison and arithmetic witnesses for that design. It retains the original 200 base seeds, policy definitions, source hashes and every deterministic validated set. A genuinely new evaluation needs new observations or new generated worlds specified before their outcomes are seen.
