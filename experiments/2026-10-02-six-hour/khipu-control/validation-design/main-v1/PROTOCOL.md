# Allocating a small validation budget by equation rank

Specified before this comparison's scoring, 2 October 2026. This is a post-outcome refinement of frozen KHI-04 observations. The analyst has seen its aggregate outcomes and two exact failure examples, including the wrong ray accepted by eight rank-one fresh account relations. The data and decoder candidates are reused. This study compares validation choices on that fixed population.

## Available observations and budget

Each world offers eight held-out account coefficient rows and three independently counted objects' feature rows. An account check supplies an equation with right side zero under the same-quantity, complete-membership and additive-function premises. A count supplies a measured right side. These targets remain unavailable to selection. Coefficient rows and observation types are available for all eleven possible checks. Their construction from the sealed source key is recorded separately; the selector receives a quantity-free projection.

Budgets are zero, one, two and three checks. Each selected account relation or measured count costs one unit. This abstraction holds check counts equal; physical acquisition effort and count precision are absent from these observations. The counted view already has KHI-04's observed unit-object count, which fixes the first positional value. Its selector may use the known anchor's coefficient row `[1,0,0]`, but receives no anchor value. The unmeasured view starts without that row. The available candidate decoder sets and measured scale remain outside selection.

## Fixed policies

Give checks stable IDs `account-0` through `account-7`, followed by `count-0` through `count-2`.

1. Account-first selects the first budget-sized prefix in that order.
2. Count-first selects the three counts first, then accounts in their original order.
3. Greedy rank selects the row with the largest increase in exact coefficient rank, including the available anchor row. Ties use the declared account-first order. It continues to spend the budget even when every remaining gain is zero.
4. Uniform random selects an unordered budget-sized subset from the eleven rows. Enumerate every subset to obtain exact expectations, rather than a sampled random outcome.

The selector receives only `(check IDs, coefficient rows, observation types, anchor coefficient rows, budget)`. It receives no numerical targets, truth, inferred values, model names or corruption condition. Each deterministic policy is checked against that interface. Uniform random uses all subsets without outcome selection.
