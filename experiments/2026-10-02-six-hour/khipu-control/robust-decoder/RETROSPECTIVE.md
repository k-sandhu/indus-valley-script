# Research record

KHI-01 showed that a few incomplete or ownership relations could produce a wrong unique decoder before later equations rejected it. This follow-up asked whether fitting a largest consistent subset could retain the numerical code while excluding incorrect memberships. The current registry already named a fresh observation-error design as an eligible retry. We used new generated seeds and preserved the earlier equality endpoints.

The primary method search led to the original Fischler-Bolles RANSAC paper through SRI, their institution. ACM retrieval returned a 403 response. SRI supplied the fifteen-page article, whose printed publication date is June 1981; the landing page displayed March 1980. We used the article's date and retained both witnesses and the discrepancy in the acquisition record. An author preprint on deterministic maximum-consensus fitting placed exact enumeration within established robust-estimation methods. Downloads remain local, with source hashes and observed rights.

The counted unit reduced the unknown model to two coordinates. This allowed an exhaustive line arrangement and an independent all-subsets proof check. A finite list of intersections alone would have lost rank-deficient answers, so the candidate class includes open positive lines and a plane. Zero and impossible constant rows also receive explicit handling. Every maximum-score tie survives in the output.

One constructed development check initially expected four best intersections. The implementation returned five, including `(1,14/5,28)` at the crossing of `h=10t` and `h=28`. The four-point expectation had missed this crossing. [Failure record](development-failure-01.json) preserves the result; the fitting algorithm stayed unchanged and the corrected assertion passed before any random world was generated.

Generation then wrote projected features and separate evaluator keys for 1,500 worlds. Inference completed and saved its hashes before evaluation opened those keys. Exact consensus recovered most two-swap and two-missing worlds, while preserving wrong vectors and ambiguities. The four-swap condition exposed the gate's recovery/rejection tradeoff: the predeclared half gate accepted 200 correct vectors and sixteen wrong ones. Its ownership false acceptance was also retained.

The seven-vote wrong decoder exposed a specific failure of unweighted consensus. Five correct accounts supplied the same carry ratio, while two corrupted equations supported another point on that line. The objective discarded the one correct account that supplied a different ratio. Held-out equations and counts rejected the resulting decoder. We retained this case as an exact observation-design example and proposed a separately observed collection total as a possible next constraint.

Independent rational subset enumeration checked every arrangement state and every best-set classification. It also reconstructed corruption operations, checked all scoring residuals and retained continuous families. No random-world run or endpoint was rerun to improve a score. The paper 003 factual review was saved separately before this follow-up, and the manuscript itself was not edited.
