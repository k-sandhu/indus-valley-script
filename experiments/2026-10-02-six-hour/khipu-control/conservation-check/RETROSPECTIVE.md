# Work record: collective conservation under local membership errors

The preceding consensus experiment showed that repeated carry relations can make a wrong decoder satisfy more accounts than the true decoder. This follow-up asked what an independently observed pooled inventory adds. The design retained the frozen consensus baseline and supplied the collection constraint separately from local assignments. Complete-pool swaps preserve quantity and give a direct control for the observation's reliability.

The source search used Hamming's primary 1950 article on error detection and correction. Its treatment of checking relations helped frame the calculation. The author PDF was acquired from a university mirror, its printed publication date and useful page coordinates were checked, and the copyright observation was recorded. The full PDF remains local. The experiment uses an additive physical operation, with the same-quantity, same-event and completeness premises written together before generation.

The protocol specified 200 fresh seeds, seven conditions, two fixed support gates, separate measured and unmeasured scale views, all tied families and fresh withheld checks. A supplement fixed observation availability before generation: the whole inventory is available at prefix one, while local memberships arrive successively. This prevents the prefix curves from being mistaken for an acquisition-budget comparison.

## Geometry challenge before generation

The inequality solver needed to retain continuous families and their closed boundaries. A development assertion used a local line `t +h = 1` and the hard constraint `t >= 1`. The positive portion of that local line is excluded, but the hard halfspace still contains positive points with zero local support. The original assertion had expected an empty domain. The fitter had returned the feasible zero-support domain correctly. The assertion was repaired to expect ambiguity with zero support, followed by rejection at either positive support threshold. The [failure record](development-failure-01.json) and [completed diagnostics](development-check.json) preserve this route.

## Results that constrain further work

A trusted complete pool improves four-swap recovery at the 50% gate from 133 to 190 of 200 worlds, with wrong accepted answers falling from nine to one. At the 75% gate, it removes two wrong accepted answers but rejects 199 worlds. The comparison exposes the role of the gate alongside the aggregate's information. Two missing local associations can be repaired by a complete independent pool; two missing physical objects give only an inequality and a much smaller improvement.

The completeness control is consequential. An equality imposed on an actual partial pool removes the true decoder from every constrained optimum. The 50% gate accepts fifteen wrong points. The ownership control rejects all worlds under either gate, retaining its feasible wrong optimum sets and its 37 empty positive domains in the raw outputs.

The complete aggregate also has an exact failure. In one world it repeats the common carry relation, so it retains a family containing both the true decoder and a wrong decoder preferred by local consensus. Independent counts reject the wrong answer. In a second world, all eight fresh held-out account equations repeat one carry relation and accept a wrong baseline decoder. Their coefficient matrix has rank one. Three independent count-feature rows have rank three and reject it. The [selected-case record](selected-case-arithmetic.json) preserves both examples and their exact arithmetic.

These failures point to a concrete next measurement question: among observations physically available to an investigator, which one supplies a row independent of the surviving relation family, or a count whose prediction differs across that family? The present result specifies an information requirement. Acquiring those observations from archaeological objects still requires separate source coverage and completeness witnesses.
