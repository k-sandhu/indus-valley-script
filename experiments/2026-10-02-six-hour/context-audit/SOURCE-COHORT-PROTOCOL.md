# Published object groups through the analysis filters

This extension was specified after the first availability audit and inspection of the full HARP introduction. It preserves the original `main/` endpoint. Its question is whether the objects with a useful physical-function description are represented in the existing complete, right-to-left, single-line sample of texts at least three signs long.

Use three contiguous groups named by Kenoyer and Meadow in their 2010 introduction, printed p. l / PDF p. 9 of the 17-page public witness:

| Group | Source identifiers | Size | Published description |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Incised tablet copies | H2218–H2239 | 22 | Steatite tablets from secondary deposits at Mound E |
| Sealings and stopper | H1719–H1725 | 7 | Six sealing fragments and one unbaked jar-stopper |
| Stamped vessels | H1727–H1766 | 40 | Vessel sherds with impressions applied before firing |

Join the published CISI bases to the export's literal CISI reference after formatting normalization. Report absent references and bases mapped to several families. Keep every matched face. Count direction, completeness, unknown glyphs, line count, sign length and the stages of eligibility; publish aggregate counts and identifier-level join status without bulk sign sequences. Separate an object with at least one eligible face from a complete account of all its surfaces.

Also check whether H1537 and H1539 occur. The introduction records a revised dating assessment for H1537 and re-examination of H1539's marks. This is a targeted check of whether those particular cautions affect the present observation export. An absent object is simply recorded as absent from this table.

Write the code hash and a new snapshot into `source-cohorts/`. Assert cohort sizes and accounting totals. The cohort comparison is descriptive and selected after the availability results. It is intended to choose a future source-coding task, with the existing prediction endpoint preserved.
