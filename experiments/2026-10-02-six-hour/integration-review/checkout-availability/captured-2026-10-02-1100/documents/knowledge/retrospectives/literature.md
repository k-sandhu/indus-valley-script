# Literature and source work

Recorded 2 October 2026. This retrospective covers the first four-hour investigation and the subsequent direction memos. It records the searches, source checks, corrections and remaining dependencies so that another researcher can continue from the evidence already collected.

The literature work changed the investigation in three practical ways. It located precedents for the structural patterns being measured, recovered original object and catalogue witnesses for several promising examples, and separated numerical equations from the physical premises needed to use them. The resulting source tables are reusable even where an interpretation remains unresolved.

## Structural patterns and publication priority

The initial pattern search concentrated on opener modules, terminal substitutions and fish modifiers. Yadav's 2019 Table 1, printed page 127, supplies prior descriptions relevant to the diamond/leaf and wheel openers followed by the two-short-stroke sign. Mahadevan's 1982 discussion, pages 313–314, compares burden-bearing and jar-like terminal signs. His 1986 grammar study, page 20, discusses modified-fish replacements. [Yadav 2019](https://insa.nic.in/writereaddata/UpLoadedFiles/IJHS/Vol54_2_2019__Art01.pdf), [Mahadevan 1982](https://rmrl.in/en/dl/research-papers/mahadevan/ebook?id=Terminal+ideograms+in+the+Indus+script_1982a), [Mahadevan 1986](https://rmrl.in/en/dl/research-papers/mahadevan/ebook?id=Towards+a+grammar+of+the+Indus+texts_1986b).

Those sources helped define what the new assays actually measure: whole-context correspondence, transfer to a withheld frame, and sensitivity to catalogue aliases or coarse allograph collapse. The investigation's exact grouped evaluations belong alongside the older descriptive patterns. The [candidate-pair audit](../../research/literature/candidate_pair_novelty_audit.md) records the distinction for each selected pair.

The W176/W904 case illustrates the value of checking examples as well as methods. Ad-3 and H-2139, which appear in a withheld correspondence frame, were already juxtaposed in the June 2025 RMRL bulletin, PDF page 23, Figure 6b. The bulletin proposes taxed commodities or licensed activities as an interpretation. Its comparison is a relevant prior observation; our assay adds a particular held-out test. [RMRL Bulletin 6](https://rmrl.in/bulletin/bulletin-No-6-June-2025.pdf).

The review also found representation choices that affect interpretation. The two short strokes coded W002 and two long strokes coded W032 occupy different graphic positions and are treated differently in parts of the literature. Pooling them would alter the hypothesis under test. Fish/asterism proposals depend on particular mark groups and rebus arguments, so they need an image-linked description of height, position and modifiers. The [matrix](../../research/literature/evidence_and_novelty_matrix.md) retains the exact source pages and sign crosswalks used in this review.

Predictive restoration also has a clear precedent in Rao and colleagues' 2009 Markov study. Their data preparation includes exclusions and duplicate handling, making a broad claim that earlier work ignored duplicates inaccurate. [Primary PNAS article](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~rao/PNASIndus.pdf). Our [restoration review](../../research/literature/restoration_review.md) instead evaluates the current artifact grouping, hidden holdout risks and distinction between an artificial known slot and an unknown damaged gap.

Recent work was checked where it could overlap the project. Khanna and Merriam's December 2025 article, *A Computational Analysis of the Indus Script: Identifying Sign Functions in Logo-Syllabic Writing Systems*, uses shared contexts and substitutions. Nair's April 2026 preprint uses administrative generators and attested comparison systems. Public 2026 projects also discuss opener communities, weighted graphs and copper constraints. These sources are indexed by version and review status in the matrix. [Khanna and Merriam](https://www.ijcaonline.org/archives/volume187/number64/khanna-2025-ijca-926075.pdf), [Nair, version 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17828v1).

## Physical sides, copies and catalogue units

The copper-tablet review began with an attractive idea: repeated inscriptions across image variants might impose equality or inequality constraints on a proposed interpretation. Parpola's 2005 and 2008 discussions already use these families. The useful task became recording each actual relation, rather than deriving a name from a repeated motif. [Parpola 2005](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/indusscript%282%29.pdf), [Parpola 2008](https://tuhat.helsinki.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/127257447/Parpola_A_2008._Copper_tablets_from_Mohenjo_daro._ECL_During_Caspers_Vol.pdf).

The [copper constraint table](../../research/literature/copper_constraints.csv) and [variant adjudication](../../research/literature/copper_variant_adjudication.md) preserve the competing graphic resolutions. In particular, a coarse W777 or Parpola 124 family can merge features that matter to the C5/C6 comparison. Feet, appended components and orientation should remain visible in the evidence table until original images permit a stable distinction.

The CISI Volume 1 source audit resolved a simpler counting hazard completely. The government-hosted PDF contains 862 pages, with every page image in the first 431 repeated at offset 431. Its introduction also distinguishes object sides, impressions, additional photographs and separate inscriptions. The resulting [counting and errata note](../../research/literature/cisi1_counting_and_errata.md) gives exact page ranges, conventions and image hashes. [Government source](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/76884.pdf).

H355 was checked against photographs, the basic-data catalogue and errata. B and C are separately labelled sides, each with the U-like sign and three long strokes. The H356 correction instead concerns extra photographs of H354. This supplies one physical object with two matching coarse modules. Most other qualifying objects in the digital analysis have one numeric face, which limits the proposed multi-face equality test.

H2238 required a different outcome. Meadow and Kenoyer's primary drawing identifies specimen 18 as H95-2613/6560-01 and shows three short strokes beside the diamond-like sign. A separate gallery image supports count variation across manufacturing groups. The exact opposing-face count on H2238 itself remained unresolved at the available resolution. The [H2238 audit](../../research/literature/h2238_primary_audit.md) keeps the object-specific and cohort observations separate.

## The external-anchor search

Gadd's Ur-seal publication and museum records were checked for an object carrying both a useful Indus sequence and readable cuneiform. The familiar Shu-ilishu evidence concerns a cuneiform identification of an interpreter with Indus-related context. The British Museum describes accession 123208 as a Gulf-type seal with possible pseudo-Indus inscription. Gadd's item numbering also exposed a citation mismatch between different cylinders. These source findings are recorded in the [anchor audit](../../research/literature/external_phonetic_anchor_audit.md). [Gadd 1932 scan](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/33779.pdf), [British Museum 123208](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1932-1008-178).

A September 2026 online story alleging a Ketu/Ku-du reading supplied no stable inventory number or inspectable primary article. It remains a search lead in the log. Further work should begin with an accession or excavation identifier, obtain both surfaces and their full readings, then test which elements have a demonstrable correspondence. This avoids repeating a broad search over the same named examples.

## Quantities: equations, measurements and object joins

The metrology work first inspected the relation proposed for three selected vessels. H370, H371 and H372 were compared with the source dimensions and estimated capacities, and the digital records were checked for long-stroke counts and U-like signs. The three-case relation is worth testing externally, but the current export's zero dimensions are placeholders, and the available comparison cases do not automatically belong to the same graphic and commodity class. [Dimension table](../../research/literature/metrology/source_dimension_table.csv), [capacity table](../../research/literature/metrology/source_capacity_table.csv).

The unit ambiguity can be written exactly: `40.4 × n = 4.04 × (10 × n)`. These models make the same volume prediction until independent evidence fixes a unit or stroke value. The structured [identifiability audit](../../research/literature/metrology/source_and_identifiability_audit.json) preserves the scope of this observation and the requirements for a new vessel test.

The second branch examined Fuls's numerical equalities, `v = 2S − L` and `vL + 1 = 7S + 5`. The methods agent independently enumerated four positive-integer solutions. The proposed archaeological range selects `(L,S,v) = (10,8,6)`; the range and quantity readings are therefore essential premises. The source footnote's assertion that integer S requires even S has a counterexample in the second equality alone. The complete conjunction's solutions happen to have even S. [Exact conditional audit](../../research/methods/NUMERICAL_CONSTRAINT_AUDIT.md).

## Independent printed-label work

The chart task used the original Koskenniemi and Parpola 1979 PDF, not inferred mappings. This agent manually read 175 cells in PDF page 20 columns 5–9, then all 337 occupied cells on PDF page 21. Each entry records the upper rank, lower Finnish code, coordinates, confidence and alternatives. Original crops and pre-adjudication tables are preserved in [the chart directory](../../research/literature/chart_complete/README.md).

Comparison of the two complete page-21 readings found 22 joint disagreements, with agreement in 315 of 337 cells before corrections. Enlarged source images resolved some cases and retained alternatives for others, especially digits 5 and 6. Duplicate-code checks provided locators for reinspection. They were not used as a rule that every printed code must be unique; two clear numeric 506 labels remain.

The frozen fresh-20 image evaluation used the earlier numeric reference with 14 ambiguous cells. Later corrections and the 16-ambiguity atlas are recorded as subsequent sensitivities. The decoder found 107 predicted units for 96 drawn units, matched row counts on 14 of 20 rows and returned two compatible whole rows at top one. Its first-candidate edit total was 69; top five reduced that total to 60 and included five compatible whole rows. These outcomes direct the next attempt toward acquisition and segmentation, with a new withheld image block. They also demonstrate why numeric-label agreement and glyph-recognition accuracy require separate assessments.

## Controls and recent research directions

Source-preserved totem-pole and weather datasets were prepared for the same grouped predictive assays. The weather grouping produced one connected component, blocking the intended independent split. The totem set retained a high held-out vocabulary novelty rate and incomplete physical-family information. The [control source note](../../research/literature/nonlinguistic_controls/README.md) records those limitations, rather than changing the groups to obtain a convenient score.

The methods agent's constructed office-routing system has a known nonphonetic purpose and nevertheless retains predictive gain beyond selected finite template families. This provides an assay-specific demonstration that a finite template failure need not identify language. Sproat's critiques and Nair's 2026 administrative scorecard are relevant conceptual precedents. [Sproat 2010](https://aclanthology.org/J10-3012.pdf), [Nair 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17828v1).

The subsequent review broadened the candidate experiments to object use, manufacturing variation and human symbol evolution. Jamison and colleagues' seal-style study, Frenez's Lothal-sealing study and 2026 symbol-transmission experiments supply specific properties to measure. Community leads were preserved by author, date, object target and a testable prediction. These are developed in the [archaeology memo](../directions/archaeology-and-iconography.md), [cross-disciplinary memo](../directions/cross-disciplinary-and-alien.md), [community record](../directions/community-proposals.md) and [roadmap](../directions/research-roadmap.md).

## Retrieval problems and remedies

| Problem encountered | Remedy preserved in this project |
|---|---|
| A plausible citation led to a different work: ACL J10-4009, a Kenoyer-labelled landing page, and an early guessed government volume title. | Check the downloaded title page and author/publication metadata; retain original transport filenames with corrected identity records. |
| Bibliographic dates and indexed upload dates disagreed. | Record original publication, reprint and retrieval dates separately. A 2007 Tamil-government volume remains a 2007 source despite a recent index entry. |
| HTTP 403, timeout, dynamic reader or generic HTML appeared where a PDF was expected. | Record access level and response; use an observed public author or institution route when available. Distinguish indexed text from inspected figure pixels. |
| Text extraction failed or scans had no useful text layer. | Try an alternative extractor or use OCR to locate metadata, followed by original-page inspection for the actual observation. |
| A PDF contained repeated volumes or extra views. | Hash page images and use catalogue conventions before counting objects. Keep image, side, inscription and physical-family units distinct. |
| A modern integer identifier reused an older number. | Join full inscription patterns, multiple faces, excavation identifiers, dimensions and original pictures; keep provisional joins explicit. |
| Source comparison suggested a correction after an evaluation had finished. | Save the initial table and score, then write a separate adjudication or sensitivity. |

These repairs are recorded in the retrieval log and source identity index. They should be reused as a checklist for the particular failure, rather than as reasons to restart every search.
