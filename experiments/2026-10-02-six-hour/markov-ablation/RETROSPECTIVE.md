# Boundary and training-order ablations

The earlier conventional comparison supplied a 34.59% top-one reference. The next question was which model features contributed to that result. We wrote this mechanism protocol after seeing that score and retained that timing in the protocol and result design field. Five variants separate the learned initial distribution, final end factor and end-event transition fitting; two training-order controls test sequence information with and without fixed endpoints.

The important distinction was between omitting the last inference factor and refitting a transition matrix without end events. End-event counts affect each source row's denominator. Their removal can therefore change candidate-to-successor likelihoods at internal positions. The two interventions were implemented separately and their conditional distributions checked against explicit full-string products.

The old comparator's source and grouping-helper snapshots were imported by their saved hashes. The helper snapshot populated the exact module name imported by the comparator, preventing a later mutable helper from silently supplying grouping functions. The new script locates the repository by its source/knowledge directories so its own preserved snapshot can also be imported from the run directory. No old source or endpoint was edited.

Every new fit reused the original five folds and their selected smoothing 20. The unchanged full model reproduced every field in the frozen scored queries exactly before any intervention. Nine constructed checks passed, covering all variant likelihoods, target independence, unknown neighbors, the locality of direct boundary-factor removal, the separate glyph-only row normalization and both shuffle invariants.

Training shuffles required an explicit identity rule. They transform only outer-training strings, preserve their glyph multisets and metadata, and keep component assignments from the original observed corpus. We recorded accidental shuffled matches to held-out strings without adapting the transform or split to them. The all-slot family had fifteen such row coincidences across twenty seeds and five folds; the endpoint-preserving family had zero. This prevents a hidden test-dependent rejection rule while keeping the original observational split identifiable.

The main run took 85.96 seconds, including all five variants, forty training-order replicates with five outer fits each, and paired component intervals. It preserved every fixed-variant top-ten prediction and every control replicate's mask-level correctness, model top mass and target loss. The control records omit top-ten suggestion lists to keep repeated endpoints smaller; their seeds and scored outcomes remain reproducible.

Independent verification constructed start, unigram, pair and outgoing counters outside the prediction classes. It recalculated all 40,045 fixed-variant rankings and probabilities, checked all 320,360 control query identities and summaries, and sampled two initial, two internal, two final and two unseen-target masks per control fold. This supplied 1,600 independent control calculations. It also verified training-count/end-count invariants and pooled calibration bins. All checks passed. No run failure or numerical repair was required.

The final boundary contributed 5.70 points at final masks. Interior-only shuffling retained exact endpoint counts but reduced overall accuracy by about ten points. Learned initial frequencies increased top-one accuracy while worsening initial log loss, which supplies a concrete reason to test start shrinkage separately. We retained all outcomes rather than selecting a preferred ablation after its result. Conditional component intervals and seed ranges are reported separately in the README.

The previous reference, current mechanism protocol, new source snapshot and exact mask-level outputs remain separate. A future smoothing or higher-order experiment should begin from these measured differences and specify its selection objective before fitting.
