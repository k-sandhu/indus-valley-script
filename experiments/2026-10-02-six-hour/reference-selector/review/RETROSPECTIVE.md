# Review methods and outcomes

The review began from the frozen reference-selector protocol, source snapshots and generated worlds. Its main numerical question was the Markov length normalizer, because the slot model already conditions on length. A scalar state-vector recursion rebuilt that normalizer, and exact rational enumeration supplied a small independent normalization check at lengths one through four.

Counter-based complete-record and cell counts reproduced the original scores. Stable scalar summation gave small floating-point differences, with no difference approaching the prespecified tolerances. The closest model-choice margin was 2.887 bits, so those numerical differences did not affect a selected model. All reference folds, answer-key comparisons, selected-map identities and held-out likelihoods reproduced.

The mapping optimizer was left unchanged. The review formulated a separate dual linear program for the slot objective, using row and column potentials to bound the returned assignment. Every condition passed. The graph outputs were scored directly from records, retaining the preceding search's scope.

After the primary review, a separate protocol specified system-level paired bootstraps and assignment-alternative checks. The bootstrap kept the eight conditions of each generated system together. Its administrative twenty-symbol comparison showed the selector's gain over fixed graph mapping, while the eight-symbol comparison preserved the selector's one-map loss relative to the supplied-family diagnostic.

The additional audit used candidate tight-edge cycles to produce alternative bijections. Integer-smoothed cell probabilities allowed exact numerator-ratio checks through prime-factor cancellation. Eleven conditions supplied exact ties, with 32 retained witnesses and no rejected near-tie candidate. Five tied administrative conditions enter the selector's slot output, so reporting point recovery alongside those alternatives is useful.

The primary review completed without a failed check or source mutation. The additional output preserves its post-outcome status and separate protocol. All work is confined to the owned review folder and helper, with no graph-search rerun, shared-report edit or Git operation.
