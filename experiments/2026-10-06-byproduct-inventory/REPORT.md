# Ranked publication assessment

## How the ranking was made

The ranking weighs four things: whether the bounded precedent search leaves a plausible contribution, whether the source witnesses and saved outputs are ready to inspect, whether the result serves a clear field audience, and how much outside validation remains. Precedent status is stated candidate by candidate in [inventory.json](inventory.json). A checked search means targeted live queries and at least one relevant source page were opened; it does not mean exhaustive coverage. Where the search did not reach close field literature, the record says so.

The strongest five candidates are source-based outputs. Their numbers are in saved reports and machine-readable files; no earlier analysis was rerun for this inventory. The public-source coverage census ranks sixth because it documents the limits of a current inventory rather than resolving object-level joins.

## Top five

The most focused correction is the exact audit of Andreas Fuls's published 10/8/6 proposal. Given its two stated equalities, four positive-integer triples remain: `(4,20,36)`, `(6,8,10)`, `(10,8,6)`, and `(36,20,4)`. The reported range selects `(10,8,6)`, while the footnote's parity argument does not establish an even short-stroke multiplier. This is a precise conditional arithmetic result, not a confirmation that the archaeological quantities or object joins are correct. A concise correction should retain those premises in the same paragraph as the solution set and invite source review. See [the audit](../../research/methods/NUMERICAL_CONSTRAINT_AUDIT.md) and [Fuls arithmetic record](inventory.json).

The Susa weight work has a useful combination of a defined population and recoverable source conflicts. The 2022 catalogue census contains 69 explicitly coded entries, with 57 usable mass/mark ratios. In the original 1911 catalogue AS 13820 is listed at 2,769 g, compared with 3,067 g in 2022; the source audit finds three named mass discrepancies and two original duck descriptions that conflict with later stroke counts. This is suitable for a source data note after museum images, dates, and accession joins receive specialist review. The census itself shows that broad mass-per-mark groups vary widely, so it does not support a single Susa unit. Evidence: [regional census](../2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/regional-west/REPORT.md), [original-source audit](../2026-10-02-source-recovery/susa/REPORT.md).

The catalogue cross-check candidate groups three independent, finite discrepancies for potential data-quality reporting. The Lothal table contains 133 impressions, while its prose says 130; the table counts 62 single-impression objects against 63 in prose. For Tamil Nadu compound 4.19C, the printed record has six components and the deployed web array has five. In Gonur, the report states a 26-token pile and both illustration panels show 23 representations. These differences are not one shared error: each needs its own source owner and explanation. A short correction request or small note should show the source locations side by side and keep unresolved scope questions open. The general precedent search found published archaeological catalogue correction and data-paper models [SRC-01, SRC-02, SRC-03]; it did not find these exact mismatches previously resolved.

The context completeness audit identifies which research questions the available corpus can actually support. It covers 5,680 transcription rows and 4,695 numeric families. A combined context subset with area, room/grid, excavation label, depth, and a chronology label contains 1,444 rows from 1,163 families. In the three published Harappa groups, 60 of 69 objects join the export, but only two enter the primary complete, right-to-left, single-line benchmark at length three or longer. Correcting placeholder rules also exposed 2,870 dash-only depth values and many zero dimensions that had been counted as present. This is a reusable data-quality note if the field meanings and rights basis are reviewed. [Context audit](../2026-10-02-six-hour/context-audit/README.md) provides the exact cohort and field definitions.

The chart atlas is the most ready digital resource, though its accuracy has not been certified by an epigrapher. It indexes 687 cells from the 1979 sign chart, retains ambiguous numeric readings, and links cells to source regions. Independent numeric-label references cover 175 cells on PDF20 and 337 on PDF21. Their comparison logged 22 initial disagreements; later crop review retained 13 lower-code ambiguities. A separate fresh 20-cell check agreed jointly on 16 readings, on rank on 19, and on code on 17. The checks concern printed digits, not glyph identity. A resource note could help readers find and compare uncertain entries, provided an epigrapher adjudicates remaining fields and the image rights permit redistribution. The search found related sign charts and digitization work, but it did not establish a priority claim for this presentation. The atlas and source/crop records are at [output/chart_atlas](../../output/chart_atlas/index.html) and [research/chart_reference](../../research/chart_reference/README.md).

## Venue and rights fit

Internet Archaeology describes data papers as peer-reviewed accounts of datasets deposited in trusted repositories, with reuse potential and open licensing [SRC-02]. The Journal of Open Archaeology Data is also a candidate for a reusable archaeological dataset [SRC-05], but the full journal page was not accessible in this search. Digital Scholarship in the Humanities, NLP4DH, the Journal of Statistical Software, EXARC Journal, Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports, and Iran are candidate venues with scopes aligned to digital scholarship, NLP, research software, experimental archaeology, archaeological science, and Iranian archaeology respectively [SRC-15, SRC-16, SRC-17, SRC-19, SRC-20, SRC-21, SRC-22, SRC-23]. Venue fit is a recommendation, not an editorial decision.

Most third-party PDFs, figures, photographs, and corpus-derived records remain local. The repository's redistribution review says that public use requires asset-level rights checks; a public data-paper venue cannot resolve those rights on the project's behalf. Until then, publishable work can cite source locators and report aggregate observations while keeping unresolved-rights payloads out of a deposit.
