# A regional test of crop retention and changing farming

This is a proposed follow-up to the completed [pilot](REPORT.md). No regional inferential result has been measured. The main question is how locally established crop groups survived the urban transition and later settlement changes, and whether retained groups were grown in different proportions or under different management.

## Observations to collect first

Build one row per flotation sample, with related samples linked to their deposit, trench, excavation and site. Record the original sample identifier, layer and phase, sediment volume, light- and heavy-fraction sieve sizes, recovery method, deposit type, disturbance, burning or storage event, taxon and identification confidence, counted part, fragmentation rule and count. Keep each source table or laboratory number beside the observation. Dates must identify the sample or context actually dated, its material, laboratory result, calibration version and uncertainty. A broad culture name is not a substitute for those links.

Use four initial food-crop groups: winter cereals, summer cereals, winter pulses and summer pulses. Preserve rice and identified and indeterminate millets separately within summer cereals. Preserve fruit, plants grown for fibre or oil, weeds and unidentified remains as separate categories. Classification decisions and ambiguous taxa must be recorded before phase comparisons. Weed ecology and processing remains supply additional observations about management; they need their own uncertainty and source fields.

Our first source queue is:

1. Jognakhera SI S1–S3 and Figure 2's layer-to-phase mapping. Resolve count denominators, taxon-by-layer observations, dated samples and PGW coverage.
2. Bates's 2016 thesis and original sample tables underlying the five comparison settlements. The accepted 2017 supplement supplies phase aggregates and some weed densities but not the full crop-count recovery frame used here.
3. Original excavation and dating reports for Bahola and Masudpur VII. Check the phase boundaries and possible single burning events, then reconcile their samples with the tables.
4. Independently excavated sites in the upper Ghaggar basin and at least one comparable Gujarat sequence, selected from published inventories before their results are coded. Sources that reproduce an existing excavation retain its dependence identifier.

The [comparison register](comparison-data-register.json) distinguishes inspected sources from leads. No correspondence, purchase, field access or outside publication has been undertaken.

## Competing explanations

Crop-repertoire retention predicts continued occurrence of locally established crop groups across a dated transition. Compositional change predicts shifts in their relative abundance or sample occurrence despite that retention. Ecological sorting predicts that contemporary sites with comparable water and soil settings resemble each other more than a broad region label suggests. These explanations can coexist; the analysis should estimate their separate contributions.

A management-continuity claim additionally predicts persistence of a defined bundle of weed, processing and cultivation observations. For example, repeated evidence for the same crop and weed associations across dated local phases would be more informative about management than two pooled lists of edible taxa. Define that bundle from discovery sources before applying it to new deposits. Climate causation requires independently measured exposure and alternatives; a low millet percentage alone cannot estimate the impact of drought.

## Measurements and comparison

Report the share of samples in which each crop group occurs, crop-remain density where volume is known, and composition under clearly named denominators. Present absolute counts alongside proportions so a fall in one crop can be distinguished from an increase in another. Count repeated specimens from one storage or burning event together when assessing uncertainty. Compare local phases first, then site-level changes across catchments; retain occupations separated by abandonment as a distinct comparison.

For each proposed paired phase, require source-supported chronology, a reconciled sample list and stated rules for recovery and deposit type. Classify intervals overlapping a proposed transition separately and repeat the comparison under the plausible assignments. Do not assign a probability distribution merely by spreading a calibrated interval uniformly. Treat unknown dates, volumes and sieve sizes as missing, with explicit consequences for inclusion.

The primary retention measure will be phase-to-phase occurrence of the four crop groups within sites. Composition measures will report percentage-point changes in winter cereals, summer cereals and millets, separately for all crop remains and cereals alone where the denominator permits. Uncertainty must resample or model independent deposits within sites and preserve site dependence. If only pooled rounded phase percentages remain available, retain descriptive results and stop before regional significance or equivalence claims.

Choose a regional model, practical equivalence margins and a power or precision target only after the sampling frame and recovery variation are known. Record those choices in a new dated protocol before scoring the reserved observations. A failure to reject change is not a measured upper bound on change.

## Discovery, confirmation and stopping

The focal paper and the nine extracted comparison phases are exposed discovery data. Record every additional source read before design finalization. Reserve independently excavated, unexamined assemblages for confirmation; a new division of familiar tables does not restore freshness. Freeze the inclusion rules, crop groups, dependence grouping, chronology handling, statistics and decision rules before that set is opened. Use the project's two-stage principles for any later registered conjecture; a sign-reading claim would also require its own applicable bench task.

The data-acquisition stage ends with an explicit eligibility decision for every candidate site-phase pair. If too few independent comparable pairs exist to meet the declared precision target, report that limit and identify the missing measurements. A long-lived crop repertoire coupled with altered crop proportions remains a useful historical result even if an unchanged management system is rejected.
