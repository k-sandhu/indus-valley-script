# Circular seals and the apparent change abroad

The source audit changes the composition of the comparison. One South Asian object counted as a circular seal, M-500, is printed among incised tablets in CISI 1. Restricting the earlier comparison to circular objects and removing that tablet leaves a foreign-minus-South-Asian difference of 0.0985 in the old sequence ranks, with a 95% family-bootstrap interval of −0.1077 to 0.3490. The observed difference remains poorly determined. We cannot yet calculate the effect of corrected transcriptions: none of the foreign objects has a complete, independently verified mapping from its published image to the export's Wells sign codes.

## What we inspected

The fixed frame contains all 33 `SEAL:C` faces in the pinned export, representing 32 provisional families defined by export prefixes. The known Ur/Dilmun physical duplicate still has two prefixes in this audit frame. It contains ten South Asian faces, eighteen foreign faces including that duplicate, and five unprovenanced faces. Seven faces have indexed images, comprising 28 crops. The separate [blind source review](../2026-10-20-foreign-script-blind-review/REPORT.md) inspected all these crops and their complete catalogue pages. The remaining 26 faces have no indexed picture. [frame-projection.json](frame-projection.json) lists every face and image count; [audit-summary.json](audit-summary.json) gives the denominators. [PACKAGING.md](PACKAGING.md) records the unchanged original frame's relocation to local-only storage.

We also inspected Laursen's complete Figure 11, printed p.116, and its related impression drawings and contextual discussion. The plate has 29 numbered entries: 28 Indus or Indus-related entries and one explicitly labelled Linear-Elamite entry, no.28. Those 28 include no.11, described as pseudo-script, and no.56, whose two V-shaped marks occupy an iconographic composition. They therefore do not constitute 28 independently verified continuous Indus texts. Figure 11 is explicitly in impression polarity; its grey areas preserve missing evidence. [plate-observations.json](plate-observations.json) records all 29 entries. This is the complete audit of this saved frame and plate, rather than a census of all finds abroad.

The author's legacy-score exposure occurred during orientation, before image annotation. The independent reviewer received opaque IDs without scores or an identity key and froze observations first. Our source annotations were then frozen before the composition calculation. The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md), [frame freeze](frame-freeze.json), [annotation freeze](annotation-freeze.json), [amendment](AMENDMENT-1.md) and [source manifest](source-manifest.json) preserve the order and limits of this work.

## Source joins and recording problems

We securely joined eight export faces to seven Laursen objects. Five are South Asian: C-32 is no.1; M-416 is no.2; M-1369 is no.3; M-417 is no.4; M-415 is no.5. These joins use excavation or original publication numbers and Laursen's explicit M-1369 reference on p.109. The CISI number M-500 is unrelated to the Mackay catalogue number 500 cited for Laursen no.5. Treating the equal suffixes as an identity would attach the wrong object.

Gadd's original account, offprint pp.13–14 (PDF pp.24–25), joins U.8685 to Gadd no.15, Laursen no.16, and U.17649/BM123208 to Gadd no.16, Laursen no.17. The latter joins both export witnesses 3898.1 and 6000.1. Gadd describes four characters for this object; the two export witnesses record six and five positions. Damage, compound-sign segmentation and an explicit sign-list concordance must be adjudicated before changing either string. Gadd's context also distinguishes recovery in tomb-shaft filling from the tomb's attributed dynasty. The object cannot acquire an exact production date merely from that attribution.

The independent photographs confirm seal/impression mirror relationships but do not independently decide reading direction for any of the seven pictured faces. M-417's surviving field shows a joined-animal composition without a securely separable inscription; Laursen's drawing proposes a small text in that field. We retain it in the main form correction and exclude it only in an additional sensitivity. M-500 is a firmer form correction: its full printed page explicitly groups it among incised tablets, and the reverse carries a tree. Dmd-1 supports a large visible mark with poor internal detail. No new Wells-code reading is asserted for these objects. All unresolved joins and alternatives remain in [annotations.json](annotations.json).

## How composition changes the old result

The statistic is the mean of the **unchanged October 12 per-object ranks**. A rank is the proportion of matched South Asian texts with a lower sequence surprisal, with half-weight for ties; a high rank means unusual order under that model. We removed rows according to frozen source observations, without retraining models, recoding signs or changing their comparison cells. West Asian ranks come from the rounded published CSV, so the final decimals inherit its precision. The calculation is a spent, exploratory eligibility sensitivity.

| Inclusion | West faces / families | South Asian faces / families | West minus South Asian mean rank | 95% family-bootstrap interval |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Original circular and cylinder group | 16 / 16 | 16 / 14 | 0.1654 | −0.0213 to 0.3623 |
| Circular objects only | 15 / 15 | 10 / 9 | 0.0788 | −0.1094 to 0.3151 |
| Circular objects, excluding M-500 tablet | 15 / 15 | 9 / 8 | 0.0985 | −0.1077 to 0.3490 |
| Also excluding uncertain M-417 text | 15 / 15 | 8 / 7 | 0.0536 | −0.1399 to 0.3256 |

Removing cylinders accounts for a larger shift than removing the misclassified tablet. The tablet correction increases the circular-only contrast by 0.0197, while the combined form restriction and tablet correction reduce the pooled contrast by 0.0670. Excluding the uncertain M-417 text reduces it by a further 0.0449. Every interval spans zero. These intervals describe the uncertainty of the fixed old rank average under family resampling, conditional on the old models and cells. They do not account for unresolved code or direction alternatives.

For the tablet-excluded circular comparison, removing any single family on either side gives differences from 0.0051 to 0.1321. Related faces remain together. Full alias-and-identical-text component deletion is not established by this source audit; our partial object joins do not certify every component in the original model. [sensitivity.json](sensitivity.json) includes all common observed lengths and the cells with at least two faces per side. Those small cells cannot support a regional explanation after splitting further by date or site. [legacy-ranks-and-eligibility.csv](legacy-ranks-and-eligibility.csv) and [verify_summary.py](verify_summary.py) make all point differences independently recomputable without the raw corpus.

## What remains identifiable

The source evidence distinguishes an object-form composition effect from an unresolved transcription effect. It establishes an eligibility error and a substantial difference between pooling cylinders and comparing circular objects alone. It supplies seven object joins and a concrete segmentation disagreement for one duplicate object. It does not yet quantify how corrected sign strings alter sequence surprisal. There are zero code-verified foreign objects, no newly established calendar dates for South Asian circular comparators, and no shared foreign/South-Asian site labels for exact site matching. The date- and site-matched regional test and the code-corrected comparison are therefore infeasible within this frame.

The [shared-loader synthetic result](shots-synthetic.json) passes its guards. The [real access record](spent-access.json) explicitly records `fresh_claim_allowed: false`; the private ledger restores the known October 12 exposure as well as inheriting shared recorded exposure. We changed no shared ledger. [Author verification](author-verification.json) separately recounts point means and checks preserved hashes; it is distinct from the separate plate review. The bootstrap resamples export families and retains their face multiplicity, estimating the face-weighted mean rather than assigning every family equal weight. The 32 rows in the rank table are the scored pooled comparison, a different population from the 33-face circular audit frame.

Our strongest working reading is that Gulf seals adapt an Indus-derived graphic practice while changing object forms, compositions and the treatment of text. The present sequence contrast cannot locate a regional linguistic change within that broader adaptation. The deciding next observation is a joined photograph-and-impression catalogue with an adjudicated sign-list concordance and dated South Asian circular comparators. It would let us retain all plausible readings, refit the frozen sequence model once, and ask whether a geographic contrast survives within the same object form and period.
