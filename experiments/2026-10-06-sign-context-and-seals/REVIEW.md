# Review of package D2: sign frequency in context, motif concentration and seal standardization

Reviewer: Claude (Sonnet 5.5), 7 October 2026. The package was at git HEAD 053dab5 with no uncommitted D2 changes. Everything this review produced sits in `review/` (scripts and aggregate outputs) and in `data/raw/2026-10-06-sign-context-and-seals/review/` (local-only logs).

## Verdict

The main results stand. Every number I traced from `README.md` and `REPORT.md` matches the saved outputs, the README commands regenerate every analytical file byte for byte (apart from timestamps, runtimes and one local path), and the three verification scripts pass with 106, 43 and 760 checks. My own implementation of the statistics, written from the protocol text and run with different seeds, reproduces the observed values exactly and the permutation p-values within Monte Carlo error. The sign-032 site association (q = 0.019), the unicorn text-length difference (q = 0.0006) and the length–area slope (1.13 on 997 seals, p = 0.0001) also hold after collapsing repeated inscriptions to one object per site and exact text. The most important problems concern reporting. The opening statement in `REPORT.md` that no sign has a supported site-area association "across the original and amended reference laws" conflicts with the frozen endpoint, where sign 520 reaches q = 0.044. The Harappa period result (q = 0.007) comes from contrasts among 3C subphase labels that share one date interval, so it says nothing about change between dated phases. The site-area table leaves out extents printed in the same Treccani source (Harappa about 100 ha, Rakhigarhi over 100 ha, Mohenjo-daro at least 2 × 2 km), which the protocol's minimum–maximum rule called for; repeating the area test with those values leaves the conclusion unchanged (minimum amended q 0.13 to 0.19). The precedent section omits the published carver-assignment and metric work on Indus seals (Jamison 2018, Green 2018), and the unicorn and bison code mapping has no documented basis.

## Method and scope findings

**Grouping and resampling.** Every step keeps whole components together. Site labels move among principal faces inside exact type blocks (omnibus, amended area test) or type and readable-length blocks (richness). Motif labels move among seal representatives inside site-and-type blocks, region and period labels inside type blocks, and lengths inside site-motif-type blocks. Bootstraps resample components within site, or whole objects within type. Grouping rests on the export's `object_family_id`, because the CISI alias rule merges nothing here. Impressions of one seal, or tablets made in one mould, would form separate families whenever the catalogue gives them separate numbers, and the protocol deliberately leaves repeated texts unlinked (3,504 distinct sequences underlie 5,680 faces). The collapsed-text checks in the claims table show that no headline association depends on that choice.

**Dimensions.** The analysis uses `horizontal(mm)` and `vertical(mm)`; zero, blank and non-numeric values are missing. For the 1,027 complete squares the millimetre values come from raw inches times 25.4 (1,515 values), centimetre strings times 10 (26), raw millimetres (54), raw centimetres without suffix (16) or have no raw string (438); two of about 2,050 pairs are inconsistent. Face rows with a positive raw value and a zero millimetre value number 157 (horizontal) and 164 (vertical), mostly tablets and only 7 and 11 on square seals; recovering them gives n 1,028 and CV 0.2932.

**Terminal expectation.** The 299.08 is the sum over texts of (count of 740) / (text length): the expected number of texts ending in 740 if each text's signs stood in random order. It conditions on every text's exact composition and length, and so on object type implicitly. I reproduced it and its spread (analytic SD 14.65; 2,000 shuffles give mean 299.27, SD 14.56). The null is conservative for the claim, since 740 stands at non-final positions in 382 of 8,538 slots (4.5%), which would predict about 112 endings. A p-value of 2.4e-275 assumes independence across objects; the unique-text version (665 of 916 occurrences terminal, 72.6%) shows that duplicates do not inflate the rate. Signs 400 (90.5% terminal), 520 (84.9%) and 390 (50.5%) share the pattern, while 002, 032, 033, 220 and 240 end texts in under 6% of their occurrences. Single-sign texts do not enter: 499 of the 509 single-sign objects have direction `NR` or blank, and the other ten fail the two-sign minimum; 34 of the 509 are 740 alone. Texts labelled L/R follow the same normalized order (39 of 127 end in 740, one starts with it), so using the last element for both directions agrees with the corpus convention. Harappa's lower fraction (253/467) tracks type composition: square seals give 80/98 at Harappa and 314/386 at Mohenjo-daro.

**Motif codes.** `startswith('Bull1')` pools 14 codes (Bull1 and its suffix variants) and nothing else; the five faces coded `Bull:i`, `Bull:i:J` and `Bull:i:S` stay in the rare cohort (three of its 155 objects). Recoding them as unicorn moves the length q from 0.0036 to 0.0024. The package cites no codebook for the mapping. Evidence I found: the unicorn share among all seals (75.54%) equals the 75.5% I computed from Oakes's independent, Mahadevan-coded Table 9; Kenoyer (pp. 15–16) writes that the offering stand occurs almost only with unicorn motifs, and the `cult` field of Bull1 faces holds object codes (mostly SAN, RAN and SAF), consistent with a unicorn facing a stand. For `Gaur`, 96 of 124 faces have cult code `Trough`, the lowered-head animal at a manger that Frenez and colleagues 2016 (pp. 116–117) call the Indian bison, *Bos gaurus*. The Salūt ST1 seal appears in the export as `Gaur` with five signs and a trough, matching that paper's description, and the 85 `Gaur` square seals are close to the 82 seals Oakes lists for the short-horned bull (code 04, head lowered over a trough). One corpus record disagrees: the second Salūt object is a `TAG` coded `Elep` with two signs, whereas the paper describes a sherd with two bison and three or four signs.

**External classification.** "External" means the export's region labels, which cover Persian Gulf, Mesopotamia, Iranian Plateau and Central Asia. The Iranian Plateau group includes Miri Qalat, Nindowari-damb, Ornach and Sibri in Pakistan, and Central Asia includes Shortughai, an Indus settlement. Dropping the four Pakistani sites from the external class gives unicorn and Gaur q of 0.0084 each; the Gulf and Mesopotamia restriction gives 0.062 and 0.017. The 13 known external circular seals are all `Gaur`, and so are 3 of the 7 known core circular seals. This agrees with the harappa.com excerpt of Frenez's encyclopedia article (almost 90% of round seals show an Indian bison) and places the external Gaur majority in the round seals.

**Period result.** All four 3C subphase labels (3C-1 to 3C-4) carry the same nominal interval, 2200–1900 BCE. A heterogeneity test on those four labels alone gives p = 0.0006 (117 objects), so the q = 0.007 comes from the contrast between 3C-1 (25 of 34 unicorns) and the other subphases (82 of 83). The unambiguous phases differ little (3B 6 of 7, 3C 112 of 123), and the wide change envelope reflects the 70 objects labelled 3B/C, whose phase is unknown. The 700-year range fits the chapter's own persistence claim of more than six centuries; the lecture's almost-a-millennium claim lies outside it.

**Richness test.** Harappa and Mohenjo-daro supply 79% of the objects in the shared reference, so the test has little power to detect a departure by either of those two sites. Rarefying them to the smaller sites' token counts gives a descriptive check: Dholavira's observed 162 against 159 and 172, Kalibangan's 160 against 142 and 153, Lothal's 149 against 162 and 175.

**Precedents.** The package makes no priority claim. The relation statements are generic, however, and the search missed the closest production work. Jamison 2018 ("Understanding Indus Seal-Carving Traditions: A Stylistic and Metric Approach", pp. 167–186), Green 2018 ("Operational Sequences and Stamp Seals", pp. 187–203; both in *Seals and Sealing in the Ancient World*, Cambridge University Press) and Jamison's 2018 *Walking with the Unicorn* chapter (pp. 272–291) address carver groups and seal metrics, which the report lists as the data that would resolve the workshop question. Ameri 2013 is the source of the 65% figure. Konasukawa and Koiso's chapter bears directly on the CV result; the package did not read it, and I could not obtain it. A search-engine summary (unverified) credits it with a modal size near 2.5 cm and a high degree of standardization. Specific relations to state: Yadav 2013's site preferences partly reflect duplicated texts; Oakes's Harappa unicorn deficit covers all object types while the package's Harappa square-seal share is the highest of six sites; Oakes's seal-only unicorn share equals the package's.

**Scope and rights.** I found no decipherment claim, sound or meaning assignment, novelty claim or resemblance presented as fact. Third-party pages, the 42-object list, arrays and family identifiers are in the ignored raw directory, and `sources.json` records no quotations. The smallest cells in `site-frequencies.json` describe one to three objects as ten sign counts, which is acceptable aggregation.

**Writing.** The scanner reports nothing, and the prose is plain. Remaining problems: three corrective sentences (`REPORT.md` lines 49, 176 and 195, for example "tests richness, not identical sign frequencies"); the "no meaning is assigned" caveat repeated in at least six places instead of once in the methods block; two README paragraphs addressed to the reviewer (lines 38 and 60); the report opens with the amendment before the primary results; and "14 eligible site/type comparisons" means six sites plus eight object types, not site-by-type cells.

**Boundaries.** At the end of the review `git status --porcelain` shows two D2 paths, `REVIEW.md` and `review/`, both new, and no modified tracked D2 file. The remaining entries belong to other packages (backward-chronology, chronology-region, evidence-census) and to the program retrospective `knowledge/retrospectives/2026-10-06-research-program.md`; none is attributable to D2 or to this review, and the set shifted during the review as other packages were committed. No registry, index, paper or README file has changed. The two D2 commits (2530a9a, 053dab5) touched only D2 files.
