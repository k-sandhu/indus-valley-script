## Question and input

We ask whether sign frequencies vary across sites after accounting for object types, whether a dominant unicorn motif accompanies measurable text differences, and how much seal dimensions vary. The input is the pinned observational export `data/normalized/lipi_observed_faces.jsonl`. Code `000` is unreadable. Report both all-position and readable-position denominators. Do not merge sign codes or translate their values.

The registry's ROOT-09 and `src/context_study.py` already address broad contextual prediction. Reuse its principal-face selection approach, its physical-family logic and its Bull1 motif definition. Inspect the saved `results/context/confirmatory/bull1_other.json`, rather than rerunning its classifier. Reuse the orientation conclusion in `research/corpus/orientation_clarification.json`. Paper 002 supplies the reason to preserve physical records in resampling. Exact repeated texts on distinct objects are an outcome here and will not create additional physical links.

## Physical grouping and face selection

Union every row sharing `object_family_id`, and every unambiguous simple CISI reference sharing its base identifier after a side suffix is removed. Do not infer aliases from ambiguous references or identical texts. Preserve unresolved references in a local-only grouping audit. Primary inference uses one representative face per physical component: greatest total token length, then lexicographically smallest `record_id`. This rule depends on length, not the sign, motif or site outcome. Site frequency descriptions additionally include all observed faces, with whole-component bootstrap intervals. All downstream resampling operates on components or their single representative and keeps their payload intact. Object-type labels are exact export categories.

Families with metadata conflicts are counted and reported. Seal analyses select one seal representative per physical component using the same ordering among seal faces. Dimensions use that representative, rather than choosing the face whose measurement looks most complete. No exact-text deduplication is applied.

## Sign comparisons

Select the ten most frequent readable codes globally, sorted by decreasing count and then code. This selection is independent of site association. For every site, report token counts, family counts, frequency and a 95% percentile bootstrap interval using 2,000 draws of whole components with replacement within site. Sites with one component have no inferential interval. Compute object-type frequency tables and descriptive odds ratios relative to `SEAL:S`.

For each sign, fit the object-type baseline by pooling readable tokens within each exact type. On principal faces, the site statistic is the sum of squared observed-minus-expected site counts divided by summed binomial variances. Include sites with at least 20 components. Generate 9,999 references by permuting whole component site labels within exact object-type blocks. This preserves object-type/site component counts; token totals per site may vary because complete face payloads move. Sparse and single-site blocks contribute no artificial exchanges. Use the inclusive Monte Carlo tail `(exceedances + 1)/(B + 1)`.

Fit a binomial count model `logit(p) = object_type + beta * log2(site_area)` on the sourced sites, using principal-face sign counts and readable denominators. Report coefficients and site-cluster sandwich standard errors as approximate descriptions, with convergence and design rank. Separately fit object type plus site-type indicators; attempt the joint area/type model only when its design is full rank. For the size hypothesis, use equal-site Spearman correlation between log area and the site's observed-minus-type-expected count divided by its readable denominator. Permute the area vector among sites 9,999 times. This tests an exchangeability assumption about the selected sites, not a randomized archaeological treatment. Repeat the predeclared area-range and two-city exclusion sensitivities. Apply Benjamini–Hochberg correction across the ten signs separately for the site-omnibus and primary size families. No significance claim comes from treating thousands of tokens as independent sites.

## Motif predictions and text comparisons

On seal components, Bull1 and all Bull1 suffixes form the unicorn category. Other motif codes are retained, with `None`, `-`, `?`, blank and `Unknown` excluded from known-motif concentration denominators and reported separately. Calculate shares, rank–abundance, HHI `sum(share^2)` and effective number `1/HHI` for all seals and separately square seals (`SEAL:S`), by site, region and Harappa `metadata.time`. Harappa period comparisons are restricted to Harappa; ambiguous Period 3B/C and unspecific Period 3 retain their own labels. Also give a predeclared coarse 3C grouping of 3C and its subphases. Known-motif groups of at least 30 objects support majority comparisons; smaller groups are descriptive. Give Wilson intervals for unicorn proportions at component level.

The testable distributional prediction is a unicorn majority with persistent minority categories across adequately sampled sites and periods. Economic regulation, competing institutions, workshop dominance and uneven excavation can all produce such concentration; motif shares alone cannot distinguish them.

A rare motif is a known non-unicorn category with less than 5% of known seal components globally after Bull1 pooling. Primary texts are complete observed, single-line seal representatives, with specified R/L or L/R direction, at least one sign and no `000`. Compare unicorn with rare motifs, and give all non-unicorn seals as a declared sensitivity. Outcomes are (1) sign-token distribution Jensen–Shannon divergence, (2) initial-sign divergence, (3) terminal-sign divergence, (4) inventory richness difference, (5) distinct-text/objects rate difference and (6) mean text-length difference. Recompute richness and uniqueness in each reference; unequal sample sizes are retained. The direction/order convention is the repository's checked `signs_reading_rtl` serialization.

Permute the motif-class label among whole seal representatives within site and exact object type, 9,999 times. For boundary outcomes the entire text moves together and its internal order is retained. For length, richness and uniqueness use two-sided tails of the absolute difference; for divergences use upper tails. Correct across the six primary outcomes. Repeat within each site having at least five objects in both cohorts, and correct across all eligible site/outcome comparisons. Different site p-values alone do not establish different effect sizes. Report rare-category sample sizes, immovable blocks and the number of objects in blocks containing both labels.

## Dimensions

Use positive `horizontal(mm)`, `vertical(mm)` and `thickness(mm)` values only; zero, nonnumeric and negative values are missing. Keep raw h/v/th strings for a local audit, but do not silently convert inconsistent fields. Record min/max and quantiles without removing positive outliers. Face area is horizontal times vertical and characteristic face size is its square root. Primary standardization descriptions use square seals with `preservation == complete`, both face dimensions and one representative per physical component. Other preservation states form a declared sensitivity. Report valid/missing counts for each dimension and joint face measurements.

For motif, site and Harappa time groups, report n, mean, SD, CV (sample SD/mean), quantiles and a 2 mm characteristic-size histogram. CV at or below 0.10 is an explicit operational narrow-size criterion for this package, not a universal archaeological standard. Broad variation or small n is a negative result for a single narrow-size population. A low CV does not establish a common workshop or an authority. Distinct size classes could remain despite a high pooled CV.

Test unicorn-versus-rare CV difference by permuting labels within site and exact object type. Test size/text-length association with Spearman correlation and an OLS slope of token-position length against log2(face area), adjusting for site and pooled motif. Permute complete length payloads within site, motif and exact object type; singleton blocks remain fixed. Correct across these two dimension tests. No year is assigned to an undated seal. A shape/size distribution is not proof of manufacturing tolerance without independent measurement and preservation checks.
