# Early Indian institutional knowledge and cultural memory

The [October 11 retrospective](../../experiments/2026-10-11-early-indian-continuity/RETROSPECTIVE.md) records two studies beyond decipherment: selective transmission of practical and institutional knowledge, and preservation or reconstruction of the past in early Indian texts. It includes source-access repairs, a preserved eligibility-filter defect, independent arithmetic and chronology review, and an explicitly post-audit hypothesis about shared beginnings in origin accounts.

Read the [integrated report](../../experiments/2026-10-11-early-indian-continuity/REPORT.md), [manuscript](../../experiments/2026-10-11-early-indian-continuity/MANUSCRIPT.md), [review](../../experiments/2026-10-11-early-indian-continuity/review/REVIEW.md) and [response](../../experiments/2026-10-11-early-indian-continuity/REVIEW-RESPONSE.md) before repeating this path. The working thesis is selective inheritance combined with intellectual recombination; the selected sources do not identify a diagnostic Harappan-to-textual transmission chain.

The source ledgers keep genres, textual strata, dates and dependent witnesses distinct. Exact synthetic constructions clarify identification and retention under declared laws. They have no calibrated historical probabilities. The [prospective material and role tests](../../experiments/2026-10-11-early-indian-continuity/institutions/TESTS.md) and [memory tests](../../experiments/2026-10-11-early-indian-continuity/memory/TESTS.md) state observations that could strengthen or reject particular routes.
