# Seal-text identity, 12 October 2026

Claude Opus 5.5 ran this study unattended after the user asked it to continue pursuing new ideas. It owned `experiments/2026-10-12-seal-text-identity/`, `data/raw/2026-10-12-seal-text-identity/` and this file.

## Approach

The structure-first ledger lists personal names, titles or offices, administrative codes and identity labels as rival descriptions of Indus seal texts. Whether duplicate seal texts are local to one city separates the first from the others, and no earlier package had measured it. The protocol fixed the population, a site-permutation null, a coincidence calculation under a bigram model, motif concordance and a sealing list before any of them was computed; only eligibility counts had been examined. Photographs in the local Lipi copy were used to check the matched sealing and seal texts. An independent verification and an adversarial review followed, and the review's checks were recomputed and saved.

## Problems and repairs

- Whole-text locality cannot separate titles from common personal names, so an exploratory segment analysis compared opening and closing sign pairs; their difference was not established.
- A length-stratified null, added as a sensitivity, lowered the chance level from 0.448 to 0.382 and showed a modest excess that the frozen null hid.
- The review found that the pooled statistic hides a difference between Mohenjo-daro, where duplicates sit at chance, and smaller sites, where some are local; that the sealing paragraph miscounted matches (13 of 16, not all, match only seals elsewhere), merged ten catalogue objects into one and described four signs as three; that motif concordance comes only from unicorn pairs; and that the mixture fraction was a point estimate. Each point was recomputed and the report rewritten. Two of the reviewer's numbers did not reproduce exactly (the restricted null, 0.286 rather than 0.248, and the mixture bound, 0.25 rather than 0.30); the recomputed values are used.
- The protocol hash file was written immediately before the first run, but later appends changed its timestamp, and the package is uncommitted, so file times are the only record of order.

## Outcomes

Under the frozen test, duplicate seal texts are not concentrated in one city (ratio 1.06). Duplicates at Mohenjo-daro sit at chance, while seven same-site pairs at smaller sites against 1.5 expected suggest local holders there. Long seal texts almost never repeat, identical texts share the animal only on unicorn seals, and most sealings carrying a seal text match seals from other cities, including ten Lothal sealings with the text of a Mohenjo-daro seal fragment.
