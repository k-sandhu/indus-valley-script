# Claim

Farming households and local specialist networks carried practical knowledge across the decline of Indus urban institutions. Operations that could be maintained through local materials, recurring work and teaching survived more readily than operations dependent on distant procurement or large coordinating networks.

# Why we think this

The [crop pilot](../../experiments/2026-10-25-crop-continuity/REPORT.md) records crop persistence with changing proportions. The [earlier transmission study](../../experiments/2026-10-11-early-indian-continuity/institutions/REPORT.md) distinguishes retained skills from changed support chains and reports losses or changes in specialized bead manufacture. Those observations motivate the mechanism; they do not identify the households that transmitted a specific operation. Crop presence alone cannot determine land ownership, political centralization or the organization of teaching.

# Kill condition

Abandon the proposed advantage of locally supported transmission if a recovery-matched, adequately informative sample bounds that advantage at zero or below, or if the supposed retained operations prove to be imports, residual objects or misdated associations. Poorly recovered or undated contexts leave the test unresolved.
