# Claim

Indus building groups changed their houses under common rules that preserved shared routes and services. The repeated unit of urban coordination was the connection between adjacent groups through streets, drains and entrances. Districts could grow, subdivide or change orientation while those rules continued. Local negotiation was one means of maintaining them.

# Why we think this

The [comparative study](../../experiments/2026-10-24-urban-form-and-inscription-ecology/REPORT.md) connects Banawali's deliberate replanning, Kanmer's infill with retained alignment, Farmana's local encroachments and the wall and hydraulic sequences at Dholavira and Lothal. These sequences support different scales of intervention. Their institutional cause remains a conjecture. The [methods](../../experiments/2026-10-24-urban-form-and-inscription-ecology/METHODS.md) distinguish a negotiated arrangement from uniform supervision and independent convergence.

# Kill condition

Reject the negotiation extension if settlement-wide supervision and engineering constraints fully account for its discriminating predictions. A lack of comparable phases postpones the test.
