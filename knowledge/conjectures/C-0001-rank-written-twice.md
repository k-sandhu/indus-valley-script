# Claim

A seal legend that closes with sign 820 marks its holder as belonging to an upper grade, and seals for that grade were cut larger. We measure size as residual log face area: the logarithm of the face area minus the value expected for a seal of the same type, site class (Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Dholavira, Lothal, Chanhu-daro, Kalibangan or other) and length. Across both halves of the corpus the ten seals ending in 820 average about +0.64 on this scale, nearly twice the face area of comparable seals, and six of the ten are among the 25 largest residuals of their half. On the photographs, 820 stands at full height and normal spacing at the closing end of the legend, as any closing sign does.

This is the surviving form of the claim frozen for Shot A, the single frozen test of this card. The frozen version named four closing signs (001, 595, 679, 820). Its central prediction passed on the confirmation half, but the adversarial review found the pass carried by three seals ending in 820, and 679 did not repeat its discovery-half effect. We formed the narrowed claim after seeing the outcome, and it has had no test of its own.

# Why it might be true

On the discovery half of the [conjecture-shot split](../../experiments/2026-10-12-conjecture-shots/DESIGN.md), the last sign of a seal legend carries information about size beyond seal type, site and text length. We measure this with the between-sign ratio: the spread of residual size between groups of seals with different last signs, divided by its average when the signs are shuffled among comparable seals, so that a ratio of one means no information. The ratio is 2.14 for last signs (p = 0.001) and 1.15 for first signs (p = 0.23; matched difference p = 0.005). The whole effect sits in four last signs: the 18 seals ending in 001, 595, 679 or 820 have mean residual log area +0.66 against −0.02 for the other 572, and 16 of the 18 are larger than expected. Without those four signs the last sign carries nothing (ratio 1.09, p = 0.35). In 300 rehearsals that chose high and low signs on one random half of the discovery seals and measured them on the other, the four signs were the only ones repeatedly selected as high; their seals on the other half averaged +0.36, while signs selected as low averaged −0.03, against 0.00 for the rest. The signal survives adding the catalogue codes for the unicorn variant and the cult object to the size model (the four signs' seals exceed the rest by +0.53, p = 0.0002), so the images do not account for it. These seals come from several excavation areas and catalogue periods at Mohenjo-daro and from Harappa, Chanhu-daro and Dholavira. The rival reading is layout: a large face leaves room at the end of the text band for an extra sign that need not say anything about grade, and both readings predict the same association. The four signs were also chosen from 28 after looking at the data.

# Kill condition

P4 does not pass (failed, or untestable with fewer than five seals ending in the set) and P1a fails on the confirmation half. That outcome leaves no size information in closing signs, through the named set or otherwise, and the card becomes dead at credence 0.02.
