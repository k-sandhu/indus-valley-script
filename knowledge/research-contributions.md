# Results available for further research

Research package, 2 October 2026. Four working manuscripts link the measured results to their methods, source observations and computational checks. The project has produced restoration measurements, recovery experiments on simulated data with known answers, exact sampling examples and corrected source frames, our tables of the objects in a publication. ## Graphic restoration on the selected Indus transcriptions

The restoration benchmark hides (masks) one known sign at a time and asks a model to name it. Aliases of one physical object and repeated copies of a text stay in the same cross-validation fold. The benchmark masks 8,009 known positions in 1,596 distinct texts and scores exact recovery of the sign code. The conventional first-order Markov model reaches 34.59% top-one accuracy. Adding second-order context, the two preceding signs, raises that to 37.27%, a paired gain of 2.6845 percentage points, with an interval of 1.9902–3.3968 from resampling groups of linked texts (a component bootstrap).

The second-order model gives less probability to the correct sign: its log loss worsens from 5.1194 to 5.2472 bits per slot. Choosing second-order smoothing by that loss returns first order in all five folds, as does a later family with extra shrinkage. The retained candidate tables make that trade-off reusable. Two interventions measure which information contributes: removing the end-of-text term lowers final-position accuracy by 5.70 points, and shuffling the interior of each training text while keeping its first and last signs lowers overall accuracy to 24.52%, averaged over twenty shuffles.

The earlier experiment on gaps of unknown length gives 46.30% correct counts of lost signs, after its simulated gaps were matched to the masking procedure, below the 51.07% of always guessing one sign. Physical gap width and surviving traces are the inputs a useful retry needs. [Paper 001](../papers/001-artifact-aware-prediction/manuscript.md), [claim files](../papers/001-artifact-aware-prediction/claim-evidence-map.md).

## Reference laws for copies, selection and recording quality

A test of structure compares the corpus with shuffled versions of it, and its reference law states which shuffled tables of records are possible and how likely each is. Enumerating every table in small examples gives exact laws for keeping only distinct texts, removing duplicates and holding unequal numbers of copies. Requiring distinct records leaves 2,880 of 8,000 possible binary tables, each equally likely. Removing duplicates from seven draws to keep six gives those tables unequal weights. With unequal copy counts, a continuation-count sampler, which picks each sign in proportion to its number of legal completions, keeps the sign counts fixed both for the underlying objects and for all copies.

The full nested prediction procedure shows practical calibration differences on simulated populations with no real structure, where a test at nominal 5% should reject 5% of them. Shuffling individual records rejects 15.9% of literal-copy populations, and shuffling matched objects rejects 5.4%. For two imperfectly recorded faces per object, the rates are 11% and 5%. The main calculations reconstruct every accepted proposal, grouping and rank, and replay selected complete fits.

When recording quality is shared across an object, the broad and quality-conditioned references differ less under that prediction statistic: 4% against 3%. A separately specified statistic of agreement between faces gives 94.2% against 1% on fresh populations. Exact conditional tails and two independently implemented integer distributions explain the mechanism. The package also keeps a table with the same sign counts on which the fit is undefined, and two explicit alternatives for such tables: extending the score, or conditioning the reference on tables where fitting is feasible. These examples supply tests for a future implementation's observation model. [Paper 002](../papers/002-conditional-reference-laws/manuscript.md), [claim files](../papers/002-conditional-reference-laws/methods.md).

## Which physical measurements resolve numerical ambiguity

In generated collections, whole–part relations, where a marked whole is made of marked parts, fix the values of the marks relative to one another, while independently counted objects fix the scale. A known-system control, the documented Puruchuco khipu accounts (knotted-cord records), reproduces sixteen of twenty published account columns, with total absolute error sixteen. Its source alignment and generated hidden-value controls have separate status and preserved inputs.

Choosing the values that satisfy the most equations (exact maximum consensus) can accept a wrong answer when many equations repeat the same algebraic direction. On 200 fresh worlds with four wrongly placed items (four-swap worlds), an independently recorded complete inventory raises correct recovery from 133 to 190 and reduces accepted wrong values from nine to one at the declared 50% gate. Wrongly calling a partial inventory complete produces fifteen accepted errors.

The validation study asks which observation to acquire next. With a counted unit already fixing scale, one withheld count eliminates all nine accepted wrong decoders, while rank-first selection, which first adds new independent whole–part relations, needs two checks. With scale free, count-first needs two and rank-first three. Exact intersections of the solution sets keep the unresolved and wrong alternatives. The choice of observation becomes a question of feasible quantities and measurement type. [Paper 003](../papers/003-physical-observations/manuscript.md), [claim files](../papers/003-physical-observations/claim-evidence-map.md).

## Reference models and symbol-mapping recovery

The mapping program tests whether a one-to-one symbol correspondence (a bijection) can be recovered by comparison with an independent known reference. It uses generated transition systems, where each symbol depends on the one before, and administrative systems, whose fixed forms have typed positions (slots). Graph and slot profiles keep different information. A fresh selector that uses only the reference recovers 309 of 384 maps, compared with 283 for graph mapping alone and 168 for slot mapping alone. Exact optima for small inventories, assignment duals and tied alternative maps distinguish search misses from objectives that favour wrong mappings.

Making administrative forms the same length and mixing their symbols reduces recovery. A fitted three-component reference mixture restores all 24 large-reference maps when the forms share zero or .35 of their symbol probability (shared mass), compared with 22 under the preceding selector. A smoothing control with coincident components recovers 21 and 22. The component count is supplied, not learned. At .70 shared mass, reference validation chooses ordinary slot prediction, and the associated failures are kept. Six failed mixture maps score below the true map, a specific search diagnostic for another optimiser. [Paper 004](../papers/004-reference-model-selection/manuscript.md), [claim files](../papers/004-reference-model-selection/methods.md).

All 36 stored profiles satisfy a published condition for identifying the model from its population distribution, under two declared exact probability conventions. Rank checks in rational arithmetic show that the full population distribution determines the form weights and slot distributions, up to relabelling of the forms, within the fixed three-component model. When the components collapse into one, different weights give identical observations. This calculation gives the finite-sample recovery program a precise parameter target. [Proof, witnesses and independent elimination](../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/latent-generator-identification/README.md).

## Source frames and experiments that need physical execution

The Harappa frame recovers the excavation unit and item number for all 22 tablets copied in its source. Nineteen readable lower-face pairs have recorded layout classes. An amendment on scale and pagination keeps the earlier statements beside the corrected source locations.

The Lothal table, transcribed from the image of a published table, contains 93 objects and 133 seal impressions, differing from the prose total of 130; independent transcriptions of its cells agree. The Kanmer frame joins registrations, dimensions and scanner captions; its continuation locates the original article, the final volume's pages and an institutional copy with CD-ROM. These records specify the missing specimen information.

A stamp-contact simulation predicts which parts of a design survive on curved clay of declared geometry and supplies a prospective laboratory design. Kanmer has a separate handling design with 42 replicas. Both protocols are ready for physical trials.
