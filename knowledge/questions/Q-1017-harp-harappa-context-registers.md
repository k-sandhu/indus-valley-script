# Question

For the Harappa objects excavated by the Harappa Archaeological Research Project (HARP) since 1986, what find unit, lot, trench, deposit type, period and associated finds does the excavators' record give, and can those records be obtained for all inscribed objects?

# Why it matters

Harappa holds 1,921 local objects, the largest site population. They carry HARP period labels (71.8% have a chronology label) but only 14.1% have all three dimensions and 38.6% a depth, and no object has a lot, deposit type or associated assemblage. Conjecture C-0005 reads the Harappa tablets as tokens: one face names what the token is for (its descriptor) and another states its tier. It predicts that tablets with the same descriptor and tier were found together, and testing that needs find contexts. Kenoyer and Meadow's 2010 introduction already distinguishes item and lot identifiers and flags secondary-dump deposits, so the structure exists in the excavators' records.

# What is needed

Read [DATA.md](../DATA.md). Inputs held: the two versions of Kenoyer and Meadow 2010 (15 and 17 pages) and the Harappa tablet frame, the project's table of a group of Harappa tablets with their published contexts. Not held: the CISI 3.1 catalogue of the *Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions* (Q-1008) and the HARP field registers. List which context fields the published sources give per object, then draft, for a maintainer decision, a data request to the HARP directors describing the fields, the object list and the intended use. Record the access outcome in the project's data manifest.

# How to decide

Done when the published sources' context fields are tabulated for every Harappa object they cover, and a request has been sent with a maintainer's approval and its answer recorded, or the maintainers have declined to send it. A partial register (published contexts only) counts as progress and is reported with its coverage.

# Prior attempts

The October context audit and the Harappa short-record study used published contexts for 22 tablets; the audit of better original views of Harappa and Lothal sources recovered a dated Harappa collection statement but no register.
