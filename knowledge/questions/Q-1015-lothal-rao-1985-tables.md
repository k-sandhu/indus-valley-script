# Question

What do Rao's *Lothal: A Harappan Port Town (1955–62)*, Volumes I (1979) and II (1985), record for each seal, sealing and inscribed object: registration, findspot, phase, material, dimensions and plate? How do these records match the 182 local Lothal objects and the 290 Lothal entries in CISI 1, the volume of the *Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions* for collections in India?

# Why it matters

Of the Indian sites, Lothal has the largest measured gap between the catalogue and the local corpus: 111 of 290 CISI 1 entries have no local identifier match, 52 of them in pottery or graffiti sections and 59 in seal, impression and tablet sections. Sealing counts disagree between sources (Rao 1985 p. 305 gives 91, on the page the census reviewer opened; Frenez and Tosi 2005 give 93; CISI 1 has 93 sealing entries; Mahadevan 1977 selects 75). Only 14.3% of Lothal objects have all three dimensions and 50.5% a spatial unit. The excavation report supplies registrations and phases for the sealings that the project's Lothal sealing studies and conjecture C-0004 use.

# What is needed

Read [DATA.md](../DATA.md). Both parts are scanned on the Internet Archive (Part 2 as `masi78lothalaharappanporttownraos.r.part2_531_D`; the Part 1 scan is labelled as missing its plates). An earlier census download saved a bot-check page instead of the book, so check the title page and hash of whatever is downloaded. Record each volume's identifier, pages and hash in the project's data manifest, and tabulate the seal and sealing catalogue with page locators. Then join the entries to CISI L-numbers and to the local objects, recording the basis of each match; the CISI 1 basic data (Q-1005), the catalogue's table pairing each CISI number with its excavation number, links the report to CISI. Keep the scans and the transcription local.

# How to decide

Done when both volumes are identified and held or their absence is recorded with the routes tried, the inscribed-object tables are transcribed with locators, and each of the 111 CISI entries without a local match has a disposition that uses the report: a recorded decision on what the entry is, as in Q-1007. The sealing count is reported with every source's figure and the reason for any difference.

# Prior attempts

The Gujarat batch of the project's evidence census and its synthesis identified Rao 1985 and the count conflicts. The project's Lothal physical-use study coded Frenez and Tosi's table, and the audit of better original views of Harappa and Lothal sources recovered CISI panels and museum registrations for selected sealings.
