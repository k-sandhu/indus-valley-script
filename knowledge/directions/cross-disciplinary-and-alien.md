# Human symbols and unfamiliar artifacts

Reviewed 2 October 2026. The experiments below are proposals. They use comparison systems whose function, production or physical organisation can help us frame new Indus tests.

## Function can shape a symbol system

A mark system used to distinguish owners faces different pressures from one used to count goods or reproduce speech. An owner badge must remain recognizable across copies. An account must preserve quantities and distinguish categories. A portable message must survive separation from its maker. These pressures give us measurable hypotheses about repetition, component reuse, errors and the relation between a mark and its supporting object.

Recent human-symbol research supplies experimental designs. Bentz and Dutkiewicz's 2026 study reports conventional patterning on Aurignacian objects, similarities to early proto-cuneiform in selected statistics, and differences in information density by object type. Its authors supply data and computational analyses. [PNAS 123(9), e2520385123, primary author text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401125892_Humans_40000_y_ago_developed_a_system_of_conventional_signs).

A September 2026 transmission-chain study compared decoration, group identity and denotational communication. It reports regularisation across conditions and different responses in some cognitive measures. [Pagnotta et al., DOI 10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2026.106902](https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/the-evolution-of-symbolic-artefacts-how-function-shapes-form-2/). A separate April 2026 study, first online in December 2025, found that delayed copying produced more regular and memorable marks, while social and functional context remained necessary to explain the archaeological patterns examined. [Simulating Symbolic Evolution in the Lab](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41406238/). Graphical simplification through repeated communication also has an experimental precedent. [Caldwell and Smith 2012](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0043807).

We can use those tasks to build controls with a known purpose and compare them with the actual constraints of making and using an Indus object.

| Later Indian punchmarked coins | Museum records distinguish obverse punches from reverse countermarks. | Andean khipu, much later | Urton compares numerical matches in records with shared provenance. [Author manuscript at Harvard](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstreams/7312037e-6b09-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/download) | Test duplicate-account and total/parts searches while preserving cord hierarchy and archive identity. |
| Rongorongo, much later | A 2024 study combines wood dating with 3D documentation of four tablets. [Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53063-7) | Test curved-surface transcription and distinguish supporting-material age from engraving age. |
| Constructed modern systems | Office routes, assembly labels, game states and annotated parts can have concealed, known rules. | Measure function recovery under short inscriptions, damage and unfamiliar graphic codes. |

For each comparison, keep both the source representation and the transformed analytical representation. A knot hierarchy or a two-dimensional countermark arrangement can lose information when flattened into a string. Compare documented order with alternative linearisations where inspection order is uncertain.

## The alien-artifact experiment

Treat an unfamiliar artifact as if its users and conventions were unavailable. Its material, geometry, operation, repeated parts and inscriptions become the available evidence. This can be tested with an answer key.

The Voyager Golden Record is a useful design example. NASA describes its cover's playback diagrams, hydrogen-transition time reference and instructions for reconstructing images. It provides a physical relationship between the diagram and the recorded signal. [NASA, Golden Record Cover](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/golden-record-cover/).

Construct twelve small artifacts with concealed functions: authentication, counting, routing, assembly and a phonetic message. Match inscription length and inventory size across selected functions. Replace familiar letter shapes and remove identifying filenames. Give separate teams progressively more evidence:

1. Images of the object and its marks.
2. Dimensions, material and wear.
3. Access to the object's operation.
4. A few paired objects and observed outputs.

At each stage, require a fixed model predicting the operation or output of a held-out artifact. Score successful predictions, wrong confident assignments and the number of adjustable conventions. Keep separate holdouts for each stage so later evidence leaves the earlier score intact. Include tool marks, rotated photographs and objects with identical marks but different capacities.

The experiment can show which physical observations resolve ambiguity and which analytical tools recover a known operation. A useful result would identify, for example, that an attachment pattern allows teams to distinguish an ownership badge from a counter, or that paired outputs identify a numerical relation. That result would guide acquisition of corresponding archaeological observations.

## Production and perception experiments

### Copying on constrained media

Randomly assign participants to produce short messages, institutional badges or inventory labels using the same stamp area. Cross the assigned task with production method, such as carving, drawing and impressing. Measure component reuse, modifier placement, sign count, copying error and successful use. Fit the analysis on some participant groups and test on new groups, with an unconstrained drawing condition as a control.

The experiment separates task effects from production effects. If the same terminal feature arises across functions only under a carving constraint, it becomes a candidate production explanation. If a feature instead follows counting or identification across methods, we gain a more specific functional prediction to test on source-reviewed objects.

### Blind judgements of disputed features

Prepare permitted crops of fish modifiers, short/tall bars and compounds with dates, sites and catalogue codes hidden. Ask annotators to record visible components and uncertainty. Vary lighting, scale and photographic source for the same object. Experimental carvings with known tool marks provide a damage control.

The resulting confusion table can identify which distinctions need new photography and which survive changes of observation. Feed the uncertainty into alternative transcriptions, then compare their predictive consequences without selecting a preferred encoding from the outcome.

### Component grammar and compression

Fit a reusable stroke/component grammar to training images and predict held-out glyph geometry. Compare it with an independent-shape model and a material-conditioned model. Hold out physical objects and carving families. Repeat with invented marks and unrelated symbols matched for stroke count.

A component model that predicts new drawings provides evidence for economical graphic construction. The next experiment would test whether those same components predict an independently observed function or context. This makes morphology useful even while their lexical roles remain unresolved.

## Assumptions and available evidence

| Issue | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Alien framing | A blind inference experiment using human-designed objects with a concealed answer key. |
| Unknown comparison meanings | Paleolithic signs, Proto-Elamite and Rongorongo have different degrees of interpretive uncertainty. Use them for the property documented by the source. |
| Historical connection | Contact or descent is a separate archaeological hypothesis. The distant comparisons above are chosen for task, medium or representation. |
| Chronology | Wood age can precede engraving; mixed find contexts require separate manufacture/use/deposition intervals. |
| Existing control results | The first investigation's office-route simulation already shows that the declared sequential statistic is compatible with a nonphonetic process. The totem archive was sparse; the weather archive formed one conservative alias component. [Control audit](../../research/literature/nonlinguistic_controls/README.md), [methods findings](../../research/methods/FINDINGS.md). |
| Access in this update | Institutional abstracts, primary author text and catalogue records were reviewed. The Central Asian volume and comparison editions were located; their full image inventories were not manually transcribed here. |

Start with the twelve-artifact design and blind feature task. They are small enough to debug observation and scoring rules before a larger study. The [cryptography memo](cryptography.md) and [roadmap](research-roadmap.md) record complementary anchor and acquisition work.
