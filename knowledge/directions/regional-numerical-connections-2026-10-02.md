# What the new data connections give us

A later source update on 2 October 2026, the [original-source follow-up](source-recovery-2026-10-02.md), recovered the Gonur figure and the South Indian numerical pages. It also found conflicting historical and modern Susa records of marks and masses; read that audit before interpreting the close group of duck-shaped weights below. The earlier computed results are preserved.

Behind the proposed regional numerical links we found actual collections to investigate. At Susa, the western study now links each weight's marks, measured mass and catalogue identity. The South Indian study supplies a finite collection of stroke groups and the graphics around them. The Harappa and Lothal studies identify objects whose improved images would decide narrower questions. The independent review places these results beside earlier research and assesses what the project can contribute to a paper.

## Which connections we made

Our clearest original work measures the consequences of connections that general methods already recognize. Before splitting texts into training and test sets, we linked every alias (another catalogue entry for the same physical object) and every repeated inscription; a discarded duplicate can otherwise hide a link between the two sets. Where a sign has several possible readings, we carried all of them through the likelihood calculation instead of choosing one convenient code. We derived the chance distribution used for statistical comparison from the way the catalogue recorded and selected its texts. These choices produced checked measurements and finite examples that another researcher can reproduce.

The numerical work linked local accounts, records covering parts of a collection, to a complete inventory, and asked which new measurement would distinguish the remaining values. In generated test data, a trusted inventory corrected some unreliable local groupings, while additional account relations sometimes left the same wrong scale or ratio intact. Field acquisition should therefore seek an independently counted or weighed object that tests the surviving ambiguity.

The mapping work, on artificial record systems, chose a record model by how well it predicted held-out records of a known reference system before assigning unknown symbols. It then tested whether hidden record types (latent forms), inferred without labels, could keep distinctions that are lost when all records are pooled. The [independent review](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/novelty/REPORT.md) found close precedents for these general ideas and placed our contribution in the exact comparisons, failure cases and source joins. It ranks the empirical paper on sign prediction first, the paper on how copying and selection determine the chance distribution next, and a checked data note on the physical sources as another useful deliverable.

## Susa: measured mass beside repeated marks

The strongest western object we acquired is Louvre SB 13677 / AS 2615, a hematite weight of 334.25 g with four marks. The Louvre describes them as points; Ascalone's catalogue describes circles. The identifiers match, so we can request the correct object's photographs and measurement history. Ascalone had already proposed that this object relates several regional weight systems. [Louvre record](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010186254), [Ascalone 2022, pp. 49 and 69–70](https://airus.unisalento.it/retrieve/c39309c8-8dae-4dba-b990-86171612edf6/E.%20Ascalone%2C%20Bronze%20Age%20Weights%20from%20Mesopotamia%2C%20Iran%20and%20Greater%20Indus%20Valley_compressed.pdf).

We reconstructed that proposal using two other identified Susa weights, of 13.43 g and 27.25 g. The proposed coefficients 25:1:2 imply unit masses of 13.37, 13.43 and 13.625 g, so a common base unit fits all three only if each mass may vary by at least 0.945% in either direction. This measures the fit of the published proposal; the marks and masses do not yet favour it over other unit interpretations. The creation dates of two of the three entries are missing. [Exact calculation and object joins](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/regional-west/REPORT.md).

We also checked the wider marked collection rather than only that favourable relation. The census found 69 Susa entries with explicitly coded marks. After excluding entries with unknown mass, mixed kinds of mark or accession numbers shared with another object, 57 mass and mark comparisons remained. Three limestone ducks with five, six and ten strokes imply approximately 492–511 g per stroke, a group worth investigating. Other groups of the same shape and material span much wider ratios. All repeated groups need better dating before a historical rule for units can be tested within one assemblage. The [census](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/regional-west/marked-weight-census-v2.json) lists every member and keeps the exclusions.

The same book contains 938 Greater Indus weight entries, including 870 Dholavira objects. Its descriptions gave no explicit ancient mark joined to a mass, and the selected compressed plates showed no readable Indus inscription on a weight. The promising connection runs through excavation context: join the DHR collection numbers, grids and levels to records of seals and sealings, then inspect complete photographs of the weights. This gives the search a finite population of sources and concrete keys.

## Oxus and Central Asia: recover the complete object group

The most specific Oxus numerical lead is a reported group of 26 possibly marked clay tokens from a room near the entrance of the Gonur North Palace. Bonora's account points to Sarianidi 1998, Figure 41. Recovering the original figure and inventory could reveal repeated forms, counts, exterior impressions or associated sealings. The known Gonur Indus seal has a different recorded location, Excavation 9, Room 19. We keep each find with its own context instead of combining them by site name. [Bonora et al. 2014, p. 66](https://www.persee.fr/doc/paleo_0153-9345_2014_num_40_1_5616), [recovery table](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/regional-west/evidence-table.json).

Altyn Depe provides another exact separation: its two-sign seal and its proposed numerical tablet came from different excavation rooms and phases. Tepe Yahya provides marked weights, including a one-mark object of 13.37 g assigned to 4500–4000 BCE. These earlier examples can help investigate how marks accompanied weights; their dates determine which historical comparisons are possible. The regional report names the original plate, room and accession needed for each follow-up.

## South India: count the graphic population and check its components

We acquired both complete 2026 volumes of *Inscribed Potsherds of Tamil Nadu: Graffiti and Tamiḻi* and saved a fixed snapshot of the project's web catalogue. Sixteen classes of plain strokes account for 480 reported occurrences. Among the compounds we queried, 194 forms have plain strokes as explicit components; 142 of these have one unbroken run of stroke components in the catalogue's component list and exactly one other component. They form a bounded population for examining a possible count-plus-category arrangement. [Printed catalogue](https://tamildigitallibrary.in/Articles/062969_Inscribed_Potsherds_of_Tamil_Nadu_Vol_1), [analysis](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/south-india/REPORT.md).

A source check also found a specific discrepancy in the digital record. In the printed concordance, the table that lists each compound's components and specimens, compound 4.19C on specimen SGI(PPT)-G-0137 has six components and two separate blocks of strokes. The web record has five components and one block, and the two label its final component differently, as a base sign or as a variant. An independent reviewer confirmed the printed cells. We kept the digital result and recorded the difference for later decision.

The next valuable join is an individually registered specimen with graffiti and readable Tamiḻi, linked to its excavation context and to an independent count or vessel measure. The books contain specimen photographs and separate concordances, while the web catalogue's data interface mostly supplies distributions over site, ware and depth. Resolving a specimen's identity across these records would add what another shape comparison cannot. The numeral discussions at Tondur and Alagankulam remain targets for acquisition, because the relevant original pages were missing from the previews we acquired.

## Harappa and Lothal: the objects that decide the next question

At Harappa, 22 tablets each carry three faces of two signs. On 20 of them the faces carry the same three exact inscriptions (modules) once the order of the faces is ignored. One tablet uses a variant of the complex form, and on H2238 the transcription we imported gives a different stroke count. The five tablets whose source drawings are fully readable all keep the same profile of three, four and three strokes. A registered, well-lit photograph of H2238's disputed face would test whether the common template has a real count exception. The [short-record report](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/harappa-short-records/REPORT.md) links its registration, production group and unresolved views.

At Lothal, the back of sealing L206 clearly preserves a curved surface it was pressed against. This contradicts the narrow proposal that the inscription it carries, which recurs on other sealings, always accompanies the angular edge of a board. On four objects in another group sharing one inscription, we could bound the number of visible cuts. The photographed zones cover different parts of the surfaces, and every range includes three. Complete views of the marked surfaces and a second resolved contact would make the next comparison informative. [Image study](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/lothal-contact/REPORT.md).

These studies narrowed the search to recorded groups of objects, exposed catalogue differences that affect analysis, and specified observations capable of testing a reading. They assigned no verified Indus numerical value.
