We found actual collections to investigate behind the proposed regional numerical links. The western study now joins marks, measured masses and catalogue identities at Susa. The South Indian study supplies a finite collection of stroke groups and their surrounding graphics. The Harappa and Lothal studies identify particular objects whose improved images would decide narrower questions. ## Which connections we made

The project's clearest original work is measuring the consequences of connections that general methods already recognize. We linked every physical alias and repeated inscription before splitting prediction data; a discarded duplicate can otherwise hide a training/test connection. We carried alternative sign readings through a likelihood calculation rather than choosing one convenient code. We connected the catalogue's recording and selection process to the distribution used for statistical comparison. These choices produced checked measurements and finite examples that another researcher can reproduce.

The numerical work connected local accounts to a complete inventory and asked which new measurement would distinguish the remaining values. In generated controls, a trusted inventory corrected some unreliable local groupings. Additional account relations sometimes left the same wrong scale or ratio intact. That result directs field acquisition toward an independently counted or weighed object that tests the surviving ambiguity.

The mapping work used held-out records from a known reference system to choose a record model before assigning unknown symbols. It then tested whether latent record forms could preserve distinctions lost by pooling. The [independent review](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/novelty/REPORT.md) found close precedents for these general ideas. Our exact comparisons, failure cases and source joins are the contribution to assess for publication. It ranks the empirical prediction paper first, the conditional sampling-law paper next, and a checked physical-source data note as another useful deliverable.

## Susa: measured mass beside repeated marks

The strongest acquired western object is Louvre **SB 13677 / AS 2615**, a hematite weight of **334.25 g** with four marks. The Louvre describes points; Ascalone's catalogue describes circles. The identifiers match, so we can request the correct object's photographs and measurement history. Ascalone had already proposed a relation among regional weight systems for this object. [Louvre record](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010186254), [Ascalone 2022, pp.49 and 69–70](https://airus.unisalento.it/retrieve/c39309c8-8dae-4dba-b990-86171612edf6/E.%20Ascalone%2C%20Bronze%20Age%20Weights%20from%20Mesopotamia%2C%20Iran%20and%20Greater%20Indus%20Valley_compressed.pdf).

We reconstructed that proposal using two other identified Susa weights, 13.43 g and 27.25 g. The proposed coefficients 25:1:2 imply unit masses 13.37, 13.43 and 13.625 g. A common base requires at least **0.945% symmetric variation**. This measures the fit of the published proposal; the observed marks and masses do not yet choose it over other unit interpretations. The creation dates of two of the three entries are missing. [Exact calculation and object joins](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/regional-west/REPORT.md).

We also checked the wider marked collection rather than selecting only that favorable relation. The census found **69 explicitly coded Susa entries**, with **57 eligible mass/mark comparisons** after exclusions for unknown mass, mixed graphic families and accession collisions. Three limestone ducks with five, six and ten strokes imply approximately 492–511 g per stroke, a potentially useful group to investigate. Other recorded shape/material groups span much wider ratios. All repeated groups need better dating before a historical unit rule can be tested within one assemblage. The [census](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/regional-west/marked-weight-census-v2.json) lists every member and preserves exclusions.

The same book contains **938 Greater Indus weight entries**, including **870 Dholavira objects**. Its descriptions supplied no explicit ancient marking joined to a mass, and the selected compressed plates supplied no readable Indus inscription on a weight. The promising connection is through excavation context: join DHR collection numbers, grids and levels to seal and sealing records, then inspect complete weight photographs. This gives the search a finite source population and concrete keys.

## Oxus and Central Asia: recover the complete object group

The most specific Oxus numerical lead is a reported group of **26 possibly marked clay tokens** from a room near the Gonur North Palace entrance. Bonora's account points to **Sarianidi 1998, Figure 41**. Recovering the original figure and inventory could reveal repeated forms, counts, exterior impressions or associated sealings. The known Gonur Indus seal has a different recorded location, Excavation 9, Room 19. Both packets retain their own context rather than being combined by site name. [Bonora et al.2014, p.66](https://www.persee.fr/doc/paleo_0153-9345_2014_num_40_1_5616), [recovery table](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/regional-west/evidence-table.json).

Altyn Depe provides another exact separation: its two-sign seal and proposed numerical tablet came from different excavation rooms and phases. Tepe Yahya provides marked weights, including a one-mark 13.37 g object assigned to 4500–4000 BCE. Those earlier examples can help investigate how marks accompanied weights; their dates determine which historical comparisons are possible. The regional report names the original plate, room and accession needed for each follow-up.

## South India: count the graphic population and check its components

We acquired both complete 2026 volumes of *Inscribed Potsherds of Tamil Nadu: Graffiti and Tamiḻi* and froze the government/library project's web catalogue. Sixteen plain stroke classes account for **480 reported occurrences**. The queried compounds include **194 forms with explicit plain-stroke components**; **142** have one consecutive run of stroke components in the normalized array and exactly one other component. This supplies a bounded population for examining a possible count-plus-category arrangement. [Printed catalogue](https://tamildigitallibrary.in/Articles/062969_Inscribed_Potsherds_of_Tamil_Nadu_Vol_1), [analysis](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/south-india/REPORT.md).

A source check also found a specific digital discrepancy. Compound **4.19C**, specimen **SGI(PPT)-G-0137**, has six components and two separated stroke blocks in the printed concordance. The web array has five components and one block. Its final component also has a base-versus-variant label difference. An independent agent confirmed the printed cells. We preserved the digital result and recorded the source difference for later adjudication.

The next valuable join is an individually registered specimen with graffiti and readable Tamiḻi, linked to excavation context and an independent count or vessel measure. The books contain joint photographs and separate concordances, while the API mostly supplies distributions over site, ware and depth. Resolving a specimen's identity across these records would add information that another shape comparison cannot supply. The existing numeral discussions at Tondur and Alagankulam remain source-acquisition targets because the original relevant pages were unavailable in the acquired previews.

## Harappa and Lothal: the objects that decide the next question

At Harappa, **20 of 22 tablets** share the same three exact inscription modules after face order is removed. One changes a complex-form variant; **H2238** changes the imported stroke count. Five fully readable source drawings preserve the same three/four/three count profile. A registered, well-lit photograph of H2238's disputed face would test whether the common template has a real count exception. The [short-record report](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/harappa-short-records/REPORT.md) links its registration, production group and unresolved views.

At Lothal, **L206** supplies a resolved curved rear contact, contradicting the narrow proposal that its repeated string always accompanies an angular board edge. Four objects in another repeated-string group have bounded visible cut counts. Their photographed zones cover different parts of the surfaces, and all ranges include three. Complete marked-surface views and a second resolved contact would make the next comparison informative. [Image study](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/lothal-contact/REPORT.md).

These studies have not assigned a verified Indus numerical value. They have narrowed the search to recorded object groups, exposed catalogue differences that affect analysis, and specified observations capable of testing a reading.
