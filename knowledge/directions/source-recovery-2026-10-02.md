# What the original sources changed

This follow-up recovered original Gonur and Altyn Depe publications, early Susa records, South Indian numerical examples and registered Lothal views. The clearest advance is a better basis for deciding which numerical hypotheses deserve testing. The early Susa records challenge a promising modern weight grouping; the Gonur figure exposes an inventory gap; the South Indian sources identify concrete numeral and object contexts.

## Susa: reconcile the records before fitting the marks

The 1911 catalogue describes two ducks as unmarked. The 2022 catalogue describes five and six strokes on the corresponding objects. Their materials also differ between descriptions. One mass changes from **2769 g to 3067 g**, a **298 g increase**. Those differences make the original inventory, modern registration concordance, conservation history and all-face photographs the next useful evidence. Our earlier close numerical fit is preserved with the modern catalogue inputs that produced it. [Source audit and arithmetic](../../experiments/2026-10-02-source-recovery/susa/REPORT.md).

The four round marks on SB 13677 / AS 2615 are now visible in a museum photograph and the original 1911 drawing. They are cup-like depressions. Soutzo interpreted them as four decashekel units and grouped a different four-stroke, 335 g weight, AS 13855, under the same value. That creates a specific comparison across graphic forms. Recovering AS 13855's modern identity and dated context could test the historical interpretation. [Original catalogue scan](https://archive.org/details/mmoires12franuoft), [Louvre object](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010186254).

Another imported Indus weight, SB 17774, has a production date of 2400–2100 BCE in the Louvre record. Kenoyer's published entry places its deposition in a twelfth-century BCE funerary repository. It could have circulated or been retained for centuries. The Met catalogue's foundation/funerary wording discrepancy is saved for excavation-record adjudication. Its production date alone therefore supplies no date for the use of other marks in the deposit. [Museum record](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010188675), [Met catalogue, pp. 401–402](https://www.metmuseum.org/met-publications/art-of-the-first-cities-the-third-millennium-bc-from-the-mediterranean-to-the-indus).

## Gonur and Altyn Depe: identify the physical sample

Sarianidi's original Gonur account reports **26 clay tokens** piled in a chamber near the palace's central northern entrance. Figure 41 has **23 photographed representations and 23 drawings**. The two panels have different row layouts and no individual identifiers. A complete quantitative dataset needs the inventory that joins each token to its views and marks. We now have the original illustration and a precise completeness question. [Recovered source packet](../../experiments/2026-10-02-source-recovery/oxus/REPORT.md).

At Altyn Depe, the original Plate 94.2 links three views of one terracotta object to Excavation 5, horizon 11, room 18. The caption calls it an imprint of a seal, tentatively. A photograph of this surface would help distinguish separate impressions, overlapping contacts and textile impressions. The original Gonur elephant-seal publication also confirms Room 19, Area 9 and autumn 2004, in the Temple of Water. It supplies an object/impression/context join for a separate find.

## South India: examples with named referents and contexts

The original Alagankulam government report joins its proposed **408** reading to **AGM-2, depth 1.40 m, stratum 2**, a rouletted-ware sherd and drawing serial 2. A recorded quantity or vessel measure associated with that object would test what the proposed numeral counted. Its modern object registration remains a recovery target. [Original government report](https://tamildigitallibrary.in/assets/docs/uploads/primary_files/book/TVA_BOK_0010617_Alagankulam.pdf).

Mahadevan's original Tondur chapter reports **three horizontal marks and three rock beds in Panchanampadi cave**, with a readable word for the beds. This is a useful published comparison of numeral, language and physical referent within Tamil-Brahmi. The recovered ink-impression scan is too dark for a fresh count. A readable inscription photograph and bed census would provide the independent check. Neighbouring caves have different bed totals, making the exact cave identity essential. [Original book scan](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.428160).

The registered Alagankulam_044 photograph joins a letter sequence and a separate graphic on one sherd, with a matching table context. Alagankulam_004 supplies a broken sherd with four visible long strokes and its catalogue context. These are practical specimen targets. The earlier 4.19C and large-count vessel records still need object photographs and measured quantities. [South Indian observations and review](../../experiments/2026-10-02-source-recovery/south-india/REPORT.md).

## Harappa and Lothal: exact archive targets

The original CISI volume supplies additional labelled Lothal panels and site-museum registrations with Helsinki photograph numbers. A separately acquired PDF has identical embedded plate images, so it improves access without adding resolution. New L190 side views leave the contact geometry unresolved under the saved rule. A colour front image helps locate its inscription but leaves its rear contact hidden. Harappa's 2010 publication gives a dated collection statement and registrations for the requested tablets. These records make an archive request concrete. [Views and acquisition packet](../../experiments/2026-10-02-source-recovery/physical-views/REPORT.md).

The immediate priorities are the Susa discordant weights and AS 13855, Gonur's individual token inventory, and registered object images with independent counts or measurements. The run has recovered evidence and corrected source assumptions. It has assigned no authenticated Indus sound or numerical value. [Run index, methods and retrospectives](../../experiments/2026-10-02-source-recovery/README.md).
