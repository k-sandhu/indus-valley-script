# What the original sources changed

This follow-up recovered the original Gonur and Altyn Depe publications, early Susa records, South Indian numerical examples and Lothal photographs labelled with registrations. Its main gain is a better basis for deciding which numerical hypotheses deserve testing. The early Susa records challenge a promising modern grouping of weights, the Gonur figure shows a gap in the token inventory, and the South Indian sources tie proposed numerals to particular objects and contexts.

## Susa: reconcile the records before fitting the marks

Soutzo's 1911 catalogue of the Susa weights describes two duck-shaped weights as unmarked, while the 2022 catalogue describes five and six strokes on the corresponding objects. The two catalogues also give different materials, and one mass changes from 2769 g to 3067 g, an increase of 298 g. Settling these differences needs the original inventory, the modern concordance matching old catalogue numbers to museum registrations, the conservation history and photographs of every face. Our earlier close numerical fit is preserved with the modern catalogue values that produced it. [Source audit and arithmetic](../../experiments/2026-10-02-source-recovery/susa/REPORT.md).

The four round marks on SB 13677 / AS 2615 are now visible in a museum photograph and in the original 1911 drawing. They are cup-like depressions. Soutzo read them as four decashekel (ten-shekel) units and grouped under the same value a different weight of 335 g, AS 13855, which carries four strokes. One value given to two kinds of mark allows a specific comparison. Recovering AS 13855's modern identity and dated context could test this historical interpretation. [Soutzo 1911, catalogue scan](https://archive.org/details/mmoires12franuoft), [Louvre object](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010186254).

Another imported Indus weight, SB 17774, has a production date of 2400–2100 BCE in the Louvre record. Kenoyer's published entry places its deposition in a twelfth-century BCE funerary repository, so the weight could have circulated or been kept for centuries, and its production date alone gives no date for the use of other marks in the deposit. The Met catalogue's wording differs on whether the deposit was a foundation or a funerary one; we saved this discrepancy for a decision from the excavation record. [Museum record](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010188675), [Met catalogue, pp. 401–402](https://www.metmuseum.org/met-publications/art-of-the-first-cities-the-third-millennium-bc-from-the-mediterranean-to-the-indus).

## Gonur and Altyn Depe: identify the physical sample

Sarianidi's original account of Gonur reports 26 clay tokens piled in a chamber near the central northern entrance of the palace. His Figure 41 has 23 photographed images and 23 drawings. The two panels arrange the rows differently and give no individual identifiers. A complete quantitative dataset needs the inventory that links each token to its views and marks. We now have the original illustration and a precise question about completeness. [Recovered source packet](../../experiments/2026-10-02-source-recovery/oxus/REPORT.md).

At Altyn Depe, the original Plate 94.2 links three views of one terracotta object to Excavation 5, horizon 11, room 18. The caption tentatively calls it the imprint of a seal. A photograph of this surface would help tell separate impressions from overlapping contacts and from textile impressions. The original publication of the Gonur elephant seal confirms Room 19, Area 9, in the Temple of Water, and autumn 2004, and so links that separate find to its impression and context.

## South India: examples with named referents and contexts

The original government report on Alagankulam links its proposed reading of 408 to AGM-2, depth 1.40 m, stratum 2, a sherd of rouletted ware with drawing serial 2. A recorded quantity or vessel measure for that object would test what the proposed numeral counted. Its modern object registration remains to be recovered. [Alagankulam government report](https://tamildigitallibrary.in/assets/docs/uploads/primary_files/book/TVA_BOK_0010617_Alagankulam.pdf).

Mahadevan's original chapter on Tondur reports three horizontal marks and three rock beds in Panchanampadi cave, with a readable word for the beds. This published case compares a numeral, its language and the physical things counted, within Tamil-Brahmi. The scan we recovered of the ink impression is too dark for a fresh count. A readable photograph of the inscription and a count of the beds would provide the independent check. Neighbouring caves have different numbers of beds, so the exact identity of the cave matters. [Mahadevan, book scan](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.428160).

The registered photograph Alagankulam_044 shows a sequence of letters and a separate graphic mark on one sherd, and the catalogue table gives a matching context. Alagankulam_004 is a broken sherd with four visible long strokes, also with its catalogue context. Both are practical targets. The earlier records of compound 4.19C and of vessels with large counts still need object photographs and measured quantities. [South Indian observations and review](../../experiments/2026-10-02-source-recovery/south-india/REPORT.md).

## Harappa and Lothal: exact archive targets

The original first volume of the *Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions* (CISI), the photographic catalogue of inscribed objects, supplies more labelled photographs of Lothal objects and their site-museum registrations with Helsinki photograph numbers. A separately acquired PDF has identical embedded plate images, so it improves access without adding resolution. New side views of L190 still leave the shape of its contact surface unresolved under the rule we fixed in advance. A colour front image helps locate its inscription but leaves its back hidden. Harappa's 2010 publication gives a dated statement of the collection and registrations for the tablets we requested. These records make an archive request concrete. [Views and acquisition packet](../../experiments/2026-10-02-source-recovery/physical-views/REPORT.md).

The immediate priorities are the Susa weights whose records disagree, AS 13855, the individual inventory of the Gonur tokens, and registered object images with independent counts or measurements. The run recovered evidence and corrected assumptions about the sources; it assigned no authenticated Indus sound or numerical value. [Run index, methods and retrospectives](../../experiments/2026-10-02-source-recovery/README.md).
