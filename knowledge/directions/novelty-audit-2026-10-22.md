# Novelty audit of 22 October 2026

Written 22 October 2026. This memo places each result the project has produced since 2 October beside the closest published work, and says which results are new to the field and which are ready to publish. The [independent novelty review of 2 October](../../experiments/2026-10-02-connections-and-priority/novelty/REPORT.md) covered the first four manuscripts; nothing after that date had been checked against the literature until now. Read this memo before writing a related-work section or choosing a result to submit.

## How the audit was made

Four literature sweeps, each run by a Claude subagent with web search and page fetching, covered fifteen results in four clusters: seals and time, structure and numerals, methods and decipherment critiques, and archaeology and language. Together they ran about 180 searches over Google Scholar-style queries, arXiv, the ACL Anthology, harappa.com, the INDOLOGY list, GitHub and the journals of Indian archaeology, and fetched the sources they cite. Each sweep was told which precedents the project already records on its conjecture cards, so that the search looked beyond them. We then verified every precedent that changes a verdict by reading the source ourselves: the list of figures of Wells 2015, the abstract and date of Raghavendra 2026, the two GitHub repositories of October 2026, the abstract of Recchia and Louwerse 2016, and Kolichala's 2016 list post. Precedents we could reach only through a quotation are marked second-hand in the table.

Verdicts use three classes. "Already published" means the same observation or rule is in print. "Partly anticipated" means the idea or a qualitative version exists, but not the measurement, control or data the project adds. "New" means no precedent was found for the measurement or the claim.

## What to publish

Three results are new and ready for a dated preprint. We hold priority on each only in a public repository, and the independent test benches of 15 and 17 October 2026 show that other groups are days behind on several of the same questions, so the date of a preprint matters more than the venue.

1. The rebus base rate. The protocol was frozen, the numbers were recomputed independently, and the blind test used sign pairs Parpola never published. Before submission a Dravidianist should check the compound parsing and the matched word pools; the Winslow result is reported as dictionary dependence.
2. Seal texts across cities. The counts and chance tests are new, and the plate finding that a different seal stamped the Lothal sealings changes how the M-895 text is read. The plate readings are by AI readers, so one epigraphist's reading of the M-895 animal and the L-161 legend is needed first.
3. The corpus audit and post-catalogue register. The Dholavira rows need Bisht's registrations to settle, and the joins stand at the submitted tier.

The stratigraphy result is publishable once an archaeologist confirms the export's mapping of Vats's strata to HARP periods. The key calibration paper should go out as a preprint that cites Raghavendra 2026 and the Savir bench and presents itself as the test of one published key. Line-division segmentation, stroke fixity, base families, the head-final corpora and the object-level calibration belong together in one paper on what a seal legend is built from. The fish order and the edge asymmetry are confirmations and should be cited as such. The town-axis test, the mīn note and the birth-star count are not ready: the first is undecided at four of eight towns and its Lothal dock bearing disagrees with the blind verification, and the other two are small.

Two gaps apply to every candidate. No package in the registry has a verification tier above "submitted", and no human archaeologist, epigraphist or Dravidianist has checked any source reading. Every count also rests on the Lipi export, whose rights are unresolved and whose gaps this audit lists.
