# Cryptography as an experimental framing

Research direction and synthetic test, updated October 2, 2026.

## Recent model-assisted cipher work

Frode Weierud's primary archive account, updated September 24, 2026, validates the key and plaintext for the 82-letter MVUEH Enigma message after Carter Leffen supplied an AI-assisted break. The account describes selecting a promising message, comparing it with a previously solved related message, a repeated place name used as a crib (a guessed stretch of plaintext), generated simulator and search software, and the discovery of transcription problems. It also records archival leads whose original message image remained unlocated. The validation depended on a known encoding, German plaintext and a related message. We did not independently reproduce the break. [Weierud's account](https://www.cryptocellar.org/bgac/the-mvueh-break.html).

The July 2026 *CryptanalysisBench* preprint reports 191 tasks and model-produced attacks, including a design flaw in SpoC and a proof flaw in KINDI. It separates attacks on a design, implementation problems and successes on weakened variants. The methodological lesson for us is automatic verification against an explicit reference problem, with the categories of success kept distinct. [CryptanalysisBench preprint, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18538v2).

A Vals AI report of August 31 proposes a solution to Urquhart's Cyphral Distich based on a book index, with caveats about counting and sources. [Vals AI report](https://www.vals.ai/blogs/fable-solves-cyphral-distich). A replication report of September 1 challenges the solution against a specific 1653 witness, that is, one printed copy of the source. [reticuli-labs replication report](https://github.com/reticuli-labs/panel-artifacts/blob/main/distich-refutation-2026-09-01/FINDINGS.md). Later discussion points to possible differences between copies and incomplete physical verification. [Schneier's update and comments](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/09/claude-fable-solves-a-historical-cipher.html). Resolving the dispute requires choosing the physical copy and reproducing the proposed coordinates against it.

These were the strongest relevant sources found in our search of October 2; the Enigma account was last updated September 24, and the benchmark's current version is dated July 29.

## Which features transfer to Indus research?

What transfers is a workflow: select tractable targets, reconstruct the process that generated the records, seek independent anchors, represent uncertain transcription, and implement searches whose outcomes can be checked. An anchor is a correspondence known from outside the texts, such as the identity of one sign. A promising result would then be tested on objects that were not selected for it. The approach applies to an ordinary writing or administrative system as well as to an intentional cipher.

| Cryptanalytic tool | Possible Indus adaptation | Required evidence and control |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency, repeated strings and n-grams | Graphic constraints and candidate segmentation | A control for the same object or text recorded twice, and metadata baselines |
| Comparison of related messages | Paired faces, recurring sealings, administrative archives | Source photographs and context establish the physical relation before the strings are compared |
| Cribs | A candidate commodity, recipient, place or count anchored by external records | Independent archaeological justification, competing anchors and predictions for withheld cases |
| Error-tolerant decoding | Transcriptions that keep a set of candidate signs, and models of damage | Photo labels fixed in advance, explicit uncertain and out-of-inventory signs, and uncertainty in sign counts |
| Constraint satisfaction and minimum-cost matching | Joint mapping of signs to graph roles or to a candidate language | Measure the classes of equally scored mappings and their sensitivity to anchors; include nonlinguistic controls and deliberately wrong comparison systems |
| Key verification | Predicted observations on untouched artifacts | Graphic and contextual accuracy can be checked first; historical word meanings require independent anchors |

Neural decipherment research offers minimum-cost flow and matching to a related language, evaluated on Ugaritic and Linear B. [Luo, Cao and Barzilay 2019](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1303/). Later work models segmentation and cognate alignment jointly under phonetic constraints. [Luo and colleagues 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.5/). An Indus application would need to test its choice of related language, its segmentation and its phonetic assumptions. The benchmark on known systems described below lets us study those requirements first.

## An identifiability obstacle we can test exactly

Let `x` be the observed sign sequence and let `pi` be any bijection of sign names. If a model and its sufficient statistics are relabelled consistently, the probability of `pi(x)` is the same as that of `x`. The transition table and graph keep their structure; only the names change. A score fitted only to the observed corpus therefore gives every consistent relabelling the same value and cannot choose among arbitrary semantic labellings.

Geometry, verified quantities, known languages, archaeological context and bilinguals supply additional constraints that may break these symmetries. A fixed external reference can therefore distinguish mappings that a model refitted to the corpus scores equally.

We implemented [an exact six-symbol demonstration](../../experiments/2026-10-02-cryptography-framing/README.md): all 720 relabellings preserve the fitted Markov score, entropy and count graph when the model is transformed consistently. Fixing the identities of two symbols reduces the remaining maps to 24. This gives the next benchmark a measurable target: how much each independently supplied anchor reduces the ambiguity of the mapping.

## Mapping benchmark and its next extensions

In each benchmark below, a generated system produces two populations. One is a known reference, a separately sampled set of records in the original symbols. The other is a set of short records whose symbols have been replaced by a secret one-to-one substitution. The solver searches for the mapping between the two, sometimes helped by anchors, and is scored on whether it recovers the true mapping. Two kinds of generator recur: transition systems, in which each symbol depends on the one before, and administrative-form systems, in which records follow forms with preferred symbols in each slot.

The first completed grid, CRYPTO-02, uses eight-symbol generators of both kinds and enumerates every mapping of the short encoded records that is compatible with the anchors. [Protocol and findings](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/anchor-benchmark/FINDINGS.md). When the transition process was unchanged, Markov scoring recovered all 12 mappings in each declared condition. When identity-dependent noise was mixed into the encoded administrative records while the reference stayed unchanged (a mixture change), recovery fell, and some wrong maps received more than 95% of the model's conditional probability. In those failures, the correct reference often still predicted held-out records better than an unrelated reference did.

The [next grid](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/reference-uncertainty/FINDINGS.md), CRYPTO-03, varies the size of the finite reference, the size of the training set and whether one supplied identity is wrong. Its 2,592 trials use 24 new systems. Integrating over the uncertain parameters of the reference reduces confident errors in unchanged administrative records, while changed processes still produce failures. A wrong anchor excludes the correct mapping, and the held-out score sometimes favours the best mapping that remains. The reference population, the supplied identities and the scoring model are part of the claim.

The [acquisition experiment](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/active-identities/FINDINGS.md), CRYPTO-04, uses 48 new systems to choose which observation to request next. Requesting the symbol whose identity is most uncertain (maximum identity entropy) reduces the observations needed to reach the exact mapping. Under a 70% administrative mixture change, it averages 4.885 observations, compared with 5.667 for random selection. Stopping as soon as one map holds 95% of the model's probability gives 60 wrong decisions among 96 condition histories for entropy selection and 44 for random selection. Choosing useful measurements and deciding that enough evidence has been gathered are separate tasks.

The [completed comparison](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/reserved-verification/FINDINGS.md) of reserved verification, CRYPTO-05, freezes the proposed map before acquiring additional identities and then uses those identities only to check it. Checks chosen by the model detect errors efficiently; checks reserved uniformly at random also give an exact sampling bound on accepting a wrong map, tied to the number of wrong identities. Every saved subset probability and the 4,032 audit records were checked independently.

The [coupled-source study](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/correlated-sources/FINDINGS.md), CRYPTO-06, treats repeated copies of one identity report as one possible shared error, as when several catalogue records inherit one reading. In a declared changed administrative condition, a wrong source reports eight identities and is copied ten times. Treating the copies as independent reports gives 46 confident wrong mappings in 48 cases. Counting the report once gives eleven, and the coupled model of the source's shared error gives four. A correct independent report improves the models that account for sources. The supplied error rates and the deliberately chosen wrong and correct sources define this generated experiment.

[Graph mapping](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/graph-mapping/FINDINGS.md) (NUM-02 and CRYPTO-07) matches the transition graph of the encoded symbols (which symbol follows which) to that of the reference, extending exact enumeration from eight symbols to optimisation over twenty. Exact maxima computed independently on small inventories separate failures of the search from scores that prefer a wrong map. A subsequent [assignment by record length and slot](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/slot-mapping/FINDINGS.md), which matches symbols by how often they occupy each position in records of each length, recovers 55 of 96 twenty-symbol administrative maps, compared with 25 for graph search on the same new systems. Graph search recovers 74 transition maps, compared with zero for slot profiles. The score must preserve the distinctions that the record process supplies.

A [fresh reference-selection grid](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/reference-selector/README.md), CRYPTO-08, chooses graph or slot modelling by cross-validation on the known reference alone, before mapping the encoded records. It recovers 309 of 384 mappings, compared with 283 for graph mapping and 168 for slot mapping. A retrospective oracle that sees the true mapping could recover 310. The independent audit checks every reference choice and all mapping scores; in eleven slot conditions, several maps tie exactly and all are kept. [Paper 004](../../papers/004-reference-model-selection/manuscript.md) states the selection rule and what it requires of the known reference.

The [stress test](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/overlapping-forms/REPORT.md) of overlapping forms, CRYPTO-09, gives every administrative form six slots, so that record length no longer tells forms apart, and mixes each slot's symbol distribution with a common background. With a reference of 400 records, the selector recovers 22/24 maps when the slot distributions do not overlap and 16/24 at .70 shared mass. With a reference of forty records it recovers 8/24 and zero. Supplying form labels rescues some failures. Independent score checks show that every wrong map returned outscores the true map under the fitted objective, so these failures lie in the score. The follow-up therefore needs a model that keeps a form distinction that record length does not show.

The [mixture follow-up](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/latent-forms/REPORT.md), CRYPTO-10, fits three form profiles to the reference symbols without the generated record labels. On the same frozen common-length conditions, it recovers 24/24 maps with the large reference at both zero and .35 shared mass, compared with 22/24 for the earlier selector. A matched control whose three profiles coincide gives 21/24 and 22/24. At .70 shared mass, selection on the reference alone prefers ordinary slot prediction. Six failed mixture maps score below the true map, so the search missed a better answer there; this gives a concrete optimisation test. Another 56 wrong maps score above it. The next independent comparison can vary the supplied number of forms, the form weights and the search budget on fresh systems.

Later controls can include short phonetic labels, inventories that mix word signs and numerals, and multilingual trade labels. Their known ground truth would let us compare permutation search, minimum-cost assignment and Bayesian constraint methods on the same target.

Keep the reference population and every supplied anchor visible in the protocol. Measure identity recovery, confidence, the size of the set of equally scored mappings and performance on separately generated records. These outcomes identify the observations an archaeological application would need.

## Choosing a tractable target

1. Begin with verified repeated artifacts or a documented sealing archive.
2. Separate the problem of repairing damaged signs from the problem of mapping signs to meanings. Improve transcription from photographs, and the record of which catalogue entries are the same object, before assigning words.
3. Seek a minimal independent anchor: an exact relation between repeated objects, an additive count, a container category or a securely associated commodity. Record what rival interpretations remain possible.
4. Prefer hypotheses that predict a previously withheld observation and fail decisively if wrong. Reserve full translation of inscriptions for a stronger stage of evidence.
5. Stop a search when many incompatible mappings achieve equivalent scores. Keep the candidates and specify the external observation needed to distinguish them.

## Starting point for the next run

Our first investigation measured sequential prediction, restoration of hidden signs and recovery of the number of missing signs. Those outputs are the starting evidence for an anchored mapping experiment. [Original methods](../../research/methods/FINDINGS.md). The next run should add a specified external constraint or test recovery on a known system. Source photographs and catalogue joins deserve as much attention as the solver: the recent cipher examples show how a transcription problem can change an otherwise promising search.
