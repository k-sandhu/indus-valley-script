# Structure before meaning: diagnosis and plan

Written 12 October 2026 after reading the 152-entry registry, the retrospectives, the direction memos and the packages begun on 11–12 October. The [interactive research map](https://claude.ai/artifact/PN2ShkmWxYKnMS5XV77dHD) shows every investigated path by line of thought and evidence type; its generator is kept in [the program directory](../../experiments/2026-10-12-structure-first/map/). The six-step program that follows from this diagnosis is recorded in [experiments/2026-10-12-structure-first](../../experiments/2026-10-12-structure-first/README.md).

Corpus figures below are descriptive counts made on 12 October over the local clean single-line Lipi frame (`data/normalized/lipi_clean_rtl_singleline.jsonl`, one record per distinct reading-order text, no unknown signs) and the full face export (`data/normalized/lipi_observed_faces.jsonl`). ## Why the work has not produced a substantive result

**The goal was set where desk research cannot reach.** The working definition of a substantive result has been a reading confirmed by an independent physical anchor: a counted collection, an attachment trace or a bilingual object. Those need museum access, new photography or excavation. By a keyword match, 80 of the 152 registry entries make their retry condition depend on new photographs, specimens, registrations, dated contexts or similar observations. Packages end with a protocol for data the project cannot obtain, and the next session opens a different topic. Results that the corpus in hand can support were never set as targets: a tested sign list, a segmentation into recurring units, a description of how the numerals behave and a function for one class of texts.

**Most measurements cannot separate the hypotheses.** Restoration accuracy, Markov gain and ordering against shuffles are high for every system tried: IVS, an invented office-routing code (0.631-bit gain) and all eleven testable Sumerian, Akkadian, Egyptian and Linear B cohorts. ROOT-12 recorded this on 1 October. The sequence lane still grew to 47 entries, and restoration rose from 33.8% to 41.2% top-one without changing what we believe about the script. Dictionary fitting has the same defect: equality-pattern word matches cover 91–97% of IVS faces in all four Bronze Age languages. A test is worth running when the hypotheses in play predict different values for it.

**The sign list was taken as given.** The corpus uses ICIT/Wells codes. In the clean frame (1,859 distinct texts, 8,535 signs) 577 codes occur; 214 of them (37%) occur once and 303 (53%) at most twice, while the 30 commonest codes carry 58% of all signs. Much of the long tail consists of compounds: a frequent base such as the jar, fish, human figure or U-shape with added strokes, hatching or an attached object. Counting each compound as an unrelated symbol fragments the evidence. Restoration fails on rare codes, substitution families depend on chart classes, and the meaning catalogue gives separate meanings to variants of one sign. Mahadevan's list has 417 signs, Parpola's about 386 and the coding used here about 700. No analysis measured how the results depend on that choice.

**Meanings were assigned before structure.** The project constructed 596 new meanings to cover all 713 codes, pruned them under review to seven families on nine codes, and began extending them to the 567 unreviewed codes. The fit criterion (a dictionary sense, a pictorial resemblance and compatible positions) admits many candidates per sign, so each anchor adds freedom faster than evidence. The language was inherited from the sources, the Lipi maintainer's Sanskrit readings and Bonta's Indo-Aryan proposal, and was never compared with Proto-Dravidian, Munda or an unknown language under matched freedom. The Rigveda, the earliest attested Sanskrit, was composed roughly 1500–1200 BCE, after the Mature Harappan period of about 2600–1900 BCE. Decipherments that succeeded proceeded from the sign list to numerals, then segmentation and inflection (Kober's triplets for Linear B), then the content of texts from context (Proskouriakoff's dates and events on Maya monuments), with sound values last, from names or bilinguals.

**The most informative material was filtered out.** The main population is complete single-line texts of at least three signs read right to left. Only 2 of 69 Harappa tablets, sealings and stamped vessels, and 2 of 355 rows in the catalogue's numerical class, pass that filter. The local export holds 190 Mohenjo-daro copper tablets carrying 77 distinct texts. The 12 tablets with a hare carry one text, the 10 with an anthropomorphic figure carry one text, and the elephant tablets carry two texts that differ by a single stroke. An image class predicts the text on these objects. The 2 October readiness review named the copper association graph as the useful next task; no package took it up. Also outside every main analysis: 2,060 bas-relief and incised tablet faces, the line breaks of 118 multi-line faces and the faces found outside South Asia.

**Rigor went where it was cheap.** 33 entries work only on invented or simulated data: eleven null-law enumerations, twelve synthetic mapping studies, khipu and measurement-acquisition simulations, a stamp-contact model and a societal renewal model. Of the 46 entries that measure real Indus material, 29 are variants of two statistics first computed on 1 October. One maintainer's transcription supplies almost every sequence, and its sign list, segmentation and reading-order conventions were adopted without a test.

**Breadth replaced accumulation.** Since 6 October the project opened about twenty lines. Five of the ten research programmes started on 11–12 October address questions outside the script. Each carries a protocol, reviews, a retrospective and packaging, and ends with next tests that later sessions rarely run. The Lothal attachment test and the Harappa short-record table recommended on 2 October each received one bounded pass.

**Reviews check for defects without asking what a result decides.** Adversarial review adds controls and caveats until a claim is safe. It does not ask which hypothesis a result moves, or by how much. A short hypothesis ledger fixes this. Candidate descriptions of the script include a logo-syllabic language, names and titles in a language, a nonlinguistic administrative code and an emblem or identity system. Each planned test should state what each candidate predicts, and a test on which they agree should not run.

## The program

The six steps use material already held. Steps 2–5 can run in parallel once step 1 has fixed the sign representation. Each step states in advance what result would change the next action.

1. **Fix the sign list.** Code every attested sign as a base plus modifiers. Build inventories at the Wells, Mahadevan/Parpola and base levels; recompute the frequency profile, held-out probability and substitution families under each; and test whether a model that shares statistics across a base predicts the original codes better than one that does not, and better than random groupings of the same sizes. A refinement made before any data were examined replaces the map's draft rule ("merged signs never contrast in otherwise identical texts"): without meanings, substitution in an identical frame cannot distinguish allographs from contrasting members of a paradigm, so the decision uses held-out prediction and random-grouping controls instead.
2. **Read copper and moulded tablets as image–text pairs.** Build the image-by-text graph, identify sign groups fixed within an image class, test whether a text's image can be predicted from other text types with the same image, and test whether copper-specific segments recur on seals with the matching field animal.
3. **Segment, then look for inflection.** Score segmenters against line breaks; count stem-and-ending alternations on the segments and compare with Sumerian seal legends and lines at matched size.
4. **Find out whether the strokes count things.** Tabulate the values each following sign takes and compare with commodity counts in Ur III accounts and Linear B.
5. **Rehearse on scripts we can read.** Run the step 3 and 4 procedures on Sumerian, Egyptian and Linear B material cut to Indus scale, with word boundaries and values hidden, and score what they recover.
6. **Compare languages only on structure.** With segments in hand, compare the measured profile with typological predictions for the candidate language families under matched freedom.

Pause further restoration models on the same masks, new null-law enumerations and synthetic mapping worlds, more Sanskrit anchors before steps 1–3, new topics outside the script, and further packaging passes until these steps report.

## Results of the program

The six steps ran the same day; the [integrated report](../../experiments/2026-10-12-structure-first/REPORT.md) gives the measurements and the [review responses](../../experiments/2026-10-12-structure-first/review/RESPONSE.md) the corrections. In brief:

- Graphic families improve prediction of rare signs. Random contiguous cuts of the Wells order recover about three quarters of that gain, and the published Parpola-level and Mahadevan-level groupings predict unseen signs better.
- Harappa tablet texts pair with their images within genre. Copper-specific sign groups show no transfer to seals showing the same animal at the fivefold level the test could detect.
- Catalogued line divisions fall between recurring units.
- Stroke values are more tied to neighbouring signs than Ur III counts are.
- At Indus scale, unsupervised segmentation and Kober-style alternation do not discriminate even on readable scripts.
- Indus texts close on a small sign class, a descriptive property that leaves every candidate language tied.

Each of the four ledger descriptions failed at least one frozen prediction. The report's next steps replace the plan's items where the results changed them, including a within-deposit seal and sealing comparison, segmentation scored against line divisions, and a head-final seal-legend corpus for the structural comparison.
