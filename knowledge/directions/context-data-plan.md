# Archaeological context data plan

The corpus exports we hold contain site, object type, material, shape and some excavation fields, but each field needs an audit of its completeness and of the source it came from. The next useful corpus is a table of observations in which each value is tied to a primary witness (a photograph, drawing, impression or printed record of the object) and missing values are recorded as missing. It should support a blinded test of whether inscriptions predict object use or context, coded independently of the text, after accounting for site, date, manufacture and repeated objects or texts.

Our first investigation in October measured an association between inscriptions and the seal or tablet type in derived metadata, and inspected a small set of objects in primary sources. It left open the join to excavation contexts and whether useful outcome labels recorded independently of the text are available. This plan specifies what to collect before we choose the endpoint of the next context test, that is, the outcome to be predicted and how it is scored. See the [corpus retrospective](../retrospectives/corpus-and-transcription.md), [source inventory](../provenance/SOURCES.md) and [original next tests](../../research/NEXT_TESTS.md).

## Observation model

Use separate tables for objects, contexts, witnesses, surfaces, graphic annotations and identity links. Each assertion carries its source, its location in that source, the observer, the annotation version and a confidence. Keep raw values beside normalized values. A minimal source record has `source_id`; author, title and year; access URL and date; the file's SHA256 hash; rights status; printed and PDF page; figure, table or caption; and the exact object label.

| Table | Key and fields | Coding basis |
|---|---|---|
| `objects` | `object_id`; raw catalogue references; excavation year and item IDs; lot ID; museum accession ID; site; material; colour; dimensions and units; shape; boss and perforation; carving, impression, relief or other manufacture; preservation; object-type label | Publication tables and physical photographs. Separate catalogued identification from direct observation. |
| `contexts` | `context_id`; excavation source; site, area, block, room, trench and grid; stratum, layer, locus and lot; depth with datum and units; phase; date interval and its basis; primary or secondary deposit; disturbance; associated assemblage | Excavators' context records. Keep a lot's identity distinct from its artifacts. |
| `object_context` | Object and context pair; asserted relation; confidence; source location; alternatives | Join through item identifiers and primary records. Record an uncertain association as a relation instead of forcing one context. |
| `witnesses` | `witness_id`; parent object or surface; photograph, drawing or impression; side caption; polarity and mirroring; scale and resolution; source hash; crop bounds and coordinate system; suffix marking a duplicate photograph | Source image and caption. A witness may show several sides or objects. |
| `surfaces` | `surface_id`; parent object; raw A/B/C label; line count; line arrangement; evidence of direction; inscribed, iconographic or plain status; relation to other sides | Source plate and caption, and direct coding of the layout. Keep uncertain side assignments explicit. |
| `graphic_annotations` | Witness, surface, line and unit IDs; displayed order; bounding polygon; observed strokes and components; height and width ratios; damage; visible partial carving; auxiliary status; candidate inventory codes and their namespace; transform to reading order | Descriptive annotation made blind, before catalogue or model candidates are seen. A component can belong to a compound glyph. |
| `motifs` | Surface; motif description; component count; animal and body posture; horn, foot and tail features; associated apparatus; style attributes; uncertainty | Image observations. Assign catalogue animal names separately from descriptive features. |
| `use_assertions` | Object; proposed use category; source author; type of evidence; direct evidence of wear, residue or sealing; reconstruction; confidence; alternatives | Physical and archaeological evidence, with the interpretation attributed. The manufacture of a seal, tag or tablet can be coded without choosing its historical purpose. |
| `identity_links` | Two identifiers; relation `same-object`, `same-side`, `extra-photo`, `impression-of`, `possible-match` or `repeated-text`; evidence from source or side; confidence; history of adjudication | Physical provenance first, then compatible graphics as additional evidence. |

For every field add `value_status`: `observed`, `catalogued`, `inferred`, `unknown`, `conflicting`, or `not-applicable`. An unknown value needs a reason such as `not-reported`, `source-not-acquired`, `illegible`, `lost-context`, `ambiguous-join`, or `outside-scope`. Store competing assertions without discarding either. Completeness statistics can then distinguish a value that was never published from a failed acquisition or an unreadable mark.

## Chronology and identity

Keep the source's chronological terms and stated range. A local “Stratum I” needs its own excavation sequence and datum; the same name at different sites is a separate label. A conversion from phase to calendar dates should cite the dating model and keep the uncertainty of the interval. Keep intrusion, residuality and secondary dumping visible. The H2218–H2239 cohort in the Kenoyer and Meadow excerpt we hold is qualified as a secondary dump, so its findspot and period support a different inference from an object sealed in a dated occupation surface.

Use archaeological item and accession identifiers to resolve physical objects; lot identifiers belong to contexts. CISI base numbers, side captions, the bis, ter and quater suffixes for extra photographs, and normalized export IDs each stay in their own source namespace. Missing or compound references can produce candidate joins; only confirmed same-object links enter the graph of physical identity. Texts that share a sign sequence form a separate dependence graph, used when splitting data for prediction. The P→W arrays, which list the Wells codes compatible with each Mayig class, remain local sets of compatible graphics.

An object with different inscriptions on several sides appears once in `objects` and several times in `surfaces`. Store the original boundaries of each surface. A row printed in the 1979 Helsinki edition can combine several sides or lines, so our digitization of that font enters the record through witness units until its link to physical boundaries is verified. Existing derived sequence fields remain intact for comparisons.

## First completeness audit

List the actual normalized metadata keys and raw values across all 5,680 rows of the Lipi export, one row per inscribed face, then group the rows into physical families, flagging conflicts. For each field report availability by face and by object, the unique values, how often placeholders occur, conflicts between faces, and concentration by site and object type. Keep `None`, which asserts that no motif is present, apart from the placeholders `-`, empty and `Unknown`. Normalization of case and spelling belongs in a versioned mapping table.

Report coverage for material, dimensions, type of manufacture, site, area, room or grid, excavation item, depth, stratum, phase, time or period, and motif. List the labels that could serve as outcomes, with their class counts within the site and period groups that have enough data. Trace a limited random sample, plus every uncommon label, back to primary tables or images. Publish a ledger of the joins that failed and were excluded. This audit decides whether a context experiment has enough data; the choice of labels should follow the available evidence rather than a desired result.

The Mayig subset can support graphic and stylistic annotation, but it is restricted to unicorn seals from Mohenjo-daro and shares its source photographs with Lipi. Its default uncertainty values and subjective unicorn subclasses need to be attributed to an observer and a source. Compare independent annotations on a frozen block of objects instead of selecting only the cases where catalogues agree.

## Blinded context test

Choose the target label and the sampling frame, the list of eligible objects, after the completeness audit, then freeze the protocol before scoring. A context coder sees primary witnesses of the object and its context, with the inscription masked where practical. A graphic coder sees crops of the inscription without the outcome labels. Record disagreements and settle them with a source rule specified in advance. Freeze the list of all eligible objects, including unknown or conflicting cases, before any model output is available.

Use the simplest outcome that rests on independently observed evidence and varies enough within groups of the same site, period and manufacture. For example, a category of use as a sealing or attachment may be testable where wear, residue or evidence of an associated closure is documented. A broad classifier of seals against tablets is available as a preliminary endpoint, but the type mixes manufacture, layout, preservation and site, so report those covariates explicitly.

Split the data so that all records of one confirmed physical object, and all records of one repeated complete text, stay together, and link aliases before selecting unique texts. Find the grouping links in the full register of observations, including faces excluded from scoring. Hold out source blocks or sites when enough groups remain. Report coverage and unsupported labels for every split. If dependence joins every record of a comparison corpus into one group, record that split as infeasible; the weather-icon control archive already did so when records sharing a station or an identical string were linked.

Compare a model using inscription features with a baseline model using site, date or phase, material, dimensions, manufacture, length, preservation, source and resolution, and motif where appropriate. Do not use an outcome or covariate derived from the sign sequence, such as Wells's text class. Include a comparison model based on templates alone. For the chance comparison, permute the outcome labels within matched groups or components specified in advance, and rerun tuning and fitting for each permuted dataset. A label that varies only between sites cannot support an estimate of an effect within a site.

Report the held-out log score, its uncertainty computed over groups of physically related or dependent records, performance for each class, coverage and missing data. The primary endpoint weights each object equally; declare any weighting by surface or sign separately. Check how sensitive the result is to unknown contexts, disputed identities and catalogue revisions, without replacing the frozen score.

## Success and failure criteria

| Test | Evidence needed for success | Failure or stopping result | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feasibility of the metadata | Enough objects joined to primary sources, and enough variation in labels, for the frozen split and groups | Sparse or confounded labels, joins that cannot be verified, a single dependence group | Whether a context endpoint can be estimated from this source block. |
| Blinded prediction of context from graphics | A gain specified in advance over baselines using context, manufacture, length and templates on a held-out source block, with a declared chance comparison and coverage | The gain disappears under controls, transfer to another source block or correction of identities | A measurable association between inscription and context within the sampled objects. |
| Physical numerical anchor | A confirmed join between figure and object, reliable mark counts and independent evidence for the relation between unit and contents | Ambiguous correspondence to the figure, damaged counts or an unsupported reconstruction range | Constraints on the premises of the published numerical proposal. |
| Prospective restoration of one missing sign | A photograph establishes that exactly one glyph is missing and shows the visible constraints; frozen predictions are calibrated on rare codes; later independent evidence resolves the sign | A broad or unbounded loss, or no independent resolution | How well the graphic candidates perform on an archaeological case. |
| Proposed reading of meaning or sound | An external prediction specified in advance, with alternatives and a decisive source outcome | Flexible relabelling, incompatible held-out evidence or no discriminating prediction | Support for or rejection of that particular reading. |

A successful context association guides the next source test. Meanings and sound values require their own external predictions, such as a join to a quantity and unit, an independently dated name or an authenticated correspondence with another script. Record those premises and the competing explanations once in the protocol, then evaluate the proposed prediction directly.
