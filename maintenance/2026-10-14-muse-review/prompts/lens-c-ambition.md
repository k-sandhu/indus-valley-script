## Your lens: ambitious goals and research strategy

Judge where the project should aim next and how to organise itself to get there. Read `README.md` ("Where things stand"), `knowledge/WORKING-MODEL.md`, `knowledge/conjectures/`, `knowledge/findings/`, `knowledge/directions/`, `knowledge/DATA.md` (sections 2 and 5), `python tools/kb.py next`, the six manuscripts in `papers/`, `experiments/2026-10-12-conjecture-shots/` (DESIGN, the shot reports, REVIEW-RESPONSE) and `bench/README.md`.

Questions to answer:

- What is the most ambitious goal this project could credibly reach in 3, 6 and 12 months, given what it holds and what it has learned? Name concrete, checkable end states (for example: a complete, citable object register with N fields; a confirmed structural result of a stated kind; a published benchmark others use; a refuted or strengthened working model). Rank them by expected value.
- Which current conjecture or finding is the strongest lever toward a real advance, and what single decisive test would move it most? Which lines of work should stop?
- What data would unlock the largest step (for example CISI 3.1 plates, ICIT access, museum images, stratigraphy, a sign-list crosswalk), and how would the project get it?
- The project uses many AI agents. What organisation of agent work would make progress faster without weakening the evidence rules: parallel lanes, adversarial review pairs, fixed confirmation budgets, data sprints, external experts? Be concrete about roles, cadence and hand-offs, and remember the project runs no CI/CD or recurring automation.
- What outside engagement (specialists, ICIT, museums, publication venues, open benchmark release) would raise the ceiling, and in what order?
- End with the boldest goal statement you think the evidence supports, and the test that would decide whether the project is on track to reach it.
