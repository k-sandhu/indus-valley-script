# Citation audit of the six manuscripts

13 October 2026. Prepared with AI assistance (Claude Code) during the academic-writing pass. No manuscript was edited; the corrections below are for the editors.

We checked all 102 external works cited in the committed versions of papers 001 to 006: 9 in paper 001, 4 in 002, 4 in 003, 6 in 004, 69 in 005 and 10 in 006. Every one of them exists, and no link leads to a different work, so the audit found no invented citation. Seventy-four citations are correct as given. Each of the other 28 has one problem that an editor should fix. Nine link labels describe the work instead of naming it. Eight entries have wrong or incomplete metadata: a wrong year (R07 in paper 005, given as 2018 for 2020), a wrong word in a title (R30, "Investigations" for "Investigation"), a wrong page range (Chen and Goodman in paper 006), a misnamed issuing body (R21), a published article labelled as an author manuscript (Urton in paper 003), and three entries in paper 001 that lack a title or a venue. Eight sentences attribute slightly more to a source than the source says. Two links in paper 005 no longer resolve (R43 and R49), and one page could not be loaded by any route (R06). Every number that a paper attributes to a source was found in that source wherever the text could be reached. Four sources named in the text have no reference entry: a Fuls example in paper 001, and the Perseus, Sangam and ORACC data in paper 006.

For each work we checked that it exists, that the authors, title, year and venue given in the reference entry, link label and running text match it, and that the URL or DOI reaches it. Where a paper attaches a number or a specific statement to a work, we found the passage. Metadata came from the publisher page or, when a publisher refused automated requests, from the Crossref, OpenAlex or DataCite records, PubMed Central, Europe PMC, arXiv or an institutional repository. PDFs were read by text extraction or as page images. Wiley, Taylor & Francis, harappa.com and several government sites refuse automated requests; there we read the same document through a request with browser headers, a repository copy or an Internet Archive capture, and the evidence column says which. Five helper agents checked parts of the list under shared written instructions. We reviewed their reports, repeated a sample of their checks (R07, R21, R29, R30, R61 and R71) and read the three sources they could not reach (R62, R63 and R70). Downloads used for checking stay in the session scratchpad, outside the repository.

In the tables, "ok" means that the work exists, its metadata and link are correct, and any statement attached to it is in the source. "Claim not supported" here always means that one sentence goes beyond what the source says; no citation was found to contradict the number it supports. Problem rows come first in each table.
