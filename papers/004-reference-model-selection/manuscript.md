# Choosing record models before symbolic mapping: reference-conditioned recovery controls

Complete private working manuscript, 2 October 2026. Authorship, affiliations and venue are pending. Computational work and writing used AI assistance. Protocols, generated systems, source snapshots, independent reviews and all outcomes are preserved in the private repository.

## Abstract

In a decipherment task with a known answer, encoded records write the symbols of a known system under new names, one for one, and the task is to recover that bijection by comparing them with a reference population: records written in the known symbols. The comparison runs through a record model, a probability model of how symbols are arranged within records, and can use only the distinctions that model keeps. We compare three on generated short records. Graph mapping matches how often each symbol follows another; slot assignment matches how often each symbol occurs at each position of records of each length; a latent-form mixture treats each record as an instance of one of several unlabelled templates with their own position-specific frequencies. Complete recovery means every symbol is mapped correctly. In a first grid of 48 new systems, slot assignment recovers 55 of 96 twenty-symbol maps for administrative records, which follow fixed forms, compared with graph mapping's 25; graph mapping recovers 74 maps for records generated by symbol-to-symbol transitions, compared with zero for slot profiles. A second fresh grid chooses the model by three-fold prediction of held-out reference records before any mapping answer is opened. Both likelihoods condition on observed length, so their scores are comparable. Selection recovers 309 of 384 maps, compared with 310 for a diagnostic told the generator family. A further matched grid makes the administrative forms the same length and lets them share part of their symbol probabilities, the shared mass. A mixture devised after that grid's outcomes and fitted to its saved records is told there are three components but not which record belongs to which. At reference size 400 it recovers 24 of 24 maps at both zero and .35 shared mass, compared with 22 under the preceding selector. Controls that give a single profile the smoothing of coincident mixture components recover 21 and 22. At .70 mass, reference cross-validation prefers ordinary slot prediction and recovery stays 16 of 24. Independent checks of counts, conditional likelihoods, exact small-inventory optima, assignment dual bounds and EM bounds accompany the results. Six failed mixture maps score below the true map, so the search missed it; 56 score above it. The study supplies known-answer controls for choosing a record model under an explicit external reference, a one-to-one inventory correspondence and a finite fitting budget.

## 1. A mapping search needs a record model

A record model decides what a mapping search can use. A frequency profile keeps how often each symbol occurs, a transition graph keeps which symbol follows which, and a position-specific form keeps which symbols occur at each position of a record type. Each can be relabelled by a candidate bijection and compared with the same summary of the reference population. A search succeeds when the retained information distinguishes the true assignment and the optimizer finds it. Those are separate empirical questions.

We earlier enumerated eight-symbol maps while varying reference uncertainty, supplying erroneous identities and changing the mixture of record types. A later graph-mapping experiment scaled the search to twenty symbols and checked eight-symbol optima exhaustively. It exposed both optimization gaps, where the search missed the best-scoring map, and training objectives that preferred wrong identities. [Reference experiment](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/reference-uncertainty/FINDINGS.md), [graph search](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/graph-mapping/FINDINGS.md).

This study follows one difficulty from that experiment. Twenty-symbol administrative mappings often placed most symbols correctly but rarely recovered the complete map under the transition objective, which pools all positions and forms. The generated records had typed slots and forms of distinct lengths. We therefore specified a model that keeps length and slot, then tested whether the reference population alone could choose between that model and a transition model. Both stages used fresh systems generated after their protocols.

Linear assignment, graph matching and model selection by held-out prediction come from earlier work. The graph search uses the FAQ algorithm of [Vogelstein et al. (2015)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0121002) with 2-opt refinement; the assignment uses SciPy's modified Jonker–Volgenant solver ([SciPy 1.17.0 linear_sum_assignment (Crouse 2016)](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.17.0/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment.html)). Neural decipherment work also uses matching or flow constraints and evaluates on known systems ([Luo, Cao and Barzilay 2019](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1303/)). We add a recovery comparison under matched conditions, a procedure that chooses the record model from reference records alone, and preserved optimization and identity failures under declared short-record generators.

## 2. Data and methods

### 2.1 Known systems and hidden identities

Each grid has twelve systems for each combination of two generator families and inventories of eight or twenty symbols. The transition family draws a start distribution and transition rows from Dirichlet distributions, then generates records of length three through eight. In the administrative family, symbols fall into four disjoint pools, one per role, and records follow three fixed forms of lengths four, five and six. Each position of a form, a typed slot, draws its symbol independently from its own distribution over one pool. Form probabilities vary across generated systems.

A random bijection renames the symbols of the training and test records, which become the encoded records. The reference keeps the original symbol names. Reference sizes are 40 and 400 records; encoded training sizes are 160 and 640. Each system has a baseline process and a .35 change between reference and encoded records. For transition records, the change mixes a shifted start distribution and a shifted successor distribution into each row. For administrative records, it changes the mixture of forms while keeping their slot distributions. Four hundred independently generated encoded records form the test set for each change condition.

Two reference sizes, two training sizes and two change conditions give eight conditions per system, or 384 per grid. Systems are the resampling units, and each system's conditions stay together. The first grid uses seed 810261002 with declared offsets for inventory, family and replicate. The selector grid uses fresh seed 910261002 and the same offset design. Generated records and hidden maps are saved; source snapshots, seeds and library versions reconstruct the generator parameters. [First protocol](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/slot-mapping/PROTOCOL.md), [selector protocol](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/reference-selector/PROTOCOL.md).

### 2.2 Two mapping objectives

Slot mapping estimates, from the reference, a symbol distribution for each cell, meaning each slot of records of each length, with one pseudocount per symbol. An absent cell is uniform. Let `C[L,j,c]` count encoded training code `c` at slot `j` in records of length `L`. The weight for mapping code `c` to reference symbol `r` is `W[c,r]=Σ C[L,j,c] log₂ q(r|L,j)`. Choosing the bijection with the largest sum of selected weights is a linear assignment problem, which the solver answers exactly. The decoder receives observed lengths, slots and the common inventory size; hidden role pools and true identities are used only in evaluation.

Graph mapping relabels the encoded transition counts by a candidate bijection and multiplies each by the reference log probability of that transition. Record start and end are fixed nodes, so the score includes boundary likelihoods. Each glyph row has one pseudocount for each of the `k` glyph destinations and END; the start row has one per glyph. The search runs FAQ from two deterministic and ten random starts, refines each result with 2-opt, and keeps the candidate, refined or not, with the largest training score. Solver seeds are declared by condition. Both decoders use the same records and inventory.

In the first grid, enumeration of all 40,320 eight-symbol bijections verifies the linear maximum. At every inventory size, a continuous relaxation of the assignment gives a dual bound, and the selected score is checked against its inequalities. The independent reviewer forbids each selected code match in turn to find tied alternative assignments. Numerical equality uses a declared `10⁻⁸` score tolerance; the dual checks use `10⁻⁷`. The selector grid has separate dual programs and certifies retained alternative maps as exact ties by prime-factor cancellation.

### 2.3 Choosing the model from reference prediction

The second grid splits the reference records into three random folds using seed `system seed + 50`. Each model fits on two folds and scores the third. The selector receives only reference records, inventory size and that seed. It receives no generator family, encoded test records, mapping answer or hidden probabilities.

Both models give the probability of a whole record conditional on its observed length. The slot model multiplies its cell probabilities. The first-order transition model multiplies start, interior transitions and end, then divides by its probability of producing that length. If `π` is its start row, `A` its glyph-to-glyph transition matrix and `e` its end-probability column, that normalizer is `π A^(L−1) e`. Both models then distribute probability over the same set of sequences. A tiny fully enumerated sequence space checks that each conditional distribution sums to one.

We choose the model with the larger pooled held-out reference log score, with ties going to graph. Both mapping methods are then fitted on the full reference and encoded training sample, and the chosen map is kept. Mapping accuracy is opened only in evaluation. A supplied-family diagnostic selects slot for administrative records and graph for transition records. Because it uses the known generator family, it measures what reference-only selection costs in this design.

### 2.4 Outcomes and uncertainty

We report complete recovery, the fraction of symbols mapped correctly and ties at the optimal score. A correct returned map counts as a recovery; tied compatible maps are reported separately. In the selector grid, each test record is scored under both conditional reference models with the same selected map and a common token denominator, so their whole-record likelihoods can be compared. The first grid keeps graph loss per edge, not conditioned on length, and slot loss per token, conditioned on it; those scores and denominators stay separate.

Twenty thousand bootstrap draws resample the twelve systems within each family and inventory, after averaging the eight conditions within each system. Intervals describe the generated design and chosen fitting procedure. Which model to develop next followed earlier outcomes, while each new grid had fresh systems and a protocol written before generation.

### 2.5 Same-length forms with shared symbol mass

A third grid of twelve fresh, matched twenty-symbol systems compares the separated-length design with three six-slot forms at shared masses 0, .35 and .70. Equalizing length adds two slots to the first form and one to the second, and the new slots draw declared role-pool distributions. In the six-slot variants, each cell distribution is `(1−alpha)p + alpha*b`, with one shared twenty-symbol background `b` per system. The shared mass alpha is the share of each slot's probability drawn from that background, so a larger alpha makes the forms overlap while record length stays fixed. Each variant has reference sizes 40 and 400, encoded training size 640 and change conditions 0 and .35, giving 192 conditions. Within a system, all variants reuse the same random draws for each record's form and slots.

The graph, slot and reference-selection rules stay fixed. A separate diagnostic receives stronger input: the form label of every reference and encoded record, on which it conditions its slot profiles. On identical symbol records, it measures what observing the form is worth. Twenty thousand paired bootstrap draws keep each system's variants and conditions together. Linear objectives have independent dual bounds and exact certificates for tied maps; the twenty-symbol graph search has no certificate of a global optimum. [Stress protocol](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/overlapping-forms/PROTOCOL.md), [independent review](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/overlapping-forms/review-v1/verification.json).

### 2.6 Unlabelled form mixtures

The stress result led us to fit a three-component mixture to the known reference: `p(x)=Σ_z π[z]∏_j θ[z,j,x[j]]`. Each component is a latent form with weight π and six independent slot distributions θ. We supply the component count, three; fitting reads the reference symbols without their form labels or role pools. Expectation-maximization (EM) is an established method for fitting such unobserved structure, including in noisy-channel decipherment. [Dempster, Laird and Rubin 1977](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1977.tb01600.x), [Knight et al. 2006](https://aclanthology.org/P06-2065/).

Its maximum a posteriori form, MAP-EM, adds one pseudocount per mixture component and per slot-and-symbol cell, corresponding to Dirichlet(2) priors. Eight fixed starts run for at most 150 iterations, and the start with the largest saved penalized objective is kept. Of 2,304 individual starts across 288 reference fits, 178 reach the iteration limit. Raw likelihood and penalized trajectories are both saved. For encoded records the reference mixture stays fixed. Under the current map, each record receives soft responsibilities, its probabilities of belonging to each form, and their expected counts define a linear assignment that updates the map. The implementation checks actual likelihood ascent and keeps the best initial or intermediate map across the declared starts, with at most 25 updates per start. [Mixture protocol and equations](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/latent-forms/PROTOCOL.md).

We designed this comparison after the stress-test outcomes and ran it on the saved six-slot systems: twelve systems, three shared-mass levels, two reference sizes and two change conditions, for 144 conditions. Three-fold reference prediction now chooses among graph, slot and mixture. Encoded test records and true mappings stay outside selection. A later, separately specified control asks how much of the mixture's gain comes from its smoothing. When the three components coincide, the mixture reduces to one profile with probabilities `(count+3)/(N+60)`, where `count` is a symbol's reference frequency at one slot and `N` is the number of reference records. The control uses that collapsed profile, and a four-model selector adds it to the three-model comparison. Paired intervals resample twelve systems, keeping their conditions together, with 20,000 draws. [Mixture methods](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/latent-forms/METHODS.md), [collapsed-profile protocol](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/latent-forms/COLLAPSED_PROTOCOL.md).

### 2.7 Identification at the stored population parameters

A separate post-outcome calculation asks whether the same-length generators are identifiable: whether their exact population distribution, given three forms, determines the form weights and slot distributions. It fixes the grouping `(0,1)`, `(2,3)`, `(4,5)` before rank checks. Each pair of slots is a 400-state variable whose conditional distribution has three form rows. Because the generator draws the six slots independently given the form, the three pairs are conditionally independent given form. [Allman, Matias and Rhodes (2009)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.5032v2), Corollary 2, then identifies the three weights and pair distributions up to a common relabelling of forms, provided the weights are positive and the Kruskal ranks of the three row sets sum to at least eight. A matrix's Kruskal rank is the largest k such that every k of its rows are linearly independent. Summing over each pair recovers its two original slot distributions. [Fixed-group protocol and proof](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/latent-generator-identification/METHODS.md).

Two conventions turn the stored binary64 probabilities into exact ones: exact rational normalization of the stored values, and probabilities implied by the generator's rounded cumulative thresholds under continuous independent uniforms. Both define population laws for a fixed three-component product model. Saved nonzero minors certify rank; singular values are diagnostics. A separate control with shared mass one makes every component identical. [Protocol](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/latent-generator-identification/PROTOCOL.md).

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Slot profiles improve administrative mapping

| Family | Symbols | Slot: complete maps | Graph: complete maps | Slot: symbol accuracy | Graph: symbol accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Transition | 8 | 21/96 | 90/96 | 60.16% | 98.31% |
| Transition | 20 | 0/96 | 74/96 | 24.43% | 89.27% |
| Administrative forms | 8 | 96/96 | 91/96 | 100% | 97.92% |
| Administrative forms | 20 | 55/96 | 25/96 | 92.76% | 85.05% |

For twenty-symbol administrative records, slot minus graph recovery is 31.25 percentage points, interval 21.875–41.667, and the symbol-accuracy difference is 7.7083 points, interval 4.5833–11.1458. For twenty-symbol transition records, slot minus graph recovery is −77.0833 points, interval −83.3333 to −70.8333. [Frozen results](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/slot-mapping/main/results.json), [paired summaries](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/slot-mapping/review/summary.json).

Each outcome follows from what the model keeps. The administrative forms have different observed lengths, so conditioning on length keeps each form's slots separate, while changes in form frequency alter any statistic pooled across forms. Transition records need the current symbol to predict its successor, and slot profiles discard that pairwise relation.

All linear optima pass enumeration or dual-bound checks. In twenty-three conditions at least one code can move without changing the optimal score, within tolerance; those alternative maps remain in the output. The largest independent numerical disagreement is `8.57 × 10⁻¹⁰`. The grid took 47.37 seconds, including both mapping methods and the exact and dual checks. [Independent verification and tied maps](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/slot-mapping/review/verification.json).

### 3.5 Exact form parameters remain identifiable in the overlap controls

For all 36 generator profiles, twelve systems at each of zero, .35 and .70 shared mass, each of the three pair matrices has Kruskal rank three under both conventions. Their sum, nine, exceeds the required eight. The form weights stay positive with and without the .35 change. Exact population probabilities therefore determine the form weights and six slot distributions within the specified model, up to a common relabelling of forms. In the controls with shared mass one, pair rows are identical and have rank one, and the distinct weight vectors (.2,.3,.5) and (.3,.3,.4) give the same population law. [Exact witnesses and controls](../../experiments/2026-10-02-six-hour/latent-generator-identification/README.md).

The independent audit reconstructs 345,600 rational pair entries, checks 34,560 slot marginals and 144 mixing vectors, and certifies 288 minors by elimination. It keeps the first audit's failed support check: at zero shared mass, a rounded cumulative sum can leave a tiny positive interval for the final code, so five symbols with positive probability become six. The corrected review leaves the main ranks and their certifying minors unchanged. The result identifies the population-model parameters; fitting a finite reference and mapping across alphabets are the empirical tasks evaluated above. ## 4. What this supplies for an unfamiliar script

The study starts from an external reference whose symbols and record process are known. The inventories correspond one to one, record boundaries and lengths are observed, and generator changes have a declared form. Those premises define the recovery problem. An Indus application would need an independently justified reference or functional observation, plus models for which sign forms count as one sign, for segmentation and for mixtures of record types. The experiments give a way to test those model choices on a known control before investing in an archaeological mapping.

The most useful observation can be the record's form or physical role. Here an observable length preserves an administrative form, and held-out reference prediction chooses the score that uses it. In other collections, a container class, face role, operational stage or measured quantity could be the variable a model conditions on. Its source must be recorded separately from the symbolic mapping so its predictive value can be checked.

The same-length stress test and mixture follow-up make that conditioning variable a measurable research target. Fitted latent forms improve several conditions when the count of three is supplied. The next evaluation can vary the number of forms, let the form weights adapt to the encoded population and use fresh systems with uncertain boundaries. The six search misses also provide a fixed optimization diagnostic. An acquired reference or independently recorded object role can replace a declared generator premise in a new experiment.

## 5. Verification and availability

The first grid's independent implementation rebuilds every cell count and profile weight, checks all training and test likelihoods and answer-key comparisons, re-enumerates eight-symbol optima, verifies dual inequalities and keeps every alternative found by excluding a code. The selector review passes 6,489 checks: every fold and choice, all 1,536 returned-row likelihoods, 384 independent slot dual programs and exact conditional mass at lengths one through four. Its state-vector recursion independently reconstructs the graph length normalizer. Prime-factor cancellation certifies 32 alternative likelihood equalities across eleven tied slot conditions. The failed first pilot is kept: a NumPy boolean prevented JSON serialization after fitting, and converting it to a Python boolean let the deterministic pilot complete into a new result directory.

Code, generated inputs, hashes, protocols, outcomes and retrospectives are kept in the private repository. [Methods and claim files](methods.md) give exact paths. Primary literature provides the algorithm precedents. New source observations and future trials should keep the current results and state the changed premise before execution.
