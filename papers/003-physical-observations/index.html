Physical observations in numerical decoding: constraints, acquisition and a khipu control

Private working manuscript, 2 October 2026. Authorship, affiliations and venue are pending. Computational analysis and writing used AI assistance. The linked protocols, outputs and source records form the review package.

Abstract

A proposed numerical reading must connect marks to independently observed quantities or relations. We study which physical observations do this when symbol values are unknown positive numbers. A whole–part relation, in which a parent object's quantity should equal the sum of the objects it demonstrably contains, fixes the ratios between values, their relative values; a count of one object fixes the scale. In a six-label simulation, twelve complete whole–part observations identified relative values in all 24 generated collections; one count fixed scale. When observations had costs, a rule choosing the observation expected to add the most independent equations per unit cost recovered absolute values in 23 of 24 collections with complete groups and exact counts, compared with 15 under random choice. Counts with understated uncertainty could identify a wrong scale that passed every withheld whole–part relation. A control on khipu accounts reproduced 16 of 20 columns in the published Puruchuco alignment. In generated decimal accounts, after a counted unit fixed the first positional value, account relations identified the rest in 299 of 300 complete collections. When children were misassigned to parents, an inventory showing that all parents and children formed one complete pool raised recovery from 133 to 190 of 200 collections with four swapped children and reduced accepted wrong answers from nine to one. Treating an incomplete pool as complete produced fifteen accepted wrong answers. One wrong set of values passed eight new account relations that all repeated one equation, but failed independent counts. We provide exact solution certificates, preserved failures and an observation table for applying these methods to unfamiliar numerical records.

1. The observation that makes a reading testable

Suppose an unfamiliar object carries a mark beside three smaller objects. A proposed reading may assign a quantity to the mark. If the smaller objects demonstrably belong to the marked parent, their measured quantities can constrain the proposal. Repeated whole–part relations constrain the ratios between values, while an independent count can establish absolute scale. Our task is to identify which observations provide those constraints and which alternatives remain compatible.

The question matters for Indus numerical proposals because short stroke groups, vessel marks and tablets inscribed on several faces invite arithmetic interpretations. Our earlier audit of a published proposal built on two equations found several positive whole-number solutions under its unrestricted premises; only the additional ranges chosen for the unknowns selected one system. The source review also found that no source tied the proposed external bangle count to the particular inscribed object. Exact audit, source premises. We therefore need a relation whose origin and units can be checked separately from the proposed reading.

Known numerical systems provide controls. For khipus, the Inca knotted-cord records, Urton's study of khipu archives documents duplicate accounts and identity labels, and the Khipu Field Guide publishes an explicit Puruchuco account comparison with its partitions, values and discrepancies. These records let us test how the encoding of values, such as their order and grouping into rows, affects recovery of an already specified relation. Urton (2005), Khosla, Khipu Field Guide, "Replicating Puruchuco".

We supply the hypothesis that marks record additive quantities and ask whether the available observations identify their positive values. The study combines generated controls, whose concealed answer keys establish numerical correctness, a published-source replication and a collection design with targets from primary artifact sources.

2. Methods

2.1 Physical membership and scale

The first simulation, PHYS-01, generates 24 collections with six concealed positive label values. A complete assembly, a parent with all its labelled children, contributes one equation: the parent's value equals the sum of its children's values. Each collection has twelve training assemblies and eight withheld assemblies kept for checking. Label frequencies are balanced across the observations that record labels without membership, so frequencies alone leave every assignment of values possible. One independently counted object supplies an absolute measurement. The solver receives label identities, membership and the count, while the answer key remains in a separate saved input. PHYS-01 protocol and current results.

Let the vector v contain the unknown positive values and let each row of A record one assembly as parent minus children. Complete relations give A v = 0. For six labels, five independent rows can identify one positive relative ray, a set of values fixed up to a common multiplier. A count picks one point on that ray and so fixes scale. Incomplete groups contribute inequalities, parent ≥ visible children, when the missingness is declared. Exact fractions preserve ranks (numbers of independent equations), solutions and counterexamples.

We distinguish unrestricted positive values from a finite diagnostic that is told the six values and only assigns them to labels. That diagnostic has a stronger prior and can select a mapping before the unrestricted relations identify it. Controls scramble membership, treat incomplete groups as complete, or make labels mark ownership, with object quantities assigned independently. A corrected run repairs an error in the ownership-control counts that we found while preparing the separated inputs. The initial output and correction remain available.

2.2 Choosing observations

A second simulation, PHYS-02, generates fresh worlds, each a collection with a concealed answer key, and asks which observation to acquire next. Each action has a cost, and the budget is 24 units. An inspection reveals a whole–part relation; a count measures a named object. Before choosing, a selection rule sees only public descriptions of the actions, including completeness and a bounded menu of possible child counts. Unopened responses and hidden quantities stay outside the selector. The surviving positive values are represented by equalities, inequalities and measured intervals. Exact optimization certificates, rational proofs that computed bounds are correct, support classification and planning. PHYS-02 protocol and methods.

Three rules were fixed in a protocol frozen before the run. The information/rank rule takes the action with the highest expected gain in independent equations, or in narrowing an unresolved range, per unit cost; the other rules choose at random or take the cheapest action. Each runs on 24 worlds in each of five conditions: complete groups, incomplete groups, count intervals that contain the true value, counts with too little reported uncertainty, and ownership labels. Success requires the correct absolute values; identified ratios and remaining scale intervals are recorded separately. Rules continue to the budget limit, so the cost of first identification differs from total spending.

A post-outcome refinement, designed after we saw the results, addresses interval measurements. The first rule gave an interval no value when the existing range was narrower than the interval width, yet a shifted interval could still trim that range. A separately specified overlap rule uses w/(w+2r), where w is the current range width and r the measurement radius. It was tested on 24 fresh interval worlds with all four rules. Its assumptions and the original failed rule remain in separate result directories.

2.3 Puruchuco representation control

The khipu control, KHI-01, uses the Open Khipu Repository, pinned to commit 4039ca51f4de661d80d0160596309c983a62a9c7. Six original Puruchuco accounts match stable database identifiers. CM009 is a field-guide reconstruction from an earlier thesis and is kept as a separate source record. In the selected records every pendant attachment-position field and every knot-cluster total field is zero, so the reference values come from the guide's published calculation, with source coordinates and hashes. KHI-01 source audit.

The fixed predictor adds three runs of twenty pendant cords on khipu UR068 (14–33, 34–53 and 54–73) column by column and compares the twenty sums with UR067 pendants 13–32. We saw the target values while reviewing the source, before the protocol was frozen, so this part is a replication; code isolates the predictor from the evaluator that holds the targets. The replication keeps the published alignment and all four mismatches.

Controls keep an ordered flat list of known width, reverse selected rows' column order, apply all 400 relative cyclic shifts (sliding rows against one another with wrap-around), perform 10,000 within-row shuffles, or reallocate the sixty pooled values into three equal rows 10,000 times. Whole-row permutations check additive invariance. Every control keeps the total of the three summed rows, so only column-wise error distinguishes them. KHI-01 methods.

2.4 Hidden positional values

The second part of KHI-01 generates 300 worlds for each of three account conditions. Each world has eight accounts, each a parent with three leaf quantities. Complete-account parents store their sum; ownership parents have independent quantities. Quantities are written as mark counts at three digit positions whose true values are 1, 10 and 100; these counts form an object's feature vector. The solver receives the counts, physical membership and a counted one-unit object, whose feature vector (1,0,0) fixes the first positional value at one. Account relations constrain the two remaining values. The solver is given no set of possible values.

Each account's parent feature vector minus its children's summed feature vectors supplies a linear equation. Exact row reduction and strict positivity classify each prefix, the first one to eight account relations in a fixed order, as uniquely positive, ambiguous or infeasible. The evaluator checks each unique decoder, the inferred positional values, against all 32 generated quantities. Encodings keep membership, reorder siblings, omit membership, collapse positions or scramble children across parents. Incomplete accounts lose one child's membership in four accounts. A protocol supplement specified prefix evaluation before execution. KHI-01 simulation and verification.

2.5 Uncertain membership and collective inventories

KHI-03 generates worlds from 300 fresh random seeds and replaces fitting all equations with maximum consensus: it selects the values satisfying the most of the eight local account equations, each counting as one vote. With the first value fixed, two values are free. Exact intersections and line families retain every tied optimum. A support gate sets how many accounts must agree: six of eight (75%) for the primary gate and four (50%) for the secondary. Conditions include missing associations, independent ownership values, and two or four swapped assignments, in which one or two pairs of parents exchange a child. Held-out account relations and independent counts evaluate each accepted decoder. The protocols and all earlier-prefix outcomes are saved. KHI-03 methods.

KHI-04 then draws 200 fresh base seeds, each generating quantities that seven observation conditions reuse. All visible local account rows vote as equalities, including rows with missing associations. An independent inventory records all eight parents and their pooled children. It assumes the same quantity type, units and event, and that parent marks describe contents pooled without loss or exchange. A complete collection gives a binding equality, the parents' total equal to the children's total; a declared partial collection gives an inequality. Swapping local members leaves the collective equality unchanged. All parent and pooled-child marks are available before the first local prefix, at no cost. The collective relation is a hard constraint and casts no vote. Two scale views are kept: the normalized view sets the first value to one, giving a positive ray, and the counted view uses an independent unit count to fix that world's absolute scale. The same 75% and 50% gates apply. Collective protocol and observation availability.

2.6 Select validation rows before opening targets

KHI-05, a post-outcome follow-up, reuses KHI-04's sealed worlds and final candidate sets to ask which validation checks to choose. Each world offers eight held-out account equations and three count equations. The selector receives their coefficients and types, the budget and any available coefficient row for the counted unit, the unit anchor. Measured targets, candidate decoders, world condition and true values are withheld. Account-first, count-first and greedy rank, which picks the check that most raises the number of independent equations, use budgets zero through three. Uniform-random subsets are enumerated exactly. Each check costs one abstract unit; acquisition effort and measurement uncertainty are absent. Validation intersects the original optimum points, rays and families of positive solutions with the selected measured equations and keeps the original support gate. KHI-05 protocol and input separation.

3. Results

3.1 Relations determine ratios; counts determine scale

Twelve complete assemblies identify the correct positive relative values in all 24 six-label collections, and one count then identifies every absolute vector. The finite diagnostic, told the six values, is unique sooner. At three assemblies it selects one assignment in 12 collections, while no unrestricted positive relative vector is unique. At eight assemblies, the finite diagnostic is unique in all 24, while the unrestricted model identifies 23. PHYS-01 corrected results.

With six incomplete groups entered as inequalities, 17 collections retain a unique relative ray. Seven admit a second positive whole-number assignment after the count. For collection 20261015, both (6,8,1,2,4,3) and (12,8,2,4,8,2) satisfy every acquired constraint and the measured value eight on label g1. Withheld complete assemblies reject the alternative. Exact alternative assignments.

With only the first few assemblies, five controls produce a unique but incorrect positive relative ray: two scrambled collections and three incomplete collections treated as complete. Each fails at least one of the eight withheld relations; they fail six, five, six, six and eight respectively. A unique solution found from part of the evidence can therefore be tested against separate observations.

3.2 Observation choice changes recovery cost

Condition Information/rank rule: correct absolute vectors Random Cheapest
Complete groups and exact counts 23/24 15/24 0/24
Incomplete groups and exact counts 22/24 17/24 1/24
Valid count intervals 1/24 1/24 0/24
Counts with insufficient uncertainty 0/24 0/24 0/24
Ownership labels 0/24 0/24 0/24

The information/rank rule identifies quantities sooner than random choice in all 15 complete-group worlds that both solve, saving a mean 6.47 cost units. Across the 17 incomplete-group worlds that both solve, it saves 9.82 units on average; it is faster in 16 and slower in one. One complete world has no available count, so its identified ratios keep an unfixed scale. Opening all available evidence after the frozen run shows that one incomplete world is irreducibly ambiguous, while another needs more acquisition than the original budget allowed. Main acquisition analysis.

Valid intervals usually leave a range of possible scales. On 24 fresh worlds the refined overlap rule recovers six absolute vectors, while each other rule recovers none, and it keeps correct ratios in all 24. Compared with the information/rank rule, its final range is narrower in 15 worlds and tied in nine. On those worlds the mean width relative to true scale is 47/192 for information/rank and 1765/10368 for overlap; median widths are 1/6 and 1/12. Eighteen overlap-rule worlds retain intervals. Fresh refinement analysis.

3.3 The check must measure the unresolved quantity

When counts report too little uncertainty, the information/rank rule produces a wrong unique absolute vector in 21 of 24 worlds. In world 2026103200 it infers (6,3,8,1,2,4) while the true values are (12,6,16,2,4,8). All ten withheld whole–part relations have zero residual, because the ratios are correct. Two withheld counts have residuals minus two and minus eight. Whole–part relations still hold when every value is multiplied by the same factor; only independent counts test that remaining freedom. Saved case.

This result guides verification design. A proposal inferred from ratios needs a scale-sensitive check. A proposal inferred from one measured count needs an independent count whose uncertainty is justified. Whether an observation can reject the remaining alternatives depends on its mathematical relation to them.

3.4 Coordinate correspondence in a known account relation

The published khipu alignment matches 16 of 20 columns, with total absolute error 16. The three summed UR068 rows total 3,498 and the UR067 target row 3,482. Ordered flattening with known start and width preserves the prediction exactly. The published alignment is the unique minimum-error result among 400 relative cyclic shifts. The best of 10,000 within-row shuffles has error 738; the best of 10,000 reallocations of the pooled values has error 828. Source aggregates.

The difference between the totals remains 16 under every allocation, so it cannot distinguish the controls. The column-wise comparison measures the correspondence supplied by the source's grouping of cords into physical rows and accounts.

3.5 Relation diversity in positional values

At eight complete accounts, 299 of 300 worlds identify the correct positional values. In the remaining world, seven nonzero equations are multiples of −10u−9t+h=0, with u, t and h the first, second and third positional values, and one equation is zero. This is a carry relation: adding the children's digits carries one into the second position and one into the third. The count fixes u=1. Both (1,2,28) and (1,10,100) satisfy the observed accounts. Because the accounts repeat one carry relation, the remaining free value stays unresolved.

Sibling permutations preserve every prefix. Removing membership leaves all 300 worlds ambiguous. Collapsing positions or scrambling membership rejects all 300 complete worlds by the eighth relation. When full membership is assumed, 97 incomplete worlds and 47 ownership worlds produce an incorrect unique decoder at some earlier prefix, which later relations reject. KHI-01 numerical report.

3.6 Consensus with uncertain associations

With two swapped assignments, the 75% consensus gate gives 281 correct, four wrong and fifteen ambiguous outcomes in 300 worlds; requiring every equation gives one correct outcome and 299 rejections. With four swaps, the 50% gate gives 200 correct, sixteen wrong and 84 ambiguous outcomes. The 75% gate rejects 297 worlds and accepts one correct, one wrong and one ambiguous set. Each wrong vector still accepted at the final prefix fails the independent checks. In one two-swap world, five true accounts repeat -10u -9t +h = 0; two corrupted accounts select (1,11/2,119/2) with seven votes, while the true vector has six. The saved exact geometry shows how individually correct relations can support a wrong point: five repeat one equation and so fix only one ratio, and the corrupted accounts fix the rest. Frozen consensus report.

3.7 An independently recorded complete pool

In the 200-seed collective experiment, two swapped assignments give 189 correct, two wrong and nine ambiguous outcomes under local consensus. Enforcing the complete-pool equality gives 197 correct, zero wrong and three ambiguous outcomes. For four swaps under the 50% gate, the corresponding counts are 133/9/58 and 190/1/9. With two missing local associations but a complete global inventory, correct recovery rises from 188 to 198. A declared partial pool gives 189 correct outcomes; treating that partial pool as complete gives fifteen accepted wrong points under the 50% gate and rejects every world under the 75% gate. Collective results.

At the final prefix, the correct complete-pool equation is the sum of the eight local equations. It adds information because it is recorded independently and stays valid when particular local associations are wrong. Maximum consensus may discard some local equations while the collective relation remains binding. All comparisons reuse the same seed's quantities and have 200 independent base seeds.

One wrong local-consensus vector, (1,4/3,22), passes all eight fresh account relations in world 20261020124-four_swapped. Those equations have rank one: they repeat the same carry relation. Three held-out count equations have rank three and reject the vector, even if its scale may vary. In the counted view, a unit count of six gives (6,8,132) and count residuals -4056, -1716 and -3484. The complete collective equation also rejects this point. Validation therefore needs measurements that separate the surviving values and scale. Exact selected-case arithmetic.

3.8 Validation choice and the remaining scale

Under four-swap local consensus, nine worlds end with a wrong ray, that is, wrong ratios. Three account-first checks leave one compatible. Greedy rank rejects all nine with two checks when the unit value has already been counted, or three without that anchor. Count-first needs fewer: one anchored count rejects all nine, and two unanchored counts do so. One unanchored count leaves all nine compatible because each wrong ray can adopt a different scale. With three checks chosen uniformly at random, the exact expected number of wrong rays remaining is .606 with the anchor and 2.024 without it. Validation report and exact fractions.

Validation also filters tied optimum sets. In the counted view, count-first with one check or greedy rank with two produces 190 correct absolute points and ten rejections from the original local-consensus sets. From the collective sets, either rule produces 199 correct points and rejects one. Without the anchor, count-first with two checks or greedy rank with three gives the same 199 collective recoveries. These are post-outcome comparisons on a fixed population.

In the rank-one example, the first unanchored count selects the wrong absolute vector (645/23,860/23,14190/23) on the previously accepted ray. It fits 6v +8w = 5160, as does the true vector (6,60,600). A second count, 6u +6v +3w = 2196, gives the wrong vector a residual of 1092/23 and rejects it. This saved intermediate point shows that fixing scale alone can leave wrong ratios. Coefficient rank describes the selected rows; the original candidate constraints determine how many further measurements are needed.

4. Implications for collecting unfamiliar numerical records

These results define the required observation table. Each proposed relation needs a physical parent, its actual associated members, completeness, source coordinates, mark features and a basis for treating the relation as additive. A numerical reading also needs a measurement tied to an identified object and its units. A remaining family of positive solutions shows which further observation could distinguish the surviving values.

Field to acquire Record Constraint it supplies
Physical identity Registration, accession, source edition and exact figure and page Joins observations to the same object and preserves aliases
Grouping and orientation Parent, associated members, face adjacency, physical end and positional address Defines which features participate in each relation
Completeness Missing member, damaged area, recovery basis and surviving extent Determines whether an equality, inequality or censored observation is appropriate
Graphic features Counts, height/tier, form alternatives and image coordinates Supplies coefficients and retains ambiguous alternatives
Quantity relation Observed operation, its units and source basis Gives the equation independently of assigned symbol values
Independent measurement Named measured object, method, units and uncertainty Fixes scale or separates remaining value assignments
Provenance Original source record, copied descriptions and shared reconstruction Identifies dependent observations and measurement errors
Reserved check A separately acquired relation or scale-sensitive count Tests the inferred values against information withheld during fitting

The official Louvre record for Susa envelope SB 1940 describes three round pastilles and three rods inside, and three circular and three elongated exterior marks. That balanced mixture supplies one equation relating class counts inside and outside, which our design calculation finds compatible with both a direct and a swapped matching of the two mark shapes to the two token shapes. Unequal mixtures or imprint shapes linked to individual tokens would add distinguishing information. Official accession, source-linked design calculation. The accession and its documented contents identify a specific object for collecting those measurements.

For Indus objects, our Harappa object table restores registrations and excavation identifiers for 22 incised tablets, and the Lothal audit links seal impressions on clay sealings with the textures and forms of the surfaces they were pressed against. Those records supply object provenance and hypotheses about use. A next numerical test needs an independently observed whole–part or measured-quantity relation attached to such an identified object. Collection should preserve original images and physical orientation before the marks are coded. Harappa frame, Lothal frame.

5. Verification, scope and availability

The simulations assume positive commensurate values, stable features and additive relations. Missing members are declared whenever inequalities are used, and the acquisition rules see public costs and uncertainty descriptions. Base collections or base seeds are the independent units; prefixes, encodings, rules and reused condition views share them. Point estimates describe these bounded designs. Mixed commodities, uncertainty about which sign a mark is, and nonadditive functions require new models and controls.

PHYS-01 was checked by exact enumeration, rank over two prime fields and residual tests across 840 records. PHYS-02 arithmetic review checked 3,801 main states and 35,312 distinct saved certificates; the refinement review checked 993 states and 9,766 certificates. In KHI-01, independent scalar sums checked 20,411 source predictions. Exact Gram determinants, row-space checks and checks of saved example solutions covered all 36,000 simulation states, with 512 states independently checked by linear programming. The largest uncertainty in application is the observed relation and measurement model, which these arithmetic checks hold fixed.

KHI-03 checks all 12,000 prefixes and 384,768 exact subset states, keeping candidate solutions and the example points that show positivity. KHI-04 checks 11,200 prefixes, 716,820 subset states and 238,623 held-out residuals. Its first independent checker falsely flagged a variable name in a code scan; the revised checker keeps that flag on record, and the fitting and scoring hashes are unchanged. KHI-05 checks 33,600 deterministic validation records and independently classifies 59,856 intersections of random check subsets with ambiguous optimum sets. Consensus verification, collective verification, validation reviews.

Code, protocols, summaries, generated worlds, certificates and retrospectives are in the private project repository. The acquired database, source pages, manuscript and detailed source-derived vectors remain local with acquisition manifests and hashes.
