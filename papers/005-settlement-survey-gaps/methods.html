Methods and evidence guide

Methods and evidence guide

The paper combines a structured narrative review, an audit of published aggregate counts and proposed investigations. We searched current archaeological survey and excavation studies, environmental records, institutional inventories and statistical-method papers. The final search record describes the scope and what we could actually access. We kept no complete count of search hits or of duplicates screened out.

The final source register has 72 records. For 50 we saved a local copy (a PDF, XML or HTML file with a recorded hash), kept outside the reusable package. Each observation has an R-number, a locator, the depth to which the source was read and a canonical pointer. Some observations were read only in publisher or institutional extracts, and the manuscript says which. The 2026 field participant's account of Mohenjo-daro cites a preliminary report we did not obtain, so we do not adopt its precise foundation dates. The teaching source for the historical total of 1,022 sites cites Possehl (2002), but the acquired excerpt does not identify which work.

The counting units are a locality, a physical mound or scatter, the footprint occupied in one phase, and an investigated deposit. Calendar BCE, calibrated calendar BP, conventional radiocarbon BP and OSL ages keep their different meanings. Historical excavation counts and remote proposals are never combined into a present excavation percentage.

The arithmetic on published counts, the reconstruction of Green's Table 1 and the local repeat-survey comparisons are in run-v3, which read the frozen v2 source register. A comparison checks the source metadata the run used; final package verification extends it through v6. Numerical constants are curated in the preserved script, not extracted from changing prose. Seven separately recorded inline calculations cover feet, hectares and calendar orientation. The claim map links each assertion to its observations and exact output paths.

The region register gives 21 overlapping hand-drawn rectangles with evidence classes, units, comparisons and demotion rules, which state the adequate observations that would downgrade a hypothesis for the units tested. It predicts no site count, site area or artifact total. Four published site points were checked for containment. An actual sampling frame must come from source footprints and mapped landforms. A cell eligible under several labels is selected once from a shared frame. Calibration units of known context and transects along Ganweriwala's known margins (P02) cannot enter random negative comparison draws.

The information, preservation-evidence and feasibility levels are separate editorial judgments of the AI-assisted drafting process. P2 records archaeological survival somewhere in a region, including other periods; it predicts neither survival in an untested parcel nor Indus affiliation. Every region is F0, meaning access is unverified. There is no combined priority score and no expert-rating exercise.

The buried-surface pilot proposes 24 blocks across four landform strata, with profiles nested inside blocks. A block is adequate when spatial coverage, depth and chronological resolution suffice. Descriptive survival reporting uses all adequately resolved blocks. Eight blocks are held out only to evaluate a prediction rule locked before their outcomes are released. The workload has no power calculation, fitted confidence interval or field authorization. A stratum with no adequate blocks remains unresolved.

The first complete draft and all review outputs are preserved. The Codex checks, Claude review 1, limited review of the revised design, Claude review 2 and the response document the changes. Claude reviewed supplied authored packets with tools disabled. The final verification and separate PDF inspection records cover the released files.
