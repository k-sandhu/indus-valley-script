Data

Data: what the project holds, what exists elsewhere, and what each object record contains

Inventory of 12 October 2026. The most recent published count with a stated basis is ICIT's 4,665 inscribed objects carrying 5,650 texts, reported in 2023 (CAA 2023 abstract 76, p. 281). It counts the objects in that database, and no source gives a current worldwide total. Against the local corpus, 1,032 of the 3,620 measured entries of CISI 1 and 2 have no identifier match, some of them aliases or non-script marks (catalogue-coverage.csv), and objects found after the catalogues closed are recorded only in excavation reports. The later volumes, which the project does not hold, continue the numbering: the export cites 1,291 of the 2,040 Mohenjo-daro and Harappa numbers that follow CISI 2 (CISI 3.1) and 172 of 550 numbers for Kalibangan, Chanhu-daro, Nausharo and Rojdi (CISI 3.2), and more than half of the absent numbers lie between marked potsherds (CISI 3 ranges). The project holds a transcription corpus of 4,695 provisional objects and 5,680 faces from 78 site labels, 8,722 photograph crops named by CISI number or by CISI volume and page, complete scans of CISI 1 and CISI 2, the Mahadevan 1977 concordance and the three Helsinki editions. For each object the site is almost always known (99.4%) and a CISI number usually (86.8%), but only 28.8% have all three dimensions, 50.6% a depth, 55.8% a period or phase label, 53.4% a local image, 5.8% a museum number, and none a calendar date. Coverage summary, headline_object_coverage_percent. Data work on 13 October raised three of these figures: objects with any local image to 54.6%, objects with a museum number to 11.5% (542, counting uncertain digits in the CISI 2 pilot; 519 without them), and 1,134 Mohenjo-daro objects gained a field number separated from the excavator's catalogue number and a level with its datum (section 2).

Machine-readable versions of this page are data/MANIFEST.json (not in the public repository), which lists 107 sources (40 held, 14 partly held, 23 project outputs and 30 not held), and python tools/fetch_data.py --list. The measurements come from the data inventory package; numbers attributed to earlier packages link to their reports. Work on these gaps is listed as question cards Q-1001 to Q-1030, ranked in section 5.

Units used throughout. A face is one row of the pinned Lipi export: one inscribed side or view as that export records it. An object is one Lipi family (object_family_id); families group faces by the export's numbering, and physical identity across catalogues has not been adjudicated, so object counts are provisional. A catalogue entry is a numbered item in a printed catalogue such as CISI; an entry missing from the local index can be an alias, an extra photograph, a non-script mark or a genuinely missing object. A value is usable when it is neither empty nor a placeholder (-, --, - -, ?, ??, Unknown, or a zero dimension); None in the motif fields is an asserted absence and counts as usable. These rules are fixed in the protocol.

Excavation tables joined (13 October). Koshy's transcriptions of the Mackay 1938 and Marshall 1931 seal tables were joined to the local objects through the registration strings, which the export writes as the field number followed by the excavator's catalogue number (DK343942 is DK 3439, Mackay No. 42). Of 1,269 table rows, 1,146 joined (1,108 exact, 38 confirmed), 2 are ambiguous and 121 unmatched with reasons; the joins cover 1,134 Mohenjo-daro objects, including 1,122 of the 1,318 Mohenjo-daro seals (85.1%). A random sample of 30 joins had 29 correct against the print and the CISI basic-data excavation number, none wrong and one unresolved. The export already carried most of the tables' values, and they agree on 98.6% of comparable sizes, 99.5% of materials and 99.7% of levels, so coverage of those fields rises only slightly (Mohenjo-daro: both face dimensions +12 objects, material +5, spatial unit +10, depth +2). The tables add, for 1,134 objects, a field number separated from the catalogue number, a reference to the excavator's table and number, a size traced to a printed page in inches (1,091) and a level with its datum (1,101: Mackay's DK datum or SD bench mark, Marshall's depth below the modern surface). The table and the export disagree for 27 objects on the field number (mostly transcription errors in the table), 15 on size, 14 on object type, 5 on material, 5 on findspot, 3 on level and 3 on thickness. The table above stays the 12 October measurement. Join report, coverage.

Museum numbers (13 October). Two packages raised the museum column. The museum harvest joined 87 museum records to local objects (the census's 68 and 19 new), and the CISI 2 basic-data pilot read the museum and registration numbers printed for M-621 to M-947; their gains do not overlap. Objects with a museum number from any source rise from 274 (5.8%, the older export) to 542 (11.5%): seals 450 of 2,239 (20.1%), copper tablets 19.5%, tags and sealings 6.1%, pottery 1.3%; Mohenjo-daro 445 of 1,795 (24.8%), Chanhu-daro 38.9%, Lothal 7.1%, Harappa 1.2%. Counting a CISI 2 museum cell only when it has no uncertain digit gives 519 (11.1%). The CISI 2 values are local until the rights in CISI are settled (combined coverage).

CISI 3 number ranges (13 October). CISI 3.1 (2010) and 3.2 (2019) continue the CISI numbering, and the export already cites many of their numbers. Counting every number from the end of the CISI 1 or 2 range to the highest number in the export, it holds 1,291 of 2,040 for Mohenjo-daro and Harappa (M-1660–2129: 389 of 470; H-1020–2589: 902 of 1,570; 1,788 faces) and 172 of 550 for Kalibangan (101 of 354), Chanhu-daro (36 of 44), Nausharo (32 of 77) and Rojdi (3 of 75). Present numbers fall in blocks of one object kind, and most hundred-number blocks of seals and tablets are 89–100% present. Of the 1,127 absent numbers, 634 lie between present pottery (the longest run is H-2406–2547, 142 numbers, and 180 of the 182 numbers K-191–372 are absent), 317 between present seals, tablets or sealings (H-1205–1258 and M-1931–1952 among them) and 176 between other or mixed neighbours. The class of an absent number is inferred from its neighbours, and the range ends come from the export, so a CISI 3 range may start later, run further or skip numbers. A second implementation from the raw export agreed on every count. Package, range-coverage.csv, absent-numbers.csv.

Line divisions (20 October). The export marks a catalogue line division ('/' in the visual code) on 118 faces, 93 of them square seals. Its visual code lists the lines of a divided legend in the reverse of reading order, and its signs_reading_rtl field is the exact reversal of the visual code on all 118, so the reading-order field starts with the line read first. Three readers (Claude Opus 5.5) viewed the 75 divided faces that have CISI 1 or 2 crops and recorded, face by face, whether the division is confirmed (65 yes, 10 unclear, none contradicted), the sign groups seen and their counts from the top, where the code's first segment sits (lower line 55, upper line 8, left, right or field 4, undetermined 8), the layout of the second group (a wrapped line 57, a group set apart in the field 7, other 11) and a confidence. The line read first is the upper line on 43 of the 46 faces placed with high or medium confidence; H-60, H-97 and K-16 read the lower line first. The other 43 divided faces have no crop: 32 carry CISI 3 numbers and 11 have no CISI number. This is the first face-level layout field in the project's records; no object gains an identifier, dimension, findspot or date. Audit table, summary, package report.

Fields not in the table. Colour is usable for 34.9% of objects, cross-section 29.7%, boss 31.5%, shape 75.1%, room or grid 39.7%, block or house 30.3%, motif (symbol, including asserted absence) 89.4%. No local file carries a calendar date, a radiocarbon sample link, a mass, an item-level image licence or a bibliography reference per object (summary, headline_object_coverage_percent). Face-level percentages are close to the object-level ones because faces repeat their object's metadata.

Placeholders and units. Missing values are written as - (period 3,362 faces, colour 3,672, boss 4,168), -- (room or grid 3,489, block or house 4,167), - - (depth 2,870), ? (completeness 667) and 0 (horizontal size 1,810, vertical 1,681, thickness 3,586) (placeholders.csv). The fields h, v and th hold each size as recorded and the millimetre fields its conversion. Of 17,040 face-axis values, 5,643 were recorded in inches (2,295 to 0.1 in, 3,298 to 0.01 in), 389 in millimetres and 102 in centimetres, mostly without a unit in the string, 4,250 exist only in millimetres (2,501 whole, 1,749 to 0.1 mm), and 6,656 are empty or zero. The millimetre value reproduces the recorded value for all but 38 (23 rounded, 15 different); the 228 inch–millimetre mismatches counted on 12 October are 180 values recorded in millimetres, 42 in centimetres and 6 true mismatches (dimension summary). Thickness is printed as a range such as 5 - 9 on 530 faces; this inventory counts a range as usable, and the census's plain-number rule gives 4,441 faces with any size and 1,151 with all three, as the census reported. Depths mix feet (2,557 usable faces), metres (72) and other forms (181, with 50 surface finds), measured from different datums at different excavations (cross-source.json, lipi_inch_vs_mm_fields; summary, flags).

Conflicts. Among the 902 objects with more than one face, the faces never disagree on an object-level field; they differ only on the face-level fields completeness (107 objects) and direction (94) (conflicts.json). Against the older website export (2,375 shared CISI keys), site agrees for all keys once the spelling Chanhujo-daro is aliased to Chanhu-daro, material agrees wherever both have a value (2,029), and sizes agree on every comparable axis for 2,340 objects and differ for 9, with 20 more giving the lower end of a Lipi thickness range; the 206 differences counted on 12 October were mostly (199) Lipi thickness ranges that the comparison rule did not read as numbers (dimension summary). Mayig and Lipi agree on site for all 179 Mayig faces. For the 278 Mohenjo-daro objects in M-621 to M-947 with an excavation number in both Lipi and the CISI 2 basic data, Lipi's excavation-idno equals the printed number for 40 and, for 182 with four-digit FC concordance numbers, is the printed number followed by the FC number without its first digit; 12 differ only in the area prefix (9 add a DK subarea letter, 3 drop DK-), 4 are records crossed between two entries (M-653 with M-655, M-656 with M-707), where measurements of the seals on the CISI 2 plates show CISI 2's table to be wrong and Lipi right (swap check), 2 differ where the page confirms CISI 2, 3 are truncated or carry another suffix, and 35 cannot be compared because the printed cell is uncertain (CISI 2 pilot). The appended digits are the excavator's catalogue number: for Mohenjo-daro the FC number is 1000 plus Marshall's 1931 number or 2000 plus Mackay's 1938 number, which holds for 216 of 219 objects joined in both packages, the exceptions being three of the four objects CISI 2 crossed (cross-check). The museum review found two Lothal faults of the same kind: family 2067 (L-122) carries the excavation number CISI 1 gives to L-100, and family 2084 (L-142) carries L-166's (independent check). These are derivatives of shared upstream records, so agreement does not verify a value against the object. The census's comparison with museum records found one site, four material, five dimension and nine sign-count disagreements among 61 joined records (join-summary.json, comparisons).

Images. The 8,722 local image files are photograph crops cut from the CISI 1 and 2 plates with their printed captions. The face-level index of 13 October (face-image-links.csv) parses every name, corrects 26 names against the printed caption, reads the caption of 231 of the 299 files named by volume and page, and places files on their plate page (CISI 1 PDF page = printed + 36, CISI 2 = printed + 35). Any image: 2,562 objects (54.6%) and 3,003 faces (52.9%); a photograph of the face's own side: 2,159 objects (46.0%; seals 68.3%) and 2,181 faces (38.4%); an impression of it: 1,489 objects (31.7%). Face-side figures cover objects with one face row and 21 objects verified by viewing; the 881 other objects with several faces have their files linked without a side. A random sample of 60 links had 60 correct identities and kinds. Of the 2,677 faces without an image, 661 have no CISI number and 1,926 have numbers beyond the ranges of CISI 1 and 2. Dholavira's objects lack CISI keys and therefore images (0.9%). The 12 October inventory's exact-key count was 2,509 objects (53.4%) (coverage.csv). The line-division audit of 20 October confirmed the index's readings of four crops named by volume and page and found four points it does not record: M-663_imp_a.jpg and M-663_a_7g.jpg are byte-identical, M-1187_A_M-1183_B_M-1189_a.jpg is captioned M-1188 and M-1189, the crop H-603 (1) a shows a different object from the H-603 (2) seal, and M-1202 carries a second inscription on edge C (data note).

Museum records. The census saved 114 online records of original objects (National Museum New Delhi 76, Indian Museum Kolkata 18, British Museum 6, Penn 5, Metropolitan 5, Louvre 4) and confirmed 68 joins to local objects; of the 114, all have an accession and material, 108 a date label, 89 dimensions and 13 a bibliography (field-completeness.csv). On 13 October the 46 open records were revisited against the CISI 1 basic data read by eye, the Marshall 1931 and Mackay 1938 seal tables and the numbers written on objects in CISI photographs: 16 are now confirmed (one of them, Indian Museum 10377/A7252/C.2803 as M-1809, found by the independent check), 21 have candidate sets, 8 are unmatched and 1 is uninscribed. Newly harvested records added three joins (V&A IS.8:1-1951 to CISI M-1727, British Museum 1947,0416.7 to M-1883, British Museum 123208 to family 6000), so 87 museum records are joined. Sixteen local objects gain their first museum number, and six newly joined families gain a dimension axis, two of them their first. 6. How data is stored and fetched

Git tracks the project's own code, protocols, aggregate results, source records and a small set of reviewed derivatives with clear rights (the chart atlas and attributed crops, for example). Everything under data/ is ignored except data/README.md and data/MANIFEST.json, and so, apart from listed exceptions, are PDFs, images, HTML and text files anywhere in the tree; the rules are in .gitignore and the reasons in REDISTRIBUTION.md. A fresh clone therefore has the manifest and the code but none of the acquired corpora.

data/MANIFEST.json (not in the public repository) lists every source in this page, held or not, with its status (held, partly-held, not-held, project-output), location (tracked, local-only, hf-private, external), version or commit, size, identifiers, rights status, SHA256 of key files and an acquisition route. tools/fetch_data.py reads it:

python tools/fetch_data.py --list [--status held|partly-held|not-held|project-output]
python tools/fetch_data.py --show lipi
python tools/fetch_data.py --fetch lipi --dry-run
python tools/fetch_data.py --fetch lipi            # clones into data/raw/<today>-fetch/lipi/ and verifies hashes
python tools/fetch_data.py --verify cisi-1         # hashes the existing local copy, read-only

--fetch handles sources with a public pinned location: a git commit (Lipi, Mayig, the website export, the epachayan tables) or a public file with a recorded hash (the 1980 and 1982 Helsinki editions, CISI 1, CISI 2, Mahadevan 1977 and others). It writes only into a new directory and never touches an existing copy; for every other source it prints the acquisition steps. A hash mismatch keeps the file for inspection and reports failure.

New acquisitions go into a new dated directory, data/raw/YYYY-MM-DD-<package>/, with an acquisition record giving URL, retrieval time, bytes, SHA256, page count and the observed rights notice (REDISTRIBUTION.md, "Asset record to keep"). The contributor records the new source, its manifest fields and the coverage change in their own package. After merging, a maintainer adds the source to the manifest (through build_manifest.py and not_held.py, or by hand) and updates this page's tables and numbers in the same change. Coverage numbers are recomputed with coverage.py into a new dated output directory; earlier runs stay as they are.
