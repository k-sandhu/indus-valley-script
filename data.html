Data

Data: what the project holds, what exists elsewhere, and what each object record contains

Inventory of 12 October 2026. The most recent published count with a stated basis is ICIT's 4,665 inscribed objects carrying 5,650 texts, reported in 2023 (CAA 2023 abstract 76, p. 281). It counts the objects in that database, and no source gives a current worldwide total. Against the local corpus, 1,032 of the 3,620 measured entries of CISI 1 and 2 have no identifier match, some of them aliases or non-script marks (catalogue-coverage.csv), and objects found after the catalogues closed are recorded only in excavation reports. The project holds a transcription corpus of 4,695 provisional objects and 5,680 faces from 78 site labels, 8,722 photograph crops named by CISI number or by CISI volume and page, complete scans of CISI 1 and CISI 2, the Mahadevan 1977 concordance and the three Helsinki editions. For each object the site is almost always known (99.4%) and a CISI number usually (86.8%), but only 28.8% have all three dimensions, 50.6% a depth, 55.8% a period or phase label, 53.4% a local image, 5.8% a museum number, and none a calendar date. Coverage summary, headline_object_coverage_percent.

Machine-readable versions of this page are data/MANIFEST.json (not in the public repository), which lists 85 sources (33 held, 9 partly held, 9 project outputs and 34 not held), and python tools/fetch_data.py --list. The measurements come from the data inventory package; numbers attributed to earlier packages link to their reports. Work on these gaps is listed as question cards Q-1001 to Q-1030, ranked in section 5.

Units used throughout. A face is one row of the pinned Lipi export: one inscribed side or view as that export records it. An object is one Lipi family (object_family_id); families group faces by the export's numbering, and physical identity across catalogues has not been adjudicated, so object counts are provisional. A catalogue entry is a numbered item in a printed catalogue such as CISI; an entry missing from the local index can be an alias, an extra photograph, a non-script mark or a genuinely missing object. A value is usable when it is neither empty nor a placeholder (-, --, - -, ?, ??, Unknown, or a zero dimension); None in the motif fields is an asserted absence and counts as usable. These rules are fixed in the protocol.

Fields not in the table. Colour is usable for 34.9% of objects, cross-section 29.7%, boss 31.5%, shape 75.1%, room or grid 39.7%, block or house 30.3%, motif (symbol, including asserted absence) 89.4%. No local file carries a calendar date, a radiocarbon sample link, a mass, an item-level image licence or a bibliography reference per object (summary, headline_object_coverage_percent). Face-level percentages are close to the object-level ones because faces repeat their object's metadata.

Placeholders and units. Missing values are written as - (period 3,362 faces, colour 3,672, boss 4,168), -- (room or grid 3,489, block or house 4,167), - - (depth 2,870), ? (completeness 667) and 0 (horizontal size 1,810, vertical 1,681, thickness 3,586) (placeholders.csv). The millimetre sizes are mostly conversions: 5,206 of 5,434 paired values equal the inch field times 25.4 within 0.6 mm, so their precision is that of the original inch record. Thickness is printed as a range such as 5 - 9 on 530 faces; this inventory counts a range as usable, and the census's plain-number rule gives 4,441 faces with any size and 1,151 with all three, as the census reported. Depths mix feet (2,557 usable faces), metres (72) and other forms (181, with 50 surface finds), measured from different datums at different excavations (cross-source.json, lipi_inch_vs_mm_fields; summary, flags).

Conflicts. Among the 902 objects with more than one face, the faces never disagree on an object-level field; they differ only on the face-level fields completeness (107 objects) and direction (94) (conflicts.json). Against the older website export (2,375 shared CISI keys), site agrees for all keys once the spelling Chanhujo-daro is aliased to Chanhu-daro, material agrees wherever both have a value (2,029), and sizes agree for 2,018 objects and differ for 206. Mayig and Lipi agree on site for all 179 Mayig faces. These are derivatives of shared upstream records, so agreement does not verify a value against the object. The census's comparison with museum records found one site, four material, five dimension and nine sign-count disagreements among 61 joined records (join-summary.json, comparisons).

Images. The 8,722 local image files are photograph crops whose names give a CISI number and side or a CISI volume 1 or 2 page. The index names 3,318 CISI keys, all with existing files; 2,152 files are outside the index, 297 of them named only by volume and page. By exact CISI key 2,509 objects (53.4%) have an image, and 2,483 have at least as many side labels in their file names as the export's side count, an approximate measure of complete side coverage. Dholavira's objects lack CISI keys and therefore images. The October 2 readiness audit counted 2,507 objects under an exact raw key; the difference of two comes from whitespace trimming here (summary, images).

Museum records. The census saved 114 online records of original objects (National Museum New Delhi 76, Indian Museum Kolkata 18, British Museum 6, Penn 5, Metropolitan 5, Louvre 4) and confirmed 68 joins to local objects by published concordance, excavation number or plate; 46 remain open. Of the 114, all have an accession and material, 108 a date label, 89 dimensions and 13 a bibliography (field-completeness.csv).

Studies with object-level data, and recent material

Jamison, Uesugi and Yamaguchi 2023, "Unicorns and seals: New multivariate approaches to exploring patterned stylistic variation in the Indus Civilization" (Archaeological Research in Asia 36, 100486) is under Elsevier copyright, and its supplementary files were not reachable (W45). A public GitHub repository (dmahesh2869/harappan-seal-analysis) republishes per-seal unicorn attributes that a search snippet traces to Jamison's 2017 dissertation, with no licence asserted (W46). Konasukawa and Koiso 2018, "The size of Indus seals and its significance", is a purchase (W47). These are the routes to carving-style and size data for seals (Q-1025).

The 12 October searches found no newly reported inscribed Indus seal or new corpus for 2024–2026 and no newer Mahadevan concordance (W61). Tamil Nadu announced a US$1 million decipherment prize on 5 January 2025, with no data release found (W57, news snippets). Recent derived datasets on Hugging Face and Zenodo (W51, W53) repackage the Lipi, website or Mayig exports or trace published photographs; they add no independent objects. Dixit and colleagues (2025, Journal of Computer Applications in Archaeology) report 963 annotated seal images whose public release was not verified (W52). Objects published after the CISI site ranges closed appear only in excavation reports and Indian Archaeology – A Review; collecting them is Q-1026.

4. Target object record

The project aims for one record per physical object, with separate rows for faces, images, identifiers and contexts and a value_status for every field (observed, catalogued, inferred, unknown, conflicting, not-applicable, with a reason for unknowns). This follows the observation model in context-data-plan.md and schema version 2 of the object ledger pilot. Each assertion carries its source, page or record locator and observer. Current coverage is the object-level share in the local corpus unless stated.

5. Acquisition and enrichment priorities

The ranking weighs three things: how many objects gain a field the open questions need, whether a conjecture or finding is waiting on the result, and how cheaply the work can start. Work on sources already held comes first, because the largest gains (museum and excavation numbers from the CISI basic data, excavation tables for Mohenjo-daro, images for half the corpus) need no purchase or permission. Acquisitions that depend on the owner's decision (purchases, data requests to ICIT, HARP or the Lipi maintainer) follow, with their cards preparing the request.

6. How data is stored and fetched

Git tracks the project's own code, protocols, aggregate results, source records and a small set of reviewed derivatives with clear rights (the chart atlas and attributed crops, for example). Everything under data/ is ignored except data/README.md and data/MANIFEST.json, and so, apart from listed exceptions, are PDFs, images, HTML and text files anywhere in the tree; the rules are in .gitignore and the reasons in REDISTRIBUTION.md. A fresh clone therefore has the manifest and the code but none of the acquired corpora.

data/MANIFEST.json (not in the public repository) lists every source in this page, held or not, with its status (held, partly-held, not-held, project-output), location (tracked, local-only, hf-private, external), version or commit, size, identifiers, rights status, SHA256 of key files and an acquisition route. tools/fetch_data.py reads it:

python tools/fetch_data.py --list [--status held|partly-held|not-held|project-output]
python tools/fetch_data.py --show lipi
python tools/fetch_data.py --fetch lipi --dry-run
python tools/fetch_data.py --fetch lipi            # clones into data/raw/<today>-fetch/lipi/ and verifies hashes
python tools/fetch_data.py --verify cisi-1         # hashes the existing local copy, read-only

--fetch handles sources with a public pinned location: a git commit (Lipi, Mayig, the website export, the epachayan tables) or a public file with a recorded hash (the 1980 and 1982 Helsinki editions, CISI 1, CISI 2, Mahadevan 1977 and others). It writes only into a new directory and never touches an existing copy; for every other source it prints the acquisition steps. A hash mismatch keeps the file for inspection and reports failure.

New acquisitions go into a new dated directory, data/raw/YYYY-MM-DD-<package>/, with an acquisition record giving URL, retrieval time, bytes, SHA256, page count and the observed rights notice (REDISTRIBUTION.md, "Asset record to keep"). Add the source to the manifest (through build_manifest.py and not_held.py, or by hand) and update this page's tables and numbers in the same change. Coverage numbers are recomputed with coverage.py into a new dated output directory; earlier runs stay as they are.
