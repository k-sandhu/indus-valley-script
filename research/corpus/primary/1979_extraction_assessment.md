# 1979 primary corpus: acquisition and extraction assessment

Koskenniemi and Parpola's *Corpus of Texts in the Indus Script* (1979) was acquired from its [official University of Helsinki repository entry](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/corpus-of-texts-in-the-indus-script/). The entry identifies the final published PDF under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The repository cover supplies the [persistent handle](http://hdl.handle.net/10138/342677). Its local provenance JSON records the unchanged file hash and the browser-download route.

This is independent primary editorial material: the preface divides data processing to Kimmo Koskenniemi and sign/text revision to Asko Parpola. It is an interim edition, preceding the 1980 additions/revisions and 1982 concordance. It should not be represented as the final 1982 corpus.

The file has 180 PDF pages, including a repository cover, and 179 book pages. A substantial OCR layer is present. Ordinary prose is recoverable with caution; signs are often misread as punctuation, Latin letters or digits. Rendered PDF pages 20 and 25 demonstrate the limitation directly. The corpus prints **drawn glyphs**, with an inscription ID and compact archaeological classification codes beside each text. It does not print a numeric sign-ID sequence next to every inscription.

The primary sign chart is usable as a visual code reference, but automatic text extraction cannot directly produce a trustworthy coded corpus. A future digitization should segment the graphic texts, match or manually annotate each glyph against the sign chart, retain damage/uncertainty and side boundaries, and validate independently before analysis. Extracting the OCR glyph strings as tokens would introduce artificial signs and false sequences.

The chart has two number systems. As explained on printed p15 (PDF p16), the lower number is a stable Finnish corpus sign code, while the upper number is the rank: graphic variants share rank. A many-to-one class conversion must preserve this distinction. The chart includes uncertain underlined versions. No deterministic Wells-to-Finnish conversion is asserted here.

The dashed lines beneath some **sign-chart drawings** are editorial uncertainty underlines, not part of the glyphs. This was verified against the primary pages: section 5.4 on printed p15 (PDF p16) explicitly includes underlined versions and refers to section 6.6; section 6.6 on printed p17 (PDF p18) defines the underline as uncertainty, ranging from slight doubt through wholly reconstructed lost content. The chart and corpus use the same auxiliary convention. Source renders are preserved as `koskenniemi1979_p16.png` and `koskenniemi1979_p18.png`; the exact structured source references are in [koskenniemi1979_definitions.json](../../../data/normalized/koskenniemi1979_definitions.json). A glyph template may strip the dashed annotation for shape matching while retaining its uncertainty status and stable code separately. It must not interpret the line as a glyph stroke or automatically merge the underlined code with a different code.

The physical-boundary conventions also matter. Printed p16 (PDF p17) explains that blanks can represent line or object-side boundaries and that multiple original lines/sides may be printed together if space permits. A single printed row therefore does not automatically equal one original inscription line. Missing, inaccessible and uncertain signs are distinguished by graphic auxiliary markers that OCR does not preserve reliably.

The 1980 *Documentation and Duplicates* volume has now also been acquired from a public University of Helsinki file link. Its printed p13 corrects an important limitation of the 1979 layout: some original physical boundaries were indicated only by the classification code or were omitted based on parallel texts, rather than always marked by a blank. Therefore, neither raster rows nor blank gaps alone recover every original line and side. The revised table on p14 adds code 1 for apparently intentional gaps in a one-line inscription and codes 4 and 8 for four lines and four sides. A putative blank may itself reflect lost signs. The structured, visually verified rules are preserved in [koskenniemi1980_definitions.json](../../../data/normalized/koskenniemi1980_definitions.json).

The same primary volume gives an explicit duplicate methodology on pp54–57. It distinguishes exact repetition from conservative compatibility of fragments, and processes duplicates both within object-type/iconography groups (its “strict” criterion) and across the entire material (its “loose” criterion). Fragment compatibility does not establish missing content. Different physical objects carrying one text also remain distinct archaeological observations. These controls are established prior methodology, not a novel result of this project.

The 1982 *Concordance* was acquired separately, with CC BY 4.0 explicitly linked on its official portal entry. That PDF is image-only: native text extraction returns page separators and cannot directly supply numeric sequences. Acquisition manifests preserve each edition's separate hash and license status; the open license of 1979 or 1982 is not assumed for the 1980 file.

The downloadable primary editions are useful reusable acquisitions and a route to a future independent digitization. They do not yet provide independently extracted machine-readable sequences for the current experiment. Root's template prototype is being evaluated separately; this assessment does not certify its outputs.
