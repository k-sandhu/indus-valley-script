# Corpus acquisition and source audit

This work provides reproducible observational data, primary-source acquisitions, and explicit limits on interpretation. It does **not** provide a decipherment, a language identification, a phonetic value, or a confirmed semantic reading.

## Orientation, unknowns, and crosswalks

The raw code lists are **visual left-to-right layouts** in the normalized right-to-left presentation. Computational reading sequences reverse the lists. Original seals are mirrored relative to their impressions. Fuls's catalogue direction field is retained as supplied; Chapter2.2 expressly takes seal texts from their impressions, and Chapter3 displays sequences for right-to-left reading. Its fragment example `]368-632-706-140+` begins with the rightmost140, confirming that the string is not already initial-sign-first. This was also checked against official Fuls2010 search examples, the labelled M1088 photograph, and CISI1 original/impression pairs. Multiline and multisided layouts require separate line/side treatment; reversing a concatenated face does not reconstruct their original traversal. `orientation_clarification.json` resolves the shorter metadata phrase without changing any normalized data.

`000` is eroded or illegible content and `999` is a physical blank. Mayig `P000` denotes a damaged or lost **section**, not necessarily one missing glyph. Its default damage/uncertainty values can both be zero, so the marker must be excluded explicitly. After this correction, 94 Mayig faces have no P000 and zero annotator damage/uncertainty. These flags remain subjective catalogue judgments. M141 has two primary visual rows even though the annotator assigned its tokens to line1.

`mayig_wells_class_crosswalk.json` contains 396 P-class records and 498 Wells IDs. Every P→W array is a local candidate compatibility set from the annotator's feature schema. Feature vectors can distinguish variants within a P class, but a whole-class array deliberately ignores that information. Overlapping arrays are **not a transitive equivalence relation**. They support coarse candidate-event scoring, not a certified exact fine-code conversion. P385 collapses W817/W861, P086 includes W390/W405, and P316 includes W705/W706; apparent substitution of such pairs can reflect graphic classification rather than linguistic interchangeability.

## Duplication and cross-catalogue comparison

Numeric IDs must not be compared across export versions: inserted rows renumber later entries. The cross-corpus audit joins by physical CISI base, keeps sides separate where possible, and compares sequences only after that join. In the older export, 2,375 rows have a nonempty CISI identity, 2,374 find a Lipi object, and 2,243 match at least one Lipi face exactly. Ignoring eroded `000` raises compatibility to 2,293, but does not restore text. All 179 Mayig objects find a Lipi CISI identity; 100 whole faces are compatible with the broad Wells arrays, including 65 of the 94 clear faces. Any-face matching does not adjudicate side identity.

An additional audit of the pinned Lipi export found no simple physical CISI base assigned to multiple numeric families. This supports use of the numeric family within that pinned export, while missing or compound source identities still require caution. Repeated whole texts on different artifacts are not independent textual evidence, though the artifacts remain distinct archaeological observations. CISI1 `bis`, `ter`, and `quater` designate additional photographs of the **same side**; lowercase labels designate impressions. The official CISI1 scan also duplicates every page as a second identical book scan, so pages432–862 are not additional material.

The 1979 edition prints plotted glyphs rather than numeric sequences for each text. Its upper chart number is a graphic rank; the lower number is a stable Finnish sign code. Dashed underlines in the chart and corpus are uncertainty annotations, not glyph strokes. The 1980 documentation corrects some original physical boundaries, which cannot always be recovered from raster row or blank positions. It already provides strict and loose duplicate methods, including conservative matching of fragments. These are prior methods, not novel discoveries here. The 1982 scan is image-only. Native OCR glyph strings are not accepted as coded inscriptions. A separate visual-template prototype is evaluated in `research/visual_digitization/`; this report does not certify its output.

The structured conventions are in `koskenniemi1979_definitions.json` and `koskenniemi1980_definitions.json`. This corpus agent manually read 175 numeric chart cells on PDF20, columns0–4, preserving both upper ranks and lower stable codes. The 140 new cells are in `primary/chart_complete/manual_columns1_4.json`; every row was reinspected at12x and selected ambiguous digits at20x. `column0_reinspection.json` explicitly corrects the initial col0 row4 reading585 to586, preserves the original reference, and flags14 dashed auxiliary underlines. Row34's solid platform is part of its glyph, not an auxiliary underline. All chart labels were read without viewing the prototype's OCR, font predictions or matched inscriptions. These qualitative AI annotations are not calibrated transcription accuracy or an accepted coded glyph corpus.

`primary/font_row_audit/manual_reference.json` adds a ten-printed-row source-font pilot on PDF30:61 drawn units,57 single-code qualitative assignments and four ambiguous bar fine codes. This is a reference for digitization of the1979 editorial font, not a new ancient reading. The full reference was released after root froze predictions, but a partial row1020 description was disclosed before freezing and must remain visible in evaluation reporting. No original physical row/side boundaries or semantic values were inferred.

The subsequent fresh20 pilot uses seed20261015 to select20 further PDF30 rows. Its full source-only reference in `primary/font_fresh20/manual_reference.json` was withheld until root reported predictions frozen. All selected rows remain:96 drawn units including5 modern editorial auxiliary units, with two auxiliary-only rectangle rows and4 rows containing loss/inaccessible spans. Seven glyph units and3 auxiliary units preserve fine-code ambiguity. These are drawn-unit counts rather than ancient text lengths. No fresh20 machine candidates were consulted.

After freezing and reference release, a source-only acquisition audit found that18 of20 fixed crops enter the target row's upper ink:14 clear losses and4 marginal raster-edge cuts. Fourteen also contain the following printed row's upper ink. Only1023/1024 preserve the entire target with margin. All20 stored PNGs exactly match their frozen crop metadata. The fixed40.25px row pitch drifts against roughly39.6px source spacing at2x, so the frozen errors cannot be attributed purely to graphic-shape generalization. `primary/font_fresh20/geometry_audit/` preserves per-row context, hashes, findings and clean source-only crop recommendations for an explicitly labelled subsequent development sensitivity. Historical truth and frozen scores remain unchanged.

Subsequent original-source numerical reinspection, prompted by cross-annotator comparison, corrects PDF20 c2r1 from359 to369 and c4r13 from350 to360. At c4r21, literature prefers465 while this annotator still favours486; this annotator retains the candidate set `[465,485,486]`. Exact fresh original crops and an explicit overlay are in `primary/chart_complete/subsequent_adjudication.json`. Historical gold and font-pilot references remain unchanged; these post-comparison adjudications must not silently alter frozen evaluation.

The final consolidated numerical chart and atlas were independently checked for coverage and provenance in `primary/chart_complete/consolidated_atlas_audit.json`:687 expected cells present once,16 lower-code ambiguities retained, no current source/adjudication mismatch, and all687 PNGs exactly matching their claimed original-PDF4x crops. Source, historical chart and fresh20 gold pins verify. This checks source-table/image correspondence, not expert numerical-label correctness or palaeographic accuracy. Context crops may contain neighbouring ink.

## Primary photographic and drawing checks

`plate_audit/` records25 preselected Mayig objects using the official IGNCA CISI1 scan. The seed and initial frame are preserved, including the later-discovered P000 and layout issues. The photographs were located by caption and include originals and normalized impressions. Seventeen of24 source-complete objects are compatible across the two catalogues; seven have segmentation, allograph, crosswalk, or damage discrepancies. This is a qualitative AI audit, not expert palaeography or a transcription-accuracy estimate. The first blind shape pass made mistakes, which are preserved separately from catalogue-informed adjudications.

`fish_strokes/` covers13 physical objects and14 fish-adjacent stroke-pair events. Ten clear short pairs, two tall pairs, and one short pair with grouping ambiguity are visible in the source photographs. M91 has both short and tall paired bars in one inscription. M140 exposes a Mayig tall-versus-source-short discrepancy; M62 later exposes the converse catalogue disagreement. The observed height categories are graphic facts, not assigned numerical or phonetic values.

The W176/W904 candidate contrast has one primary photographed M289 frame and a derivative H697A photograph. A newly acquired primary-author reproduction in Parpola2011 Fig15 independently confirms **H697B's reverse photograph**, U motif plus two tall bars, but does not show H697A. Other contrast members still lack independently acquired primary photographic plates. The exact Ad3/H2139 frame was already drawn in RMRL2025 Fig6; novelty cannot be claimed for its discovery. Fine Wells variants remain unadjudicated.

For H2238, excavators' Figure4 specimen18 identifies H95-2613/6560-01. Its middle face visibly shows three short bars plus a diamond-like unit, while the opposite face's dense hatching does not securely resolve three versus four long bars. The primary HARP *Three groups of tablets* photograph separately shows four bars in Group1 and three in Group2 next to a two-armed joined motif, but has no object IDs. This confirms a **cohort-level** count contrast and cannot be transferred to H2238's individual opposite face.

## Missing-slot audit and negative results

All17 Mayig faces with one P000 were inspected against primary photographs. None securely establishes exactly one missing glyph. An initial tentative M169 nomination was downgraded after a second source-width inspection and the historical Lipi segmentation of two unknown units; the original blind judgment is preserved along with its adjudication.

A second exhaustive frame selected seven Lipi M1–M650 rows with one internal W000. Five have plates in acquired CISI1, whose original Mohenjo-daro photographic sequence ends at M620. M61 contains one bounded surviving motif, but Mayig already proposes W081 with uncertainty90. M62's unknown position visibly preserves a U motif and Mayig already assigns W700. These are respectively an uncertain historical postdiction and a catalogue-recoding candidate. No new unattested exact restoration was certified. The remaining available cases have broad or unbounded damage; M636/M646 primary plates were not acquired.

No open independently coded full EBUDS/Mahadevan/Wells corpus was acquired from the searched primary supplements. ICIT bulk access required registration. Primary publications and public images were acquired through ordinary public links or visible browser download controls; failed direct requests and the absence of accessible source data are recorded. No author was contacted and no restricted account was created. This corpus work advances reproducibility and source constraints while leaving linguistic decipherment unresolved.
