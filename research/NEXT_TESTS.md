# Prioritized tests that could falsify or strengthen these results

This is a research agenda, not a decipherment claim. Fix the evaluation protocol and competing explanations before examining new answers. Preserve negative cases and historical scores.

## 1. Validate acquisition on new source rows

**Unresolved result:** The fresh20 reference was withheld until predictions froze, but 18 crops cut into the target row's upper ink and 14 mix in the following row. Re-cropping these revealed rows is development. The 687-cell numerical atlas verifies source correspondence, not expert glyph transcription.

Freeze a crop algorithm, chart version and matcher, then select new rows from additional source pages. An independent annotator must work from complete original context, without model candidates. Retain loss graphics, uncertainty underlines and ambiguous fine codes. Cross-link every evaluated text to its original artifact, side and line; a 1979 plotted row can combine physical boundaries. Report crop purity, drawn-unit segmentation, graphic candidate-set accuracy, candidate-set size and exact-code accuracy separately.

**Proposed success criterion:** No detected truncation or neighbouring-row contamination, at least 95% faithful drawn-unit segmentation and 95% graphic candidate coverage on the retained sample, with confidence intervals and no improvement obtained by expanding candidate sets indiscriminately. **Failure:** Any crop defect or accuracy below those prespecified bars. Success establishes usable acquisition for the sampled source regime; it establishes neither semantic reading nor general accuracy across the full corpus.

## 2. Obtain physical anchors before numerical glosses

**Unresolved result:** H904's Vats (1940) no. 477/J213(D) photographs support a coarse contrast between three long strokes beside a U and two long strokes beside circles, but its identity with Fuls (2020) historical ICIT 3786 remains unconfirmed. Current numeric IDs must not be joined across versions. M1383's alleged 82 fragments and 15–20 bangles lack the acquired primary inventory/refit evidence; general manufacturing-package counts are not its artifact count.

Recover the exact publication/catalogue cross-links, complete opposing faces, original scale and inventory/refit records. Have blind readers count observable strokes and independently counted physical units before applying a proposed equivalence. Fit numerical coefficients on separate anchors, then predict held-out counts or measurements with declared tolerances and competing coefficient models.

**Success:** Verified object identity and independently measured units discriminate the proposed model from alternatives on held-out artifacts. **Failure:** Identity cannot be established, fragment counts do not determine object counts, or predictions require adjusting units/coefficients after disclosure. Graphic counts alone establish graphic facts; numerical semantics require the external predictive test.

## 3. Test rare-glyph restoration prospectively

**Unresolved result:** The photo audit certified no new unattested exactly-one-glyph gap. M61 has an existing uncertain reading; M62 is catalogue recoding. P000 and broad damage cannot certify one missing slot.

First calibrate on independently transcribed complete photographs with one masked slot, stratified by sign rarity and blocked by physical object and repeated whole-text family. Compare against frequency, positional and preserved-template baselines. Then nominate prospective damaged cases from photographs alone, requiring a visibly bounded one-glyph span and surviving ink constraints; freeze candidate sets before acquiring an improved photograph or independent reading.

**Success:** Candidate sets show their declared coverage at useful size and outperform the baselines; prospective candidates survive independent photo adjudication. **Failure:** Extent permits multiple glyphs, historical readings supplied the answer, or rare-sign coverage fails. Success establishes calibrated graphic restoration, not meanings or word boundaries.

## 4. Require risky external predictions for meaning or sound

**Unresolved result:** Positional structure, archaeological-context prediction and candidate-compatible transcription agreement constrain a notation system but do not identify its language or lexicon. Catalogue aliases and shared source photographs further limit independence.

State a semantic or phonetic hypothesis precisely enough to predict unseen evidence: for example independently identified goods, quantities, administrative roles, or a historically justified name/spelling in an external inscription. Freeze the mapping and tolerance, identify alternative readings that predict differently, and adjudicate without access to the model answer.

**Success:** Multiple independent, previously withheld anchors support the same mapping, including cases where alternatives predict failure. **Failure:** The reading only reproduces sign order, relies on adjustable rebuses/unit assignments, or changes after observing the anchor. Only this stronger result could support a decipherment advance beyond structural constraints.
