# Fine-context association extension

Exploratory plan recorded before similarity test outcomes, 2026-10-01 23:37:36 UTC.

Question: among complete Mohenjo-daro seals, do different inscriptions from the same recorded excavation block/house have more sign-sequence similarity than those from other blocks of the same excavation area, phase/period, and length?

The available metadata contain approximately 731 seals with nonempty block-house fields. Values also include street or trench descriptions; the unit is therefore a *recorded excavation context*, not an independently confirmed household or owner.

Primary similarity: fraction of same-position signs, excluding exact identical sequences as pairwise comparisons. Full inscription weighting will downweight each repeated sequence by its observed frequency. Report a sensitivity restricted to inscriptions observed on only one physical object and a conservative three-allograph collapse.

Conditional randomization permutes recorded block labels among objects within excavation area, broad period, and length. This preserves sign strings, group sizes, period/length/area composition, and object class. It does not control every workshop, preservation, chronological, or excavation-selection process.

A positive association constrains spatial distribution. It cannot identify a personal name, household name, deity, guild, or commodity. Exact repeated texts alone are not sufficient evidence for a new lexicon. All results remain exploratory and require a separate corpus before being represented as a semantic advance.
