# Copper reverse devices: graphic identity and inferential limits

Prepared 1 October 2026. Sign identifiers below belong to several incompatible catalogues. `P124` means Parpola's 1994/2005 sign-list number, whereas Mayig's internal `P118` is its own class identifier. The latter is **not** Parpola sign 118. A raw Wells number, a Mayig class, and a published broad sign family are not interchangeable identity statements.

## Primary disagreement

[Parpola 2005](https://www.harappa.com/sites/default/files/pdf/indusscript%282%29.pdf), printed p.55 Fig.3, depicts seven P124 forms with object examples. His prose on pp.57–59 treats internal forms as variants and proposes a divine-name rebus. [Mahadevan's 1997 review](https://www.harappa.com/script/maha15.html), Fig.IV, disputes the merger: the outer additions resemble either a leaf (P118) or a person (P13), while internal crab-like forms have feet (P88a) or lack feet (P88e). He says the bare internal forms occur in different seal-text contexts. This is an author disagreement, not a settled independent-sign determination.

| Parpola form | Exact example in 2005 Fig.3 | Outer addition in the published drawing | Internal drawing | What is licensed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 124a | H598 | Two solid leaf/heart tops | Joined body with crossing short strokes | Distinct graphic form within author's family |
| 124b | L11 | Two solid leaf/heart tops | Rotated body and short strokes | Rotation is visibly recorded; functional equivalence still inferred |
| 124c | M1555 | Two outlined leaves | Oval plus two right-facing prongs; no crossed feet | Do not merge with a solely from broad P124 label |
| 124d | M1566 | Two outlined leaves | Oval plus two right-facing prongs; no crossed feet | Comparable c/d inner shape in the drawing |
| 124e | M604 | Two outlined leaves | Two detached upper chevrons and two lower curved/angular elements | Copper C6 example; differs from continuous c/d body |
| 124f | M600 | Left outlined leaf preserved; right half shaded missing | Preserved detached upper and lower angular strokes | Copper C5 fragment; incomplete comparison with e |
| 124g | M1563 | Paired short strokes down two stems, resembling anthropomorphic arms | Separated curved/angular elements | Outer element differs visibly from leaf-ended forms |

These descriptions concern the printed drawings. They are not independent readings of the excavated originals. The chart crop is `renders/parpola_2005_fig3_detail.png`; the original page is `renders/parpola_2005_p28.png`. ## C5 versus C6

[Parpola 2008](https://tuhat.helsinki.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/127257447/Parpola_A_2008._Copper_tablets_from_Mohenjo_daro._ECL_During_Caspers_Vol.pdf), printed p.135 Fig.1b, draws C5a, C5b, and C6 with the same generalized leaf-ended enclosure and internal crab-like element. It explicitly connects C5 to B9 and C6 to B19. These type drawings summarize a classification and should not be counted as photographs proving all copies identical.

The government-hosted [CISI1 corpus](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/76884.pdf), printed pp.151–152, supplies M599–M604 reverse photographs. Their image crops and page coordinates are in `copper_variant_image_manifest.json`.

| Surface | Publication group | Direct photograph assessment from this scan | Identity verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| M599B | C5 candidate | Upper enclosure visible; internal strokes very faint | Broad family plausible; internal feet unresolved |
| M600B | C5 fragment; explicit 124f example | Broken right region and poor contrast | Cannot certify full identity; published reconstruction supports comparison |
| M601B | C6 candidate | Corrosion obscures inner strokes | Broad family plausible; finer form unresolved |
| M602B | C6 candidate | Some upper lateral strokes visible; interior unclear | Finer form unresolved |
| M603B | C6 candidate | General enclosure visible; corroded interior | Finer form unresolved |
| M604B / M604B bis | C6; explicit 124e example | Enclosure clearer in alternate view, interior still uncertain | Published form e is usable as author reading; bis is one object's extra view |

Conclusion: the **published type-level identity** of C5/C6 is well documented. Strict photographic certification of every feet distinction is not achieved here. The 2005 e/f drawings are compatible with one fine form across both label families, but M600 is incomplete. Mahadevan's critique of the *whole* P124 family does not by itself prove C5 and C6 different. Conversely the broad P124/W777/Mayig grouping does not prove them identical.

Mayig's retrieved schema groups W777, W778, and W782 in class P118 and maps them to M362, M363, and M364. Its only listed per-glyph feature is the shape of the outer flower; it does not explicitly encode internal feet. A test should therefore retain raw Wells identifiers and perform a separate inner/outer-element annotation. The schema is a secondary transcription aid, not archaeological evidence.

## Formal constraints and alternative models

Let `L(o)` be a normalized complete front sequence, `R(o)` a directly annotated reverse device, and `T(o)` the independently observed object class. An exact repeated `L` alongside two `R` values establishes a **one-to-many association**, subject to direction and damage audit. The inference `meaning(R1)=meaning(R2)` additionally assumes that the reverse surfaces fill the same semantic field and that the label does not contain distinct clauses licensing either device.

A strict species-name model would impose: if the same relevant reverse sign `r` replaces two animal scenes, both scenes denote the same literal species. C5/B9 and C6/B19 can falsify that narrow model only after fine-form identity and literal-referent assumptions are met. A deity shown in two forms, clan emblem, institutional label, shared status, or complementary administrative field can accommodate the association. None supplies a phonetic value.

| Evidence pattern | Minimum supported statement | Additional premise needed for semantic equality |
|---|---|---|
| Same complete label, image versus one sign | Two reverse devices share one label in this object class | Reverse device is a substitutable label value rather than complementary information |
| Same reverse sign, different complete labels | Many labels can license that device | Relevant sign identity survives variant adjudication |
| One picture, multiple label sequences | Picture is not a unique whole-text identifier | Sequences have been normalized without conflating mirror/damage variants |
| Association persists across separately made artifacts | Pairing is replicated | Copies are separate production events, not mould duplicates or repeated photography |
| Prediction transfers to unseen label families | Some compositional association generalizes | Held-out families are truly independent and matched on site/type/date |

The advance available here is a testable exclusion or constrained functional relation. An unrestricted latent-category model can fit any finite association graph, so graph fit alone cannot establish the historical category. New archaeology, repeated subphrases with independently labelled referents, or external linguistic anchors must choose between the surviving models.
