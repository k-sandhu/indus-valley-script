## W176 and W904

Independent crosswalk supplied by methods: W176 = old Parpola194 / Mahadevan048, seated figure; W904 = old Parpola172 / Mahadevan294, nested parentheses. Parpola194 also contains W177–179, and Parpola172 also contains W927; the broad crosswalk is not a fine allograph identity declaration.

Methods reports three exact complete-context substitutions, including a held-out `[235,705,033,*,740]` frame. **That held-out pair's two inscriptions were already juxtaposed in a 2025 publication.** [RMRL bulletin6, June2025](https://rmrl-site-files.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/PDFFIles/bulletin/bulletin-No-6-June-2025.pdf), printed p.17/PDF23, Fig.6b first column, shows Ad-3 and H-2139 in adjacent rows. Viewed right to left, their diagrams have the common fish-hat, U-like/stemmed sign, three long strokes, and terminal jar with the candidate parentheses/seated-figure difference. The diagram establishes that the graphic comparison was publicly available; it is not an independent excavation photograph.

The bulletin's author, Bahata Ansumali Mukhopadhyay, uses this chart in section4.1 (printed pp.16–17/PDF22–23) to argue for fish/gold-sign co-occurrence and related commodities/crafts. The surrounding text presupposes tax/license interpretations. These are author interpretations and supply no external reading of W176 or W904. No explicit W176/W904 substitution-class test was found in the inspected section or extracted text. A full historical priority claim would require reviewing earlier sources cited by this paper and the relevant sign distributions.
