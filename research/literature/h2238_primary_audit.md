## Archaeological dependence and interpretation

The authors describe 22 tablets with matching inscription sets from the same dumped debris, including 16 in a clump, with other tablets nearby or scattered. Microscopic examination attributed manufacture to at least three engravers. Fig.4 groups specimens2–7,8–16,17–23 accordingly; specimen18 belongs to the third group. This supplies a concrete reason to model copying and workshop dependence rather than treat every tablet as an independent semantic experiment.

The source discusses variation in face sequence/orientation and says the same inscription set can occur despite those differences. This is the authors' observation/interpretation of copying. The primary drawing's three-stroke middle face is still a visible exception to the frequent four-stroke counterpart in the digital set. It should be retained as a supported exception; do not replace it with four strokes merely to enforce a universal count mapping.

The finding neither assigns quantities to the short strokes nor identifies the diamond-like sign. A number/classifier, grammatical marker, emblem combination, workshop copying irregularity, or recording/drawing error remain possible explanations. A photograph or direct object inspection would discriminate a copying irregularity from a source transcription error more strongly than this drawing does.
