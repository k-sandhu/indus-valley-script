# Source review of numerical and chart report pages

The report's pages 13–16 were read after the original Vats and Vidale sources were acquired. The capacity-scale identifiability argument, complete conditional integer solutions, candidate H904/Vats join and failure of the image decoder are appropriately separated from semantic decipherment. No authenticated translation or phonetic assignment follows.

Three wording refinements were sent to the root author:

- Describe Vidale's fused slag block as having an **unestablished identity with M1383**, rather than asserting it is a different object. All seven original chapter pages were inspected; they do not provide the inscription-specific join, 82 fragments or 15–20 reconstructed total. Their absence here does not prove the historical count false.
- Describe L and S as multipliers assigned to selected stroke positions. The source assigns values according to position and proposed grouping; it does not establish an unconditional value for every long or short mark.
- Preserve the frozen 14-ambiguity numeric reference used for fresh20 scores. The later atlas has 16 ambiguous cells and additional source corrections; it is a subsequent sensitivity, not retroactive validation. All original numeric tables and blind scores remain archived.

The exact recent article title is *A Computational Analysis of the Indus Script: Identifying Sign Functions in Logo-Syllabic Writing Systems*, Khanna and Merriam, IJCA 187(64), December 2025, pp.19–22, DOI10.5120/ijca2025926075. It supplies priority for shared-context and substitution-resistant methods, not established linguistic/semantic ground truth.

The inspected Fuls source is the [2023 author reprint of *Ancient Writing and Modern Technologies – Structural Analysis of Numerical Indus Inscriptions*](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Andreas-Fuls/publication/373522958_Ancient_Writing_and_Modern_Technologies_-_Structural_Analysis_of_Numerical_Indus_Inscriptions/links/64f04b160f7ab20a8667db0b/Ancient-Writing-and-Modern-Technologies-Structural-Analysis-of-Numerical-Indus-Inscriptions.pdf), ResearchGate publication373522958. Its 34 PDF pages carry printed15–48. Printed29/PDF15 contains Eq1 and Fig15; printed30/PDF16 contains Eq2 and the historical tablet ID3786; printed32/PDF18 contains the M331 interpretation. These coordinates were verified in the indexed primary PDF text, not its figure pixels. The report should link this inspected reprint when citing those coordinates, rather than the separate 2020-paper posting361812318.

Fig15's printed29/PDF15 caption explicitly labels the reported bangle pot **M-1383**, not ICIT1383. The current corpus record bearing source_cisi M-1383 is reported by the root audit as a clay tag, excavationMN82a, with the proposed 017–031 pair among seven signs. That overlap does not settle the physical identity or the pot-content reconstruction. Keep the author's M-1383 label as a reported identifier and flag the unresolved catalogue/material join; neither a number-only reassignment nor a finding that the count is false is justified.

[Nair's *How Non-Linguistic Is the Indus Sign System? A Synthetic-Baseline Scorecard*, arXiv2604.17828v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17828v1), is prior unreviewed work dated20April2026. Section4.2 uses administrative generators with5–10 templates; section5.7 discusses seven attested comparators, including Sproat's datasets. Our particular held-out predictive/template assay and office-routing counterexample differ, but administrative templates and nonlinguistic comparator tests are not broad first-use claims. Its classification of Pictish, proto-cuneiform and textual heraldic blazons should not be adopted as accepted nonphonetic ground truth.
