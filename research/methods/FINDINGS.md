# Structural findings and limits

Research report; completed structural and substitution assays are reported together with their limitations. Exact values, seeds, hashes, completion states, and superseded-output cautions are in `RESULT_INDEX.json` and the machine-readable result files named below. No translation is proposed.

## What survives the stricter tests

The primary structural subset contains 2,107 complete, single-line records of length at least three, annotated right-to-left in the source catalogue. It has 1,596 distinct whole sequences and 1,526 transitive groups linked by physical artifact or an identical whole sequence. No such group crosses an outer validation fold. The largest group has 36 records.

After selecting all smoothing/model choices on three inner training folds, a model with previous-sign information improves held-out noninitial sign prediction over the strongest selected independent unigram/position/site/object-type baseline by **0.890 bits per sign** (physical/sequence-group bootstrap 95% interval **0.817–0.965**). Its relative perplexity is 0.540. Each outer fold selects independent PM smoothing 500 and dependent PMB smoothing 100. This is a structural result, not a probability that the script is linguistic.

Removing repeated whole sequences while preserving every original physical-artifact alias gives **0.848 bits** (95% interval **0.807–0.890**). Merging only three externally classified, catalogue-compatible code pairs gives **0.930 bits** (95% interval **0.862–0.997**). The structural gain is therefore not driven entirely by these duplicates or three specific possible inventory splits. This crosswalk uses shared historical catalogues and is not independent archaeological replication or token-level proof of allography.

Strict leave-site-out tests remove every entire physical-artifact/identical-string component touching the held-out site. Gains remain positive for Mohenjo-daro (0.715 bits, 1,044 test records), Harappa (0.893 bits, 785), and Lothal (1.014 bits, 74). The earlier direct-overlap rule left 21 transitive relatives in Mohenjo-daro training and 33 in Lothal training; those site-transfer numbers are superseded by `transitive_site_transfer.json`, while the grouped outer-validation results are unaffected. These are transfer tests between excavation sites, not identifications of place names.

The same full-component exclusion after the three compatible-class merges gives Mohenjo-daro 0.752, Harappa 0.905, and Lothal 1.049 bits. The length-two-inclusive unique sensitivity gives Mohenjo-daro 0.655, Harappa 0.806, and Dholavira 0.429 bits. These secondary comparisons support transfer of observed ordering constraints across these metadata splits; they do not resolve photographic transcription disagreements.

The root team's exploratory independent-annotator check uses all 86 Mayig faces marked clear, single-line, and at least three glyphs; 74 have mapped, training-supported candidate events. Training excludes every entire component touched by any source object M1–M199 or a fine-code whole text compatible with an eligible test event sequence, leaving 1,426 training strings. The forward event recursion is mathematically correct for this model. Gains are 0.725 bits per noninitial event (conditional component-bootstrap interval 0.551–0.899), 0.777 with unspecified seal subtype (0.601–0.955), and 0.878 on the 54 faces with every fine candidate training-supported (0.670–1.090). This is independent annotation of shared archaeological sources, using shared catalogue compatibility; overlapping candidate sets do not form a normalized Mayig-label alphabet. It supplies a structural sensitivity, not new ancient evidence, an event-shuffle significance claim, or phonetic meanings. The read-only review is `independent_annotator_audit.json`.

Post-audit full-procedure nulls repeat sequence-dependent grouping, folds, inner training-only smoothing choice, and outer prediction for each shuffled corpus. The primary observed gain 0.890 is far above its length/position/site/type-null mean -0.103 bits (95% null range -0.127 to -0.080). None of 1,000 shuffles equals or exceeds the observed gain, giving Monte Carlo reference p=1/1001=0.000999. The original 100 outcomes are preserved exactly, with 900 additional corpora continuing the same seed sequence; the full source versions and completion checks are retained. The alias-preserving unique gain 0.848 also exceeds the older unconstrained 100-shuffle null's mean 0.008 bits (-0.005 to 0.021), reference p=1/101=0.00990. Null means can be negative because the more flexible predictor is fitted and regularized; the appropriate comparison is against its fitted null distribution, not zero alone. These nulls describe the supplied transcription population and do not quantify language identification or solve source uncertainty.

A completed stronger post-pilot unique-text reference conditions the exact metadata-column permutation law on no newly duplicated complete strings. It accepts 1,000 corpora from 6,569 proposals and repeats the entire nested fit on every accepted corpus. Its mean is 0.007094 bits, with 95% null range -0.006549 to 0.021355; none equals or exceeds the observed 0.848358, giving reference p=1/1001=0.000999. All nulls retain 1,526 physical/text components and maximum nine strings per component. The 44.78-minute run records every accepted seed and hash; a separate completion audit matches all seed/proposal counts to the previously audited rejection sampler and all observed fold scores to the alias-preserving prior. This conditions on the selected unique-text transcription population, whose deterministic representative metadata remains a sensitivity choice. Latest result: `results/joint_nested_unique_conditioned_1000/joint_nested_results.json`; audit: `results/joint_nested_unique_conditioned_1000/completion_audit.json`.

The declared latent administrative-template model uses independently distributed slots inside each template, but mixing over unknown templates induces dependencies. Its training-only selected maximum component count is 16 and smoothing 20 in all five folds. It improves the independent baseline by 0.078 bits (95% interval 0.016–0.138), while PMB improves it by 0.812 bits (0.750–0.875). An extended-capacity grid of 16/32/64 components improves the independent baseline by 0.142 bits (0.087–0.195); PMB still improves the selected template by 0.748 bits (0.688–0.809). Failure of these finite families does not reject all administrative code systems: direct emblem adjacency constraints could reproduce Markov dependence without phonetic encoding.

A constructive specificity control demonstrates this limit using the same fitting protocol. In 3,000 generated office-routing records, each sign identifies an office entity, and the next office follows one of two legal handoffs. The ground truth does not encode speech. Nevertheless, its Markov model improves the selected 16/32/64-template family by **0.631 bits** (95% interval **0.588–0.677**). The control does not model actual Indus administration; it shows that residual prediction gains beyond this finite template family are not specific to language. That general limitation predates this project, including [Sproat (2010), *Last Words*](https://aclanthology.org/J10-3012/).

Including length-two texts produces a giant 804-record transitive component, 27.4% of the 2,930-record population; its apparent precision is therefore difficult to interpret. The alias-preserving unique version has 1,859 strings, 1,571 groups, a largest group of 206 strings, and gain 0.841 bits (0.801–0.881). Global reversal also retains a positive gain, 0.775 bits (0.695–0.857); predictive fit therefore supplies no independent direction evidence. The source's stored visual left-to-right codes are reversed for computational reading order, corroborated by explicit Fuls examples and photographs. Catalogue conventions use impressions for seals; the direction field should not be presented as an independent measurement of literal incised-seal orientation. See `../corpus/orientation_clarification.json`.

## Documented nonphonetic comparison data

The literature team obtained Sproat's author-published symbol XML with source bibliography and order conventions. For totem poles, the XML preserves vertical-stack order; simultaneous composite figures remain one unit and are not converted into invented adjacency edges. Conservatively matching named-pole/origin aliases gives 180 clear strings of at least three units, 173 physical/sequence components, and 878 units. There are 423 token types; 43.7% of held-out noninitial tokens are unseen in training, compared with 2.17% in the Indus subset. Physical identity remains incompletely documented, and the chosen composite encoding differs from an all-component inventory.

With the same nested protocol, totem gain is 0.0117 bits (group-bootstrap interval 0.0020–0.0224). It is unremarkable under the full-procedure metadata-preserving null (mean 0.0091, reference p=0.356 in 100 shuffles). The finite templates are worse than the selected independent baseline by 0.197 bits, so the Markov-versus-template gain of 0.209 bits chiefly exposes that fitted family's inadequacy in this sparse sample. None of these numbers supplies a threshold separating speech encoding from other cultural organization.

The source also has 10,142 chronological five-day weather-icon strings, with 16 types and 4,018 unique strings. Conservative station-plus-identical-string closure connects the entire archive into one component, including the alias-preserving unique version. Five-fold validation respecting those dependencies is infeasible. We report that obstacle instead of treating overlapping forecasts as independent objects to obtain a desired result. The source conventions and caveats are in `../literature/nonlinguistic_controls/README.md`; counts/OOV rates are in `controls/comparability_assessment.json`; fitted comparisons are in `results/totem_joint_nested/joint_nested_results.json` and `results/totem_templates/template_results.json`.

## Why the attractive substitution result weakens

The top raw exact-frame substitution pairs include 817/861 (10 different frames), 390/405 (six), and 705/706 (five). The external Mayig compatibility catalogue places each of these three pairs in a shared P class. Their recurrence can therefore reflect catalogue splitting of graphic variants, although the crosswalk alone does not adjudicate every original glyph. Pairwise class intersection is used only as a sensitivity; the many-to-many crosswalk is not converted into a transitive equivalence relation.

The strongest pair mapped to disjoint Mayig P classes is 817/820 (eight raw frames, ten after the three compatible-class merges). "Distinct class" here means separate classes in that external coding scheme, not independently certified token-level glyph or semantic difference. With 1,000 shuffles preserving exact length/position/site/object-type marginals, the raw selection-aware maximum-support test gives primary p=0.620, unique-string p=0.001, and merged p=0.311. These different null references are sensitive to repeated-string frequency.

The strongest transfer assay repeats the entire procedure on each shuffled corpus: grouping and folds, pair discovery on at least two training frames, and validation in a wholly new held-out frame. Its class-separated results are:

| Corpus weighting/inventory | Observed disjoint-P-class transferred pair-frames | Metadata-null mean | Null 95% range | Joint reference p, 1,000 shuffles |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| All complete records, original inventory | 5 | 5.005 | 1–11 | 0.541 |
| One string representative, all physical aliases retained | 3 | 0.096 | 0–1 | 0.001 |
| All records, three compatible-class merges | 5 | 5.658 | 1–11 | 0.639 |

The unique-string result establishes a constraint relative to those marginals in that sensitivity. It does not rescue a universal new semantic-family claim: the primary and merged transfer counts are unremarkable, and the familiar fish-class correspondence accounts for one of the three unique confirmations. The remaining confirmations are W176/W904 and W220/W231, each appearing in only one unseen frame. The aggregate p value is **not** an individual significance level for any of these pairs.

These results do **not** establish new semantic substitution families. Selection-aware tests matter: finding eight compelling contexts after searching many possible pairs is different from predicting an independently selected pair's contexts. A pooled, frequency-adjusted full-universe maximum statistic is a further exploratory sensitivity, since raw maximum counts can be conservative for rarer pairs. It detects excess frame agreement globally in both original and unique-string populations; its result must not replace the declared raw and new-frame transfer tests. At 1,000 metadata shuffles, W176/W904 has adjusted-max FWER p=0.130 in the primary corpus and 0.013 in the unique sensitivity; the raw unique maximum-support FWER p is 0.400. The individual candidate therefore has sensitivity-dependent support, not a robust confirmed breakthrough.

The 10,000-shuffle primary refinement confirms global adjusted-max p=0.000100 under both nulls. The strongest metadata-adjusted pairs are 817/820 (eight frames, FWER p=0.000100), 233/235 (seven, p=0.000200), and 820/861 (six, p=0.000700), all within broad prior-known families. W176/W904 remains individually nonsignificant (p=0.129887), as does W220/W231 (p=0.862614).

A further uniqueness-conditioned joint null accepts only random corpora preserving whole-string uniqueness as well as exact marginals. This addresses the fact that unconstrained shuffles of a unique-string population can generate occasional repeated strings. Distinct-class transfer remains three pair-frames against metadata-null mean 0.116, reference p=0.002997 in 1,000 accepted corpora. Thus the unique sensitivity's aggregate constraint survives this conditioning too; it still does not provide individual significance or a new semantic interpretation for any pair. Exact accepted seeds and rejected-attempt counts are saved in `results/joint_transfer_unique_conditioned/joint_null_results.json`.

## Independent review of masked-sign restoration

The root team's masked-sign assay uses held-out physical/whole-text components and selects parameters using only training components. Expanding its independent baseline grid to include unigram and position-only candidates leaves the selected metadata baseline unchanged in every fold. Our separate read-only audit reproduces the 16.99% baseline versus 33.81% local-context top-one accuracy. Giving each of the 1,596 whole texts equal weight instead of each glyph slot gives 17.36% versus 32.60%; the gain is 15.24 percentage points, with a conditional component-bootstrap interval of 14.08–16.37 points. This supports a predictive restoration aid under the known-location, one-sign masking experiment.

Performance is strongly concentrated in frequent signs. For the 76 types occurring at least 20 times, local-context accuracy is 41.07%; for the 205 singleton types, every test instance is unseen in training and accuracy is zero. Macro-type accuracy is 5.13% for local context versus 0.63% for the independent baseline. The model's raw maximum probabilities also exceed the corresponding observed accuracy in every reported confidence bin. They should not be presented as calibrated probabilities that an ancient reading is correct. Artificially hiding one intact sign provides a declared benchmark; real erosion can involve unknown sign count, segmentation, direction, and inventory ambiguity. The audit files are `root_restoration_audit.json` and `restoration_frequency_audit.json`; no root source files were modified.

The unknown-count gap prototype initially omits the artificial mask sampling factor `1/min(3,L-2)` from its count posterior. Our separate design-matched reweighting keeps every fitted model, query and fold unchanged. It gives 45.61% independent accuracy and 46.30% local accuracy, below the 51.07% constant-one design control. Local-minus-independent gain is 0.689 percentage points (conditional component-bootstrap interval -0.569 to 1.940); local-minus-constant-one is -4.762 points (-7.775 to -1.726). This negative result rules out promoting the prototype to reliable count recovery in this benchmark. Its artificial mask prior also does not identify a historical erosion mechanism. The derivation is `GAP_MODEL_REVIEW.md`; the reproducible sensitivity is `results/gap_design_matched/gap_design_matched.json`.

## Source-font acquisition audits

The separate arithmetic/provenance reviews reproduce the development pilot and frozen fresh20 endpoint, including all IDs, hashes, candidate-set edit calculations and score summaries. The fresh endpoint has 96 modern drawn units (91 charted glyphs and five editorial marks), 107 predictions, matching counts on 14/20 rows, 69 strict first-template edits and two whole-compatible rows; five templates yield 60 edits and five rows. Frozen numeric alternatives can broaden the candidate sets, so these are compatibility scores rather than uniquely certified code accuracy.

Subsequent source inspection finds top clipping on 18 target crops and following-row contamination on 14. The negative fresh result therefore identifies failure of the complete frozen acquisition pipeline, rather than isolated shape recognition. A post-reference development diagnostic changes only vertical geometry while retaining the exact frozen chart, matcher, penalty and horizontal limits. It gives 96 predictions, 18/20 matching row counts, 24 strict first-template edits/six whole-compatible rows, and six top5 edits/16 rows. Its top5 code sets have mean width 5.198 and maximum 9. This isolates a substantial geometry defect but does not supply fresh validation or replace the frozen failure. No resulting strings were added to the structural corpus, and original physical lines/sides remain unknown. See `FONT_PILOT_AUDIT.md`, `results/font_fresh20_audit/audit.json`, and `results/font_geometry_sensitivity/evaluation_post_reference.json`.

## Historical priority

The recovered opener module is already quantified in Yadav's primary overview: M267+M99 and M391+M99 are predominantly initial, citing earlier work. Our corresponding W817/861 and W820 substitutions before W002 reproduce that pattern. The paper is [Yadav (2019), DOI 10.16943/ijhs/2019/v54i2/49656](https://insa.nic.in/writereaddata/UpLoadedFiles/IJHS/Vol54_2_2019__Art01.pdf).

The terminal correspondence between the burden-bearing person (M12/W151) and jar (M342/W740) is explicitly discussed by Mahadevan (1982), who considers their positional and contextual similarities evidence of a common category while treating proposed meanings as speculative. The primary scan/OCR is available locally in `../literature/sources/mahadevan_1982_terminal.txt`. Mahadevan (1986), printed page 20, also discusses interchangeable modified fish signs. Our direct whole-context audit refines the evidence granularity; it does not discover these categories.

W176/W904 (seated person M48 versus nested parentheses M294) shares three whole frames, always immediately before W740. One exact Ad-3/H-2139 correspondence already appears in adjacent rows of Figure 6, printed page 17, of the [RMRL June 2025 bulletin](https://rmrl-site-files.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/PDFFIles/bulletin/bulletin-No-6-June-2025.pdf). The article uses that chart for speculative commodity interpretation; we do not adopt those meanings. Literature review has not yet established a prior explicit substitution-class claim, but prior depiction prevents calling the contrast newly noticed. An original CISI1 photograph of M289a confirms the broad graphic classes of a second frame, with fine diamond variant unresolved. Labelled H697 and Ad3 photographs from the derivative repository also support broad shapes and mirrored seal orientation, but their uncited provenance does not supply independent primary verification. Original photographs for all six distinct strings and physical alias/copy relationships remain an audit requirement; the current secondary drawings alone do not resolve them. Complete source identifiers are in `candidate_176_904_plate_audit.json`; provenance grades and photo hashes are in `../corpus/plate_audit/pair_176_904/audit_status.json`.

[Rao et al. (2009), DOI 10.1073/pnas.0906237106](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0906237106) already demonstrated probabilistic sequencing, restoration, and examples of functional interchangeability. [Recchia and Louwerse (2016), DOI 10.1111/cogs.12311](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12311) supplied an earlier precedent for archaeological provenance prediction. The present stricter grouping, smoothing, null, allograph, and transfer safeguards are a methodological robustness contribution, not first discovery of order or terminals.

[Khanna and Merriam (2025), DOI 10.5120/ijca2025926075](https://www.ijcaonline.org/archives/volume187/number64/khanna-2025-ijca-926075.pdf) also describe promotion from singleton contexts, substitution/shared-context classes, bigram clusters and substitution-resistant pairs. Their published examples include W002 paired with W817/W820/W861 as complete-word candidates. We do not adopt their word-status or syllabic inference. Shared-context discovery is therefore published prior; the specific duplicate/alias-safe held-out validation and full-procedure null are the present assay's contribution.

[Nair (April 2026), arXiv:2604.17828v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17828v1), an unreviewed preprint, already compares synthetic administrative templates and several empirical symbol datasets, including Sproat's totems and weather icons. It explicitly limits its conclusions to the finite generator architectures tested. Neither comparator introduction nor that conceptual limitation is new here. Our contribution is the particular held-out predictive/template comparison with source-preserved units, artifact/whole-text grouping, training-only selection, and full-procedure nulls. We do not adopt the preprint's undifferentiated nonlinguistic labels for disputed-status systems or textual descriptions of visual designs.

Duplicate methodology is also established prior. Koskenniemi and Parpola (1980), pages 54–56, distinguish strict duplicates within object/iconography classes from loose duplicates globally, and account for uncertain sign renderings and compatible fragments. The original document is saved in `../corpus/primary/koskenniemi_parpola1980.pdf`. Our exact intact-string and transitive-alias implementation is a reproducible safeguard for this dataset, not the invention of duplicate control.

## What these results cannot identify

A separate exact audit of Fuls's published numerical proposal yields a conditional constraint. If integer positive multipliers satisfy `v=2S-L` and `vL+1=7S+5`, the complete positive solution set is `(L,S,v)=(4,20,36),(6,8,10),(10,8,6),(36,20,4)`. Both assumed ranges `L8..13` and `L8..18` leave only `(10,8,6)`, without a prime-base or even-S exclusion. This improves the precision of the arithmetic argument; it does not independently ground equal quantities, positional ranks or the bangle referent. The source's inference that integer `(v+L)/2` makes S even is invalid, although the joint solutions happen to be even. The exact derivation, source assumptions and outstanding W032-versus-three-stroke discrepancy are in `NUMERICAL_CONSTRAINT_AUDIT.md` and `results/numerical_equalities/numerical_equalities_audit.json`. The proposal itself is prior literature, and no new numeral or phonetic decipherment is claimed.

An unlabeled distribution over sign strings is unchanged if proposed meanings are relabeled along with their latent generative model. To identify sounds or lexical meanings one needs an externally fixed language/writing-system model, independently grounded anchors, or risky predictions tied to evidence beyond the sign strings. A statistical fit to a freely adapted dictionary cannot supply those anchors itself.

The conservative conclusion is that these transcriptions contain transferable dependencies beyond independent position and known metadata. The analyses do not select a spoken language, identify phonetic values, prove a grammatical category's meaning, or provide a defensible translation. Their usefulness is to specify empirical constraints and remove misleading routes toward a purported decipherment.

## Audit cautions

- The large source is a derivative export associated with a disputed decipherment; only observational columns were used, and proposed Sanskrit/translations/canonical reading files were excluded. Full photographic adjudication remains incomplete.
- Whole-text uniqueness is a sensitivity with a deterministic representative record, so its retained metadata can vary from the complete multi-artifact distribution.
- Position shuffles preserve record-level marginals while destroying duplicated-text cohort identity. Both the grouped and unique-sequence assays must be considered.
- Earlier nulls kept observed data-derived groups/folds fixed, and earlier aggregate transfer nulls fixed discoveries in five overlapping training sets. These remain archived diagnostics. The post-audit `joint_nested_*` and `joint_transfer_*` assays repeat grouping and discovery/fitting for every full-corpus shuffle.
- Finite vocabulary maps unseen test signs to one UNKNOWN bucket. Paired models share this treatment; the reported scores are predictions over that declared alphabet.
- The original fixed smoothing-20 endpoint used an overfitted independent metadata baseline. It remains archived as preregistered, but the stronger nested model selection is the appropriate robustness result.
- Eight integrity tests pass, including probabilistic normalization, group leakage protection, preservation of every physical alias after uniqueness filtering, exclusion of all aliases and transitive component relatives in site transfer, preserved null marginals, duplicate-safe frame counting, and a nonphonetic latent-template counterexample. Known independent synthetic symbols produce gain -0.020; paired dependent symbols produce +1.714. Neither synthetic system encodes speech.
- Bootstrap intervals condition on fitted cross-validation predictions; they do not refit the entire discovery process in each draw. They quantify held-out cluster score variation rather than every source of model-selection or corpus-curation uncertainty.
