Claim

Indus building groups changed their houses under common rules that preserved shared routes and services. The repeated unit of urban coordination was the connection between adjacent groups through streets, drains and entrances. Districts could grow, subdivide or change orientation while those rules continued. Local negotiation was one means of maintaining them.

Why we think this

The comparative study connects Banawali's deliberate replanning, Kanmer's infill with retained alignment, Farmana's local encroachments and the wall and hydraulic sequences at Dholavira and Lothal. These sequences support different scales of intervention. Their institutional cause remains a conjecture. The methods distinguish a negotiated arrangement from uniform supervision and independent convergence.

P3 Repair timing groups by connected infrastructure more strongly than by proximity alone Use construction dates independent of the tested infrastructure connection; omit phase dates inferred from that connection. Compare connection-based and distance-only models on entirely withheld episode groups. The former improves the prespecified predictive score with leave-one-town-out stability. Pilot power and scoring family must be frozen before confirmation. untested

P1 and P2 share evidence and receive one joint update. If both pass and independent review retains the phase and hydraulic joins, credence in the common-rule claim becomes 0.70. If P1 fails with adequate sensitivity to a 0.25 difference, credence becomes 0.25. P3 is a deciding follow-up and cannot be combined as independent evidence when it reuses those episodes. These outcomes do not by themselves choose negotiation over central supervision or hydraulic constraint. A separate frozen comparison of their repair timing, component choices and access predictions must discriminate those institutions.

Kill condition

Abandon the common-rule claim if new phase-keyed surveys show that supposed retained shared routes are composite-plan or restoration artifacts, or a powered comparison finds no preferential continuity after matching preservation. Reject the negotiation extension if settlement-wide supervision and engineering constraints fully account for its discriminating predictions. A lack of comparable phases postpones the test.
