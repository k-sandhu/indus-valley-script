Claim

The builders of the Indus-plain cities fixed true north by observing the sky and laid out Harappa's Mound F, the house blocks of Mohenjo-daro and the Lothal dock within two degrees of it. The walled towns of Kutch, Saurashtra and the Ghaggar plain were set out on local axes that follow the landform beside them, the river channel, ridge or shoreline, and are turned anticlockwise from the cardinal directions by 5 to 30 degrees. A town's deviation records its setting, not its date.

Why it might be true

On Esri World Imagery, measured by two agents blind to each other, Harappa's Mound F lies at +0.01 ± 0.51°, Mohenjo-daro's house blocks at +0.5 ± 0.4° (its main streets at +6.0 ± 1.0°), the Lothal dock at −1.9 ± 0.3° and the Sutkagen-dor enclosure at −0.4 ± 0.5° (one measurer, low confidence). Dholavira's castle lies at −5.5 ± 1.5°, as Vahia and Menon (2013) found ("inclined 6° to the west of north"). The published plans of the regional towns, whose north arrows are not stated to be true, put Surkotada at −15.1° (and −15.5° by a second measurer), Kalibangan's lower-city grid at −15.5° (−12.9°), Chanhu-daro at −19.9° on a dated magnetic plan corrected with IGRF, Farmana at −22.3°, Kanmer at −29.3° and Rojdi at −8.4° (table, exploratory table). The plan arrows err by about 3° (root mean square against imagery at four towns), far less than these deviations. Jansen's 1979 values agree for Surkotada (−16 gon) and Kalibangan's main street (−13 gon). These observations were made after the frozen star test was scored.
