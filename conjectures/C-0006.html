Claim

At Mohenjo-daro the stock of signs that close seal legends was fixed before the lowest levels the excavators reached, and it did not change while the city was rebuilt above them. Sign 740 and eleven other closing signs (520, 090, 400, 151, 156, 527, 617, 621, 226, 892 and 407) close about three legends in four at every height of an excavated sequence, and the two-sign closing formulae, most of them ending in 740, close about one legend in three at every height. The front of the legend was not fixed. The choice of opening sign, the variant cut within a sign class and the length of the legend drifted through the sequence, so that seals from the top of an area's deposits open with different signs, use different variants and run longer than seals from its bottom.

Why it might be true

In Mackay's DK-G excavation, where levels are measured against one fixed datum over some 30 ft of deposit, the twelve closing-stock signs end 73%, 75% and 72% of legends in the bottom, middle and top thirds of height, and the 61 legends below −18 ft close with the same signs (74% in the stock). Which sign closes a legend carries no information about its height (z between −1.05 and 1.20 for three groupings of signs) (exploration, exploration.json). At Harappa, sign frequencies differ between Periods 3B and 3C beyond their permutation reference and legends lengthen, although both effects shrink inside single excavation frames (CHRONOLOGY-REGION-01). A fixed closing formula with a changing front is the pattern expected if the closing part of a legend named a standing institution or office and the front named its holder. Rao and Mahadevan grouped the same DK-G seals into Early, Middle and Late levels in the 1980s and found the frequent sign pairs unchanged; this card separates the closing stock from the front of the legend, which a test of pooled pairs cannot do.

The DK-G seals give the drift half little support: opening signs carry no height information (z = 0.42, p = 0.31), and variant choice (p = 0.13), length (p = 0.11) and the overall sign inventory (p = 0.066) move in the predicted direction without reaching p = 0.05. What moved most clearly is the seal format: rectangular seals rise from 10% to 20% of the seals between the bottom and top thirds (p = 0.002).

A drift pass (P4 to P8) counts at half weight when its Holm-adjusted p-value across the five drift tests exceeds 0.05. The credence after the test is 0.20 plus the effects, held between 0.02 and 0.90.

Kill condition

The card is dead if the closing-stock share differs by 0.20 or more between the top and bottom thirds of Marshall's sequences (P1 point estimate of 0.20 or more in absolute value), or if P4, P5, P6 and P7 all fail, which leaves no drift in openings, variants or length for the closing stock to stand apart from.

Record

  • 20 October 2026: card written at credence 0.35 for the stratigraphy brief.
  • 20 October 2026: discovery on Mackay's 625 DK-G seals with a level (EXPLORATION.md). The fixity half holds and the drift half is weak; credence lowered to 0.20 before the freeze.
  • 20 October 2026: predictions frozen by hash with the bench shots loader and tested once on 434 Marshall seals in 15 depth strata (REPORT.md, results.json). The closing stock held: 73.1% of bottom-third legends close in it, against 73.2% in DK-G, and the top-minus-bottom difference is −0.006 (90% interval −0.105 to 0.096). Nothing at the front of the legend moved: opening signs p = 0.17, the opening-closing contrast p = 0.61, variants p = 0.097, length p = 0.38, DK-G sign heights p = 0.16. P4 to P7 all failed, so the card is dead at 0.02 under its frozen rule. The test detects an opening drift of one legend in four with probability 0.80 after a quarter of the seals are displaced, and the DK-G rise of rectangular seals did not repeat in Marshall's sections, whose bottom and top thirds lie under 4 ft apart at the median; small drifts within short sections remain untested. The fixity half survives as part of a stronger successor reading stated in the report: the legend was written to one fixed pattern for the whole excavated occupation, front and back, to be decided on a sequence with an independent clock such as HARP Periods 3A to 3C within one Harappa mound.
  • 20 October 2026: literature check for prior proposals (listed above). The fixed-pattern successor reading has a direct precedent in Rao and Mahadevan's finding of unchanged sign-pair frequencies across Mackay's DK-G periods, and the DK-G rise of rectangular seals was reported by Fuls; Kenoyer and Meadow's Harappa sequence is the main published case for change.
