Claim

The four-sign text carried by the Lothal sealings L-161 to L-170, clay impressions of a seal, and by the Mohenjo-daro seal fragment M-895 is the title of an office, held by different people in the two cities. The Lothal sealings were made with a different seal from M-895, cut for a holder of the same office in another affiliation, which is why the sealings show an elephant and M-895 a unicorn.

Why it might be true

Ten Lothal sealings with an elephant carry the text of M-895, which is recorded with a unicorn. Most sealings that carry a seal text match seals from other cities: 13 of the 16 sealings that match a seal (seal-text identity). If one holder's goods had moved, the sealings would carry M-895's animal. The rival readings are an error in the catalogue's record of either animal, and a single holder who used two seals.

Record

  • 12 October 2026: card written from the seal-text identity results. The plate check is the cheapest decisive test in the project.
  • 13 October 2026: the predictions were not fully fresh when the plates were read. The seal-text identity report had already described the photographs of L-161 and M-895, so the plate check used a second reader who saw only renamed images.
  • 13 October 2026, plate check: AI readings of the CISI 1 and CISI 2 photographs by two readers, pending specialist confirmation. P1 passed: the sealings and M-895 carry the same four signs in the same order. P3 passed on carved form: the sealings come from one seal whose face is as wide as M-895's (23.4 against 23.3 mm) but whose signs are spaced and cut differently, which fits Frenez and Tosi 2005's grouping of L-161 to L-172 as impressions of one elephant seal. P2 is unresolved because the readers disagree on M-895's animal. The rival "one seal used in both cities" is now unlikely; "one holder with two seals" remains. Credence 0.6. Next: a specialist reading of M-895's field and of the trunk-and-tusk element on the sealings.
