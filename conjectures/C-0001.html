Claim

A seal legend that closes with sign 820 marks its holder as belonging to an upper grade, and seals for that grade were cut larger: across both halves of the corpus the ten seals ending in 820 average about +0.64 in residual log face area, nearly twice the face area of seals of the same type, site class and length. Six of the ten are among the 25 largest residuals of their half. On the photographs, 820 stands at full height and normal spacing at the closing end of the legend, as any closing sign does.

This is the surviving form of the claim frozen for Shot A. The frozen version named four closing signs (001, 595, 679, 820); its central prediction passed on the confirmation half, but the adversarial review found the pass carried by three 820 seals, and 679 did not repeat. The narrowed claim was formed after the outcome and has had no test of its own.

Why it might be true

On the discovery half of the conjecture-shot split, the last sign of a seal legend carries size information beyond seal type, site and text length (between-sign ratio 2.14, p = 0.001), and the first sign carries less (1.15, p = 0.23; matched difference p = 0.005). The whole effect sits in four last signs: the 18 seals ending in 001, 595, 679 or 820 have mean residual log area +0.66 against −0.02 for the other 572, and 16 of the 18 are larger than expected. Without those four signs the last sign carries nothing (ratio 1.09, p = 0.35). In 300 split-half rehearsals the four signs were the only ones repeatedly selected as high, and their held-out seals averaged +0.36, while signs selected as low averaged −0.03 against 0.00. The signal survives adding the unicorn variant and cult-object codes to the size model (high-set contrast +0.53, p = 0.0002), so the image codes do not account for it. These seals come from several excavation areas and catalogue periods at Mohenjo-daro and from Harappa, Chanhu-daro and Dholavira. Outputs: unit-size-tests.json, high-closers.json, power-split.json, iconography.json; narrative in EXPLORATION.md.

The rival reading is layout. A large face leaves room at the end of the text band, and the extra sign there need not mean anything about grade; both readings predict the same association. The four signs were also chosen from 28 after looking.

Kill condition

P4 does not pass (failed, or untestable with fewer than five seals ending in the set) and P1a fails on the confirmation half. That outcome leaves no size information in closing signs, through the named set or otherwise, and the card becomes dead at credence 0.02.

Post-outcome breakdowns (amendment, exploratory) show the P4 pass resting mainly on 820 (five seals, mean +0.62) while 679 did not repeat (four seals, mean −0.08), closing signs outside the set carrying size on this half (ratio 1.62, p = 0.027), and the locality order of the two halves disagreeing. The sentences on opening-sign locality and on the silence of other closers are contradicted by this half; the next test of the claim, narrowed to 820 after the outcome, is new material.
  • 13 October 2026, adversarial review (REVIEW.md): the freeze was clean and the confirmation half could not have informed the choice of signs. P4 depends on three seals ending in 820: dropping M-6, M-16 or M-649 gives p = 0.138, 0.071 or 0.066; a rank version gives p = 0.087 to 0.093; without 820 the other six seals give +0.122, p = 0.40. M-649 is catalogued as silver and M-6 has the fourth-largest face of 582 confirmation seals. Measured against its null mean the matched excess is ×1.35 in face area. P1a's signal comes mostly from signs outside the set, and first signs carry as much size as last signs on this half. A Holm correction over the six predictions keeps P1a and drops P4. Review credence 0.13; 0.21 remains the frozen-rule value.
  • 13 October 2026, plate check (plate checks), AI readings of CISI photographs pending specialist confirmation: PP1 met (the Lothal sealings' impressed face measures 23.4 mm for a legend ending in 740, inside the predicted 19.9–35.4 mm); PP3 passed (14 of 22 photographed set seals and all seven photographed 820 seals show the closing sign in the band at normal size and spacing; none of 22 set seals or 29 matched comparison seals shows a layout or direction pattern). Frozen-rule value 0.24; review credence 0.15.
  • 13 October 2026: claim narrowed to 820. The frozen four-sign wording, kept for the record: "Indus seals were issued in at least two grades, and the upper grade is written twice: the seal is cut larger, and its legend closes with one of four signs, 001, 595, 679 or 820, often in the recurring closing pairs 740-679, 368-001, 420-595, 595-820 and 820-595. A seal whose legend closes this way has about 1.5 to 2 times the face area of a seal of the same type, site class and text length. No other closing sign marks size: the jar sign 740, the X-740 formulas and the other common closers carry no grade. Opening signs individualise the holder and are more local than closings. Seals with identical ordinary legends are not cut to a common size."
