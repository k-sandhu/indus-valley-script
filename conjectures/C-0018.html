Claim

Indus sealings recorded responsibility at handovers between urban groups. Their recurring legends connected institutions across districts and towns, while the particular stamp identified an authorising holder. The relevant architectural settings were controlled passages, storage access and points where goods changed custody.

Evidence and extension

The project has physical fastening evidence from Lothal and repeated texts carried by different stamps across sites. The new study adds the distinction between a use context, a workshop, a discarded assemblage and a building associated only by a reused label. A sealing's fastening trace supports closure or attachment; responsibility and institutional handover remain the proposed function. No sound value or sign gloss follows from this claim.

Compare a context-and-style model with the same model after adding text units, on new districts and towns with identities withheld. Added text improves the frozen predictive score beyond within-period, frequency- and production-group-preserving rearrangements, surviving influence checks. Report the reciprocal style contribution. untested
P3 The architectural relation survives use and discard coding Independently code fastening, breakage and redeposition. P1's association remains in the primary-use subset and is not generated entirely by secondary dumps. untested

All predictions share a joint update. P1 and P3 passing moves credence to 0.50. P2 also passing on new material moves it to 0.65. A powered failure of P1 and P3 moves it to 0.10. These are proposed update rules; the pilot must fix sample sufficiency and scoring before a real confirmation round.

Kill condition

Abandon the handover claim if adequately sampled, primary-use contexts show that the material is explained by household possession or workshop disposal and does not predict independently coded custody changes. Recurring text alone cannot rescue a failed physical-use prediction.
