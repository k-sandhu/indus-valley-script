Claim

Short-stroke groups immediately before plain fish on seals chiefly form the asterism names three-star, six-star and seven-star. Their distribution follows a fixed celestial-name inventory.

Why it might be true

The graphic seven-plus-fish inscription on H-9 is clear, and Parpola compares it with later Dravidian star names. This card tests a stronger population claim than that particular reading. The registered model puts 90% of its mass on values 3, 6 and 7, excludes the two-long-stroke compounds and compares its predictive score with generic quantities and fish-specific expressions. Frozen specification, bench freeze.

Only 17 qualifying reserved groups exist. Eleven have canonical values, a share of 0.647 with a 95% interval of 0.413–0.827. The model loses 0.340 bits per group to the quantity reference and 0.753 to fish-specific expressions. It fails the minimum-20, coverage and score rules. Some short strokes could be connective constructions, so the failure applies to this population model and its classification of the strokes. Exact results, field /comparisons/primary_seals/numbers/narrow_celestial.

Kill condition

Drop this population claim when a source-qualified sample of at least 40 independent numeral-fish objects gives a 95% upper bound below 0.8 for the canonical share, or the frozen celestial model consistently loses to both rivals on that sample. A particular celestial reading needs its own linguistic and archaeological test.
