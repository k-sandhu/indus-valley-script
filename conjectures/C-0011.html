Claim

Proto-Dravidian mīn meant both "fish" and "star". The ancestor of all Dravidian languages, spoken about 4,400 years ago, called a star "the shining one" with a word built on miṉ "to shine", and that word had the same form as mīn "fish", so a writer of the third millennium BCE could draw a fish to write "star". Parpola's astral reading of the Indus fish sign rests on this pun.

Why it might be true

Tamil mīṉ means both fish and star in the oldest Tamil literature. In the Dravidian Etymological Dictionary the two senses belong to separate entries: 4885 gives mīn "fish" in thirteen of the twenty languages of the dated tree of Kolipakam and colleagues (Royal Society Open Science 5: 171504, 2018), and 4876, headed miṉ "flash, lightning", gives a star sense in Tamil, Malayalam, Kannada, Kota, Toda, Gondi, Kurukh and Malto. On that tree, whose root is 4,433 years old, the star sense reaches the root with a maximum-likelihood probability of 0.524 and the North Dravidian node with 0.681, and the meanings Parpola's readings need reach the root far more often than unproposed meanings from the same entries (report, results). Kodava boḷḷi mi·nï "morning star", literally "white star", has the form of Parpola's veḷ-mīn "Venus", with a second member identical to Kodava mi·nï "fish".

Kill condition

The claim is dead if "star" is confined to South Dravidian, with no Central or North language coded for it, and its maximum-likelihood root probability is below 0.2. The frozen test did not meet it: two North languages carry the star sense in DEDR. Removing them, as a failure of P5 would, leaves the root probability at 0.34–0.47 because Gondi keeps the star word, so P5 alone cannot kill the claim; it would reduce it to a South Dravidian pun.

P1, P2 and P3 passed and the kill condition failed; parsimony places "star" absent at the root, so the frozen rule set the credence to 0.35. The posterior trees (mean 0.520, 89% above 0.5) and the two-rate model (0.51–0.53) agree with the primary estimate, which is close to the model's uninformative value. Review lowered the credence to 0.20: a star form homophonous with mīn reaches the root at 0.41 and the North node at 0.22, the fieldwork word lists of DravLex use a mīn form for "star" only in Tamil, and the Kurukh and Malto star words begin with b-, which in the coded entries answers Tamil v- (DEDR cross-refers entry 4876 to viṇ "sky"). P5 was added after the result to test that last point. An independent implementation reproduces every root probability. Verification tier: submitted.
