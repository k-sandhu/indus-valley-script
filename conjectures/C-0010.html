Claim

The Indus seal legend was written to one fixed pattern through the urban period, and the seal that carried it changed around it. At Harappa, from the end of Period 3B to the end of Period 3C, some three to four centuries of Vats's Mound F sequence, the same fifteen signs close about seven legends in ten (740 above all, then 520, 405, 154, 090 and the rest of the stock shared with Mohenjo-daro), the same signs open them, the same variants are cut within each sign class, legends keep their length, and no sign enters or leaves the inventory with the period. When the long rectangular seal with script alone arrives in Period 3C it takes the same legend. Mohenjo-daro's legends show the same pattern at the bottom and top of both excavated sequences. The legend was a standard already in place before the earliest seals these excavations reached, and each city kept it while it rebuilt.

Why it might be true

At Mohenjo-daro the twelve-sign closing stock closes 73% of legends at the bottom and at the top of Mackay's DK-G sequence and of Marshall's sections, and opening signs, variants and length show no change that repeats from one excavation to the other (stratigraphy report). That test left one gap: Marshall's sections span little depth, and the rise of rectangular seals seen in DK-G did not repeat in them, so the drift half might have been untested. At Harappa the 3B–3C differences in sign frequency and legend length measured across all inscribed faces shrank inside single excavation frames (CHRONOLOGY-REGION-01). On the 99 period-labelled Harappa seals outside Mound F, the closing stock closes 0.71 of early-third and 0.73 of late-third legends, no drift test comes near significance except variant choice (p = 0.084), and rectangular seals rise from 0.09 to 0.29 of the seals (p = 0.015) (exploration, exploration.json, rehearsal). A legend that names a standing office, household or institution in a fixed formula is what a seal-using administration needs to keep recognisable across generations; the seal's material form carried the fashion.

Predictions

The frozen statistics, nulls, influence rules and power are in PREDICTIONS.md. P1 to P7 and PC are tested once on the 175 Mound F seals with a Vats stratum, with strata ranked over the mound (Stratum VII oldest) and permutations within Vats's trenches. A drift test reads "no drift" when p exceeds 0.10 and "drift" when p is at most 0.05 and survives single deletions.

Kill condition

The card is dead if P1 fails or any of P3 to P7 finds drift, with a Holm-adjusted p-value at most 0.05 over P1 and P3 to P7: the closing stock changes its share between the early and late thirds of Mound F, or the opening signs, closing signs, variants, length or inventory change with the period beyond what single misplaced seals explain.

The closing frame held: the fifteen-sign stock closes 0.848 of early-third and 0.805 of late-third legends, and neither the opening sign (p = 0.39) nor the closing sign (p = 0.70) changes. The positive control passed, with rectangular seals rising from 0.07 to 0.23 of the seals (p = 0.0017). The body of the legend changed: complete legends average 4.0, 4.8 and 5.3 signs by third (p = 0.003), the sign inventory changes (p = 0.0016) and so does variant choice (p = 0.0001), each at a Holm-adjusted p below 0.05 and surviving single deletions. The card is dead at 0.02 under its frozen rule. Exploratory analyses after the read show the drift on square seals alone and with format held fixed, the added length between the first and last sign, signs 806, 817 and 233 absent or nearly absent from the early third, and interior signs that become less like Mohenjo-daro's. The report states the successor reading, a fixed closing formula joined to a body that changed with time, and freezes three predictions for HARP's Period 3A–3C seals in CISI 3.1. PP1 is left untested with the card.
