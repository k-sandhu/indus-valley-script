# Find-site map and research overview (8 October 2026)

This folder holds two standalone pages built on 8 October 2026 at the user's request: an interactive map of the places where Indus-script inscribed objects have been found, and a slide deck presenting the project's findings in seven parts organised around research questions (101 slides), opening with background on the civilization for first-time readers. Both are written for readers outside the project: they omit session dates, internal file paths, registry and package codes, and tooling details, and the map shows full citations resolved from the census source lists in place of internal source keys. Status: Exploratory. No new statistical analysis was run.

## How the map values were derived

Places and census coordinates come from the 2026-10-06 evidence census. The census accepted a coordinate only with a site-specific witness, which left 30 places unlocated; 27 of them now carry a point from a cited gazetteer or report (Pleiades, the Pakistan Department of Archaeology and Museums gazetteer, Uesugi 2018, excavation reports, and Wikidata or Wikipedia as labelled last resorts). Wattoowala, Luristan and the "Dilmun" label remain unplaced.

"When found" is the earliest recorded fieldwork at a place (discovery, survey or excavation) from the census campaign records, not necessarily the year of the first inscribed find; where a source gives that year it is listed separately in the site panel.

"How old" uses the dated phases of each site's Indus-period layers, because the working corpus holds no calendar date for any single inscribed object. 70 places have phase dates from sources about that site; the others apply the Early (c. 3200–2600), Mature (2600–1900) and Late (1900–1300 BCE) Harappan convention to a phase a source identifies, and are drawn pale or dashed. Uncalibrated radiocarbon ranges are kept in the site notes and left off the time axis.

Several dating entries rest on search snippets, news reports or Wikipedia because the primary pages were blocked; each entry's source field says so. The research parts flagged these follow-ups: Surkotada and Kanmer radiocarbon dates not seen in the original; Bhirrana's published ages (Sarkar et al. 2016) far older than the regional convention; Nindowari's register coordinate about 78 km from other published points; the census Tell Abraq locator (Laursen Table 1 no. 121) appears to point to Tarut Island; the census reading of the Miri Qalat seal as Early Harappan conflicts with Bisht 2015 p. 23 (early Period IV).

The abroad-site table was generated by a helper script kept outside the repository; the JSON and notes are the record.
