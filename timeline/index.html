Where identical seal texts are found

The seal-text identity study tests whether recurring Indus seal texts behave like one owner's seals or like labels used across cities. Long seal texts almost never repeat. Copies of the 65 recurring texts are found together at Mohenjo-daro no more often than chance predicts, while some duplicates at smaller sites stay local. Identical texts share the animal in the seal's picture field only on unicorn seals, and most sealings (clay impressions of seals) that carry a seal text match seals from other cities. The synthesis of the 12 October tests reads these results together with the structure-first, Bronze Age and published-claim packages.

Structure before meaning

The October 12 diagnosis and its interactive map set out why the project had not reached a substantive result. The six-step program that followed measures families of graphically related signs, the pairing of texts with images, line divisions recorded in catalogues, stroke numerals and the concentration of a few signs at the ends of texts. It calibrates methods for dividing texts into words on readable scripts of Indus size, and records an independent verification and an adversarial review with responses. No language, sound value or meaning is assigned.

Tests of published decipherment and usage claims

The October 12 published-claim tests reproduce Yajnadevam's complete Sanskrit sign key, which assigns a sound to every sign, from the local Lipi copy. Random consonant strings pass our emulation of the key's dictionary-based readability criterion in 92% to 100% of cases. Under a Sanskrit language model the key's consonant sequences score far below Sanskrit text and 1.2 to 2.4 standard deviations above rearrangements of its own values, and their order gives no advantage. On synthetic corpora shaped like the Indus texts, a correct key in which several signs share a sound (a homophonic key) stands 27 to 72 standard deviations clear. Keys fitted to one half of the corpus fit the other half equally well under Sanskrit, Latin and Old Tamil models. Two further studies find that seals from West Asia and round seals of Gulf type carry unusual orders of common signs, and that the signs the key groups as homophones do not substitute for one another in the texts. No language is identified.

Four Bronze Age documentary comparisons

The October 12 manuscript and findings compare Sumerian, Akkadian, Egyptian and Mycenaean Greek records, observed in their primary editions, with the Indus Valley script (IVS). The exact tables hold the controls in which methods must recover known word categories, the preserved repairs to sources, and the number of candidate words that fit each Indus face. The work selects no historical Indus language.

2026-10-02

Current empirical position

The first investigation began October 1, 2026 and ran for four hours and two minutes. Its code and evidence paths are preserved under src/, research/, results/ and output/. The original README and import manifest are retained in the archive.

  • With each distinct text counted once, using the preceding signs improved the predictive log score by approximately 0.848 bits for every sign after the first, under the declared filters and controls.
  • When one known sign at a time is hidden and a model must name it, second-order context gives the exact sign as the first guess (top-one) 37.27% of the time, compared with 34.59% for first order and 33.81% for the earlier local model, on the same 8,009 hidden positions. The second-order model gives less probability to the correct sign, and tuning by that loss selects first order in every cross-validation fold.
  • An independent transcription of the same archaeological sources, in which a position may stand for a set of candidate signs, kept a positive gain from local context after excluding sources and texts that overlap with the training data.
  • Predicting how many signs are lost in a gap failed to beat always guessing one sign: 46.3% versus 51.1%.
  • Read as equations, a published numerical proposal has four solutions in positive integers before an additional archaeological range restriction is applied. The values selected under that restriction, 10, 8 and 6, depend on the proposal's unverified premises.

These measurements concern the prediction of sign codes in selected transcriptions, including artificially hidden signs and an alternative transcription of shared sources. The manuscript's claim-evidence map defines each population and outcome; the priority audit places the methods beside earlier research on Markov models, control of duplicate texts and administrative systems.

The continuation adds exact examples of sampling laws, controls on symbol mapping in known systems, studies of which numerical observations to acquire, and source frames, our tables of the objects in a publication, for Harappa, Lothal and Kanmer. At a nominal 5% threshold, simulated populations of literal copies are rejected 5.4% of the time when the chance comparison shuffles matched objects and 15.9% when it shuffles individual records; simulations with imperfectly recorded faces give 5% and 11%. A selector that chooses a record model from the reference alone recovers 309 of 384 simulated symbol maps, compared with 283 for graph mapping and 168 for slot mapping alone. Requiring a complete inventory raises numerical recovery from 133 to 190 of 200 simulated worlds with four incorrect local assignments. The session record links each experiment, its preserved failures and its independent review.

No top-level blanket licence is assigned to third-party material. See reuse status. The local import preserves acquired inputs and source copies; Git contains our written findings, reusable code, selected resources, summaries, and records of acquisition and provenance. A GitHub checkout alone therefore does not contain every input required for all original analyses.

Research memory rule

Before a new run, search the registry, the retrospectives and research/. Revisit an unsuccessful path when a named new dataset, method, corrected assumption or independent source makes the retry informative. Keep the old endpoint, the result the earlier run reported, and explain the change. Interesting imaginative proposals belong in the knowledge base with an explicit test and a status; they do not become readings by repetition.

Archived introduction

This repository preserves evidence, methods, manuscripts, exploratory ideas and failed approaches from an ongoing Indus script investigation. It currently contains no authenticated translation, sound value, language identification or decipherment.

The public research package retains the manuscripts, code, protocols and reviewed outputs. Acquired corpora, source photographs and downloaded source copies remain local. The public asset review records the source exclusions and preservation backups. Scripts run manually; no CI/CD workflows are included.
