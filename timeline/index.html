Where identical seal texts are found

The seal-text identity study tests whether recurring Indus seal texts behave like one owner's seals or like labels used across cities. Long seal texts almost never repeat. The 65 recurring texts are placed at Mohenjo-daro as freely as chance would place them, while some duplicates at smaller sites stay local; identical texts share the field animal only on unicorn seals; and most sealings carrying a seal text match seals from other cities. The synthesis of the 12 October tests reads these results together with the structure-first, Bronze Age and published-claim packages.

Structure before meaning

The October 12 diagnosis and its interactive map set out why the project had not reached a substantive result. The six-step program that followed measures sign families, image–text pairing, catalogued line divisions, stroke numerals and text-final concentration, calibrates segmentation on readable scripts at Indus scale, and records an independent verification and an adversarial review with responses. No language, sound value or meaning is assigned.

Tests of published decipherment and usage claims

The October 12 published-claim tests reproduce Yajnadevam's complete Sanskrit sign key from the local Lipi copy. Random consonant strings pass a lexical emulation of its readability criterion in 92% to 100% of cases. Under a Sanskrit language model its consonant sequences score far below Sanskrit text and 1.2 to 2.4 standard deviations above rearrangements of its own values, with no advantage in their order; on synthetic Indus-shaped corpora a correct homophonic key stands 27 to 72 standard deviations clear. Fitted keys transfer equally under Sanskrit, Latin and Old Tamil models. Two further studies find that West Asian and round Gulf-type seals carry unusual orders of common signs, and that the key's homophone groups have no substitution support. No language is identified.

Four Bronze Age documentary comparisons

The October 12 manuscript and findings compare source-observed Sumerian, Akkadian, Egyptian and Mycenaean Greek records with IVS. Known-category controls, preserved source repairs and per-face lexical ambiguity are linked to exact tables. The work selects no historical Indus language.

Excavation and overlooked settlement evidence

The October 11 settlement paper and 33-page PDF examine excavation counts, area estimates and archaeology concealed by later construction, alluvium and changing landscapes. A 72-record source register and 21 regions of interest support proposed comparisons; no new archaeological site or national find total is claimed. The research package keeps calculations, source observations, two actual Claude Code Opus 5.5 reviews, Codex checks and the final response together.

2026-10-02

Current empirical position

The first investigation began October 1, 2026 and ran for four hours and two minutes. Its code and evidence paths are preserved under src/, research/, results/ and output/. The original README and import manifest are retained in the archive.

  • In the unique-text evaluation, local sequential dependencies improved predictive log score by approximately 0.848 bits per noninitial sign under the declared filters and controls.
  • Artificial one-known-slot restoration reaches 37.27% exact top-one with second-order context, compared with 34.59% for first order and 33.81% for the earlier local model on the same 8,009 masks. Second order has greater probability loss; loss-based tuning selects first order in every fold.
  • A different annotation of shared archaeological sources retained a positive candidate-event score gain after source/text-overlap exclusions.
  • Unknown gap-count prediction failed to beat the constant-one control: 46.3% versus 51.1%.
  • A published numerical proposal has four positive-integer solutions before an additional archaeological range restriction. The selected 10/8/6 values remain conditional on the proposal's unverified premises.

These measurements describe graphic-code prediction in selected transcriptions, including artificial masking and alternate annotation of shared sources. The manuscript's claim-evidence map defines each population and outcome; the priority audit places the methods alongside earlier Markov, duplication-control and administrative-system research.

The current continuation also has exact sampling-law examples, known-system mapping controls, numerical acquisition studies and source frames for Harappa, Lothal and Kanmer. At a nominal 5% threshold, literal-copy simulations reject 5.4% with matched object references and 15.9% with record-wise references; imperfect-face simulations give 5% and 11%. A reference-only model selector recovers 309 of 384 simulated symbol maps, compared with 283 for graph mapping and 168 for slot mapping alone. A complete-inventory constraint increases numerical recovery from 133 to 190 of 200 worlds with four incorrect local assignments. The session record links each experiment, its preserved failures and its independent review.

No top-level blanket licence is assigned to third-party material. See reuse status. The local import preserves acquired inputs and source witnesses; Git contains our written findings, reusable code, selected resources, summaries, and acquisition/provenance records. A GitHub checkout alone therefore does not contain every input required for all original analyses.

Research memory rule

Before a new run, search the registry, the retrospectives and research/. Revisit an unsuccessful path when a named new dataset, method, corrected assumption or independent source makes the retry informative. Keep the old endpoint and explain the change. Interesting imaginative proposals belong in the knowledge base with an explicit test and a status; they do not become readings by repetition.

Archived introduction

This repository preserves evidence, methods, manuscripts, exploratory ideas and failed approaches from an ongoing Indus script investigation. It currently contains no authenticated translation, sound value, language identification or decipherment.

The public research package retains the manuscripts, code, protocols and reviewed outputs. Acquired corpora, source photographs and downloaded witnesses remain local. The public asset review records the source exclusions and preservation backups. Scripts run manually; no CI/CD workflows are included.
