# Third-party material in the public research record

The project preserves the source-specific rights and qualifications in [REDISTRIBUTION.md](knowledge/provenance/REDISTRIBUTION.md) and the [October 11 asset review](experiments/2026-10-11-public-release/ASSET-REVIEW.md). Its downloaded source corpus, unlicensed font, papers and archaeological photographs remain local.

The chart atlas, selected digitization crops and new catalogue detail crops derive from Kimmo Koskenniemi and Asko Parpola, *Corpus of Texts in the Indus Script* (1979), the [University of Helsinki edition](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/corpus-of-texts-in-the-indus-script/), licensed [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The recorded source SHA256 is `7bfc35f2a8bbcb58f13c42e1835f5b3ebd17a1f9947786b6c5933623df83cf27`. Transformations include cropping, code/class annotations and model predictions. Ambiguous chart numbers and crosswalk variants retain their qualifications. The new [crop manifest](experiments/2026-10-11-bonta-adversarial/run-v6/chart-crop-manifest.json) identifies every focused drawing's parent and rectangle.

The join table of the [Mackay and Marshall package](experiments/2026-10-13-mackay-marshall-tables/README.md) carries values from Nitin Stephen Koshy, "Indus script: seal catalogues and text-structure analysis" (2026), https://github.com/epachayan/indus-script, commit `5c14a01`: machine-readable transcriptions of the seal tabulations in E. J. H. Mackay, *Further Excavations at Mohenjo-daro* (1938) and J. Marshall, *Mohenjo-daro and the Indus Civilization* (1931), licensed [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Those values keep that licence and attribution and are outside the CC0 dedication; the package's verification files also quote about fifteen individual Lipi metadata values as evidence for specific join decisions, which likewise stay outside the CC0 dedication.

Selected plate-selection/restoration metadata and Mayig-derived examples use the pinned [Mayig corpus](https://github.com/mayig/indus-valley-script-corpus/tree/ad2f1e218a34b8c33c57de0d6cb8d99272765bbb), copyright Michael Carlson 2024, under its [MIT notice](LICENSES/Mayig-MIT.md). Its licence does not grant rights in separately sourced archaeological photographs.

The October 11 sign catalogue publishes twenty short, attributed Lipi code windows, at most three consecutive codes each, to identify the inscriptions supporting specific reviewed hypotheses. The [example ledger](experiments/2026-10-11-bonta-adversarial/run-v6/example-provenance.json) supplies the purpose, pin, record and source CISI identifiers. The October 12 anchor-expansion catalogues add 44 distinct attributed examples of two or three codes, 42 of them new, with the same ledger fields (`experiments/2026-10-12-anchor-expansion/run-v*/example-provenance.json`). The published-claim tests list twenty frequent signs with their letter in the Lipi key under test (`experiments/2026-10-12-published-claim-tests/sanskrit-key/stage3f/summary.json`). No bulk Lipi corpus, key or font is included. These source-derived excerpts remain outside the CC0 dedication and every other project licence.

Bibliographic records, URLs, hashes and authored source summaries identify further dependencies. Quoted source wording retains the source's rights. Sanskrit sense paraphrases point to selected Monier-Williams 1899 dictionary entries in the pinned Cologne edition; the full digital index is excluded. Twelve demonstration rows in `experiments/2026-10-12-published-claim-tests/sanskrit-key/stage4/demo.json` quote 115 short first-gloss fragments (78 distinct, at most 60 characters each) from the Cologne Digital Sanskrit Dictionaries edition of Monier-Williams (csl-orig), licensed [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/); those fragments keep that licence, with attribution to the Cologne Digital Sanskrit Dictionaries, and are outside the CC0 dedication.

The satellite and terrain screening of 12 October derives its tracked aggregates from Copernicus Sentinel-2 data, the Copernicus GLO-30 digital elevation model and ESA WorldCover (CC BY 4.0), with notices in [its package](experiments/2026-10-12-satellite-prospection/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md); those derivatives keep their source attribution and are outside the CC0 dedication. Precise locations of its candidate anomalies are withheld from the repository. Natural Earth basemap files in that package are in the public domain.
