F-0005: Estimating the number of lost signs does worse than always guessing one

status class
failed
area
structure

Finding

Our estimate of how many signs were lost in an artificial gap was right in 46.30% of tests; always guessing one sign was right in 51.07%.

Evidence

  • research/methods/results/gap_design_matched/gap_design_matched.json, JSON field /design_matched_models/local/exact_count_accuracy.
  • research/methods/results/gap_design_matched/gap_design_matched.json, JSON field /design_matched_models/local/constant_one_glyph_design_control_accuracy.

Scope

Each test erased one to three signs inside a text. The estimator reads the signs on both sides through a sign-to-sign transition model, corrected for the rule that drew gap sizes. No physical evidence, such as gap width or traces, was available, and longer gaps were excluded.
