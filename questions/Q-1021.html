Question

For every distinct chronology label in the local export (the Lipi transcription corpus; fields time, period, phase), what calendar interval does the excavator's dating model give, on what basis, and with what uncertainty?

Why it matters

No local object has a calendar date: the time, period and phase fields carry 21, 57 and 38 distinct site-specific labels (periods of the Harappa Archaeological Research Project, HARP, such as Period 3C, Mohenjo-daro levels such as Late, strata such as Stratum IV), and 55.8% of objects have at least one of them. A mapping table converts these into intervals that can be compared across sites, while keeping each label's own sequence and datum. Tests of change over time, including conjecture C-0005's prediction about tablet tiers across Harappa periods, depend on it.

What is needed

Read DATA.md. List each distinct label with its site and object count from the local export. For each, find the excavator's phasing and its published radiocarbon or comparative dating: HARP publications for Harappa (Kenoyer and Meadow; Meadow and Kenoyer), Marshall, Mackay and the Aachen work for Mohenjo-daro, Bisht for Dholavira, the Kalibangan and Lothal reports. Record interval, basis, citation with page, and whether the label is a deposit, a building level or a cultural period. Labels with the same name at different sites stay separate.

How to decide

Done when every label carrying at least five objects has an interval with a citation or a recorded reason why none exists, and the table reports how many objects gain a calendar interval. Rare labels can stay unmapped with a reason.

Prior attempts

The chronology-region study compared periods inside Harappa without calendar mapping; the backward-chronology assembly dated later continuity evidence, not the Indus objects themselves.
