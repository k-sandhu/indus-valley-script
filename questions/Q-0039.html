Question

Can complete phonetic keys, rebus proposals and meaning maps be evaluated with declared choices, checked lexical inputs, known-answer controls and predictions withheld from proposal construction?

Why it matters

The shared bench already scores several proposal types. The latest rebus audits identify frequency matching, ambiguous lexical forms and incomplete compound extraction as obstacles to a fair comparison. A comparison needs to account for the choices each proposal permits as well as the observations it predicts.

What is needed

Repair exact lexical matching in a new dated package and test normalization against explicit ambiguous cases. Preserve the earlier frozen results. Record the compound evidence that requires source-page or specialist review. Specify participant inputs, permitted alternatives, dictionary coverage, controls and outcome rules before any new confirmation read. Join object-level predictions to the versioned register and keep aliases and repeated texts together when splitting observations.

How to decide

The first stage is complete when input checks and synthetic bench controls pass, every known data defect has a disposition, and the participant contract states what evidence blocks a real comparison. A substantive comparison additionally requires checked real known-script controls, comparable proposal freedom and a defensible confirmation cohort. Failure of the controls stops the comparison before an Indus conclusion is drawn.

Prior attempts

Read the bench guide, the field review, and the launch plan. A new seed or repaired computation on examined material does not supply a fresh test.
