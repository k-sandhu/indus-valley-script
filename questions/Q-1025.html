Question

Which published studies give per-seal measurements or carving-style attributes, under what terms are their data available, and how many local seals would they describe?

Why it matters

Seal size and carving style test workshop and affiliation hypotheses, including conjecture C-0003 on unicorn seals and the seal-identity prediction of C-0004. Local seals have all three dimensions for 43.0% of objects, and no carving-style attributes. Candidate sources found on 12 October 2026: Jamison, Uesugi and Yamaguchi 2023, "Unicorns and seals: New multivariate approaches to exploring patterned stylistic variation in the Indus Civilization" (Archaeological Research in Asia 36, article 100486; Elsevier, supplementary files not seen); Konasukawa and Koiso, "The size of Indus seals and its significance", in Walking with the Unicorn (Archaeopress 2018); and a public GitHub repository (dmahesh2869/harappan-seal-analysis) with per-seal unicorn attribute files that a search snippet attributes to Jamison's 2017 dissertation, with no licence asserted.

What is needed

Read DATA.md. Obtain the articles through a library or the publishers, check for supplementary tables and their terms, and confirm the provenance of the GitHub attribute files against the dissertation before any use. Record which seals each dataset identifies by CISI number (its entry in the Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions) or by excavation number.

How to decide

Done when each candidate has a recorded data status (held with terms, available on request, or none), and the held datasets are joined to local seals, each match recorded with its basis, with a count of the seals covered.

Prior attempts

The sign-context and seal study measured motif concentration and size classes from catalogue dimensions and named calibrated seal dimensions as the missing input.
