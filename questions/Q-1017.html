Question

For the Harappa objects excavated by the Harappa Archaeological Research Project since 1986, what find unit, lot, trench, deposit type, period and associated finds does the excavators' record give, and can those records be obtained for all inscribed objects?

Why it matters

Harappa holds 1,921 local objects, the largest site population. They carry HARP period labels (71.8% have a chronology label) but only 14.1% have all three dimensions and 38.6% a depth, and no object has a lot, deposit type or associated assemblage. Conjecture C-0005 predicts that tablets with the same descriptor and tier were found together, which needs find contexts. Kenoyer and Meadow's 2010 introduction already distinguishes item and lot identifiers and flags secondary-dump deposits, so the structure exists in the excavators' records.

What is needed

Read DATA.md. Inputs held: the two versions of Kenoyer and Meadow 2010 (15 and 17 pages) and the Harappa tablet frame. Not held: the CISI 3.1 catalogue (Q-1008) and the HARP field registers. List which context fields the published sources give per object, then prepare, for the owner's decision, a data request to the HARP directors describing the fields, the object list and the intended use. Record the access outcome in the manifest.

How to decide

Done when the published sources' context fields are tabulated for every Harappa object they cover, and a request has been sent with the owner's approval and its answer recorded, or the owner has declined. A partial register (published contexts only) counts as progress and is reported with its coverage.

Prior attempts

The October context audit and the Harappa short-record study used published contexts for 22 tablets; the registered-view audit recovered a dated Harappa collection statement but no register.
