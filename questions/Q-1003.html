Question

Mackay (1938) and Marshall (1931) each printed a table of the seals from their Mohenjo-daro excavations; Q-1002 acquired transcriptions of both. Which table rows describe which objects in the Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions (CISI, whose Mohenjo-daro numbers begin with M-) and in Lipi, our local transcription corpus? Joining a row means matching it to its object with a recorded basis; what size, material, findspot and level does each joined object gain?

Why it matters

The excavation tables carry field numbers (the numbers given to finds when they were excavated) and findspots that the local export holds only in part. Joined, they give Mohenjo-daro seals an excavation registration, a findspot within a block and house, a level measured from a stated datum (the excavator's fixed reference height) and a size from the excavator. The project's evidence census already used Marshall's p. 402 table to confirm two joins between British Museum records and local objects, so the route is known to work.

What is needed

Read DATA.md. Inputs: the tables acquired under Q-1002; the local Lipi excavation-idno field, which carries strings such as DK field numbers for Mohenjo-daro; the CISI 1 and CISI 2 basic-data sections (Q-1005, Q-1006), the catalogue tables that pair each CISI number with its excavation number; the held Marshall 1931 scan; and Mackay 1938, which is not held but is scanned on the Internet Archive (entry mackay-1938 in the project's data manifest). The tables are AI transcriptions, so every sampled join is checked against the printed table, not only against the CSV. Keep the excavator's units: Mackay's sizes are in inches and his levels are relative to the DK datum, and Marshall's depths are below the modern surface. Store every join with its basis (exact field number, normalized field number, published concordance) and keep ambiguous candidates separate.

How to decide

Done when every table row has a recorded outcome (joined to one CISI number, a set of candidate objects, or unmatched with a reason) and a coverage table reports how many Mohenjo-daro objects gain each field. Check a random sample of 30 joins against the printed tables and CISI plates before reporting; stop and revise the join rule if more than two of the 30 are wrong.

Prior attempts

No join of these tables has been attempted. The object ledger pilot (a trial version of the planned object register) and the museum batch of the census joined a few Mohenjo-daro objects through Marshall's table by hand.

13 October 2026, package. Every one of the 1,269 rows has an outcome (row-dispositions.csv): 1,108 exact joins, 38 confirmed, 2 ambiguous and 121 unmatched with a reason. The joined rows cover 1,134 Lipi families (the export's groups of records for one object) and CISI numbers, 1,122 of them seals (85.1% of the 1,318 Mohenjo-daro seals). The join key is the export's registration string, which writes the field number followed by the excavator's catalogue number; the CISI basic data served as the check. Of 30 randomly sampled joins checked against the printed tables and the CISI excavation numbers, 29 are correct, none wrong and one unresolved, within the card's limit of two errors. Mohenjo-daro objects gain both face dimensions (+12), material (+5), a spatial unit (+10) and a level (+2). In addition, 1,134 gain a field number separated from the catalogue number and a reference to the excavator's table, 1,091 a size traced to the printed table in inches and 1,101 a level with its datum. The rules were amended three times after development runs (registration forms, variant keys, adjudication of contradicted candidates against the print); the amendments are in the package protocol.

Remaining items, for a successor card:

  • Mackay No. 181: the printed table gives DK 14921, CISI gives DK 11492 for M-1195, and the export joins them. Compare Mackay's vol. II plate photograph of No. 181 with the CISI photograph of M-1195.
  • Two ambiguous rows: Mackay No. 434 (two Lipi families, 2301 and 4045, carry the same registration DK5081434) and Marshall No. 483 (registration only; the print gives yellow faience, the export steatite).
  • Mackay's plate volume is not held, and objects numbered above M-1659 (CISI 3.1) have no catalogue entry here, so joins to them were checked on the print and the registration alone.
  • The 14 differences between the transcription and the print (print-corrections.csv) are recorded but not applied to the join table.

13 October 2026, follow-up (verification). The CISI basic data gives an independent check on this join. Each CISI entry carries an FC concordance number, its number in the Helsinki concordance of Indus texts. For Mohenjo-daro that number is 1000 plus Marshall's catalogue number or 2000 plus Mackay's, and it agrees with the joined row for 216 of 219 objects in M-621 to M-947. The three exceptions, with M-655, are two pairs of seals whose records CISI 2 assigns the other way round from Lipi; measured on the CISI 2 plates, all four seals fit the sizes of the rows this join gives them, so the join stands and CISI 2's basic data has the pairs crossed.
