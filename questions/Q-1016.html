Question

What do the Kalibangan final reports record for each inscribed object (registration, findspot, stratum, period, material, dimensions), and how do those records match the 197 local Kalibangan objects and the 122 entries in CISI 1, the volume of the Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions for collections in India?

Why it matters

Every local Kalibangan object has a CISI number, but context is weak: 25.9% of objects have a depth and 36.0% a shape. The project's evidence census found 26 CISI 1 Kalibangan entries with no local match. The final report gives the excavators' stratigraphy for the Early and Mature phases, which the chronology fields need.

What is needed

Read DATA.md. Held: Excavations at Kalibangan: The Harappans (1960–69), Part II (ASI PDF, 876 pages) at data/raw/2026-10-06-evidence-census/sites-deep/c-north-india/kalibangan_report2020.pdf. Not held: The Early Harappans (1960–1969) (2003, MASI 98), which is scanned on the Internet Archive; Part I of The Harappans, known only from a PDF address in a search result and a bookseller listing; and the Kalibangan section of CISI 3.2 (Q-1009). Acquire the 2003 volume and, once confirmed, Part I; record each in the project's data manifest with its hash; locate the seal, sealing and inscribed-object tables; and transcribe them with page locators. Keep transcriptions local.

How to decide

Done when the report's inscribed objects are tabulated with locators, each is joined (matched with a recorded basis) to a local object and CISI number or listed as unmatched, and a coverage table reports gains in stratum, period and dimensions. Unresolved joins are listed with the evidence that blocks them.

Prior attempts

The north-India batch of the census identified the reports and read selected pages for counts; it did not extract an object table.
