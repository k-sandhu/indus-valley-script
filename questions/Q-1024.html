Question

Which inscribed Indus and Indus-style objects have been found in Mesopotamia, Iran, the Gulf and Central Asia, and what registration, findspot, layer, date, material and dimensions does the primary publication give for each?

Why it matters

Finds abroad carry dated contexts that South Asian finds often lack, which makes them anchors for chronology. The local corpus has 42 faces from these sites, and the census could not compute coverage fractions because the published populations mix Indus inscriptions with local seals. No consolidated catalogue of Indus-type seals in the Gulf and Mesopotamia was found on 12 October 2026, and the published CISI titles do not name those regions.

What is needed

Read DATA.md. Held: Gadd 1932 on the Ur seals, Laursen 2010 on Gulf-type seals, Nippur III, the 2005 Gonur volume, the 2016 Salūt paper and the CISI 3.3 excerpt. Not held: Kjærum 1983, Failaka/Dilmun. The Second Millennium Settlements, Volume 1:1: The Stamp and Cylinder Seals (print, Jutland Archaeological Society), the SAHI Tell Jokha report with Fig. 6.61, and the CISI 3.3 plates (Q-1010). Tabulate each object with its primary locator and classify it as Indus inscription, Indus-style seal without script, or local seal with Indus traits.

How to decide

Done when every object named in the census's foreign-find rows has a row with class, locator and context fields, or a recorded reason why the primary source is unavailable.

Prior attempts

The foreign-find census, the Tell Jokha lead, the Oxus recovery and the Susa audit each covered part of this population.
